# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 6 (transition 6T)

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [French Immersion Language Arts](../README.md) > Transition 6T

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Late French Immersion at its Grade 6 entry point. The official French compilation identifies this curriculum as *6e année tardive* [S2]. This record is distinct from [regular Grade 6 Early Immersion language arts](../curriculum.md), Core French, and [Francophone first-language arts](../../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary source is French (`fr`). This original synthesis is neither an authorized translation nor a French classroom resource. The source locators below are English descriptions of French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Build the ability to understand and communicate familiar meanings in French, using story clues, language patterns, questions, and cultural connections.

**Prerequisites:** Literacy, conversation, and storytelling experience in languages a learner already uses may help. This reference does not require prior French proficiency or establish program-admission criteria.

**Materials:** Use the linked official source for the full expectations. Accessible French picture books and youth novels, together with definition, translation, and verb-reference tools, can support teaching; no particular publication is required by this reference.

**How to use:** Pair this entry-specific curriculum with the [Grade 6 program overview](../../README.md), not with an assumed translation of English Language Arts. Local IDs support mappings to this summary and are not ministry standard codes.

The target grade is **6**; **6T** identifies the distinct Late Immersion entry, not an extra grade or a shared 6-7 band. The content elaboration names picture books with simple accompanying sentences and youth novels. The ministry recommends an entirely French first year of Late Immersion [S3]. Section 5 of the required-areas order [S4] removes the obligation to offer English Language Arts to Grade 6 Late Immersion students; it does not prohibit offering it. Confirm local delivery rather than treating the Early Immersion ELA timetable as universal.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Trying unfamiliar French and expanding vocabulary create more possibilities for communication and thought.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), vocabulary and thinking; risk-taking in a new language.

### BI-02

Finding connections with one's own culture provides an entry into understanding another culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), discovering culture through similarities.

### BI-03

Understanding involves anticipating what a text may reveal and learning the language's shared codes and conventions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), clues that anticipate plot; effective communication through language codes.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use key words to grasp a text's overall meaning. Respond with inferences and predictions, and ask questions that check, clarify, or extend understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), general meaning and key words; Créer et communiquer, questions and responses using inferences/predictions.

### CC-02

Trace how one story event influences another. Locate the action in time and place using clues in the text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), cause/effect in narrative; place and time indicators and setting elaboration.

### CC-03

Notice both cultural similarities and differences. Connect themes and cultural elements in Francophone and Indigenous texts with one's own experiences of culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), similarities/differences between cultures; themes in Francophone and Indigenous texts.

### CC-04

Describe everyday situations and give reasons for opinions about familiar topics. Practise clear articulation and pronunciation when reading.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), familiar opinions, everyday descriptions, and articulated/pronounced reading.

### CC-05

Identify the groups of words that form a sentence. Distinguish and use reference tools for explaining meanings and translating rather than assuming they serve the same purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), sentence structure and word groups; definition/translation reference tools and elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Learn active listening and willingness to attempt communication in French, alongside cultural elements encountered through learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), communication strategies and cultural elements; active-listening elaboration.

### C-02

Recognize characters, settings, and events within stories. Understand basic narrative and information-text organization, using time and place markers to keep meaning connected.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), story elements; narrative/information structures and place/time markers with elaborations.

### C-03

Attend to French phonemes, silent letters, and liaison. Develop spelling patterns involving gender and number agreement of nouns and adjectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), conscience phonologique; régularités orthographiques and agreement elaboration.

### C-04

Build simple and compound sentences, questions, and connections between ideas. Recognize word classes and use roots, prefixes, and suffixes as language tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), outils langagiers: linking words, sentence/question structures, word classes, roots and affixes.

### C-05

Work with verb forms suited to the studied texts. The elaboration specifies present-tense auxiliary and modal verbs, and first- and second-group verbs in the present and near future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), modes et temps verbaux and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full French learning standards and embedded elaborations were reviewed at [S1]. Oral, written, and visual texts may use print, digital, or multimedia supports. The introductory picture-book context does not mean the learner's grade or interests should be treated as those of a younger child.

The language-tools elaborations identify nouns, prepositions, adjectives, pronouns, and adverbs, and illustrate questions with *est-ce que* and question words. Consult the source for its exact terminology. Do not replace these entry-specific tools with the regular Grade 6 emphasis on *imparfait* and *passé composé*, or import the separate 7T writing expectations.

This is a selective synthesis, not a complete vocabulary list, translated worksheet, or prescribed sequence. A named dictionary or conjugation reference in an elaboration is an example, not a purchase requirement. Read definitions and translations critically in context.

The cultural comparison expectation includes Indigenous texts; it does not make Indigenous cultures interchangeable with each other or with Francophone cultures. A source-linked summary gives no permission to copy, translate, or adapt a community's cultural works.

Early and Late Immersion share many non-language provincial subject expectations, but this does not make their Grade 6 language-arts records equivalent. Keep **6** and **6T** separate in navigation and resource mappings. Provincial policy's first-year recommendation and legal ELA exception [S3] and [S4] are distinct from a particular district's timetable.
