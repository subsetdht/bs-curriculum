# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../README.md) > [French Immersion Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. This is the regular Grade 6 entry, not the [Late Immersion transition 6T record](transition-6t/curriculum.md), Core French, or [Francophone first-language arts](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md). The official compilation [S2] distinguishes regular sixth-year expectations from *6e année tardive*.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary curriculum is French (`fr`). This is an original reference summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators give English descriptions of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and discuss French texts with growing independence, and produce imaginative, coherent communication while considering cultural perspectives and the effects of language choices.

**Prerequisites:** Experience discussing French stories and drafting French texts is helpful, not an admission requirement or a proficiency test established by this reference.

**Materials:** The linked official curriculum is sufficient for using this reference. For teaching, select accessible, appropriately licensed French texts and reference tools; this page does not prescribe books or purchases.

**How to use:** Read the three curriculum components together and consult [S1] for the complete wording and elaborations. Local IDs support resource mappings; they are not official BC standard codes or a teaching sequence.

These are Grade 6 standards, not a K-9 grade band merely because [S2] is a multi-grade download. The source's literary contexts are youth novels and legends. Early Immersion also includes English Language Arts at this grade: provincial policy [S3] and the required-areas order, section 4(c) [S4], address that separate subject. Do not transfer the Grade 6 Late Immersion exception in section 5 to this pathway.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

An author's vocabulary and style shape a message's effects; attention to language form helps make that message coherent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), word choice and authorial style; reflection on language form.

### BI-02

Questioning connects ideas and develops critical thought. Encounters with other cultures also invite learners to reconsider familiar customs and values.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), questioning and connections; discovery of cultures and one's own values.

### BI-03

Authors draw on experience and imagination to create worlds that readers or listeners can enter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), the author's distinctive world; elaboration on authors of oral and written messages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret texts independently, respond to them, and revise initial hypotheses as understanding develops. Restating the main idea in one's own words helps make that understanding clear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), independent interpretation and adjusted hypotheses; Créer et communiquer, reformulating the main idea.

### CC-02

Distinguish imagined events from reality in the studied genres. Use time and place clues to explain events, and consider how different ways of telling a story affect its audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), fiction/reality, spatial-temporal indicators, and ways of telling a story.

### CC-03

Compare cultural and historical references in texts, including Indigenous texts, with one's own cultural and historical points of reference.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), cultural and historical elements, including Indigenous texts.

### CC-04

Contribute to exchanges in ways that value others' ideas and broaden both personal and shared perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), valuing others' ideas in expression.

### CC-05

Develop imaginative writing into organized, coherent texts. Use verb tenses purposefully and revise with the spelling and grammar conventions already learned.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), creative writing, coherent structured composition and its verb-tense elaboration, and revision.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how youth novels and legends work as narratives. Attend to characters and events, the development and resolution of a story, and the transformation around which a legend is structured.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), outils littéraires: youth novel and legend; organisation textuelle: narrative and legend structures.

### C-02

Recognize cultural and historical elements alongside features of Indigenous oral traditions. The source's elaboration includes oral narrative, song, and cyclical thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), cultural/historical elements; literary tools: Indigenous oral traditions and elaboration.

### C-03

Understand how information develops through a text and how linking expressions and time/place indicators connect ideas and orient the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), information-text structure, transitions between ideas, and spatial-temporal indicators.

### C-04

Use communication strategies that combine engaged listening with clarification, explanation, and consideration of another person's perspective.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), stratégies de communication and active-listening elaboration.

### C-05

Develop temporal coherence, particularly through the relationship between the *imparfait* and *passé composé*. Word roots and affixes support language work; rereading and reference tools support revision.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), outils langagiers: temporal coherence, roots and affixes; stratégies de révision and elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full French curriculum and its embedded elaborations were reviewed at [S1]. Texts include oral, written, and visual forms, with digital, print, and multimedia supports; the youth-novel and legend contexts do not restrict learning to printed books.

This selective synthesis does not provide an exhaustive grammar checklist, reading-level scale, prescribed legend collection, or assessment schedule. Retain the precise tense distinction in [C-05](#c-05); do not import the different introductory verb expectations of 6T or later-grade requirements. Elaboration examples clarify learning rather than mandate particular reference books.

Indigenous cultural and historical perspectives remain part of this language-arts curriculum. The examples of oral tradition are not claims that all First Peoples share one storytelling form. Select works with appropriate attribution, community context, and permissions; a curricular reference does not authorize retelling, translating, or adapting a community's stories or songs.

Regular 6 and transition 6T are separately sourced entries, not interchangeable difficulty settings. Provincial policy differentiates Early and Late Immersion entry and English-instruction timing [S3] and [S4]; local timetables and individual placement must be confirmed with the school or district.
