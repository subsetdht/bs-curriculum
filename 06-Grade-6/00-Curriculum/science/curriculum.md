# Science - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > Science

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared non-language expectations under [S2], sections 1-2. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for instruction-language distinctions and applicable exceptions.

**Document language:** English; classroom language is a separate program decision.

**Learning objective:** Investigate body systems, mixtures, force and motion, and the solar system using evidence, supported investigation planning, and attention to place and consequences.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience observing, measuring, and explaining natural phenomena is helpful, not a formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Any investigation needs its own accessible equipment, supervision, risk assessment, and safe procedures.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for official standards and elaborations. This record groups related expectations, not experiments or assessment tasks. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

The life of a multicellular organism depends on internal systems that support survival, reproduction, and responses to its surroundings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, internal systems of multicellular organisms.

### BI-02

Many familiar materials contain more than one substance; understanding a mixture involves considering its components and their properties.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, everyday materials as mixtures.

### BI-03

Newton's laws provide a framework for explaining how forces relate to changes in motion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, three laws of motion.

### BI-04

Our solar system belongs to a galaxy within a much larger universe. Understanding that nesting changes how we think about scale and Earth's place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, solar system, Milky Way, and billions of galaxies.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Sustain curiosity, observe in familiar and unfamiliar settings, and turn questions or problems into possible scientific inquiries. Make predictions that can later be compared with findings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Questioning and predicting, curiosity, observations, inquiry questions, and predictions.

### CC-02

With support, plan an investigation, decide what to change and measure in a fair test, and select useful data. Use appropriate tools to observe, measure, and record, while identifying risks and handling equipment safely.

**Source locator:** [S1], Planning and conducting, supported planning, variables, data collection, tools, and safety.

### CC-03

Interpret experiences of the local environment and identify First Peoples knowledge and perspectives as information sources. Represent patterns in data, compare findings with predictions, and develop explanations while remaining open to alternatives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Processing and analyzing data and information, local environment, First Peoples knowledge, representations, patterns, and explanations.

### CC-04

Judge whether an investigation was fair, identify potential errors, and suggest better methods. Recognize assumptions in secondary sources, attend to evidence, and consider social, ethical, and environmental implications of findings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Evaluating, fair tests, error, improvements, assumptions, evidence, and implications.

### CC-05

Apply learning in new settings, develop or refine ideas, and contribute to collaborative design and care for people and community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applying and innovating, care, cooperative design, transfer, and problem solving.

### CC-06

Communicate explanations and the processes used to reach them in different forms. Reflect on personal and shared experiences of place, including how place influences inquiry and communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating, ideas/explanations/processes and experiences of place.

## Content

### C-01

Study the basic structures and functions of the excretory, reproductive, hormonal, and nervous systems. Relate the systems to how a body maintains life and responds to information; the elaboration also includes First Peoples understandings of human and animal body systems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, basic structures and functions of body systems and its elaboration.

### C-02

Recognize heterogeneous mixtures and explain separation in terms of differences in component properties, such as density or particle size. Include local First Peoples knowledge of separation and extraction methods, with historical and contemporary contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, heterogeneous mixtures, separating mixtures, and local First Peoples separation/extraction knowledge.

### C-03

Use Newton's three laws to reason about motion, including the effects of balanced and unbalanced forces in daily physical activity. Understand gravity as an attraction between objects, not merely a name for falling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Newton's laws, balanced/unbalanced forces, and gravity.

### C-04

Place the solar system within the universe's overall structure, scale, and age. Study the solar system's components and their positions and motion, including First Peoples perspectives on the aurora borealis and other celestial phenomena.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, universe, solar system, and First Peoples perspectives on celestial phenomena.

### C-05

Investigate extreme environments in our galaxy. The elaboration connects exploration with technological adaptations and Canadian contributions, rather than prescribing a particular mission or device.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, extreme environments in our galaxy and its exploration-technology elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 6 names particular body systems; this summary is not an exhaustive anatomy syllabus. Planning investigations is explicitly **with support**, and identifying a variable for a fair test does not mean independent mastery of advanced experimental design.

Keep force distinctions clear: a balanced set of forces on one object and a third-law interaction between objects are different relationships. The record does not introduce a required algebraic mechanics course.

The source's examples explain mixtures, exploration, and other concepts; they are not directions to extract medicines, reproduce cultural accounts, handle unsafe substances, or copy a fixed experiment. First Peoples knowledge belongs within the relevant science topics and inquiry processes, with appropriate local guidance and permissions. The complete elaborations remain at [S1].
