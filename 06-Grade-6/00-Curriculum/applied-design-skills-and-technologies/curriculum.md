# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared non-language expectations under [S2], sections 1-2. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for instruction-language distinctions and applicable exceptions.

**Document language:** English; classroom instruction language is separate.

**Learning objective:** Develop useful designs by understanding users, making and improving prototypes, acquiring relevant skills, and considering the effects of tools and technologies.

**Prerequisites:** Prior opportunities to design, make, and reflect can help; no prior coding experience, workshop course, or equipment ownership is required by this reference.

**Materials:** No materials are needed to read the record. Chosen modules require their own equipment, accessibility arrangements, supervision, and physical/digital safety procedures.

**How to use:** [S1] explicitly uses **ADST 6-7** expectations. Students experience **at least three modules in each of Grades 6 and 7**, not three spread over both years. Schools may select from the twelve provincial modules or use locally developed content with the 6-7 competencies, either in addition to or instead of provincial modules. The Content entries below describe choices, not twelve compulsory courses. Local IDs are repository references, not official ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding a need gives design a purpose and a basis for judging whether a solution is useful.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, design responding to identified needs.

### BI-02

More complex making calls for learning new skills and sometimes combining several tools or technologies, rather than relying on one familiar technique.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, additional skills and multiple tools/technologies for complex tasks.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Learn about potential users through empathy, identify a worthwhile design opportunity, and define user requirements, success criteria, and constraints. Use these to compare possible ideas and consider ethical, social, and environmental effects before choosing a direction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Use relevant information and expertise to plan stages and resources, explore materials, and build an initial version. Keep track of iterations; seek feedback, troubleshoot, and retest to improve the design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Choose suitable materials, tools, and technologies for production, follow and revise a production plan, and limit material waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Making.

### CC-04

Select an audience and way to share a product, explain the reasoning behind its development, and evaluate it against the original criteria and intended contribution. Reflect on individual and cooperative work, including the shared workspace, and identify further design issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Sharing.

### CC-05

Recognize precautionary and emergency procedures in physical and digital settings. Assess the skills needed by an individual or group for a particular task and develop them as necessary.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Skills, safety and evaluating/developing skills.

### CC-06

Select and learn appropriate technologies while examining intended and unintended consequences of their use. Consider how land, resources, and culture shape the tools people develop and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Technologies, capability, impacts, and land/resources/culture.

## Content

### C-01

**Computational Thinking** explores ways to represent a problem or data, express a simple algorithm, and use visual programming. It also situates programming languages in their historical development.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Computational Thinking module.

### C-02

**Computers and Communications Devices** concerns how hardware, software, networks, and input/output devices work together, including troubleshooting, accessible devices, ergonomics, and effective keyboard use. **Digital Literacy** instead focuses on participation: safety and privacy, digital identity and relationships, ethical use and credit, organizing media, evaluating search results, and learning networks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Computers and Communications Devices and Digital Literacy modules.

### C-03

**Drafting** represents a design through sketches, technical plans, and introductory computer-aided drawing. **Media Arts** uses digital and non-digital forms to communicate through images, sound, and text, including capturing and editing material and considering how media shape expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Drafting and Media Arts modules.

### C-04

**Entrepreneurship and Marketing** connects a product or service with a need and a potential market. It includes decisions about branding and pricing alongside basic budgeting and financial records; seeking profit is a choice to examine, not a requirement for every design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Entrepreneurship and Marketing module.

### C-05

**Food Studies** joins safe, basic preparation with decisions about ingredients and eating. Nutrition, ingredient function, dietary restrictions, cost, access, and family or cultural influences all help explain food choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Food Studies module.

### C-06

**Metalwork** and **Woodwork** examine the properties and uses of their respective materials and the hand-tool processes suited to them. Metalwork includes the non-renewable nature of metal resources; Woodwork includes basic joinery and local cultural and economic uses of wood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Metalwork and Woodwork modules.

### C-07

**Power Technology** studies energy forms and transformations, conservation of energy, and power as the rate of transformation. **Robotics** connects sensing, control, and action in automated machines, including movement, programming logic, uses, and different platforms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Power Technology and Robotics modules.

### C-08

**Textiles** considers the range of textile uses and materials, ways to construct or repair items by hand, and the practical choices consumers make about suitability, cost, availability, and care.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Textiles module.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The twelve module names identify the provincial menu: Computational Thinking; Computers and Communications Devices; Digital Literacy; Drafting; Entrepreneurship and Marketing; Food Studies; Media Arts; Metalwork; Power Technology; Robotics; Textiles; and Woodwork. The grouped Content items preserve those distinctions without making every module mandatory. Locally developed modules still use the ADST 6-7 competencies.

This is not the K-3 integration rule carried forward: Grade 6 has a minimum-module requirement and a content menu. Neither the menu nor its elaborations require a particular commercial application, robot kit, product, or material.

Design constraints include appropriation and sacred knowledge in the source's elaboration. Consulting expertise, including First Peoples Elders where appropriate, does not remove obligations concerning permissions and relationships. Tools, textile uses, and local wood practices must not be detached from their cultural contexts. Full examples and safety-related elaborations remain at [S1]; this reference is not workshop instruction.
