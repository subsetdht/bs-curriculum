# Arts Education - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared non-language expectations under [S2], sections 1-2. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for instruction-language distinctions and applicable exceptions.

**Document language:** English; the summary's language does not determine classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Make, interpret, refine, and share artistic work while connecting artistic choices with identity, culture, place, and others' experiences.

**Prerequisites:** Prior exploration of dance, drama, music, and visual arts is helpful; this reference sets no audition or private-lesson requirement.

**Materials:** No specialist equipment is needed to read this reference. Particular art forms need separately chosen accessible materials, spaces, and safe practices.

**How to use:** Use [S1] for the official expectations across **dance, drama, music, and visual arts**. This Grade 6 entry is an arts curriculum, not a menu of senior specialist courses or a fixed performance schedule. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Creating and experiencing art can strengthen a sense of identity and community while opening a way to understand experiences and perspectives different from one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative expression/identity/community and empathy through experiencing art.

### BI-02

Art is shaped by when and where it is made; different places and times support different forms of artistic expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artistic expressions across time and place.

### BI-03

Dance, drama, music, and visual arts each offer distinctive means of making meaning and communicating it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the four arts as unique languages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Make deliberate choices about artistic elements, techniques, materials, tools, and settings, combining them in individual and collaborative work. Use imagination, inquiry, experimentation, and purposeful play to develop ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, intentional selection/combination and creating artistic works.

### CC-02

Explore how art relates to identity, belonging, place, and society. Attend to the personal, cultural, historical, social, and environmental contexts of artworks and arts practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, identity/place/culture/society/belonging and contexts.

### CC-03

Research and interpret artists' choices, evaluate how their processes work, and refine one's own ideas and technical skills. Reflect on artistic intentions and use connections with other learning and the wider world when interpreting work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, artists' processes, refinement, intentions, cross-curricular interpretation, and wider-world relationships.

### CC-04

Adapt artistic learning for new contexts, audiences, and purposes. Take creative risks and use elements and symbols to express ideas and feelings, respond to others' intentions, and document or present work with increasingly developed use of the curriculum's content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, adaptation, symbols, expression, response, documentation/presentation, and increasing sophistication.

## Content

### C-01

In **dance**, purposeful movement connects the body, space, energy, timing, relationships, and overall form; movement principles support coordination and control. In **drama**, character, setting in time and place, plot, tension, mood, focus, and contrast contribute to dramatic meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, purposeful application of dance and drama elements/principles.

### C-02

In **music**, organize sound and silence through pulse, metre, rhythm, duration, and tempo, alongside pitch, tone colour, and changes in volume. Musical form and layered sounds contribute to the structure and texture of a work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements, including form and texture.

### C-03

In **visual arts**, use the features of an image, including line, shape, space, texture, colour, form, and value, to achieve an effect. Organization through repetition and pattern, balance and contrast, emphasis and rhythm, variety, and relationships of unity or harmony supports meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual elements and principles of design.

### C-04

Learn processes and techniques suited to different art forms, including ways to develop choreography, work in dramatic forms, represent music or dance through notation, and transform ideas into images. Use symbolism and metaphor to explore ideas and viewpoints.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, processes/materials/tools/techniques, choreographic devices, dramatic forms, notation, image development, and symbolism/metaphor.

### C-05

Encounter traditional and contemporary First Peoples arts and ways of making art alongside works and traditions from diverse Canadian and international settings. Relate a work to its community, time, and place rather than treating culture as a uniform style.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional/contemporary Aboriginal arts and arts-making processes; national/international works and artistic traditions.

### C-06

Understand responsibilities for physical and emotional safety when making, experiencing, or presenting art, including care for other people, materials, spaces, and sensitive content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal and collective responsibility in a safe learning environment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's terminology and elaborations explain how elements and principles develop in sophistication; they do not require a particular instrument, dance, script, artist, commercial technology, or public performance. Manual tools as well as digital technologies are included.

Notation need not mean only conventional musical staff notation, and documentation is broader than a finished display. The source includes ways to record artistic thinking and development, not a prescribed portfolio format.

First Peoples arts remain part of this subject's content. Their presence does not authorize copying designs, performing ceremonies, reproducing stories, or adapting restricted cultural works. Use appropriate attribution, permissions, and community guidance. Full definitions and examples remain at [S1].
