# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../README.md) > [Francophone Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone (*Français langue première*), the distinct first-language program. This is not [Early Immersion](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), [Late Immersion transition 6T](../french-immersion-language-arts/transition-6t/curriculum.md), or Core French. Provincial program guidance [S2] distinguishes instruction in French from the program's separate English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative curriculum is French (`fr`). This original English summary is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use English descriptions of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and create French texts by connecting words, images, narrative relationships, and oral expression, while exploring worldviews and Francophone belonging.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading, discussing, and creating French texts is helpful. This reference does not establish admission criteria, language-proficiency thresholds, or eligibility for Francophone schooling.

**Materials:** Read the linked curriculum and its elaborations. For learning activities, use accessible, appropriately attributed French texts and reference tools; there is no prescribed commercial book list here.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the full expectations and use the repository IDs to connect resources to this summary. The IDs are not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a mandated teaching sequence.

The source standards are specifically for Grade 6. They give particular attention to comics, youth novels, and poetry, including song; the content preamble's elaboration also strongly recommends other text types and genres. This record supplies only French first-language arts. The seven non-language areas remain part of the provincial program under the required-areas order [S3]; shared subject expectations do not make English instructional resources suitable for French instruction.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Meaning in illustrated texts emerges from the relationship between words and images, which need not convey identical information. In stories, characters' decisions and circumstances shape the unfolding action.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), image/word relationships; plot, characters' decisions, actions, and context.

### BI-02

Texts can reinforce or change a reader's perceptions. Indigenous narratives also belong within the study of Canadian Francophone cultural history.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), texts and changing perceptions; Indigenous narratives, history, and Canadian Francophone culture.

### BI-03

Communicating in French supports a sense of belonging to Francophone communities, from everyday relationships to wider communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Grandes idées (English gloss), French communication and belonging; communauté elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Identify a text's genre and examine how images and words work together. Interpret their symbolic meanings to better understand the author's intentions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), text characteristics/genre, text-image relationships, and symbolic value.

### CC-02

Explain relationships among characters, connecting their motivations with their roles in the plot.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), character relationships, roles, and motivations.

### CC-03

Recognize cultural elements, values, and worldviews expressed in texts, together with the existence of different registers, dialects, and accents.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explorer et réfléchir (English gloss), values/worldviews, cultural elements, and language varieties.

### CC-04

Combine words, images, and vocal expression to convey emotion. Interpret varied poetry with fluency and intonation that communicate the author's intention.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), words/images/prosody and its elaboration; fluent, expressive interpretation of poetry.

### CC-05

Restate main ideas to test and deepen understanding, and explore how playing with language can transform a text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), reformulating main ideas; wordplay and transforming texts.

### CC-06

Compose texts that suit their forms and the language conventions being studied. Revise and enrich both their organization and expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Créer et communiquer (English gloss), composition by text type and linguistic conventions; revision, correction, and enrichment.

## Content

### C-01

Understand narrative development and how characters' roles and relationships organize action. Build paragraphs around a main idea with explanation, supporting examples, and a conclusion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), organisation textuelle: narrative structure, schéma actanciel, and paragraph structure with elaborations.

### C-02

Recognize features of comics, youth novels, and poetry, including song. Read images both for their own meanings and for their relation to words, attending to what is explicit, implied, or open to interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], content preamble and Contenu (English gloss), literary tools: studied genres and image interpretation with elaborations.

### C-03

Explore how figurative language, sound patterns, and register affect expression. The elaborations illustrate comparison, metaphor, alliteration, and differences in formality.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), outils littéraires: figures de style and registres de langue with elaborations.

### C-04

Maintain connections by referring back to information already introduced. Use verb forms in relation to the genres studied; the tense elaboration identifies the future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), outils langagiers: reprise de l'information and modes/temps with the future-tense elaboration.

### C-05

Develop reading strategies that connect prior knowledge, questioning, inference, and important ideas. Use vocal expression in communication and work through planning, drafting, revision, correction, and publication in writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Contenu (English gloss), stratégies à l'étude: communication/socialization, reading, and writing with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete French curriculum and embedded elaborations were reviewed at [S1]. A text may be oral, written, visual, digital, or printed. The named genres deserve particular attention; they do not restrict learning to printed material or exclude other genres.

The genre elaborations include features such as panels, dialogue, omitted intervals, and sound effects in comics, and rhythm, rhyme, verse, and imagery in poetry. These are condensed here, not replaced with a required author list or a single correct interpretation. Refer to the source for the full range.

The *schéma actanciel* concerns characters and their relationships; it is not just another name for a chronological plot outline. The language-tools expectations include cohesion through reference and contextual verb use. The future-tense elaboration is not a claim that every other tense has ceased to matter. The subject introduction [S4] explains why grammar and conventions should be learned in meaningful contexts.

The cultural expectations include Indigenous narratives and recognition of varied Francophone language practices. Their place in Canadian Francophone history does not erase the distinct identities, origins, or ownership of First Peoples knowledge. No story, song, image, or other cultural work is reproduced; attribution and appropriate permissions are needed for classroom use or adaptation.

This selective summary is not the complete curriculum, an assessment checklist, or an immersion version with a different label. Program eligibility, classroom language, resource suitability, and curriculum alignment are separate questions.
