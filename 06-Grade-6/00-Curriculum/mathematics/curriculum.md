# Mathematics - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared non-language expectations under [S2], sections 1-2. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for instruction-language distinctions and applicable exceptions.

**Document language:** English; this is a reference document, not a prescription for classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Use number, algebraic, spatial, and statistical reasoning to represent relationships, solve problems, and justify decisions in familiar and unfamiliar contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with whole-number operations, decimal place value, fractions, and measurement is helpful prior learning, not a formal admission requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. It does not prescribe a calculator, software product, or manipulative kit.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the complete Grade 6 expectations and elaborations. The local IDs link resources to this summary; they are not official BC standard codes, source item numbers, or a teaching sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

A quantity can be represented as whole units together with fractional or decimal parts. Moving between representations helps make the relationship between a part and its whole visible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, mixed numbers and decimal numbers as parts and wholes.

### BI-02

Flexible calculation depends on understanding numbers and relationships among operations, including when calculations involve decimals rather than only whole numbers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, computational fluency and flexibility with whole numbers and decimals.

### BI-03

A changing pattern can express a general relationship. Variables and graphs provide different ways to describe and reason about linear change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, linear relations, variable expressions, and line graphs.

### BI-04

Measurements of boundaries, surfaces, space occupied, and turns describe different properties of a shape or object and support comparisons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, volume, area, perimeter, and angles.

### BI-05

Experimental results and a model of possible outcomes can inform one another when estimating and interpreting the chance of an event.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, experimental data and theoretical probability.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use logical reasoning, patterns, estimates, and mental strategies to investigate mathematical relationships. Model situations and use suitable tools or technology to explore patterns and test conjectures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and analyzing, logic and patterns, estimation, mental mathematics, tools, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Develop understanding through inquiry, visualization, and problem solving, comparing more than one approach in abstract and real contexts. Connect problems to place, stories, and cultural practices, including perspectives relevant to local First Peoples communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Understanding and solving, multiple strategies, inquiry, visualization, and community/cultural contexts.

### CC-03

Make reasoning understandable through mathematical discussion, explanations, arguments, and concrete, pictorial, and symbolic representations. Give reasons for decisions rather than reporting only an answer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and representing, mathematical vocabulary, justification, communication, and representations.

### CC-04

Reconsider strategies and solutions, connect mathematical ideas with other learning and interests, and use mathematical arguments when making personal choices. Incorporate First Peoples worldviews and perspectives into these connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and reflecting, reflection, connections, personal choices, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Compare, order, estimate, and represent quantities across place values from thousandths to billions. Relate improper fractions and mixed numbers to one another and to their positions among other numbers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, small to large numbers; improper fractions and mixed numbers, including their elaborations.

### C-02

Develop fluency with multiplication and division facts to 100 and extend calculation to multiplying and dividing decimals. Apply order of operations to whole-number expressions, including brackets but not exponents; the source allows a quotient that is not a whole number.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multiplication/division facts to 100, decimal multiplication/division, and order of operations with whole numbers.

### C-03

Use the multiplicative structure of numbers to find greatest common factors and least common multiples. Introduce ratios as comparisons between quantities, and connect whole-number percents with percentage discounts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, factors and multiples, introduction to ratios, and whole-number percents/percentage discounts.

### C-04

Describe increasing and decreasing functional patterns with expressions, tables, and graphs. The pattern-graphing elaboration uses discrete points in the first quadrant. Solve one-step equations while preserving equality, with whole-number coefficients and solutions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, increasing/decreasing patterns and one-step equations, including graphing and equality elaborations.

### C-05

Determine the perimeter of composite shapes and the areas of triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoids. Relating or rearranging familiar shapes helps explain area formulas rather than treating them as disconnected rules.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, perimeter of complex shapes and area of triangles, parallelograms, and trapezoids.

### C-06

Measure and classify angles, and classify triangles by their sides and angles regardless of orientation. Investigate volume and capacity, including suitable units, referents, and the relationship between units of space and liquid measure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, angle measurement/classification, triangles, and volume/capacity.

### C-07

Combine translations, rotations, or reflections of a two-dimensional shape, describing the resulting image. The elaboration limits the coordinate work to the first quadrant and whole-number ordered pairs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, combinations of transformations and its coordinate-plane elaboration.

### C-08

Create and interpret line graphs from data. For single-outcome chance events, compare the probability derived from possible outcomes with results collected experimentally.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, line graphs and single-outcome probability, both theoretical and experimental.

### C-09

Use simple budgets and consumer calculations to reason about saving, purchasing, and the consequences of financial choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: simple budgeting and consumer mathematics.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The different limits matter: facts to 100 are not the entire number range; the place-value range reaches from thousandths to billions. Whole-number order-of-operations work excludes exponents, and the equation standard specifies whole-number coefficients and solutions. Do not replace these with unrestricted algebra, four-quadrant graphing, compound probability, or a financial-investment syllabus.

The source supplies examples of strategies, contexts, and cultural connections. Those examples are not a mandatory resource list. Its complex-shape perimeter elaboration describes joined shapes without holes. Its triangle categories consider both sides and angles; orientation does not change a category.

First Peoples connections remain part of mathematical learning. A reference to a cultural design, game, story, or practice in [S1] is not permission to reproduce it or to detach it from community knowledge and protocols.
