# English Language Arts - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > English Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2], sections 2 and 4(c), establishes the Grade 6 English-language pathway and English Language Arts for immersion learners. [S3] identifies English Language Arts within the Francophone program; section 4(b) of [S2] itself specifies a language other than French, not English by name. Section 5 removes the obligation to offer ELA to **Late French Immersion students in Grade 6**; this is not a prohibition on offering it. Consult the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) before selecting language-arts records.

**Document language:** English. This is an English-language-arts reference, not a translation-based substitute for French Immersion or Francophone language arts.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and evaluate communication across media, connect texts with identities and perspectives, and create purposeful, original communication for different audiences.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience reading, listening, discussing, writing, and revising is helpful, not a formal entry requirement or a prescribed commercial reading level.

**Materials:** No special materials are required for this reference. Text selections need their own age-appropriateness, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** [S1] includes oral, written, visual, and digital texts and both individual and collaborative work. Read the official source alongside this synthesis; local IDs are repository references, not BC standard codes or an assessment sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Language and texts can bring pleasure and creative possibilities while helping people understand themselves, others, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity/joy and connections through stories and other texts.

### BI-02

Encountering and sharing different viewpoints can extend thinking beyond one's own initial interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, multiple perspectives.

### BI-03

Understanding how language works enables more purposeful communication. Questioning messages also supports informed participation in public life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, purposeful language use and questioning as engaged citizens.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Find information for a purpose and assess whether it is relevant, accurate, and reliable. Use reading, listening, viewing, and inquiry strategies to build understanding, including synthesizing ideas from more than one source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, accessing/evaluating information, comprehension/inquiry strategies, and synthesis.

### CC-02

Interpret how features, forms, genres, structures, and literary techniques shape a message for its audience. Think critically, creatively, and reflectively across texts rather than treating form as separate from meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, features/forms/genres, critical thinking, literary elements/devices, and text structures.

### CC-03

Identify the contexts, values, and perspectives expressed in texts, and consider how language contributes to personal and collective identity. Build meaningful connections among self, text, and world and respond in personal, creative, and critical ways.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, contexts/perspectives, language and identity, personal connections, and responses.

### CC-04

Recognize the significance of story, narrative, and oral tradition in communicating First Peoples values, beliefs, and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples story, narrative, and oral tradition.

### CC-05

Exchange ideas to deepen shared understanding. Use writing and design processes to create literary and informational texts, choosing forms and features suited to audience, purpose, and message and transforming information into original work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchanging viewpoints, writing/design, selecting features/forms/genres, and original texts.

### CC-06

Evaluate and revise texts for clarity and effect, using a growing command of Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation. Experiment with oral storytelling processes as well as written and other forms of communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, refining texts, language conventions, and oral storytelling.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how a text's form, purpose, genre, and visual organization guide its audience. Narrative elements and literary devices shape experience and meaning, while persuasive techniques can appeal to feelings or reasoning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text: forms/functions/genres, text features, literary elements/devices, and techniques of persuasion.

### C-02

Develop strategies for interpreting texts and participating in spoken exchanges, alongside ways to reflect on one's own learning. Writing processes include planning, revising, and editing with an audience in mind.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes: reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing.

### C-03

Use the expressive features of speech and learn how varieties of English and levels of formality relate to context. Presentation decisions include choosing a medium and organizing a message for its intended purpose and audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: oral language, language varieties, and presentation techniques.

### C-04

Develop coherent paragraphs and varied, grammatically connected sentences. Attend to pronouns, agreement, transitions, fragments and run-ons, and conventions of punctuation, capitalization, and Canadian spelling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: paragraphing, sentence structure/grammar, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's use of "text" includes speech, images, digital communication, and combinations of modes, not just printed literature. Its examples are not a required reading list, genre quota, word-count target, or commercial proficiency level.

Critical evaluation of a source concerns its credibility and usefulness, not merely whether it agrees with a reader. Literary interpretation and personal response do not replace attention to the evidence and contexts of a text.

First Peoples oral traditions are a curricular expectation, not optional enrichment. This reference does not reproduce a story or authorize retelling one; the source itself identifies permission when an existing story is used. The full terminology and elaborations remain at [S1].
