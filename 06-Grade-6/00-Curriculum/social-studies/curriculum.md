# Social Studies - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6 overview](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared non-language expectations under [S2], sections 1-2. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for instruction-language distinctions and applicable exceptions.

**Document language:** English; this does not set classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Examine global issues and governance by evaluating evidence, comparing perspectives, and reasoning about power, interdependence, responsibility, and possible action.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with inquiry, maps, community questions, and Canadian society can support this work; it is not a formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are required for this reference. Sources used in later learning need their own currency, accessibility, suitability, and rights review.

**How to use:** The Grade 6 focus is **Global Issues and Governance**, not a prescribed survey of all countries or a chronological world-history course. Consult [S1] for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Economic interests can place groups and governments in conflict. Understanding those interests helps explain choices without reducing every conflict to a single cause.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, economic self-interest and conflict.

### BI-02

Global problems connect people across borders, making cooperation and difficult choices about the future important.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, international cooperation and complex global problems.

### BI-03

Government arrangements affect the protection of people's rights and freedoms; those protections are not the same in every system.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, systems of government and human rights/freedoms.

### BI-04

How events are represented in media can improve understanding or distort it, so information about public issues needs examination.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, positive and negative effects of media sources.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Frame an inquiry, gather and analyze information, and communicate findings and decisions. Develop a supported plan of action on a selected issue rather than stopping at description.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and developing a plan of action.

### CC-02

Build an argument for why a person, group, place, event, or development matters. Ask questions about sources and their origins, corroborate inferences, and draw conclusions using evidence, including evidence from mass media.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance and evidence.

### CC-03

Sequence evidence and events to identify continuities and changes, considering their benefits and harms rather than assuming that all change is progress.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, continuity and change.

### CC-04

Distinguish immediate causes from longer-term conditions, and intended results from unintended consequences, when explaining events and decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, cause and consequence.

### CC-05

Infer stakeholders' beliefs, values, and motivations to understand their perspectives. Consider how different positions and experiences shape responses to the same issue.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-06

Make ethical judgments with attention to the conditions of a particular time and place, then assess appropriate responses to decisions, actions, or events.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Examine why populations move and become concentrated in cities, alongside global poverty and inequality. Consider the roles of class and gender in unequal opportunities and living conditions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, urbanization/migration and global poverty/inequality, including class structure and gender.

### C-02

Compare forms of government and the roles of individuals, government bodies, and non-governmental organizations in public life. Include organizations representing Indigenous peoples and, within the elaborations, Indigenous governance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, systems of government and roles of individuals, governmental organizations, and NGOs.

### C-03

Investigate how economic policies and resource-management decisions affect people and places, including Indigenous peoples. Connect these decisions to globalization and economic interdependence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, economic policies/resource management and globalization/economic interdependence.

### C-04

Study cooperation across borders in responding to global issues, together with regional and international conflicts. Consider both the problems being addressed and the competing interests that complicate a response.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, international cooperation/responses to global issues and regional/international conflict.

### C-05

Examine media technologies and the coverage of current events. Consider how selection, ownership, bias, and presentation can influence public understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, media technologies/current-events coverage and its elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source includes illustrative topics, questions, mapping skills, and civic-action examples. They are not a compulsory list of countries, historical atrocities, organizations, or campaigns. Geographic representations and source evaluation support inquiry; a source-linked summary does not prescribe political conclusions.

Some examples in the official elaborations name historically specific arrangements, including NAFTA. Do not treat an illustrative name as a statement of current treaty status. Use current authoritative information for any present-day issue while retaining the curriculum's broader inquiry.

Indigenous peoples appear as participants in governance and global issues, and as communities affected by economic decisions, not only as historical case studies. Select age-appropriate sources and respect community-specific knowledge and cultural rights. The complete elaborations remain at [S1].
