# Grade 6 curriculum

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > 6

**Browse:** [Programs](#choose-a-program) | [Subjects and learning standards](#subjects-and-learning-standards) | [Languages](languages/README.md) | [Coverage and limits](#coverage-and-limits)

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Programs represented:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. **Document language:** English (`en-CA`). **Source review date:** 2026-09-14.

These original, condensed references describe provincial expectations, not official wording, a teaching schedule, or an assessment checklist. **Twenty records are source-checked:** ten regular subject records, the distinct **6T** record, and all nine courses in the [Languages inventory](languages/README.md). Those nine alternatives are not a set of courses that every learner must take.

## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Late French Immersion](#late-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

The **seven shared non-language subjects** are ADST, Arts Education, Career Education, Mathematics, Physical and Health Education, Science, and Social Studies. Sections 1-2 of the [Required Areas of Learning order][required-areas] establish their provision across the four programs, explicitly including Francophone authorities, students, and programs. Their shared expectations do not imply the same classroom language or interchangeable teaching resources.

The [eight non-French language courses](#language-course-choices) are also available as source-backed elective/additional-study references for immersion and Francophone programs under section 2(b) of the [Educational Program Guide Order][program-guides]. This adds reference options, not compulsory courses, guaranteed local offerings, or substitutes for language arts. Core subject-pathway counts below are unchanged; totals including course options are shown under [Coverage and limits](#coverage-and-limits).

### English-language program

Start with [English Language Arts](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects below: **eight subject records**, plus a separately selected language pathway. Section 4(a) of the [order][required-areas] requires boards to offer a language other than English in Grades 5-8, subject to the applicable exceptions. The [Languages landing](languages/README.md) distinguishes actual course choices from the general requirement. Core French is one choice, not the only possible provincial language curriculum.

### Early French Immersion

Use [regular French Immersion Language Arts 6](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects: **nine records**.

The [provincial immersion policy][immersion-policy] places normal Early Immersion entry in Kindergarten or Grade 1 and provides English Language Arts from Grade 4. For the band containing Grade 6, its recommended allocation is 80% French and 20% English instruction; that is provincial guidance, not a statement of a school's timetable. The Grade 6 Late Immersion exception does not apply to this regular pathway.

### Late French Immersion

Use [French Immersion Language Arts 6T](french-immersion-language-arts/transition-6t/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects: **eight records** in this entry-year pathway.

Grade 6 is the normal Late Immersion entry point under [provincial policy][immersion-policy]. The official French compilation identifies the distinct standards as *6e année tardive*. The metadata remains `grade: "6"` with `course: transition-6t`; **6T is not an additional grade**, a shared 6-7 band, or a version of the regular entry to combine with it.

The policy recommends an entirely French first year. Section 4(c) of the [order][required-areas] generally requires boards to offer English Language Arts to immersion learners in Grades 4-9, but **section 5 removes that obligation for Late Immersion students in Grade 6**. It does not prohibit offering ELA. The ELA record is therefore not counted in this entry-year pathway; confirm any local provision with the school. Do not carry the Grade 6 exception forward to another year.

### Francophone

Use [Francophone Language Arts](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects: **nine records**. French first-language arts is not the immersion curriculum.

The [provincial program comparison][french-programs] identifies both French and English Language Arts within the Francophone program and describes other subject areas as taught in French. This supports ELA program applicability, not a particular ELA starting grade. Section 4(b) of the [order][required-areas] requires a language **other than French** in Grades 5-8; that clause does not itself name English or require every Languages catalog course.

### Individual exceptions and local delivery

Section 3 of the [order][required-areas] makes specific exceptions for learners who require an IEP because they cannot demonstrate learning against the program-guide outcomes, or who receive English-language-learning services and cannot do so because of English proficiency. It does not exempt every learner with an IEP or every multilingual learner. Section 6 also permits a qualifying locally developed Indigenous-language program under an agreement with the relevant band council or treaty First Nation. Those provisions are distinct from the Grade 6 Late Immersion rule.

Program eligibility, course availability, individual supports, and district timetables require local confirmation. No SD43 schedule or other district implementation is presented here as a provincial requirement.

## How to use these references

Choose a program, then read a subject's Scope and use and follow its source locators. Big Ideas, Curricular Competencies, and Content work together in the [BC curriculum model](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/overview); cross-curricular competencies do not replace subject-specific expectations.

For a precise local reference, use a descriptive link such as [Mathematics 6: C-04](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-04). Its canonical structured reference is `bc-g06-mathematics#c-04`. These IDs belong to the repository, not the ministry, and do not specify priority or teaching order.

No special materials or formal prerequisites are required to read these references. The Scope and use sections identify helpful prior learning without setting admission or proficiency thresholds.

ADST and Career Education use **6-7** source bands. ADST additionally requires at least three modules in **each** year, drawn from the twelve provincial choices or locally developed alternatives using the same competencies. Do not carry forward the K-3 integration rule or require every listed module.

First Peoples perspectives remain within the relevant subject expectations, including inquiry, texts, arts, design, and cultural or community knowledge. A curriculum citation does not confer permission to reproduce or adapt a community's stories, designs, songs, or practices.

## Coverage and limits

All twelve original catalog landings are populated: eleven lead to subject/variant records, and Languages leads to nine named courses. The twenty records are **core summaries**, not copies of every learning standard or inventories of all elaboration examples. Regular 6 and 6T have separate sources, IDs, content, and program paths. Source revision dates were not established; the review date is not a curriculum edition.

The following totals count references carrying each program label, **not courses a learner must take**. Shared records count in more than one program without being duplicated.

| Program | Subject-pathway records | Language-course choices | Total labelled records |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| English-language | 8 | 9 alternatives | 17 |
| Early French Immersion | 9 | 8 elective/additional-study options | 17 |
| Late French Immersion | 8 | 8 elective/additional-study options | 16 |
| Francophone | 9 | 8 elective/additional-study options | 17 |

**Source-access gaps:** None for these twenty records or the current nine-course inventory. **Applicability limits:** The eight non-French language options are supported across the four programs by explicit guide designations, not merely by translated page availability. Core French remains an English-language pathway record; optional-course labels do not imply required additional study, French classroom resources, or local availability. Local offerings and additional local-language curricula are not inventoried here.

All summaries are written in English. French primary sources, English descriptions of those sources, and French classroom resources are different things. French-language editions, lessons, assessments, and classroom tools have not been added. The [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) folders remain empty.

Local language offerings and qualifying locally developed Indigenous-language programs are not inferred from the provincial course catalog. Program availability and a learner's placement need local confirmation, independently of whether a source-linked summary exists.

Maintain existing landings and stable item IDs using the [format guide](../../curriculum-format.md) and [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page). Keep central written-summary coverage synchronized when integrating this grade's content.

## Sources and rights

Each record cites its actual subject source and item locators. Full main-page content, including embedded elaborations, was reviewed rather than relying on the BC site's sometimes incomplete simplified extraction.

- [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order][required-areas], sections 1-6: shared areas, language requirements, individual exceptions, and the specific Late Immersion Grade 6 exception.
- [Educational Program Guide Order (M231/19)][program-guides], sections 2(a)-(b): English guides and French-language guides explicitly designated for Francophone and immersion programs, including the eight non-French language-course counterparts. The legacy filename contains the repealed M333/99 number.
- [Provincial French Immersion policy][immersion-policy]: separate entry models, parallel curriculum, and recommended French/English instruction allocations.
- [Provincial French-program comparison][french-programs]: distinct program purposes and the Francophone program's two language-arts subjects.
- [Language Education Policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/legislation-policy/public-schools/language-education-policy): board choice, continuity, and Core French fallback, read alongside the governing order's specific exceptions.
- [BC curriculum model](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/overview): relationships among the three curriculum components and program considerations.

These supporting sources were reviewed on **2026-09-14**. The original [catalog capture](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum) date is also 2026-09-14, but catalog capture is not the evidence for alignment. Official documents remain with their publishers.

BC curriculum material is subject to the [province's copyright policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/copyright). This collection contains original summaries and links, not official wording, authorized translations, mirrored source files, or third-party cultural works.
