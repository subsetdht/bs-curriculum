# Mandarin - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Mandarin

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, as a second-language course option. Applicability to other programs has not been established here.

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. Mandarin is the target language; this summary does not prescribe classroom language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange familiar messages in Mandarin, connecting sound and written forms with meaning while exploring cultural perspectives and Chinese communities in Canada.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier exposure to Mandarin sounds, characters, or everyday exchanges can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; Mandarin is not required of every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending closely to what is heard and seen helps learners use familiar Mandarin patterns to take part in a two-way exchange.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

Learning another language provides ways to explore identity. Stories connect that learning with experiences and understandings of the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning about language across communities develops awareness of cultural perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate Mandarin sounds, including tone and basic intonation, to phonetic representations, Chinese characters, and meaning. Understand stories and familiar language patterns and identify key information in slow, clear speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sounds/phonetic representation/Chinese characters/meaning, key information, stories, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Use context, familiar language, and non-verbal clues to make sense of a message. Ask for clarification and respond to straightforward questions and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Exchange ideas orally and in writing, choosing ways to present information that support both the communicator's and the audience's abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Consider how culture shapes personal and shared experience and differing worldviews. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing in this exploration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural lens and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how Mandarin pronunciation can be represented phonetically, and distinguish tonal variations. Develop knowledge of common Chinese characters through their basic strokes, stroke order, and arrangement of components.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phonetic systems, tonal variations, commonly used Chinese characters, and basic strokes and structure of Chinese characters.

### C-02

Build familiar language for asking questions and describing other people, interests, emotions, and physical well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions of others, hobbies/interests, and emotions/physical health.

### C-03

Learn about Chinese cultural practices and creative works, including Chinese communities in Canada and their festivals or celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of Chinese communities, Chinese works of art, Chinese communities in Canada, and Chinese cultural festivals and celebrations in Canada.

### C-04

Explore how First Peoples connect language and culture with oral histories, identity, and relationships with place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The competency elaborations identify Zhuyin symbols or Pinyin letters as phonetic representations and traditional or simplified Chinese characters. These are distinctions to retain, not a requirement to master both alternatives simultaneously. The source introduces stroke order, components, radicals, and character structures without assigning a Grade 6 character-count target.

Tone is part of the language's sound–meaning relationships, not only an expression of the speaker's mood. The source's tone examples clarify variations; they do not supply an entire pronunciation course.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms. Festivals named in elaborations are possibilities, not a compulsory calendar. Chinese cultural identity should not be reduced to one uniform language experience. The art elaboration addresses appropriation and plagiarism, and First Peoples stories and knowledge require their own context, protocols, and permissions before reuse.
