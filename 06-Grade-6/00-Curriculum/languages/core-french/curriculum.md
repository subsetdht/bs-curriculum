# Core French - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Core French

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language. This record remains scoped to the English-language program's Core French option; it is not French Immersion Language Arts or Francophone language arts. Core French cannot satisfy the Francophone requirement for a language other than French under [S3], section 4(b), and is not an extra required course for immersion learners.

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. French is the target language of learning; the summary is not a French-language instructional resource.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange familiar information in French, extending descriptions of people and interests while connecting language learning with Francophone communities in Canada.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with earlier French sounds and everyday exchanges is helpful, not an admission requirement set by this reference.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Classroom texts or recordings need their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes. See [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the English-language program's Grades 5–8 second-language requirement and its exceptions: Core French is the fallback when a board does not offer an alternative, not a requirement to take French in addition to every other listed language.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing, supported by deliberate learning strategies, help learners make sense of French and build their ability to use it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and strategies for understanding and acquiring language.

### BI-02

A useful stock of familiar language makes two-way exchanges possible, including describing other people and what interests them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, describing others and their interests and reciprocal communication with high-frequency vocabulary and sentence structures.

### BI-03

Stories and encounters with Francophone communities connect language development with broader understanding of people, culture, and the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, understanding the world, and cultural awareness through Francophone communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Begin connecting French spelling patterns with pronunciation, and use intonation to interpret a message. Understand the main information and some supporting details in slow, clear speech and simple texts, including the key events of a simple story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, letter patterns/pronunciation, intonation/meaning, key information and some details, and simple stories.

### CC-02

Use strategies suited to the learner and situation to understand or express meaning. Ask for clarification and respond to familiar questions and straightforward directions, using verbal and non-verbal support as needed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to support communication, seeking clarification, and responses to questions/commands/instructions.

### CC-03

Exchange ideas orally and in writing using complete sentences. Select presentation modes that support both understanding others and expressing oneself, including accessibility for different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, complete-sentence exchanges and various modes of presentation.

### CC-04

Explore where French is used in Canada, including its connections with First Peoples communities. Investigate a Canadian Francophone festival or celebration as part of understanding these living communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Francophone communities across Canada, First Peoples/French-language connections, and a Francophone cultural festival or celebration.

## Content

### C-01

Develop knowledge of French speech sounds and recurring written patterns, including vowel combinations, nasal vowels, and similar-sounding contrasts that can affect understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, French phonemes and French letter patterns, including their sound-pattern elaborations.

### C-02

Learn familiar question and sentence patterns that support descriptions of people and things, discussion of hobbies, explanations of preferences, and communication about emotions or physical states.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary and sentence structures: questions, descriptions, interests, reasons for likes/dislikes/preferences, and emotions/physical states.

### C-03

Know how to convey essential information about an event, such as its purpose, time, and location.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, basic information about events and the posters/invitations elaboration.

### C-04

Learn about cultural life in communities where French is spoken across Canada, including a Francophone festival or celebration. Develop an understanding of plagiarism and of why using another culture's expressions without permission or context can misrepresent that culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of communities, French-speaking communities across Canada, a Francophone cultural festival or celebration, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The relationship between spelling and pronunciation is introduced at this level; the source does not prescribe mastery of every French spelling rule. Complete sentences and some comprehension details are explicit Grade 6 expectations, but this summary does not set a vocabulary quota, proficiency score, or fixed grammar sequence.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital communication. The official elaborations provide possible strategies, expressions, events, and communities, not a required list of materials or visits. A Canadian Francophone festival or celebration is part of the content; no single named festival is compulsory.

French-speaking First Nations, Métis, and Inuit communities are included in the source's cultural scope. Their histories and traditions should not be merged into one generic Francophone identity. Studying a story, song, regalia, or celebration does not authorize copying or re-enacting it; follow community protocols and the permissions of the relevant knowledge holders.
