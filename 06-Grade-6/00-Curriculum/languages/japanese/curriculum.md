# Japanese - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Japanese

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This is a language-course option; the added immersion and Francophone coverage is for elective/additional study where offered. [S4], sections 2(a)-(b), designates the corresponding English and French guides, establishing applicability rather than an extra course requirement or local availability. The program-specific obligations and Grade 6 Late Immersion ELA exception remain as set out in [S3] and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice).

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. Japanese is the target language; this summary does not prescribe classroom language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Understand and share familiar messages in Japanese by connecting sounds, writing, and basic patterns with everyday communication and cultural perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience with Japanese sounds, writing, or simple exchanges can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; Japanese is not compulsory for every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Close attention to what is heard and seen, together with familiar Japanese patterns, supports participation in two-way communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

Learning another language offers ways to explore identity. Stories help learners develop that language while considering experiences and ideas about the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Looking at language across communities broadens understanding of culture rather than suggesting that one experience represents everyone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Japanese characters and sounds with meaning, attending also to intonation and vocal expression. Identify important information and familiar patterns in slow, clear speech and simple texts, and understand stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, characters/sounds/intonation/tone/meaning, key information, stories, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Use known language, context, and non-verbal information to improve understanding. Ask for clarification and respond to straightforward questions and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Exchange ideas and information through both speech and writing. Select presentation forms that support communication across different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Consider how culture informs personal and shared experiences and different worldviews. Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge alongside local cultural knowledge and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural lens and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn about Japanese syllabic writing and recurring written patterns, linking what is written with how the language sounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese letter patterns and Japanese syllabaries; Thinking and communicating, relationships between Japanese characters and sounds.

### C-02

Build a familiar repertoire for asking questions and describing people, interests, feelings, and physical well-being. Basic particle use is part of learning how these messages are structured.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, basic particles, descriptions of people, hobbies/interests, and emotions/physical health.

### C-03

Learn about Japanese artistic expression and the cultural life of Japanese communities internationally. Include the Indigenous peoples of Japan identified in the source: Ainu and Okinawan peoples.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese works of art, indigenous peoples of Japan, and cultural aspects of Japanese communities around the world.

### C-04

Explore First Peoples connections between language and culture through oral histories, identity, and relationships with place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source specifies Japanese syllabaries and letter patterns; a small `つ` illustrates one pattern. Basic particle examples include `は` and `が`. These examples do not establish a required character-count target, a kanji list, or mastery of every particle use.

Comprehension extends to oral, written, visual, and digital texts. The elaborations suggest communication strategies and possible presentation supports without prescribing materials or a teaching sequence.

The Indigenous peoples of Japan and First Peoples perspectives in the BC curriculum are both within scope; they must not be collapsed into one interchangeable cultural account. Cultural communities and their language experiences are diverse. The art elaboration calls attention to appropriation and plagiarism, and learning about a story or tradition does not grant permission to reproduce, retell, or perform it.
