# German - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > German

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, as a second-language course option. Applicability to other programs has not been established here.

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. German is the target language; document language does not determine a classroom's language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Make meaning from familiar German and participate in everyday exchanges while connecting language patterns with personal identity and cultural understanding.

**Prerequisites:** Prior exposure to German sounds and basic exchanges can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; the catalog does not require every learner to take German.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending closely to spoken and visual messages and using familiar language patterns lets learners participate in a two-way exchange before they know the language extensively.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

German offers another way to explore identity, while stories make language learning a means of understanding experiences and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Encountering language in different communities helps learners recognize cultural perspectives beyond their own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use pronunciation, intonation, and vocal expression to interpret meaning. Recognize familiar words and patterns and identify important information in slowly and clearly delivered speech, other texts, and stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation/intonation/tone/meaning, key information, stories, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Combine context, prior knowledge, and non-verbal clues to support understanding. Ask for clarification and respond to straightforward questions or instructions so that an exchange can continue.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Communicate ideas and information in spoken and written German. Choose presentation formats and supports with attention to the abilities of both the communicator and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Consider how cultural experience shapes one's own and others' perspectives. Recognize First Peoples knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing as part of this inquiry.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural lens and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how German sounds relate to recurring letter combinations, including distinctions involving umlauted vowels. Understand noun capitalization and the roles of gender, number, and case, with an introductory concept of how word forms change through declension.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German phonemes, letter patterns, noun characteristics, and German declination (basic concept).

### C-02

Build everyday vocabulary and sentence patterns for obtaining information and describing people, interests, emotions, and physical well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions of people, hobbies/interests, and emotions/physical health.

### C-03

Learn about German communities in Canada and elsewhere through their cultural practices and artistic expression, with attention to the context and ownership of creative works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German works of art and cultural aspects of German communities in Canada and around the world; art elaboration on appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-04

Understand that First Peoples language and culture are connected through oral histories, identity, and relationships with place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The grammar scope is introductory. The declension elaboration names masculine, feminine, and neuter gender; singular/plural number; and nominative, accusative, dative, and genitive cases. This establishes concepts to encounter, not an additional requirement to master every inflection table at Grade 6. The source's third-person descriptions and pronunciation examples clarify the scope without prescribing a fixed phrase list.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital communication. Elaborations suggest strategies, contexts, and possible works; they are not a mandatory textbook, festival calendar, or teaching sequence.

First Peoples knowledge belongs within the language curriculum. Local stories and teachings are not interchangeable, and learning about a tradition does not authorize copying or performing it. Follow the relevant community's protocols and permissions for cultural and artistic works.
