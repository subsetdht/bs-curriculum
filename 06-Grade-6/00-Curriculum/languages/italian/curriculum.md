# Italian - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Italian

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This is a language-course option; the added immersion and Francophone coverage is for elective/additional study where offered. [S4], sections 2(a)-(b), designates the corresponding English and French guides, establishing applicability rather than an extra course requirement or local availability. The program-specific obligations and Grade 6 Late Immersion ELA exception remain as set out in [S3] and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice).

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. Italian is the target language; this summary does not prescribe classroom language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Understand and share everyday information and stories in Italian while examining cultural perspectives and Italian connections with life in Canada.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier encounters with Italian sounds and familiar expressions can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; Italian is an option, not an additional requirement for every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Learners can begin sustaining a conversation through familiar language patterns when they attend closely to what they hear and see.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

Learning Italian provides another way to explore identity. Stories connect that language learning with experiences and understandings beyond one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning about language in different communities broadens cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Italian sounds with written combinations and punctuation, noticing how intonation contributes to meaning. Understand familiar language and identify key information in slow, clear speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, intonation patterns, sounds/pronunciation/letter combinations/punctuation, key information, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Understand stories and develop the ability to narrate or retell them, rather than treating comprehension as isolated word recognition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehending stories and narrating or retelling stories.

### CC-03

Use context, known language, and non-verbal clues to understand an exchange. Ask for clarification when needed and respond to straightforward questions and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-04

Exchange ideas orally and in writing, selecting ways to present information that accommodate the abilities of both the learner and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-05

Analyze experiences and worldviews with attention to how culture shapes them. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing within that analysis.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, analyzing through a cultural lens and recognizing First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn Italian speech sounds and recurring spelling patterns, including letter combinations whose pronunciation differs from what an English reader might expect.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian phonemes and Italian letter patterns; Thinking and communicating, combinations of letters elaboration.

### C-02

Develop everyday language for questions and descriptions, quantities, preferences, and emotional or physical states. This repertoire supports explaining what one likes as well as naming or describing it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: simple questions/descriptions, quantity, likes/dislikes/preferences, and emotions/physical states.

### C-03

Learn about Italian cultural life through creative works, resources, events, and communities. Include Italian Canadians' contributions so that culture is connected with people and experiences in Canada, not only with Italy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian creative works, resources and events, cultural aspects of Italian communities, and contributions of Italian Canadians.

### C-04

Understand the connections First Peoples make among language, oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The pronunciation elaborations include double letters and combinations such as `gn`, while spelling examples illustrate how differently written combinations can share a sound. These clarify sound–print relationships, not a prescribed vocabulary list. Quantity is part of the content, but the Grade 6 source supplies no compulsory numerical range.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms. Both understanding stories and narrating or retelling them are explicit in this course. A choice of story, artist, event, occupation, or Italian-Canadian contribution in an elaboration is not a province-wide required selection.

Use the creative-work elaboration's cautions about appropriation and plagiarism. First Peoples oral histories and other community works require the relevant permissions and cultural context; an expectation to engage with culture does not authorize public retelling or adaptation.
