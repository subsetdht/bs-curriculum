# Punjabi - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Punjabi

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This is a language-course option; the added immersion and Francophone coverage is for elective/additional study where offered. [S4], sections 2(a)-(b), designates the corresponding English and French guides, establishing applicability rather than an extra course requirement or local availability. The program-specific obligations and Grade 6 Late Immersion ELA exception remain as set out in [S3] and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice).

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. Punjabi is the target language; this summary does not prescribe classroom language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange familiar information in Punjabi, connecting speech and Gurmukhi literacy with everyday relationships and cultural perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier exposure to Punjabi sounds, writing, or basic exchanges can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; the catalog does not require every learner to take Punjabi.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing help learners use familiar Punjabi patterns in an exchange where both participants contribute.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

A new language offers another way to explore identity. Stories support language learning while opening up experiences and understandings of the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning about language in different communities develops cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret how pronunciation, intonation, and vocal expression affect meaning. Understand stories and familiar language patterns, locating key information in slow, clear speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation/intonation/tone/meaning, key information, stories, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Use prior knowledge, context, and non-verbal clues to improve understanding. Request clarification and respond to straightforward questions and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Exchange ideas through spoken and written Punjabi, choosing ways to present information that suit the abilities of both the learner and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Consider how culture informs personal and shared experiences and different worldviews. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing within that exploration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural lens and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Develop Punjabi literacy through Gurmukhi forms, sound relationships, and recurring letter patterns. The sound repertoire includes consonants, short and long vowels, nasal and stress-related features, and blended sounds; numeral knowledge also includes Gurmukhi symbols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi symbols/sounds/letter patterns, Punjabi phonemes, and numerals with their Gurmukhi elaborations.

### C-02

Build everyday language for asking questions, describing people and family relationships, and sharing daily events or emotional and physical states. Recognize that formal and informal forms of address differ.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, family relationships, descriptions, modes of address, daily events, and emotions/physical states.

### C-03

Learn about Punjabi cultural practices and creative works in connection with communities in Canada and around the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi works of art and cultural aspects of Punjabi communities in Canada and around the world.

### C-04

Explore First Peoples connections between language and culture through oral histories, identity, and relationships with place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source explicitly identifies Gurmukhi script and numeral symbols; transliteration alone would omit that literacy scope. The phoneme elaboration distinguishes nasal, stress-related, and conjunct or blended sounds rather than only naming an alphabet. No compulsory numerical range or word-count target is specified here.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms. Formal and informal address, and the roles discussed within family relationships, should be understood in context without assuming a single family structure or identical customs among Punjabi speakers.

Elaborations suggest expressions, cultural contexts, and presentation supports; these do not establish a fixed phrase list or festival calendar. The art elaboration includes appropriation and plagiarism. First Peoples oral histories and other community knowledge require appropriate protocols and permissions before reproduction, retelling, or performance.
