# Korean - Grade 6

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 6](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Korean

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This is a language-course option; the added immersion and Francophone coverage is for elective/additional study where offered. [S4], sections 2(a)-(b), designates the corresponding English and French guides, establishing applicability rather than an extra course requirement or local availability. The program-specific obligations and Grade 6 Late Immersion ELA exception remain as set out in [S3] and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice).

**Document language:** English, as is the official source. Korean is the target language; this summary does not prescribe classroom language-of-instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange familiar information in Korean while developing awareness of how sound, written forms, grammar, and social conventions contribute to meaning.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier exposure to Korean sounds, characters, or everyday exchanges can help; this reference sets no admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None beyond this reference and access to [S1]. Later texts or media require their own language, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the Grade 6 standards and elaborations in [S1]. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes. Consult [S2], [S3], and the [Languages inventory](../README.md#requirement-and-course-choice) for the second-language requirement, board-selected offerings, and conditional exceptions; the course list does not make Korean compulsory.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Careful attention to spoken and visual messages helps learners use familiar patterns in a back-and-forth exchange.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal communication with high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

A new language can provide another perspective on identity. Through stories, learners develop language and explore experiences and understandings of the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through a new language and stories for language acquisition and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Studying language in different communities helps learners build cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language from diverse communities and cultural awareness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate Korean pronunciation and sounds to their written or phonetic representation and meaning. Understand stories and familiar patterns, and locate key information in slow, clear speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation/sounds/phonetic representation/Korean characters/meaning, key information, stories, and high-frequency words and patterns.

### CC-02

Use contextual and non-verbal clues together with known language to support understanding. Ask for clarification and respond to straightforward questions and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, non-verbal cues, responses to questions/commands/instructions, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Share ideas and information in spoken and written Korean, selecting presentation forms that suit the abilities of both the learner and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, oral/written exchanges and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Consider how cultural experience influences one's own and others' perspectives. Recognize First Peoples knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing within these comparisons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural lens and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn Korean speech sounds and the role of particles in structuring a message. Connect language formality with etiquette, including the distinction among formal-polite, informal-polite, and casual endings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean phonemes, particles (functional words), and language formality and etiquette, including the three-ending elaboration.

### C-02

Build an everyday vocabulary and sentence repertoire for questions and for describing people, interests, emotions, and physical well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions of people, hobbies/interests, and emotions/physical states.

### C-03

Learn about Korean communities in Canada and around the world through their cultural practices and artistic expression, with attention to the context and ownership of creative works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean works of art and cultural aspects of Korean communities in Canada and around the world; art elaboration on appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-04

Explore First Peoples relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place as an integral part of cultural understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The phonetic-representation competency also names Korean characters: replacing written Korean entirely with an English pronunciation guide would omit part of the scope. Particle examples include `은/는` and `이/가`; the source distinguishes three levels of ending formality without specifying an exhaustive conjugation syllabus.

"Text" includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms. The communication focus is familiar language and key information in slow, clear speech and simple texts, not an externally assigned fluency score. Examples of cultural practices, media, and presentation supports do not become a compulsory set of classroom materials.

Etiquette should be considered in context rather than used to stereotype every Korean speaker. The art elaboration includes appropriation and plagiarism. First Peoples oral histories and community knowledge require their own protocols and permissions; a curricular reference does not authorize their copying or performance.
