# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, a first-language program distinct from French Immersion and Core French. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary source is French. This is an original summary, not an authorized translation or a statement that instruction should be in English. Locators are English glosses of the French sections.

**Learning objective:** Read, speak, and write critically and fluently in French, using textual evidence and cultural understanding to interpret and create accounts of people, places, and times.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier French first-language literacy is useful background. This reference does not determine constitutional eligibility or school admission.

**Materials:** None are required for reading. Texts and reference tools for learning should be selected separately for French-language suitability, accessibility, and rights.

**How to use:** Consult [S1], especially its Grade 5 emphasis on **legends, biographies, and autobiographies**. It also encourages other text types; these genres are not a closed reading list. Local IDs support mappings and are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Texts can reveal a period's values, practices, and beliefs. Their form contributes to meaning alongside their words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), portraits of an era/population and meaning through form and words.

### BI-02

An account or argument becomes more credible when the writer supplies relevant references and examples.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), references/examples and credibility.

### BI-03

Using French can strengthen a sense of community belonging. Canada's Francophone history and culture also include Indigenous narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), French communication/community and Indigenous narratives in Canadian Francophone culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Distinguish central ideas from secondary ones, test interpretations against clues, and examine how characters, events, and resolutions relate. Recognize non-linear narration, including shifts backward or forward in time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), important ideas, hypotheses, non-linear progression, and narrative analysis.

### CC-02

Support ideas with facts and arguments from texts. Compare cultural references with one's own experience, recognize varieties of French, and consider how Indigenous narratives reflect beliefs and the importance of storytellers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), supporting opinions, cultural references, linguistic variety, Indigenous beliefs, and storytelling.

### CC-03

Communicate accurately and fluently using the strategies studied. Prepare for a particular audience and justify a viewpoint with examples and sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), expression, justification, and planning for an audience.

### CC-04

Use explicit and implicit evidence to portray a person, period, or place, and summarize texts while retaining their important meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), evidence-based portraits and summaries.

### CC-05

Write in the genres under study using the relevant language conventions. Revise, correct, and enrich work with the help of reference tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), writing by text type/conventions and revision.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how legends combine elements of reality and imagination, and how biography and autobiography present a life from different viewpoints. Include features of oral tradition within Indigenous texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools and the introductory emphasis on legends, biography, and autobiography.

### C-02

Organize narratives and everyday informational texts coherently. Use chronological relationships, connecting language and punctuation, and bibliographies to guide a reader.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative structure, ordinary-text structure, coherence, chronology, and bibliography.

### C-03

Understand comparisons and superlatives, the grammatical work of adjectives and adverbs, and consistency among verb tenses. The elaboration names the **present indicative, compound past, imperfect, and near future** as the tense context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools and sequence-of-tenses elaboration.

### C-04

Develop strategies for communication and social interaction, reading for meaning, and planning, drafting, and improving writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication/socialization, reading, and writing strategies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This curriculum develops French first-language literacy and Francophone cultural identity; it is not beginner second-language French or the immersion curriculum. [English Language Arts 5](../english-language-arts/curriculum.md) is a separate component, not a translation of this record.

The source covers oral, written, visual, print, and digital forms. Its genre emphasis and grammar should be used in meaningful text work rather than read as an exhaustive list of isolated exercises. Indigenous narratives belong to particular peoples and knowledge holders; recognition within the curriculum does not authorize reproduction, adaptation, or an invented universal account.
