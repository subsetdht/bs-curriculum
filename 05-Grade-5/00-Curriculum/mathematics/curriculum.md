# Mathematics - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English; this is not a prescription for classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Extend number sense and flexible computation while using patterns, measurement, geometric reasoning, data, probability, and financial thinking to solve meaningful problems.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience with place value, fractions, the four operations, and measurement is useful background, not a formal placement test.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Later learning materials should identify their own representations, tools, and accessible ways to participate.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], paying particular attention to the different numerical limits and levels of fluency. Use the local IDs for mappings; they are not ministry codes or a teaching sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Different fraction representations can describe the same quantity. Understanding number relationships also supports flexible methods for calculating with larger whole numbers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, equivalent fractions and multi-digit computational fluency.

### BI-02

A table can expose a pattern's regularity and help express a rule that applies beyond the entries already shown.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, regularities in number patterns represented in tables.

### BI-03

Measuring a shape's boundary and the space it encloses provides different, related ways to compare shapes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, area and perimeter of closed shapes.

### BI-04

Graphs can summarize larger quantities by assigning a group of observations to each displayed unit, making the scale important to interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, many-to-one correspondence in graphs.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Reason about quantities, estimate sensibly, and develop mental strategies. Use models and appropriate technology to investigate mathematics in meaningful situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and analyzing.

### CC-02

Build understanding through inquiry, play, visualization, and varied problem-solving strategies. Work with contexts connected to place, stories, and cultural practices, including those relevant to local First Peoples and other communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Understanding and solving.

### CC-03

Represent ideas with objects, images, and symbols; explain decisions using mathematical language and reasons. Contribute to discussion in ways that make one's thinking available to others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and representing.

### CC-04

Reflect on mathematical thinking and connect ideas with other learning and personal interests. Bring First Peoples perspectives and worldviews into meaningful mathematical connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and reflecting.

## Content

### C-01

Use place value and benchmarks to interpret, compare, and order whole numbers through **1,000,000**, decimals through **thousandths**, and fractions. Understand equivalent fractions as different representations of an equal amount when the whole is the same.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 1,000,000; decimals to thousandths; equivalent fractions; whole-number, fraction, and decimal benchmarks.

### C-02

Add and subtract whole numbers through **1,000,000** and decimals through **thousandths**. Develop whole-number multiplication and division at the source's **three-digit** scope, including interpretation of remainders, using flexible methods and sensible estimates.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, whole-number addition/subtraction, multiplication/division to three digits with remainders, and decimal addition/subtraction.

### C-03

Extend fluency with addition and subtraction facts to **20**, while developing emerging fluency with multiplication and division facts to **100**. Use number relationships and strategies, not only recall.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, extending and emerging computational fluency; accompanying fact-fluency elaborations.

### C-04

Describe increasing and decreasing patterns with words, numbers, symbols, and variables. Use a variable to represent an unknown in a **one-step equation**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, pattern rules and one-step equations with variables.

### C-05

Measure the area of squares and rectangles, and investigate how area relates to perimeter without assuming one determines the other. Use time measurements to reason about duration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, area measurement, area/perimeter relationships, and duration.

### C-06

Classify prisms and pyramids by their properties. Investigate a **single** translation, reflection, or rotation as a movement of a shape.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, classification of prisms and pyramids and single transformations.

### C-07

Read and construct double bar graphs using one-to-one or many-to-one scales. Investigate chance through experiments concerned with **single events or outcomes**, including fractional representations described in the elaboration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, double bar graphs and probability experiments.

### C-08

Calculate money amounts and change through **$1,000**. Develop a simple financial plan that connects a goal with available income and anticipated expenses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy and its budgeting elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The limits above are not interchangeable. Equivalent fractions do not introduce a general fraction-arithmetic requirement; one-step equations are not multi-step algebra; single-event probability is not a compound-event syllabus.

The fact-fluency elaboration explicitly does **not** intend memorization of the entire multiplication table at this level. It anticipates growing fluency and recall of many facts by the end of Grade 5, not a timed mastery test of all facts. The three-digit operation wording is retained rather than inventing an additional factor-size rule.

Official examples of tools, financial situations, and cultural practices remain at [S1]. Local First Peoples mathematical knowledge should be approached through appropriate relationships and permissions, not copied as a generic design exercise.
