# Social Studies - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations supported by the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English, separately from classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Investigate **Canadian Issues and Governance**, relating identity, migration, discrimination, rights, government, land, and resources through evidence and multiple perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier work with communities, maps, timelines, and historical questions is useful, not a formal prerequisite. Do not assume a shared family migration history.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Any later inquiry requires its own appropriate sources, permissions, and support for sensitive historical topics.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for the official Grade 5 expectations. The local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes or a mandated order of historical events.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Canadian identity continues to be shaped by immigration and cultural diversity, as well as by the lasting effects of public policies and treatment of minority peoples. These legacies need examination rather than a single celebratory account.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, immigration/multiculturalism and the positive and negative legacies of policies toward minority peoples.

### BI-02

Regional differences matter to Canada's economic life and governance: resources influence regional identities, while institutions must respond to a diverse country.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, natural resources and regional identity; institutions, government, and regional diversity.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Frame questions, gather and analyze information, and communicate reasoned findings. Develop a plan of action for an identified issue, using inquiry to connect understanding with civic participation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and developing a plan of action.

### CC-02

Defend judgments about historical significance and examine the origins and content of sources, including mass media. Corroborate inferences before drawing conclusions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance and evidence.

### CC-03

Use chronology to examine what has changed or continued and who has benefited or been harmed. Distinguish intended from unintended consequences and consider plausible alternative outcomes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, continuity and change; cause and consequence.

### CC-04

Infer stakeholders' beliefs, values, and motivations from evidence when considering their perspectives on events or issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-05

Make ethical judgments with attention to the conditions of a particular time and place, and assess appropriate responses to actions and decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Explore how Canadian identity and immigration have changed over time, including the relationships among migration, settlement, and the development of society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, evolution of Canadian identity and changing immigration.

### C-02

Examine discriminatory government actions and their effects, including the source's named cases of the **Head Tax, Komagata Maru incident, residential schools, and internments**. Connect this history with human rights and responses to discrimination in Canadian society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past discriminatory government policies/actions and human rights/responses to discrimination.

### C-03

Understand the responsibilities and funding of **First Peoples, federal, provincial, and municipal governments**, together with how people participate and are represented in Canada's systems of government.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, government levels/functions/funding and participation/representation.

### C-04

Investigate how the location and use of resources relate to economic development in different Canadian regions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, resources and regional economic development.

### C-05

Study First Peoples land ownership and use, including the relationships and issues explored through the source's treaty, land-use, and land-claim elaborations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples land ownership and use.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This grade's focus is Canadian issues and governance, not the distinct adjacent-grade studies of early contact or global issues. Maps, timelines, source evaluation, and civic action support inquiry; the many examples in [S1] do not all become mandatory case studies.

First Peoples governance and land relationships are present-day as well as historical subjects. Use specific Nations' authoritative perspectives and appropriate permissions. Historical context supports ethical inquiry; it does not require learners to justify discrimination or reenact victimization. No activity here requires disclosure of family trauma, ancestry, citizenship, or migration status.
