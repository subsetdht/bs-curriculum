# Arts Education - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English, separately from classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Make, interpret, and share meaning through dance, drama, music, and visual arts, using increasingly intentional choices and reflection.

**Prerequisites:** Previous creative experiences are helpful; this reference does not require private lessons, specialist training, or a particular performance ability.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Later activities must specify appropriate materials, accessible participation, safety, and permissions for creative works.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] across all four art forms. Local IDs are repository references for curriculum mapping, not ministry codes or a prescribed sequence of projects.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Creative expression can deepen identity and belonging while opening possibilities through experimentation and encounters with other perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative expression/identity/belonging and artistic experimentation.

### BI-02

Dance, drama, music, and visual arts have distinctive ways to convey meaning; no single art form represents the whole curriculum.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the four arts as languages for creating and communicating.

### BI-03

Art responds to its surroundings and can also influence how people experience and understand the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, reciprocal relationships between artworks and the world.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Choose elements, materials, movement, tools, technologies, and settings deliberately to convey meaning. Develop work independently and with others through imagination, inquiry, experimentation, and purposeful play.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, intentional selection and individual/collaborative creation.

### CC-02

Explore identity, place, culture, and belonging through expression, and investigate relationships among cultures, societies, and the arts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, identity/place and cultural relationships.

### CC-03

Observe how artists work, ask questions about their choices, and refine ideas and technical skills. Reflect individually and collectively, bringing knowledge from other subjects into making and interpreting art and examining its wider-world connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting.

### CC-04

Use symbols and artistic elements to communicate feelings, experiences, and ideas. Respond to works with attention to artists' intentions, and adapt learning for different contexts, purposes, and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, adaptation, expression, interpretation, and responses to artists' intent.

### CC-05

Document, experience, and present creative work in varied ways, showing growing sophistication in the use of curricular knowledge and skills.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, documentation/presentation and increasingly sophisticated application.

## Content

### C-01

In dance, connect body movement with space, dynamics, time, relationships, form, and movement principles. In drama, explore how characters and events within a time and place establish tension, mood, and focus.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance and drama elements.

### C-02

Explore how music organizes sound and silence through pulse, metre, rhythm, duration, and tempo, and how pitch, tone colour, loudness, texture, and form shape a work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements.

### C-03

Use visual elements such as line, shape, space, form, texture, colour, and value in purposeful relationships. Consider balance, contrast and emphasis, patterned repetition and rhythm, and combinations that create unity, harmony, or variety.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual-art elements and principles of design.

### C-04

Learn ways to develop work through processes, materials, technologies, tools, and techniques. These include choreographic devices, dramatic forms, notation in music and dance, and strategies for developing images. Symbolism and metaphor provide ways to explore ideas and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, arts-making processes; choreography; dramatic forms; notation; image development; symbolism/metaphor.

### C-05

Encounter traditional and contemporary Indigenous arts and arts-making processes, alongside regional and national works and traditions from diverse communities, periods, and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional/contemporary Aboriginal arts and diverse regional/national works and traditions.

### C-06

Understand individual and shared responsibilities for physical and emotional safety when creating, experiencing, and presenting art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal and collective responsibility in a safe learning environment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's elements and principles are expressly non-exhaustive. This summary preserves the four art forms without turning each elaboration example into a compulsory activity, instrument, or performance. Presentation can take different forms and does not necessarily mean public performance.

Indigenous arts remain within the subject's curriculum. Learning about a work does not authorize copying a design, song, dance, ceremony, or story. Respect the permissions and protocols of the people whose works are being considered.
