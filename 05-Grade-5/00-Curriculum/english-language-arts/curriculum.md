# English Language Arts - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > English Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; the English Language Arts component of Early French Immersion and of the Francophone program. See the [sourced program basis](../README.md#program-basis). Provincial policy provides ELA from Grade 4 onward in Early Immersion; it does not replace that program's French language arts.

**Document language:** English. This record concerns English literacy, not the separate [immersion](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) or [Francophone](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) French language-arts expectations.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and create varied texts through inquiry, critical thinking, creative language, and communication that considers purpose, audience, identity, and perspective.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing experiences provide background, not a prescribed reading-level admission requirement.

**Materials:** None are required for reading this reference. Text selections and activities need their own accessibility, age-suitability, and rights review.

**How to use:** Read [S1] across comprehension and creation, individually and collaboratively. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes or a required book list.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Playing creatively with language can bring enjoyment and reveal how language works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity/joy and creative, playful language use.

### BI-02

Stories and other texts can connect personal experience with other lives and the wider world, while inviting more than one interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, connections through texts and different perspectives.

### BI-03

An informed participant in society questions messages rather than accepting everything heard, read, or viewed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, questioning texts and engaged citizenship.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Draw on prior knowledge and varied sources, using strategies before, during, and after engaging with a text. Combine and evaluate ideas to guide inquiry and deepen understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, accessing information, comprehension strategies, synthesis, and thinking skills.

### CC-02

Consider purpose, audience, context, perspective, and voice when interpreting meaning. Explain connections between language and identity, and respond personally and creatively using experience and knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, purposes/audiences/perspectives, context/voice, identity, connections, and responses.

### CC-03

Explain how literary choices and the organization of a text contribute to its meaning, rather than identifying features without considering their effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, literary elements/techniques/devices and organization.

### CC-04

Recognize the roles of oral tradition and texts within First Peoples cultures, including how stories connect people with land.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples oral tradition, purposes of texts, and connections to land.

### CC-05

Exchange ideas to build shared understanding, then use writing and design processes to develop original texts for varied purposes and audiences. Experiment with language to develop style and transform existing information into one's own expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchanges, writing/design processes, creative language, and original texts.

### CC-06

Organize written communication in paragraphs using Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation. Expand knowledge of how words are formed and used, and apply processes for preparing and sharing oral stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, written conventions, word knowledge, and oral storytelling.

## Content

### C-01

Explore relationships among a text's form, purpose, and genre; its displayed features; and the perspective it presents. Learn how characterization, narrative structure, imagery, and figurative language shape a reader's experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text and its literary-element/device elaborations.

### C-02

Develop reading and oral-language strategies, ways to reflect on one's own learning, and writing processes that include developing and improving a text for an audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-03

Understand how oral delivery influences communication. Develop paragraphs with a central idea and supporting detail, and use sentence structure, grammatical relationships, and written conventions to clarify meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: oral features, paragraphing, sentence structure/grammar, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Texts include oral, written, visual, digital, and combined forms. This source does not establish a commercial reading level, set novel, universal word count, or a separate curriculum consisting only of grammar exercises.

Source examples clarify rather than prescribe every activity. First Peoples storytelling must retain its cultural and land-based context; permission may be needed to retell an existing story. The French-language-arts records are distinct curricula, not translations of ELA.
