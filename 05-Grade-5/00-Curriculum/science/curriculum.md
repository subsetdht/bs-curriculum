# Science - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Science

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English, not a statement of classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Investigate body systems, solutions, machines, and Earth materials while developing fair testing, evidence-based explanations, and responsible relationships with the environment.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier observation, measurement, and inquiry experiences are useful background, not formal course prerequisites.

**Materials:** None are required to read the reference. Investigations need their own appropriate equipment, supervision, risk assessment, and accessibility arrangements.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the complete learning standards and elaborations. Local IDs support precise mappings but are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding interacting parts helps explain how systems work: organ systems support organisms' survival and interaction with their surroundings, while natural and constructed machines transfer force and energy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, multicellular organisms and organ systems; machines and transfers of force/energy.

### BI-02

A solution is uniform throughout, even though it contains substances combined through dissolving.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, homogeneous solutions; Content elaboration on dissolving.

### BI-03

Earth materials are transformed through the rock cycle. Their use as resources connects these natural processes with decisions about environmental care.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, rock cycle and natural resources; associated stewardship inquiry.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Sustain curiosity about a scientific problem, observe in familiar and unfamiliar settings, and develop questions and predictions that can guide an investigation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Questioning and predicting.

### CC-02

With support, plan an investigation and decide what to change, measure, and record for a fair test. Select relevant data and suitable tools, including digital tools where appropriate, and identify safety risks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Planning and conducting.

### CC-03

Interpret local experiences and recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives as information sources. Organize data in suitable representations, identify relationships, compare results with predictions, and remain open to alternative explanations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Processing and analyzing data and information.

### CC-04

Assess fairness, possible errors, and ways to improve an investigation. Notice assumptions in secondary sources, value evidence, and consider social, ethical, and environmental implications of findings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Evaluating.

### CC-05

Apply learning to unfamiliar situations and refine ideas through collaborative design and problem solving. Use scientific learning to contribute to care for people and the community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applying and innovating.

### CC-06

Communicate explanations, ideas, and processes in different forms, and reflect on how personal and shared experiences of place inform learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating.

## Content

### C-01

Relate basic structures to functions in the **digestive, musculo-skeletal, respiratory, and circulatory systems**, considering how they support the body's needs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, basic structures and functions of body systems.

### C-02

Investigate solutions, how substances dissolve, and their solubility. The elaborations connect this learning with solution properties and ways to separate dissolved materials.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, solutions and solubility, with elaborations.

### C-03

Explore how simple machines change the direction or size of a force and how machines can combine these effects. Include both constructed machines and mechanisms found in nature. Understand power as the rate of energy transfer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, simple machines and force effects; constructed/natural machines; power.

### C-04

Learn the rock cycle and identify types of Earth materials found locally, rather than assuming the same rocks and soils occur everywhere.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, rock cycle and local types of Earth materials.

### C-05

Consider sustainable use of BC resources alongside First Peoples understandings of environmental interconnectedness and knowledge of sustainable practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, interconnectedness, BC resource sustainability, and First Peoples sustainable practices.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The body-system scope is the four systems named above; this is not an unrestricted anatomy syllabus. The study of machines includes power, but the source does not prescribe a numerical formula-drill sequence. Examples of solutions, machinery, and local materials remain in the official elaborations, not a required experiment list.

First Peoples environmental knowledge is part of both inquiry and content. Respect local protocols, attribution, and permissions; no stories, songs, or community knowledge are reproduced here. Environmental stewardship does not require students to disclose personal or family resource-use practices.
