# Mandarin - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Mandarin

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education, subject to local offering. See the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English. The source explains Mandarin learning in English with Chinese-language examples; source language is not the target language.

**Learning objective:** Begin connecting Mandarin sounds, tones, phonetic notation, and Chinese characters with meaning in everyday communication and cultural contexts.

**Prerequisites:** No formal prior Mandarin or character knowledge is asserted by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed for reading. Later materials should supply appropriate visual and audio access and use texts with suitable permissions.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 standards in [S1] alongside the [course-selection notes](../README.md#course-selection-and-programs). The 5-10 download contains separate grades, not a shared 5-10 standard. Local IDs are not official BC codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Intentional listening and viewing, including attention to non-verbal cues, support comprehension. Frequently used words and patterns can sustain an initial two-way conversation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing, cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

Learning another language can offer a new perspective on identity and on cultural traditions and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and traditions.

### BI-03

Stories help language learning by presenting words and meanings in a connected context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize relationships among sounds and tones, phonetic representations, characters, and meaning. Understand commonly used vocabulary and important information in slow, clear speech and other texts, including stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sound/representation/character relationships and comprehension.

### CC-02

Draw on language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues to interpret messages. Follow basic instructions and seek clarification when meaning is uncertain.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, cues, commands, and clarification.

### CC-03

Take part in simple exchanges and communicate information through formats suited to one's own and others' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Consider experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens, recognizing First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Explore phonetic notation and tonal variations in Mandarin. The elaborations relate sound to systems such as Pinyin or Zhuyin rather than treating Roman letters as Chinese characters.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phonetic systems and tonal variations; Thinking and communicating, phonetic-representation elaboration.

### C-02

Learn commonly used Chinese characters and their component strokes and structures, including introductory stroke order. Recognize that traditional and simplified character systems exist.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, characters, basic strokes, and structure; character-system elaboration under Thinking and communicating.

### C-03

Develop common vocabulary and patterns for questions, descriptions, personal information about self and others, and basic commands.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-04

Learn about Chinese communities in Canada, Chinese artworks, and common elements of cultural festivals and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, communities in Canada, works of art, and cultural festivals/celebrations.

### C-05

Consider First Peoples relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples language/culture connections.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source distinguishes vocabulary words from individual characters. It does not prescribe a character-count quota or require mastery of both character systems. The introductory awareness of scripts and phonetic notation should not be expanded into adjacent-grade literacy requirements.

Do not equate all Chinese communities with a single language variety or cultural practice. Cultural examples are not a required repertoire, and artworks and First Peoples knowledge require appropriate context, attribution, and permissions.
