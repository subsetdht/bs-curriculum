# German - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > German

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also an approved language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education where selected. See the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis) and [course-selection notes](../README.md#course-selection-and-programs).

**Document language:** English; the primary source is written in English with German examples. Summary language does not determine instruction language.

**Learning objective:** Begin understanding and exchanging familiar German messages while exploring how language connects with community, culture, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** No formal prior German study is asserted. Earlier language experiences may provide useful connections.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Any later texts, recordings, and supports need their own suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 source [S1], not every grade in its 5-10 compilation. Local IDs are repository references rather than official BC standard codes; German is one course choice, not a universal additional requirement.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attention to spoken language and visible cues can support understanding, and frequently used language patterns make an initial two-way exchange possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing, verbal/non-verbal cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

Learning another language offers ways to explore identity and encounter different traditions and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and cultural traditions.

### BI-03

Stories give language a meaningful setting in which learners can develop understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories and language acquisition.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect pronunciation and intonation with meaning, and identify familiar language and key information in slow, clear speech and other texts, including stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sound/meaning relationships and comprehension.

### CC-02

Use language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues, ask for clarification, and respond to simple instructions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, clarification, non-verbal cues, and commands.

### CC-03

Participate in simple exchanges and communicate information in a presentation format appropriate to one's own and others' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Consider experiences and perspectives through culture, recognizing First Peoples knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Explore connections through cognates and German speech sounds. Develop a basic understanding of how German word forms vary with **gender, case, and number**, rather than treating grammar as a full declension-table syllabus.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cognates, phonemes, and German declination as a basic concept.

### C-02

Use common language for questions and statements, information about self and others, and basic commands. Recognize formal and informal ways of addressing someone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency language, including modes of address.

### C-03

Learn about German communities in Canada, artistic works, and common features of German cultural celebrations and festivals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, communities, works of art, and festivals/celebrations.

### C-04

Explore First Peoples perspectives on how language relates to oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples language/culture connections.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source introduces declination; it does not require mastery of every inflected form. Its expressions and named festivals are illustrations, not a compulsory vocabulary inventory or a claim that German-speaking communities have identical practices.

Use culturally appropriate sources and permissions for artworks and oral histories. The arts elaboration includes appropriation and plagiarism concerns. This record does not reproduce songs, texts, or cultural designs.
