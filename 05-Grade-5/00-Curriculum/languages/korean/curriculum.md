# Korean - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Korean

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education. See the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis) for authorization rather than an assurance of local offering.

**Document language:** English; the official source is English with Korean examples. This does not prescribe the language used to support instruction.

**Learning objective:** Develop initial Korean communication and literacy while considering how language form, etiquette, culture, and community affect meaning.

**Prerequisites:** No formal prior Korean proficiency is specified by this reference.

**Materials:** None are required to read the summary. Later language materials and communication supports need separate accessibility, suitability, and rights review.

**How to use:** Follow the Grade 5 standards in [S1] and the [course-selection notes](../README.md#course-selection-and-programs). The multi-grade download does not broaden this record beyond Grade 5. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending to what is heard and seen helps build understanding, and familiar patterns can support a basic exchange even when language knowledge is still developing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing, verbal/non-verbal cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

Exploring a new language can broaden understanding of identity and of the traditions through which cultures celebrate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and traditions.

### BI-03

Stories offer meaningful opportunities to encounter and acquire language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect pronunciation with meaning. Identify familiar vocabulary and key information in slow, clear speech and other texts, and understand stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation and comprehension.

### CC-02

Use language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues, respond to basic commands, and ask for clarification when needed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, cues, instructions, and clarification.

### CC-03

Participate in simple interactions and present information in a form suited to participants' differing abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Consider experiences, perspectives, and worldviews through culture, recognizing First Peoples knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Learn the Korean alphabet and speech sounds, how strokes are ordered, and how letters are combined into syllables.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, alphabet, phonemes, stroke order, and syllable construction.

### C-02

Explore how formality and etiquette influence language choices. The elaboration introduces formal polite, informal polite, and casual language endings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, language formality and etiquette, with elaboration.

### C-03

Develop common language for questions, descriptions, everyday information, and basic commands.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-04

Learn about Korean communities in Canada, creative works, and common elements of festivals and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean communities, works of art, and cultural celebrations.

### C-05

Consider First Peoples perspectives connecting language with oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples language/culture connections.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This is an introductory course, not a requirement to master every honorific form or an advanced writing syllabus. The source does not set a universal vocabulary count.

Cultural and language examples illustrate meaning in context; avoid treating them as a script everyone must use regardless of relationship or situation. Use works with appropriate attribution and permissions, including for Indigenous knowledge and cultural expression.
