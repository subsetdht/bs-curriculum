# American Sign Language (ASL) - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > American Sign Language

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education. The [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis) establishes authorization separately from local availability and compulsory subjects.

**Document language:** English. The source explains ASL learning in English; ASL itself is a signed language, not spoken English or French.

**Learning objective:** Begin communicating visually in ASL while developing understanding of Deaf communities and relationships among language, identity, and culture.

**Prerequisites:** No previous signing proficiency is asserted by this reference. Learners' communication and access needs should inform participation.

**Materials:** None are required to read the reference. Learning activities need appropriate visual access and separately selected, permission-appropriate signed materials.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 standards in [S1], not the whole 5-10 download as one grade band. Choose courses through the [Languages inventory](../README.md); local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Careful visual attention makes signed communication possible. Meaning comes through non-manual signals as well as signs and gestures, enabling an exchange between participants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional viewing, non-verbal cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

Learning a language can reveal new ways of understanding identity and the traditions through which communities celebrate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and cultural traditions.

### BI-03

Stories provide meaningful contexts for acquiring and using language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language learning through stories.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret how facial expression, gesture, and changes in a sign's size, style, or intensity affect meaning. Understand key information in simple signed phrases, familiar vocabulary, and simple stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, relationships between form and meaning; signed phrases, vocabulary, and stories.

### CC-02

Use context, prior knowledge, and other language-learning strategies to support understanding. Respond to simple instructions and ask for clarification when meaning is uncertain.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, learning strategies, commands/instructions, and clarification.

### CC-03

Take part in simple interactions and share information in formats suited to participants' diverse abilities and visual communication needs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Develop awareness of Deaf-community conventions and consider experiences through a cultural lens. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Understand ASL as a complete natural language. Learn its manual alphabet and signs for **numbers 1-20**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ASL as a natural language, manual alphabet, and number range.

### C-02

Explore how non-manual signals and the placement of signs contribute to meaning, including the source's introductory treatment of gender placement. Use common iconic signs and basic ASL structures to ask questions, describe people or things, and exchange information and preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, gender placement, non-manual signals, and common iconic signs/basic ASL structure.

### C-03

Explore a **number story using 1-5**, in which number signs form a short narrative rather than simply label quantities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number story 1-5 and its elaboration.

### C-04

Learn about Deaf communities and their cultural conventions, together with common features of cultural traditions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural traditions and Deaf-community information/cultural aspects.

### C-05

Consider First Peoples understandings of the connections among language, histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The number-sign range **1-20** and the number-story range **1-5** are different expectations. Non-manual signals are meaningful features of ASL, not incidental facial decoration. Do not replace signed-language learning with a spoken-language task or treat ASL as English translated word by word.

Gender placement describes a linguistic convention in the source, not a requirement to label students' identities. Respect Deaf cultural expertise and First Peoples protocols; learning about signed stories or community traditions does not permit copying or distributing them.
