## Course selection and programs

**These are choices, not nine compulsory subjects.** The [Language Education Policy][policy] assigns boards responsibility for which second languages to offer; Core French is the default when no alternative is offered. The [Required Areas of Learning order][areas], rather than a catalog's existence, establishes the applicable requirements and exceptions.

| Program | How to use this inventory |
| --- | --- |
| English-language | The ordinary Grade 5 requirement is a language other than English. All nine catalog courses are represented as choices, subject to the board's offering and the order's exceptions. |
| Early French Immersion | [French Immersion Language Arts](../french-immersion-language-arts/README.md) and [English Language Arts](../english-language-arts/README.md) are separate subject components. Section 4(c) requires ELA in immersion Grades 4-9; it does not require all these language courses in addition. The eight non-French courses are mapped as additional options where selected. |
| Francophone | Use [Francophone Language Arts](../francophone-language-arts/README.md) for French first-language literacy and [English Language Arts](../english-language-arts/README.md) for the English component. Section 4(b) concerns a language other than French; the eight non-French courses are available in this inventory as options, not eight additional requirements. Core French is neither French first-language arts nor a way to satisfy a non-French-language requirement. |

The [Educational Program Guide Order][guides], section 2(a)-(b), identifies the language guides in English and the corresponding French documents for immersion and Francophone programs. This supports the eight non-French courses' program applicability; it does not guarantee a school's staffing, timetable, resources, or course offering. Core French's record is mapped to the English-language pathway rather than used to imply a requirement in either French program.

Section 3 of the [Required Areas of Learning order][areas] addresses specified circumstances involving IEPs or English-language-learning services; an exemption should not be inferred merely from a disability or an ELL label. Section 5 concerns Late Immersion in **Grade 6**, not Grade 5. Section 6 also permits specified local Indigenous-language study under an agreement with a band council or treaty First Nation. Read these provisions alongside the general policy; this page does not decide individual eligibility or exemptions.

See the [Grade 5 program basis](../README.md#program-basis) for the reviewed provincial sources. School and district implementation, including any SD43 arrangements, remains separate.

## Coverage and limits

The nine entries above match the published [Languages 5 course list][catalog]. There are **no unresolved source-access or missing-summary blockers for those nine entries**. The course downloads span several grades, but these records summarize only Grade 5.

This is not a claim to inventory every locally developed or locally owned language curriculum in BC. The official [Languages Template][template] describes a separate development/approval process, and the current order includes the local Indigenous-language provision described above. Such curricula and local offerings must be established with their authorities rather than invented as extra Grade 5 courses or confused with unmapped enrichment.

The summaries preserve differences such as ASL's visual grammar and number-story range, Italian story retelling, and distinct writing systems. Similar communication strands do not make the courses interchangeable. First Peoples language/culture connections and permissions remain within the individual records.

No French-language editions, lessons, assessments, resources, or tools are supplied by this pass. Source revision dates were not established; review dates are not curriculum editions.
