# Spanish - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Spanish

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education. See the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis) for authorization and requirement distinctions.

**Document language:** English. The official source is English with Spanish examples; the curriculum concerns learning Spanish.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange simple Spanish messages about people, descriptions, and preferences while exploring cultural perspectives and communities.

**Prerequisites:** No formal previous Spanish study is asserted. Learners may draw on varied prior language experiences.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Later texts and communication supports require separate suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 source [S1] and the [Languages course-selection notes](../README.md#course-selection-and-programs). The 5-10 compilation contains separate grades. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing support interpretation of spoken and non-verbal clues. Frequently used language patterns can make a simple exchange possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing, cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

Learning another language creates opportunities to explore identity and the traditions through which people celebrate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and cultural traditions.

### BI-03

Stories help learners develop language in meaningful contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect pronunciation, letters, punctuation, and intonation with meaning. Understand frequently used vocabulary and important information in slow, clear speech and other texts, including simple stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sound/writing/meaning relationships and comprehension.

### CC-02

Use language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues, follow simple instructions, and seek clarification.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, cues, commands, and clarification.

### CC-03

Take part in simple interactions and share information through forms that fit different participants' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Consider experiences and worldviews through culture, recognizing First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Learn Spanish speech sounds and introductory grammatical distinctions of gender and singular/plural number.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Spanish phonemes and gender/number.

### C-02

Develop frequently used language for simple questions and descriptions, information about oneself and others, and statements of opinions and preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-03

Explore Hispanic communities in Canada, artistic works, and common elements of cultural festivals and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Hispanic communities, works of art, and festivals/celebrations.

### C-04

Consider First Peoples perspectives connecting language with oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples language/culture connections.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's example expressions and cultural activities are not a required phrasebook, artwork list, or fixed sequence. This is beginner communication rather than an unrestricted grammar syllabus.

The source uses the term "Hispanic"; it does not establish one uniform identity or practice for all Spanish-speaking communities. Use cultural works with appropriate attribution and permissions, including respect for First Peoples knowledge and the ethics of appropriation and plagiarism.
