# Japanese - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Japanese

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education, as distinguished from required subjects in the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English. The official source is English with Japanese examples; Japanese is the language being learned.

**Learning objective:** Begin interpreting Japanese speech and writing systems, exchanging familiar messages, and exploring language in cultural and community contexts.

**Prerequisites:** This reference asserts no formal prior Japanese study or script proficiency.

**Materials:** None are needed for reading. Any later language texts, recordings, or supports require separate accessibility, suitability, and rights decisions.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 portion of [S1], not the whole 5-10 download as a single grade band. The [Languages inventory](../README.md) distinguishes course choices. Local IDs are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Listening and viewing attentively help learners combine language with visible cues. Common words and patterns provide a starting point for two-way communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening/viewing, cues, and reciprocal communication.

### BI-02

A new language offers another way to explore identity and encounter cultural traditions and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and culture.

### BI-03

Stories can help learners acquire language in a connected, meaningful form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories and language learning.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize different scripts and syllabic sounds. Understand familiar vocabulary and important information in slow, clear speech and other texts, including stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, scripts/syllabic sounds and comprehension.

### CC-02

Use language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues to support understanding. Respond to simple instructions and seek clarification when a message is unclear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, non-verbal cues, commands, and clarification.

### CC-03

Take part in simple interactions and share information in forms that suit participants' diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, interactions and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Consider one's own and others' experiences through a cultural lens. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Explore **romaji**, which represents Japanese sounds using the Roman alphabet, and the **Japanese syllabaries**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, romaji and Japanese syllabaries.

### C-02

Develop common language for simple questions and descriptions, greetings and information about self and others, and basic commands.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-03

Learn about Japanese communities in Canada, Japanese artworks, and common elements of cultural festivals and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, works of art, communities in Canada, and festivals/celebrations.

### C-04

Consider First Peoples perspectives on relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples language/culture connections.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source names romaji and syllabaries but does not establish a kanji inventory, universal character count, or mastery deadline here. Keep introductory script recognition distinct from assumptions about advanced literacy.

The cultural examples are not a prescribed festival itinerary or a claim that all Japanese communities share one practice. Respect permissions for artworks and First Peoples oral histories, including the source's cautions about appropriation and plagiarism.
