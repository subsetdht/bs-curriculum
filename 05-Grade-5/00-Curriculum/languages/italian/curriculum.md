# Italian - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Italian

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language; also a selected language-course option in Early French Immersion and Francophone education. Authorization and local availability are distinguished in the [program basis](../../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English; the official source is English with Italian examples. The target language is Italian, independently of this document's language.

**Learning objective:** Understand familiar Italian, take part in simple exchanges and storytelling, and examine connections between language and cultural life.

**Prerequisites:** No formal prior Italian proficiency is asserted. Connections with languages already known can support learning.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Cultural works and communication supports must be selected separately with accessibility and permissions in mind.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 5 learning standards in [S1] and the [course-selection notes](../README.md#course-selection-and-programs). The 5-10 source compilation is not a single grade band. Local IDs are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful listening and viewing help learners combine spoken and non-verbal clues, while familiar words and patterns support reciprocal communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing, cues, and high-frequency patterns.

### BI-02

Another language can offer ways to explore identity and understand traditions through which people celebrate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and cultural traditions.

### BI-03

Stories make language meaningful and support its acquisition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize Italian letter sounds and common intonation patterns. Understand frequently used vocabulary and important information in slow, clear speech and other texts, including stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sounds/intonation and comprehension.

### CC-02

Use language-learning strategies and non-verbal clues to make meaning. Request clarification, respond to simple instructions, and participate in basic exchanges.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies, cues, clarification, instructions, and interactions.

### CC-03

Narrate or retell simple stories and share information using formats suited to diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating/retelling and presentation format.

### CC-04

Analyze experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens, recognizing First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness.

## Content

### C-01

Develop knowledge of the Italian alphabet and speech sounds, together with introductory grammatical gender and number.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, alphabet, phonemes, gender, and number.

### C-02

Use common vocabulary and patterns for simple questions, information, descriptions, and expressions of likes, dislikes, and preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-03

Explore traditional Italian stories, rhymes, and songs alongside other creative works. Learn about Italian cultural practices, festivals, and celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional narrative/song forms, cultural elements, festivals, and creative works.

### C-04

Consider First Peoples understandings of relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Unlike a generic receptive-language summary, this Grade 5 course explicitly includes **narrating or retelling simple stories**. That does not authorize retelling a protected cultural work without permission.

The source's phrases, celebrations, and creative-work examples remain illustrations rather than a compulsory repertoire. Respect cultural ownership and avoid plagiarism or appropriation. Course selection is separate from compulsory French or English language arts in the French programs.
