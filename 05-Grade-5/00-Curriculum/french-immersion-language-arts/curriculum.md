# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. Late Immersion begins at Grade 6 in provincial policy, so no Grade 5 Late Immersion applicability is asserted. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary source is French. This is an original summary, not an authorized translation or French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and communicate in French with attention to perspective, historical and cultural context, the interaction of image and text, and the use of information from several sources.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier immersion literacy provides background; this reference does not set admission or proficiency criteria. Program placement belongs with the school or district.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Appropriate French-language texts and reference tools should be selected separately.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the precise French expectations. The source identifies **comics and biography** as contexts for Grade 5 content. Pair this record with [English Language Arts 5](../english-language-arts/curriculum.md), not with Core French as a substitute. Local IDs are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Interactions reveal diverse perspectives, while fictional works present cultural and historical worlds shaped by an author's choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), interaction/human diversity and sociocultural/historical elements in fiction.

### BI-02

Research across sources can broaden both knowledge and vocabulary by bringing different viewpoints into consideration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), information from multiple sources.

### BI-03

Readers make meaning from the organization and visual presentation of a text as well as from its content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), interpretation, structure, and visual form.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Place texts, including Indigenous texts, in cultural and historical context. Identify a work's mood, separate central ideas from supporting ideas, and distinguish objective information from subjective treatment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), context, mood, main/secondary ideas, and objectivity/subjectivity.

### CC-02

Examine how words and images depend on one another. Gather information from several sources and select what is most relevant.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), text-image relationships and selecting information.

### CC-03

Adapt communication to its purpose and recipient. Explain characters through actions and social/historical context, and reconstruct a person's life using biographical details drawn from different sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), audience/intention, character description, and biographical evidence.

### CC-04

Synthesize information across texts, organize one's own text using the structures under study, and revise with reference tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), synthesis, organization/writing, and revision.

## Content

### C-01

Develop active listening, attention to other viewpoints, awareness of language register, and self-correction as parts of effective communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies.

### C-02

Explore cultural and historical elements through the distinctive features of comics and biographies. Consider, for example, the relationships of panels, dialogue, and images in comics and of life events, viewpoint, and evidence in biography.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), cultural/historical elements and literary tools; introductory context elaboration.

### C-03

Learn narrative and informational organization, chronological ordering, bibliographies, and punctuation that helps present another voice or additional information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization, including quotation marks, colon, and parentheses in its punctuation elaboration.

### C-04

Use French adverbs, simple relative pronouns, and negative constructions, together with verb forms suited to the genres studied. The French elaboration identifies the **compound past, imperfect, and present imperative**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools and verb-mood/tense elaboration.

### C-05

Develop revision approaches that involve rereading and consulting appropriate reference tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), revision strategies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source includes oral, written, visual, print, and digital contexts; comics and biography are named study contexts, not a licence to replace the curriculum with a single book. This is not the first-language Francophone curriculum and does not import Grade 5 English grammar as French expectations.

Read the French source for exact language terminology and elaborations, including details not exposed in every English rendering. No dialogue, literary text, or Indigenous narrative is reproduced or translated here; cultural works require appropriate attribution and permissions.
