# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English. This does not specify classroom language or make this a French-language teaching resource.

**Learning objective:** Use information about users, iterative design, practical skills, and appropriate technologies to make and improve a purposeful product.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier exploration and making experiences are helpful, not formal entry requirements. This reference assumes no particular device, software, or coding experience.

**Materials:** None are required to read the reference. Any later making activity must identify suitable materials, accessible participation, and physical and digital safety arrangements.

**How to use:** Apply the **Grades 4-5 competencies** in [S1] to **Grade 5 content from another learning area**. Cite both subjects when mapping an integrated activity. Local BI, CC, and C IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

A design becomes more useful when an initial version is tried out, assessed, and changed rather than treated as a finished answer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, improvement through prototyping and testing.

### BI-02

Making involves developing skills through sustained practice and selecting tools in relation to the work they need to accomplish.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, skill development and task-dependent choice of tools and technology.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate potential users and turn an opportunity into a defined design problem. Establish what the product should achieve, which features users need, and what limits the design must respect.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Understanding context and Defining.

### CC-02

Develop alternatives with others, compare them against the objective and constraints, and choose an idea that can be taken forward.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Ideating.

### CC-03

Plan materials and tools, build a first version, and keep track of successive attempts. Test it, gather peer responses, and revise the approach as evidence suggests improvements.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-04

Make the final version and choose how and with whom to share it. Explain the process, assess the product's usefulness and contribution, and reflect on individual and group work, including care of a shared workspace. Notice further design problems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Making and Sharing.

### CC-05

Identify and practise the skills a task needs. Use materials and technologies with regard for one's own safety and other people's safety, including in digital settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Use familiar technologies to extend what can be accomplished, select tools suited to particular tasks, and be willing to learn unfamiliar technologies when needed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

The source does not prescribe a separate Grade 5 technology-content syllabus. It directs learners to combine the Grades 4-5 ADST competencies with grade-level content from other subjects. The partner subject supplies the content knowledge; ADST contributes the design-and-making processes.

**Source locator:** [S1], introductory instruction on cross-curricular use of ADST 4-5 competencies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

These are shared **4-5** standards, not the K-3 approach or the later modular ADST courses. A product may be more than a physical object, and technology is not limited to computers. No required programming language, device, or product is established here.

The source's design-constraint elaboration includes environmental effects, cultural appropriation, and knowledge considered sacred. Investigating a cultural design does not provide permission to reproduce it. The full elaborations remain at [S1]; example users, materials, and products are not a compulsory project list.
