# Physical and Health Education - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Physical and Health Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations supported by the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English, separately from classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Develop capable, enjoyable participation in physical activity alongside health literacy, personal safety, healthy relationships, and understanding of changes during puberty.

**Prerequisites:** Build from each learner's current movement and communication experiences. This reference does not set a fitness threshold or assume a particular body or ability.

**Materials:** None are required for reading. Activities need appropriate supervision, accessible options, equipment, and safety arrangements; this reference is not individualized medical advice.

**How to use:** Read [S1] across physical literacy, healthy and active living, social and community health, and mental well-being. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes or health assessments.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Daily movement can build skill and fitness. An active lifestyle also depends on knowing which activities are enjoyable and where participation is possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily physical activity, personal fitness, preferences, and opportunities.

### BI-02

Balanced living requires attention to different dimensions of health and to the ways personal choices interact with social and environmental conditions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, understanding self/health and influences on well-being.

### BI-03

Healthy relationships contribute to feeling supported, connected, and valued.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, relationships and belonging.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Apply fundamental movement skills, concepts, and strategies in varied activities and settings. Monitor and adjust exertion, act safely and fairly, demonstrate leadership, and identify preferred activities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Physical literacy.

### CC-02

Participate daily in activity that develops or maintains health-related fitness. Consider both opportunities and barriers to preferred activities at home, school, and in the community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Healthy and active living, daily participation and opportunities/challenges.

### CC-03

Explain connections among eating, activity, and mental well-being, and the effects of personal choices. Pursue and reflect on healthy-living goals, and practise ways to communicate a health concern and obtain help.

**Source locator:** [S1], Healthy and active living, health connections, choices, communicating concerns, and goals.

### CC-04

Assess responses to unsafe, abusive, or exploitative situations and to discrimination, stereotyping, and bullying. Develop healthy relationships and a caring environment through respectful boundaries and consent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Social and community health, including the boundary-setting and consent elaborations.

### CC-05

Consider strategies supporting mental well-being for oneself and others. Assess ways to help others with problems related to mental well-being or substance use, including access to appropriate support.

**Source locator:** [S1], Mental well-being, promoting well-being and managing others' mental-well-being/substance-use problems.

### CC-06

Explore ways to manage physical, emotional, and social changes during puberty, and how personal identities can change across situations and settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Mental well-being, puberty and changing personal identities.

## Content

### C-01

Develop techniques for movement in place, travelling, and controlling objects, together with movement concepts, strategies, and ways to monitor exertion. Learn participation approaches across individual, paired, rhythmic, and game activities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, fundamental movement skills, concepts/strategies, exertion, and activity types.

### C-02

Distinguish health-related fitness components: muscular strength and endurance, cardiovascular endurance, flexibility, and body composition. Understand the benefits of activity and the **FITT** dimensions of training: frequency, intensity, type, and time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, health components of fitness, training principles/FITT, and activity benefits.

### C-03

Consider foods that support activity and overall health, alongside practices that help prevent communicable and non-communicable illnesses. Food choices include cultural, family, seasonal, and enjoyment dimensions in the source elaboration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, food choices and health-promoting/illness-prevention practices.

### C-04

Know appropriate sources of health information and support. Learn strategies that protect oneself and others from abuse, exploitation, and harm, including understanding consent and identifying unsafe online and offline situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, health information/support services and protective strategies, with elaborations.

### C-05

Understand influences on psychoactive-substance use and the potential harms associated with it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, factors influencing substance use and potential harms.

### C-06

Learn about physical, emotional, and social changes during puberty, including sexuality, sexual identity, and changing relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, puberty and changes involving sexuality, sexual identity, and relationships.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This curriculum includes health, consent, puberty, and mental well-being, not only sport. Use accurate, inclusive, developmentally appropriate language. Examples at [S1] do not prescribe a sport, a public fitness comparison, a body measurement, or personal disclosure. Knowing about body composition is not a requirement to assess or rank students' bodies.

The daily-activity expectation does not specify a new duration or fitness target in this record. Learners' access needs and circumstances matter. Seeking help for a peer does not make a child responsible for diagnosing or treating another person. No activity should require disclosure of abuse, identity, or private health information.
