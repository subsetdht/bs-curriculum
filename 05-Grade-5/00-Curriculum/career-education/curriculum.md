# Career Education - Grade 5

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [5](../README.md) > Career Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations supported by the [program basis](../README.md#program-basis).

**Document language:** English, separately from classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Connect developing self-knowledge, responsible choices, supportive relationships, and learning habits with personal goals and future possibilities.

**Prerequisites:** Everyday experiences of learning and contributing to groups provide starting points. No employment experience or occupational choice is required.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. It does not require a public online profile or disclosure of family employment, finances, or personal information.

**How to use:** [S1] is the Grade 5 view of the **Grades 4-5 standards**, confirmed by the heading in [S2]. Use that band, not the later career-planning expectations in the same compilation. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding one's strengths can inform goals, while everyday decisions help shape the identity other people encounter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, strengths and goals; choices and public identity.

### BI-02

Leadership is relational: listening respectfully and drawing on family and community support can improve decisions and responses to problems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, leadership and supportive family/community relationships.

### BI-03

Habits developed in learning and work can support both immediate progress and longer-term success.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, learning and work habits over different time horizons.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize personal attributes, accomplishments, and growth, along with people who can support further learning. Examine how personal identity and a public digital presence intersect, including beneficial and harmful consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, personal growth, support from others, and personal/public digital identities.

### CC-02

Use creative problem solving to set realistic short- and longer-term learning goals. Identify steps, monitor progress, and connect effective work habits with success.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, innovative thinking, goal setting and monitoring, and work habits.

### CC-03

Respect differences among classmates and consider how peers, family, and community influence choices and aspirations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, respect for differences and relationship influences.

### CC-04

Act safely in different environments and ask questions about how technology is changing workplaces.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, safe behaviours and technology in the changing workplace.

## Content

### C-01

Learn approaches for planning goals, making decisions, and solving problems, together with emerging leadership skills that help a group move forward.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Personal Development.

### C-02

Develop cultural and social awareness through community connections. Explore responsibilities across generations and recognize hazards and safety rules at home, school, and in the community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Connections to Community: cultural/social awareness, generational roles, and safety.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Career learning at this stage is not a demand to select a profession. Goals may change as learners discover interests and capabilities. Digital-identity learning does not require creating an account or publishing personal information.

The cultural-awareness elaboration includes honouring Indigenous traditions; use appropriate local sources rather than assuming one set of traditions applies everywhere. The named goal-setting model and leadership examples at [S1] illustrate the content, not a compulsory worksheet or assessment method.
