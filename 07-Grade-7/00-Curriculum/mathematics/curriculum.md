# Mathematics - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Mathematics](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject expectations. The immersion policy requires parallel curriculum content [S2]. The required-areas order explicitly includes Francophone education [S3], and the guide order identifies the shared mathematics documents in English and in French for Francophone and immersion programs [S4]. These programs remain distinct.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing the English provincial source (`en`). This is not a French-language instructional resource or an authorized translation, and it does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Use connected representations and justified strategies to reason with numbers, linear relationships, circular and three-dimensional measurement, spatial transformations, data, chance, and financial percentages.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with whole-number operations, place value, fractions, and measurement can help readers make connections. This is preparation guidance, not a new formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Any later learning activity must identify its own materials, technology, and accessible alternatives.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], including its elaborations. The local IDs are stable repository references, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence. The source standards here are specifically for Grade 7; the K–9 span of the supporting program orders does not turn them into grade-banded expectations.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

A part of a quantity can be expressed in different numerical forms. Connecting fractions, decimals, and percentages makes it possible to choose representations that reveal the relationship between a part and its whole.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, decimals, fractions, and percents as representations of parts and wholes.

### BI-02

Flexible calculation grows from understanding numerical relationships, not just remembering procedures. Strategies can be extended to calculations involving integers and decimals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, computational fluency and flexibility with integers and decimals.

### BI-03

An algebraic rule, a table, and a graph can describe the same linear pattern. Reading across these representations helps identify what remains regular and make generalizations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, connected representations of linear relations.

### BI-04

Every circle has the same relationship between its circumference and diameter. That relationship provides a basis for reasoning about circular measurements and comparing spatial quantities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the constant circumference-to-diameter ratio.

### BI-05

A circle graph organizes a whole into proportional parts. Interpreting those parts supports comparison, but requires attention to what the whole represents and whether the display is informative or misleading.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, proportion, comparison, and interpretation using circle graphs; associated inquiry elaborations.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate mathematical situations through logical reasoning and pattern recognition, including puzzles and games. Use examples to develop conjectures and generalizations, and reason about why a conclusion follows rather than relying only on an observed answer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and analyzing, logic, patterns, inductive and deductive reasoning, and conclusions.

### CC-02

Judge the reasonableness of quantities using estimates, benchmarks, and rounding. Extend mental strategies to new numbers, and use models, tools, or technology to explore relationships and test conjectures in meaningful situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and analyzing, estimation, mental mathematics, tools/technology, and contextual modelling.

### CC-03

Build understanding through inquiry, purposeful play, visualization, and problem solving. Try more than one strategy in both abstract problems and practical contexts, drawing on familiar approaches, personal reasoning, and approaches from other cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving, multiple strategies, inquiry, visualization, and abstract/contextual problems.

### CC-04

Make reasoning understandable to others through mathematical vocabulary, discussion, and arguments that justify choices. Connect concrete models, pictures, symbols, and spoken or written explanations rather than treating them as unrelated answers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing, mathematical language, justification, communication, and representations.

### CC-05

Reconsider strategies and solutions in light of one's own and others' thinking, and use that reflection to pose further questions. Connect mathematics across topics, other learning, and personal interests; use mathematical arguments to inform personal choices and anticipate consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting, reflection, connections, and arguments supporting personal choices.

### CC-06

Explore mathematics through relationships with place, stories, and cultural practices, including perspectives relevant to local First Peoples and other communities. Connect mathematical ideas with First Peoples worldviews through appropriately guided engagement with local knowledge, rather than treating cultural objects as interchangeable illustrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving, place/story/cultural problem-solving contexts; Connecting and reflecting, First Peoples worldviews and knowledge-keeper elaborations.

## Content

### C-01

Extend computational fluency from multiplication and division facts to 100 into flexible calculation with integers and decimals. Work with addition, subtraction, multiplication, and division, connecting models and representations to the operations. Apply order of operations with brackets; exponent calculations are excluded from this Grade 7 order-of-operations scope.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multiplication/division facts to 100; operations with integers; operations with decimals; associated representation and order-of-operations elaborations.

### C-02

Connect fractions, decimals, ratios, and percentages as ways of expressing related quantities. Use equivalence and conversion, distinguish terminating from repeating decimals, and compare and order fractions and decimals using place value, benchmarks, and number-line positions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, relationships between decimals, fractions, ratios, and percents; conversions, equivalency, and comparison elaborations.

### C-03

Describe discrete linear relationships using expressions, tables of values, and graphs. Recover a relationship from a table or graph and explain its starting value and regular change. Coordinate work for these relations uses integer-valued coordinates in all four quadrants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, discrete linear relations using expressions, tables, and graphs; integral-coordinate and rule-derivation elaborations.

### C-04

Solve two-step equations by maintaining equality between their two sides, and verify that the proposed solution satisfies the original equation. The specified coefficients, constants, and solutions are whole numbers; this is narrower than the integer operations elsewhere in the grade.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, two-step equations with whole-number coefficients, constants, and solutions; preservation of equality and verification elaborations.

### C-05

Relate a circle's radius and diameter to its circumference and area. Develop the circumference relationship using the constant ratio pi, and use the area relationship based on pi times the radius times the radius. Distinguish measuring a circular boundary from measuring the region it encloses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, circumference and area of circles; relationships among radius, diameter, circumference, and area.

### C-06

Determine the volume of rectangular prisms and cylinders by connecting the area of the base with the height. Recognize how a rectangular or circular base changes the base-area calculation while preserving this common volume relationship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, volume of rectangular prisms and cylinders; base-area-times-height elaboration.

### C-07

Locate and graph positions using the Cartesian origin and all four quadrants, with integer-valued coordinates. Track the effect of successive translations, rotations, and reflections on a two-dimensional shape, including combinations of those transformations rather than only isolated moves.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Cartesian coordinates and graphing; combinations of transformations; four-quadrant, integral-coordinate, and successive-transformation elaborations.

### C-08

Construct, label, and interpret circle graphs. Move between the percentages shown and the quantities they represent, using the size of the whole to make comparisons meaningful.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, circle graphs; construction, labelling, interpretation, and percentage-to-quantity elaborations.

### C-09

Investigate experimental probability for two independent events by collecting results across repeated trials. Keep the pair of events in view when interpreting outcomes, and distinguish the observed results of a trial set from a guarantee about a future trial.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, experimental probability with two independent events; multiple-trial elaborations.

### C-10

Use percentages in financial calculations, including sales tax, tips, discounts, and sale prices. Connect the percentage with its reference amount so that changes in price can be calculated and interpreted in context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy — financial percentage; tax, tip, discount, and sale-price elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The numerical restrictions apply to particular topics, not to every task in the grade. Facts to 100 support wider calculation; they are not a ceiling on all quantities. Integer and decimal operations include brackets but exclude exponents in order-of-operations work. Two-step equations have whole-number coefficients, constants, and solutions, while coordinate and transformation work can use negative integers in the four quadrants. Fraction equivalence is part of this record; a separate requirement to perform every operation on fractions is not added.

The source's graphing focus is **discrete** linear relations with integral coordinates, not an unrestricted study of continuous functions. Its probability focus is experimental work with **two independent events**, not a substituted unit on dependent events. Geometry retains both circular measurement and the volumes of the two specified solids.

The complete elaborations at [S1] explain strategies and offer contexts such as circle construction, tessellations, games, and community applications. These are not a compulsory list of projects, software, or cultural activities. First Peoples connections belong within mathematical learning, but an official example does not grant permission to reproduce designs, stories, ceremonies, or community-specific knowledge. Follow local protocols and appropriate knowledge-holder guidance.

Program coverage refers to shared curriculum expectations. This English summary does not supply French classroom materials or establish a resource's suitability for any particular program.
