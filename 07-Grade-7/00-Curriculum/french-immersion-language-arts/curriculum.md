# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [French Immersion Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. This is the regular Grade 7 entry, not the separate [Late French Immersion 7T record](transition-7t/curriculum.md). The official compilation [S2] distinguishes regular Grade 7 from Grade 7 late immersion.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed curriculum is in French. This independent summary is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use English descriptions of the French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Use French to interpret layered meaning, explain responses to literature, consider cultural perspectives, and communicate increasingly well-supported ideas in coherent texts.

**Prerequisites:** Builds on earlier immersion language learning; this reference does not set admission or proficiency thresholds. School or district procedures govern placement.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Any selected literary, cultural, or media work needs its own suitability, accessibility, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Compare the grouped items with [S1] and its elaborations. Youth novels and poetry are the specified literary contexts; oral, written, and visual communication includes digital and multimedia forms. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes or a teaching sequence.

English Language Arts is also part of the Grade 7 Early Immersion pathway: provincial policy [S3] provides for it from Grade 4. Neither language-arts record substitutes for the other.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Putting ideas into words helps learners recognize their own cultural position and engage with other people's cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), expressing thought in relation to one's own and others' cultures.

### BI-02

Meaning is not always stated directly. Emotional responses and what a message leaves unsaid offer interpretive clues; a story's themes also emerge through the situations characters face and their responses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), feelings and unstated meaning; characters' experiences and themes.

### BI-03

How a text is made affects what it communicates. Its organization and expressive form contribute to the message and the response it produces, alongside its subject matter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), form, content, message, and intended effect.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Compare cultural elements across texts, including Indigenous texts. Build interpretations of implied information from precise textual evidence and prior knowledge rather than relying only on explicitly stated details.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), cultural comparison and implicit information.

### CC-02

Trace how characters influence a plot and identify its important developments. Examine poetic choices and explain their effects on readers; connect emotional responses to identifiable passages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), characters in the plot and poetic elements; Create and communicate, passages evoking feelings.

### CC-03

Identify how a text organizes and connects ideas, then restate its central message clearly through an organized summary rather than reproducing the text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), text structure and sequence of ideas; Create and communicate, summarizing the main idea.

### CC-04

Respond to an audience's spoken and unspoken reactions when adjusting speech. Contribute spontaneous viewpoints with reasons, attending to the interaction rather than only delivering prepared language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), adjusting discourse to verbal and non-verbal reactions; Create and communicate, spontaneous justification of viewpoints.

### CC-05

Create a character portrait that addresses physical and psychological qualities. For broader compositions and presentations, choose an effective structure, develop a clear and coherent message, and revise ideas, organization, and grammar together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), character portrait, organization, composition, and revision.

## Content

### C-01

Communication involves managing voice and bodily expression as well as words. Clarification, restating an idea, considering another perspective, and weighing arguments for and against a position help speakers make meaning together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies: verbal/non-verbal communication, argumentation, clarification, reformulation, and others' perspectives.

### C-02

Study poetry and youth novels with attention to cultural and historical elements. Knowledge of poetic meaning, tone, and expressive features complements understanding of a novel's characters, setting, plot development, and themes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), cultural/historical elements, literary tools, and the youth-novel/poetry context.

### C-03

Distinguish the organization of a narrative, the development of a topic in an informational text, and a paragraph that advances an argument. An argumentative paragraph connects its point, explanation, and evidence and uses relationships such as cause and consequence to guide the reader.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), narrative framework, informational structure, and argumentative paragraph.

### C-04

Use French superlative structures and make logically consistent choices of verb moods and tenses within a text. Develop revision strategies that draw on rereading and suitable reference tools, so language choices support the intended meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), relative/absolute superlatives, tense concordance, and revision strategies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Regular 7 and 7T both use youth novels and poetry, but they do not specify the same language progression. This entry foregrounds implicit meaning, argumentative paragraphs, and consistency of verb choices; [7T](transition-7t/curriculum.md) explicitly develops other sentence, vocabulary, pronunciation, and verb tools. Do not collapse the two records or require both as interchangeable courses.

Grammar and literary tools serve communication in context. The source does not prescribe a commercial reading level, a particular novel or poem, or an exhaustive conjugation programme in this summary. Refer to [S1] for precise French terminology and elaborations.

Cultural comparison includes Indigenous texts within language arts, not as an optional enrichment topic. Selection, discussion, quotation, retelling, or adaptation must respect the rights and protocols of the particular creators and communities.
