# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 7, transition 7T

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../../README.md) > [French Immersion Language Arts](../README.md) > [7T](README.md)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Late French Immersion, normally the second year following entry in Grade 6. The catalog label `7T` corresponds to the official compilation's Grade 7 late-immersion section [S2]. The repository grade is `"7"` and the variant is `transition-7t`; this is not another numbered grade or the regular [Early Immersion 7 record](../curriculum.md).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), describing a French-language source. This is an independent reference, not an authorized translation, an English-instruction requirement, or a French teaching resource. Source locators give English glosses of French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Strengthen thinking and communication in French through increasingly fluent interaction, interpretation of literature in context, and structured, revised expression.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier late-immersion learning is a useful foundation, not a new admission rule imposed by this reference. Confirm placement and supports with the school or district.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this record. Learning materials need separate language-suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for precise expectations and elaborations. The identified literary contexts are youth novels and poetry; work includes oral, written, and visual texts in print, digital, and multimedia forms. Local IDs identify this summary only, not official standards or a fixed teaching order.

Provincial policy [S3] distinguishes the first and second years of Late Immersion. In the second year, English Language Arts and other English-taught courses should occupy no more than 20 percent of instructional time. Do not import the first-year fully French recommendation into Grade 7 or treat this record as a substitute for English Language Arts.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Thinking directly in French supports expression that makes sense in French; word-for-word transfer from another language can obscure meaning and interrupt fluency.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas and elaboration (English gloss), thinking in the language and the limits of literal translation.

### BI-02

Texts offer evidence of the communities and circumstances in which they are made. Authors' language and perspectives show diversity across the French-speaking world, while characters' behaviour can illuminate social relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), Francophone variation, texts in context, and characters within society.

### BI-03

A message depends on its presentation as well as its subject matter. Recognizing the relationship between form and meaning supports both interpretation and communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), form and content in conveying a message.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize linguistic and cultural variety within the Francophone world. Compare cultural elements in texts, including Indigenous texts, rather than treating either Francophone or Indigenous experience as uniform.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), language/cultural varieties and cultural comparison including Indigenous texts.

### CC-02

Distinguish supporting ideas, develop hypotheses from textual clues, and interpret a text in order to respond to it. Explain how characters contribute to the plot and how poetic elements affect a reader.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), secondary ideas, hypotheses, interpretation, characters, and poetic effects.

### CC-03

Maintain spontaneous conversation while noticing other people's non-verbal responses. Express opinions with suitable, varied vocabulary and reasons, and develop fluent reading that connects words through French liaison.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), opinion, spontaneous conversation, and fluent reading with liaison.

### CC-04

Build a portrait of a character's physical and psychological qualities and communicate a text's main idea through a clear, organized summary.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), character portrait and main-idea summary.

### CC-05

Compose structured texts in which paragraph transitions and varied sentence patterns help carry the message. Revisit work using the spelling and grammar already studied to improve the effectiveness of expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), paragraphing, sentence variation, structured composition, and revision.

## Content

### C-01

Develop active listening and appropriate ways of addressing others, including the distinction between familiar and formal address. Connect communication with the cultural and historical elements encountered in texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), active listening, politeness including familiar/formal address, and cultural/historical elements.

### C-02

Learn how youth novels develop characters, settings, and events, and how poetry creates effects through features such as rhythm, repetition, comparison, and theme. These literary tools provide a basis for interpreting and composing, not a prescribed reading list.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), youth-novel and poetry characteristics; literary context.

### C-03

Use narrative development, the introduction and organized development of information, and suitable linking words to connect a text. Recognize how an ending resolves or concludes what the text has developed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), narrative framework, informational text structure, and relationship markers.

### C-04

Extend French sentence construction through affirmative and negative compound sentences, questions using inversion or interrogative pronouns, and simple relative pronouns. Study how adverbs are formed and how adjective comparisons and relative or absolute superlatives change meaning. Relationships among synonyms, homonyms, and antonyms expand vocabulary choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), sentence and question structures, relative pronouns, adverbs, comparison, superlatives, and word relationships.

### C-05

Select verb forms for the genres being studied. The elaboration identifies third-group verbs in the present indicative, compound past, imperfect, and present imperative. Use rereading and reference tools to revise with growing control of these language patterns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), verb groups/moods/tenses associated with genres and revision strategies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The `T` entry is not a translation of regular 7 or a generic support supplement. Its explicit work on liaison, compound and interrogative sentences, relative pronouns, adverbs, word relationships, and verb forms makes its progression distinct. Shared literary contexts do not establish interchangeable learning standards.

The official elaborations clarify the scope of grammar and literary study. This record neither adds the regular Grade 7 argumentative-paragraph requirement nor imports the first-language Francophone essay curriculum. It also does not prescribe a fixed rate of language acquisition.

Indigenous texts remain within cultural and literary learning. Curriculum inclusion does not grant permission to reproduce, translate, retell, or adapt a community's cultural works. Seek the relevant permissions and respect community-specific protocols.
