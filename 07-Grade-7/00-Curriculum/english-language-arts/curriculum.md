# English Language Arts - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [English Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The reviewed Francophone applicability is supported by CSF's use of the provincial curriculum and its English-language provision [S4]; the provider's delivery policy is kept in the separate [CSF context](../csf-context.md). This record does not replace the applicable French language-arts record.

Provincial immersion policy [S2] includes English Language Arts from Grade 4 in Early Immersion and describes its place in the second year of Late Immersion. The Required Areas Order [S3], sections 4(c)-5, expressly provides for immersion English Language Arts in Grades 4-9, except Late Immersion Grade 6. The exception does not apply in Grade 7.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Programme timetables and instructional-language proportions are distinct from this document's language.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and question diverse communications, bring evidence and perspectives together, and create purposeful, original expression through reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experiences interpreting and creating texts provide a foundation; no commercial reading level or admission threshold is specified here.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Text selections and activities require their own accessibility, age-suitability, and rights review.

**How to use:** Use [S1] for complete standards and elaborations. "Text" includes oral, written, visual, digital, and combined forms, not only books. Local IDs identify the grouped repository summaries, not official BC codes or a sequence of lessons.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Language offers creative enjoyment as well as practical ways to communicate. Understanding how it works expands the choices available for expressing a particular purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity and joy; understanding language for purposeful use.

### BI-02

Engaging with stories and other communications connects personal experience with other people and the wider world. Considering several perspectives can reshape and extend one's thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, connections through stories/texts and multiple perspectives.

### BI-03

An informed participant in society questions messages rather than accepting everything encountered through reading, listening, or viewing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, questioning texts and educated, engaged citizenship.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Gather ideas and information for different purposes and judge whether sources are useful, accurate, and trustworthy. Bring findings from more than one source together to build understanding, including when navigating digital media.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, accessing/evaluating sources and synthesizing ideas.

### CC-02

Use comprehension strategies and inquiry to develop thinking within and across texts. Compare how structures, features, genres, and literary techniques shape meaning for particular audiences and purposes, and use critical, creative, and reflective thinking to move beyond a first interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, strategies, inquiry, thinking within/between/beyond texts, form/function, literary effects, and text structures.

### CC-03

Examine the values, perspectives, and personal, social, and cultural circumstances represented in texts. Recognize language's role in identity and develop meaningful personal, creative, and critical responses that connect self, text, and world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, contexts/values/perspectives, language and identity, personal connections, and responses.

### CC-04

Recognize how stories, narrative, and oral tradition express First Peoples perspectives, beliefs, and values. Understand oral tradition as a valid and enduring form of knowledge and record for its communities, not an inferior substitute for writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples story/narrative/oral tradition and the validity of oral tradition; associated elaboration.

### CC-05

Develop shared understanding through exchanges of ideas and viewpoints. Plan and design literary and informational compositions for varied audiences, transforming ideas and information into original texts rather than merely assembling copied material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchanging viewpoints, writing/design processes, and transforming ideas into original texts.

### CC-06

Choose forms and genres that suit a message, and refine work for clarity and impact on its audience. Extend command of Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation, and experiment with oral storytelling processes, including expressive delivery and appropriate permission for an existing story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, assessment/refinement, conventions, form/genre selection, and oral storytelling processes and elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Understand differences among a text's form, purpose, and genre. Study the role of text features, narrative structure, characterization, setting, sensory and figurative devices, and argument in shaping meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text: forms/functions/genres, text features, literary elements/devices, and argument.

### C-02

Develop strategies for reading and oral interaction, and ways to reflect on one's own learning as a reader and writer. Writing processes include planning and improving work with its audience in mind; strategies such as inference, summarizing, clarification, and self-evaluation support these processes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Strategies and processes: reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing.

### C-03

Build coherent paragraphs and fluent sentences using suitable syntax and transitions. Understand conventions of Canadian spelling, capitalization, punctuation, and grammar, while recognizing that English also varies by region, social situation, and level of formality.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Language features, structures, and conventions: paragraphing, language varieties, syntax/sentence fluency, and conventions.

### C-04

Consider how oral delivery and presentation choices affect communication. Voice, pace, gesture, medium, and careful organization need to fit the intended audience and purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, features of oral language and presentation techniques.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Critical source evaluation includes distinguishing fact and opinion and considering currency, source authority, and relevance to the inquiry. Examples of media platforms or classroom routines in [S1] are illustrations, not compulsory products or activities.

The source validates First Peoples oral tradition and situates it within communities. Its inclusion does not authorize sharing, adapting, or publishing a community's stories. Respect the particular teller's and community's permissions and protocols, including when working with recorded or digital texts.

This is a condensed curriculum reference, not a book list, marking rubric, universal reading-level target, or programme timetable. Grade 7 immersion learners also use their distinct French language-arts curriculum; English spelling and genre knowledge cannot simply be relabelled as French expectations.

For Francophone students, [S3] distinguishes first-language French from the requirement in section 4(b) to study a language other than French. That provision does not itself name English Language Arts 7. The [CSF context](../csf-context.md) establishes the provider basis for including this reference in that pathway; it does not turn CSF delivery into a universal timetable or extend the immersion-specific clause to Francophone students. Individual exceptions in section 3 must also be respected.
