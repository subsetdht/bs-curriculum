# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Francophone Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the French first-language programme. This is distinct from both immersion pathways, Core French, and English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the official source is French. This reference is an original synthesis, not an authorized translation or a change in instructional language. Source locators are English glosses of French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Interpret literature in its historical and cultural setting, evaluate the effects of language choices, and develop well-supported oral and written expression while participating in Francophone community life.

**Prerequisites:** Builds on earlier first-language literacy experiences. No admission, citizenship, or language-proficiency criteria are created by this reference; programme eligibility is governed separately.

**Materials:** None are required for reading this summary. Selected works need separate age-suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], which gives particular attention to the short story (`nouvelle`), myth, and essay (`dissertation`). Other genres are encouraged, not excluded. These genre labels are not a mandated set of books. Local IDs refer to the repository's grouped summaries, not ministry standard codes.

The introduction [S2] explains the first-language programme's Francophone identity and intercultural purposes and its integration of reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Literature can illuminate human experience through imagined situations rooted in reality. The concerns expressed in a work also connect it to the society and period in which it was created.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), literature and the human condition; themes reflecting their era.

### BI-02

Literal meanings, associations, and stylistic choices influence how audiences understand a message. Experimenting with those choices helps writers develop a voice, while organized expression, vocabulary, and language conventions make that voice understandable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), connotation/denotation, personal writing style, and effective expression.

### BI-03

French communication provides ways to participate in Francophone communities. Relationships between Indigenous peoples and Canadian Francophone communities have influenced both language and culture in reciprocal ways.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), participation in Francophone community and mutual Indigenous/Francophone linguistic and cultural influences.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Identify a text's genre and distinctive features, and explain how an author's stylistic decisions contribute to its individual character.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), genre characteristics and authors' stylistic tools.

### CC-02

Interpret a work using its time, place, characters, and relationships. Question how a message's purpose and reception relate to its social and historical setting, and compare myths across periods to understand their development.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), contextualizing a work, messages across societies/eras, and the evolution of myths.

### CC-03

Analyze cultural symbols in texts, including Indigenous narratives. Treat those symbols as culturally situated rather than assigning a single meaning to all communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), cultural symbols including those in an Indigenous narrative.

### CC-04

Structure oral presentations and make a defensible response to a text. Select examples and quotations that support the point being advanced rather than presenting an unsupported opinion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), organized oral presentation, text-based opinions, and supporting quotations.

### CC-05

Write within the studied genres and language conventions while using creative writing to develop imagination and abstraction. Enrich expression through stylistic tools and revise with attention to both the quality of form and the effectiveness of language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), composition, creative writing, stylistic enrichment, and improving form/expression.

## Content

### C-01

Understand narrative development and the structure of a reasoned essay. An essay introduces and organizes its subject, develops claims through examples and explanation, and closes with synthesis and an opening for further thought. Logical sequencing, transitions, and a bibliography support the communication of ideas and sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), textual organization: narrative framework, essay structure, logical connections, relationship markers, and bibliography.

### C-02

Study short stories and myths through their settings, historical connections, characters, relationships, and character development. Explore figurative devices and language registers as deliberate ways of shaping a text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), literary tools: short-story/myth characteristics, figures of speech, and registers.

### C-03

Develop sentence types and forms and the verb moods and tenses used in the selected genres. The source's grammatical elaboration specifically includes the present conditional and present subjunctive.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), language tools: sentence construction and genre-linked moods/tenses.

### C-04

Use strategies for oral interaction, reading, and writing as connected processes. These include expressive speech, activating prior knowledge, inference and identification of significant information, and moving from planning through drafting, revision, and publication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations (English gloss), communication/socialization, reading, and writing strategies.

### C-05

Build a flexible vocabulary by distinguishing literal meaning from association and by attending to nuance and imagery. Word variety contributes to precise, expressive language rather than merely making a text longer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), enrichment tools: figurative language, connotations/denotations, nuances, and vocabulary variety.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Grade 7 genre focus differs from the youth-novel and poetry contexts in immersion 7 and 7T. Do not substitute one language-arts curriculum for another or apply this record's essay and verb expectations to every French learner.

The word `dissertation` here denotes the source's school essay form, not a university thesis. Its structure and the narrative framework are expanded in [S1]. Texts may be oral, visual, digital, or written; the examples do not establish a compulsory reading or viewing list.

Indigenous/Francophone relationships and Indigenous narratives are embedded in the curriculum. The literary term "myth" is not permission to dismiss a people's knowledge or sacred histories. Cultural symbols and works require contextual interpretation, appropriate relationships, attribution, and permissions. No stories, quotations, or cultural works are supplied here.
