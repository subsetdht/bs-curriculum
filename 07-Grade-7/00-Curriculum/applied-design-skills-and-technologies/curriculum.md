# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. Immersion policy [S3] requires parallel curriculum content. The Required Areas Order [S4], sections 1-2, includes Francophone students and prescribes the same ADST guide; section 3 provides individual exceptions. Neither source makes instructional language identical.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. This does not prescribe classroom language or establish the suitability of English learning materials for immersion.

**Learning objective:** Identify needs, develop and improve designs, and acquire the skills and technologies needed to make and share useful outcomes responsibly.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier design and making experiences provide entry points. No prior coding, workshop experience, or ownership of equipment is required by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the summary. Actual making requires task-specific accessible materials, safe facilities, instruction, supervision, and emergency procedures.

**How to use:** This applies the **Grades 6-7** standards to Grade 7. [S1] requires experience with **at least three modules in each of Grades 6 and 7**, not three across the two years. Schools may select from the twelve provincial options below or use locally developed content with the same Grades 6-7 competencies, in addition to or instead of listed modules. These are module choices within ADST, not twelve compulsory courses.

Use [S1] for the precise standards and [S2] for the modular approach. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a fixed production sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Design starts with an understanding of needs and can produce responses suited to the people or situations involved.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, design responding to identified needs.

### BI-02

More demanding work can require learners to develop new skills and combine several tools or technologies rather than rely on a single familiar method.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, additional skills and multiple tools/technologies for complex tasks.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate a possible user's experience to uncover a worthwhile design opportunity. Define the requirements, success criteria, and constraints, including environmental and cultural considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Understanding context and Defining; elaborations on users and constraints.

### CC-02

Generate possibilities collaboratively, compare them with the design requirements, and choose a direction after considering ethical, personal, social, and environmental effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Ideating.

### CC-03

Use appropriate information and expertise to plan stages and resources. Try materials and construct an initial version, documenting successive changes to the design, tools, materials, and procedures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Prototyping; sources of information and iterations elaborations.

### CC-04

Test the developing product and use feedback to troubleshoot and improve it. Organize and carry out production with suitable tools and materials, adjusting the plan as necessary and reducing waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Testing and Making.

### CC-05

Choose how and with whom to share a result, demonstrate it, and explain the decisions behind it. Evaluate its contribution and performance against the criteria, reflect on individual and group work and the shared workspace, and identify further design issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Sharing.

### CC-06

Recognize precautionary and emergency procedures in physical and digital settings. Assess the abilities needed for a task and develop missing skills, individually and with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Skills.

### CC-07

Select and learn tools that extend what can be accomplished. Consider unintended effects of technology choices and how land, resources, and culture influence both the creation and use of technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Technologies.

## Content

The following items summarize the twelve **available provincial modules**, not a requirement to complete them all. The minimum-three rule and locally developed alternatives are explained in Scope and use.

### C-01

**Computational Thinking:** Represent a problem or data so that its structure is visible, and develop simple algorithms involving ordered steps, decisions, repetition, searching, or sorting. Explore visual programming and the historical development of programming languages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Computational Thinking.

### C-02

**Computers and Communications Devices:** Understand how hardware, software, local networks, and communication devices fit together. Learn basic troubleshooting, the purposes of input/output and adaptive devices, and effective, ergonomic keyboard and computer use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Computers and Communications Devices.

### C-03

**Digital Literacy:** Manage online participation, personal information, media, and relationships with attention to safety, citizenship, copyright, and ethical conduct. Understand how searches select and rank results, assess the information they return, and identify digital networks or tools that support learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Digital Literacy.

### C-04

**Drafting:** Communicate design information through sketches, plans, manual technical drawing, and simple computer-aided drafting. Learn how drawing conventions and scale make a proposed object or arrangement understandable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Drafting.

### C-05

**Entrepreneurship and Marketing:** Connect an identified need with a product or service and a possible market. Study branding and pricing alongside budgeting and basic records, including the distinction between seeking profit and covering costs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Entrepreneurship and Marketing.

### C-06

**Food Studies:** Develop basic food handling and preparation knowledge with appropriate equipment. Consider how ingredients function and how nutrition, dietary restrictions, cost, availability, family, and culture influence preparation and food choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Food Studies.

### C-07

**Media Arts:** Compare digital and non-digital media as ways of representing ideas and stories. Learn to capture, arrange, edit, and combine image, sound, and text for a purpose, and consider digital media's influence on communication and self-expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Media Arts.

### C-08

**Metalwork:** Relate metals' properties to their uses and learn suitable hand-tool processes for working them. Recognize the resource implications of using a non-renewable material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Metalwork.

### C-09

**Power Technology:** Distinguish energy from power: energy changes form, while power describes the rate of that change. Investigate energy forms, conservation, and devices that carry out transformations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Power Technology.

### C-10

**Robotics:** Understand how an automated machine combines sensing, control, and action. Connect robot uses and movement patterns with programming, logic, and available platforms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Robotics.

### C-11

**Textiles:** Explore the purposes and properties of textile materials and hand methods for constructing or repairing items. Evaluate choices through availability, price, function, and care, including the significance of textiles in practical and cultural settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Textiles.

### C-12

**Woodwork:** Connect wood's characteristics and local cultural and economic uses with appropriate hand-tool techniques and basic joining methods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, Woodwork.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Unlike K-5 ADST, this grade has explicit modular content options. Locally developed modules are permitted alternatives using the provincial competencies; the listed twelve do not inventory every school's choices. Grade 8 and Grade 9 content options should not be imported into this Grades 6-7 record.

The design process is iterative. A product can be a service, process, system, or environment as well as an object, and technology is not limited to computers. Software brands, robotics kits, tools, and example products in [S1] are not compulsory purchases.

First Peoples knowledge and expertise appear in the source's information-gathering, land/resource/culture, and design-constraint discussions. [S2] explicitly includes contemporary and traditional First Peoples practices and warns about appropriation and sacred knowledge. Consult local communities and obtain permissions; a suggested textile material or cultural context is not permission to copy regalia, designs, or knowledge.

Tool lists and processes in the elaborations do not replace safety training. This reference does not authorize unsupervised use of workshop equipment or hazardous materials.
