# Science - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Science](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject expectations. The immersion policy requires parallel curriculum content [S2]. The required-areas order includes Francophone authorities, programs, and students [S3], and the guide order identifies science documents in English and in French for Francophone and immersion programs [S4].

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English provincial source (`en`). This reference does not prescribe classroom language or provide a French-language teaching resource.

**Learning objective:** Investigate how populations, substances, electromagnetic interactions, and Earth's environment change, and use evidence and careful inquiry to develop and evaluate explanations.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing, measuring, representing information, and discussing possible explanations is useful preparation, not an additional formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Fieldwork and experiments require their own materials, accessibility arrangements, supervision, and safety and ethical planning.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], including its complete elaborations. Follow the locators for official expectations. Local IDs support traceable references; they are not official BC codes, a lesson sequence, or an assessment checklist. These are Grade 7 standards, not a K–9 grade band despite the broader scope of the supporting program orders.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Differences among organisms matter to the survival and reproduction of populations. Natural selection helps explain how living things have diversified and changed over time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, evolution by natural selection and the diversity and survival of living things.

### BI-02

The identity and arrangement of atoms distinguish elements from compounds. A material made from one kind of atom differs from one in which different elements are chemically bonded together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, atoms, elements, and chemically combined compounds.

### BI-03

Electricity and magnetism are connected manifestations of electromagnetic interaction. Understanding that connection helps explain how electrical energy can be generated and how magnetic effects can be produced.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the electromagnetic force; associated electricity-generation and electricity/magnetism inquiry questions.

### BI-04

Earth's environment and climate have a long history of change. Evidence from different periods helps explain those changes and investigate the influence of present-day human practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, Earth and climate over geological time; associated human-impact inquiry questions.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Sustain curiosity about a scientific problem and use deliberate observation to identify questions worth investigating. Frame an investigable question or problem, propose alternative if–then hypotheses, and predict what evidence an inquiry might produce.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Questioning and predicting, personal curiosity, observations, inquiry questions, alternative hypotheses, and predictions.

### CC-02

Plan investigations collaboratively, choosing approaches such as fieldwork or experiments that fit the question. In fair tests, identify and manage independent and dependent variables and relevant controls. Record qualitative and quantitative observations carefully, using suitable equipment or digital tools; attend to accuracy and precision, SI units, and simple unit conversions. Follow safety and ethical guidelines throughout.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Planning and conducting, investigation types, variables, data collection, accuracy/precision, SI units, and safety/ethics.

### CC-03

Interpret the local environment using First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local knowledge as information sources. Organize evidence through suitable tables, graphs, keys, models, or digital representations. Look for relationships across one's own observations and secondary sources, and use scientific understanding to draw conclusions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Processing and analyzing data and information, local experience, knowledge sources, representations, patterns, and conclusions.

### CC-04

Examine how the investigation method affects the strength of a conclusion. Consider whether variables were adequately controlled, whether the data are suitable, where errors might have entered, and what changes would improve the investigation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Evaluating, methods, controls, data quality, sources of error, and improvements.

### CC-05

Recognize assumptions and possible bias in one's own work and in secondary sources. Evaluate claims with informed skepticism, drawing on scientific knowledge and both numerical and descriptive evidence. Consider the social, ethical, and environmental consequences of findings rather than treating a technically plausible result as the only consideration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Evaluating, assumptions/bias, qualitative and quantitative evidence, secondary-source claims, and implications.

### CC-06

Use scientific learning in new situations, generate or improve possible solutions, and design projects cooperatively. Apply individual and shared effort toward the well-being of oneself, other people, the community, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applying and innovating, care, cooperative design, transfer, and new or refined ideas.

### CC-07

Communicate explanations, findings, and proposed solutions with appropriate scientific language and representations, including digital forms where useful. Reflect on different experiences and perspectives of place and how they shape questions, evidence, collaboration, and the meaning of findings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating, scientific communication and experiences/perspectives of place; place elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Investigate evolution as changes in the traits of populations over time. Living things depend on space, food, water, and other resources; genetic variation within a population can give some traits a reproductive advantage in a particular environment. Natural selection concerns these differences over generations, not an individual organism choosing to change because it has a need.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, organisms evolving over time, survival needs, and natural selection; population, genetic-variation, and reproductive-advantage elaborations.

### C-02

Distinguish elements and compounds as kinds of pure substance. An element has one type of atom, identified by atomic number; a compound contains different elements joined in a defined arrangement through chemical bonds. A pure substance consists of one type of particle with characteristic properties, such as density, solubility, boiling behaviour, or electrical conductivity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements and compounds as pure substances; atom, bonding, and characteristic-property elaborations.

### C-03

Recognize that crystalline solids have organized arrangements of particles. In chemical change, atoms are rearranged into different products and energy changes accompany the process. The focus connects the organization of matter with observable properties and changes, without requiring every named crystal or reaction example in the source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, crystalline structure of solids and chemical changes; particle-arrangement, new-product, and energy-change elaborations.

### C-04

Investigate the two-way relationship between electrical current and magnetic effects: current in a wire produces a magnetic field, while a changing magnetic field relative to a wire can produce current. Use this connection to understand electromagnets and electrical generation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, electromagnetism; magnetic-field/current, generation, and electromagnet elaborations.

### C-05

Compare ways of generating electricity and their environmental consequences. The source's examples span wind, water, coal, geothermal energy, and sunlight; their inclusion supports examining alternatives and impacts, rather than assuming that every generating method has the same mechanism or environmental effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, electricity generated in different ways with different environmental impacts; generation-method elaboration.

### C-06

Use the fossil record to investigate changes in biodiversity through geological time. Relate this evidence to the geological time scale and to relative and absolute methods for determining the ages of rocks and fossils. Include First Peoples knowledge of changes in biodiversity over time within this study.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, the fossil record and biodiversity over geological time; time-scale and dating elaborations; First Peoples knowledge of biodiversity changes.

### C-07

Investigate evidence of climate change over geological time alongside more recent human impacts on Earth's systems. Consider physical records, including ice and fossil evidence, and local First Peoples knowledge, including oral histories and changes in traditional practice. Connect changing environmental conditions with plants, animals, water availability, and the timing of harvesting, and examine the effectiveness of sustainable practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, evidence of climate change over geological time and recent human impacts; physical records, local First Peoples knowledge, ecological connections, and sustainable-practice elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The record retains biology, chemistry, electromagnetism, and Earth-history/climate learning together. Natural selection describes population change over generations; survival needs alone are not an explanation for directed or purposeful evolution. The geological strand includes fossils and rock ages as evidence, not only a present-day environmental theme.

The source distinguishes **accuracy** (closeness to the actual value) from **precision** (agreement among repeated measurements). It also distinguishes descriptive from numerical evidence. These distinctions matter when evaluating investigations and should not be reduced to collecting more decimal places or preferring numbers regardless of evidence quality.

The climate expectations include both long-term evidence and recent human impacts. Local First Peoples knowledge of climate and biodiversity changes is part of the Content, as well as the broader inquiry competencies. Use appropriate local sources, relationships, and permissions; do not invent an undifferentiated Indigenous account or reproduce community knowledge because it appears relevant to a scientific topic. Place concerns relationships with environment, history, culture, and identity, not merely a site on a map.

The complete elaborations at [S1] contain definitions, examples, and inquiry prompts. They are not a mandatory experiment inventory or permission to perform chemical or electrical activities without suitable safety planning. The curriculum reference supplies neither laboratory procedures nor required equipment.

Shared program coverage does not mean that English materials are automatically suitable for French-medium instruction. No French edition of this summary is supplied here.
