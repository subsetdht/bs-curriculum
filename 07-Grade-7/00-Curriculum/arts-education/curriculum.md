# Arts Education - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Arts Education](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject expectations. The immersion policy establishes parallel curriculum content [S2]. The required-areas order explicitly includes Francophone education [S3], and the guide order identifies arts education documents in English and in French for Francophone and immersion programs [S4].

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English provincial source (`en`). This is not a French teaching resource or an authorized translation and does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Create, interpret, and communicate through dance, drama, music, and visual arts, making increasingly intentional artistic choices and examining relationships among identity, culture, community, and the wider world.

**Prerequisites:** Experiences exploring movement, dramatic situations, sound, and images provide useful starting points. This reference does not impose formal arts training, auditions, or specialist equipment as entry requirements.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Artistic activities must specify their own suitable materials, spaces, tools, permissions, safety provisions, and accessible participation options.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and consult its complete elaborations through the locators. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC standard codes, required projects, or a sequence of performances. These are Grade 7 expectations, not a K–9 grade band; all four named art disciplines remain in scope.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Making art can change how people understand themselves and their participation in a community. Identity and belonging develop through the creative process rather than remaining fixed subjects to depict.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, art making and evolving identity and community.

### BI-02

Encountering art can unsettle familiar assumptions and open ways of understanding other people's experiences and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, experiencing art, challenging points of view, and understanding others.

### BI-03

Movement, dramatic action, organized sound, and visual form offer distinct expressive possibilities. Dance, drama, music, and visual arts each provide a language for creating and communicating meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the unique languages of dance, drama, music, and visual arts.

### BI-04

Engagement with the arts builds the capacity to interpret and express ideas that are layered or complex, not only to reproduce a technique or finished object.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, understanding and expressing complex ideas through the arts.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Make purposeful choices about materials, movement, technologies, tools, techniques, and setting, considering how artistic elements and principles work together. Generate and develop work independently and collaboratively through imagination, inquiry, experimentation, and purposeful play.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating, intentional selection/application and individual/collaborative creation.

### CC-02

Explore how art relates to identity, place, culture, society, and belonging. Consider how personal, social, historical, cultural, and environmental circumstances affect the creation and experience of art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating, identity/place/culture/society/belonging and artistic contexts.

### CC-03

Research and evaluate how dancers, actors, musicians, and visual artists work with their chosen processes and environments. Use reflection to refine ideas, technical skills, and creative processes, improving artistic work and investigating the intentions behind it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and reflecting, artists' practices, refinement of ideas/processes/skills, and artists' intentions.

### CC-04

Interpret artistic work using knowledge from other areas of learning as well as artistic understanding. Examine relationships between the arts and the wider world rather than treating a work as disconnected from other experiences and ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and reflecting, interpretation across areas of learning and relationships with the wider world.

### CC-05

Use symbols and artistic elements to communicate meaning, taking creative risks when expressing feelings, ideas, and experiences. Adapt skills and processes to different purposes, audiences, and contexts rather than assuming one expressive choice will work in every setting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting, adaptation, symbols/elements, creative risk, and expression.

### CC-06

Describe, interpret, and respond to artworks, including work in progress. Experience, document, choreograph, perform, and share creative work in varied ways, using documentation to reflect on learning. Apply the curriculum's knowledge and processes with growing sophistication across these experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting, responses, documentation/choreography/performance/sharing, and increasingly sophisticated engagement.

## Content

### C-01

In dance, shape meaning through what the body does, where and how it moves, its timing, its relationships, and the organization of a dance. Work with body, space, dynamics, time, relationships, and form together. Movement principles connect alignment and weight transfer with flexibility, strength, balance, and coordination; choreographic devices develop and vary movement choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements and movement principles; choreographic devices; movement, energy, structure, and coordination elaborations.

### C-02

In drama, develop characters and events in a particular time and place, considering how plot, tension, mood, focus, and contrast affect meaning. Explore drama forms and conventions as ways to organize and communicate dramatic ideas, rather than reducing drama to memorizing a script.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements, forms, and conventions; character and dramatic-meaning elaborations.

### C-03

In music, connect beat or pulse and metre with duration, rhythm, and tempo to organize musical time. Shape sound through pitch, timbre, and dynamics, and consider how form and texture organize a work and its layers. Use notation as a means of representing and communicating musical sounds and ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements and notation; beat/metre, duration/rhythm/tempo, pitch/timbre/dynamics, form, and texture elaborations.

### C-04

In visual arts, develop images using line, shape, space, texture, colour, form, and value. Make compositional decisions about pattern, repetition, rhythm, balance, contrast, and emphasis; consider movement and variety, the proportions of parts, and the unity or harmony of the whole. Image-development strategies help transform and refine an initial idea into a visual expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual-arts elements and principles of design; image development strategies; composition and image-transformation elaborations.

### C-05

Connect creative intentions with suitable processes, materials, movement, tools, techniques, and strategies. Technologies include both manual and digital means of making art, not only electronic equipment. Explore notation in dance and music as a way to represent movement, sound, ideas, elements, and actions; formal and informal systems can serve different purposes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, processes/materials/movements/technologies/tools/strategies/techniques; notation in music and dance; technology and notation elaborations.

### C-06

Use symbolism and metaphor to explore ideas and perspectives. Consider how an artistic choice can stand for something beyond its literal appearance or sound, and how that meaning depends on the work and its context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, symbolism and metaphor to explore ideas and perspective; symbolism elaboration.

### C-07

Encounter traditional and contemporary Indigenous arts and arts-making processes, together with national and international works from varied cultures, communities, periods, and places. Consider the relationship between a work's cultural setting, purpose, and creative process; Indigenous arts are not limited to historical artifacts or one shared tradition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional/contemporary Aboriginal arts and arts-making processes; national/international works and artistic traditions from diverse cultures, communities, times, and places.

### C-08

Understand the ethical responsibilities of creating, experiencing, and sharing art. These include inclusion and diversity, copyright and ownership, and recognizing cultural appropriation: using cultural expressions without permission or appropriate context, or in ways that misrepresent their originating communities.

Attend to physical and emotional safety for oneself and others, including sensitive content, shared spaces, equipment, and materials. Personal choices and collective responsibilities both contribute to a safe creative environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethical considerations and cultural appropriation; personal/collective responsibility in a safe learning environment; permissions, representation, and safety elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The four disciplines are complementary parts of Arts Education. A visual-art project alone does not stand in for dance, drama, music, and visual arts together. The source describes its artistic elements and principles as non-exhaustive; the lists here retain the named Grade 7 concepts without creating a prescribed project sequence.

Some terms have discipline-specific meanings. Dance dynamics concern how energy is directed through movement; musical dynamics concern levels and changes of sound volume. Visual rhythm concerns the organization of a composition, whereas musical rhythm organizes sounds and silences in time. Form and notation also vary across disciplines. Consult [S1] for the complete definitions and examples.

The elaborations illustrate possible media, forms, strategies, and ways of documenting work. They do not require one instrument, software package, public venue, or online publication method. Documentation and performance should respect physical and emotional safety, privacy, and accessible participation.

Studying Indigenous arts is a curricular expectation, not permission to copy or re-enact protected designs, songs, dances, narratives, or ceremonies. Attend to the originating community, ownership, context, and permissions; local First Peoples guidance is important where their knowledge or works are involved. The source's explicit treatment of cultural appropriation must not be reduced to attribution alone.

Program coverage establishes shared subject expectations, not the interchangeability of language resources or cultural settings. This record supplies an English summary only, not a French edition.
