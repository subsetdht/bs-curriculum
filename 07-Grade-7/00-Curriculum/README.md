# Grade 7 curriculum

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > Grade 7

**Browse:** [Programs](#choose-a-program) | [Subjects and learning standards](#subjects-and-learning-standards) | [Language-course choices](#language-course-choices) | [Provider context](#provider-context) | [Coverage](#coverage-and-limits)

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Programs represented:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. **Document language:** English (`en-CA`). **Source review date:** 2026-09-14.

There are **20 source-checked records**: eleven core subject/language-arts-variant summaries and nine additional-language course summaries. All twelve original Grade 7 catalog landings now lead to written content or the completed course inventory. These references are not official curriculum documents, lessons, an assessment checklist, or a fixed timetable. Consult the primary sources for complete expectations and elaborations.

## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Late French Immersion](#late-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

The seven non-language subjects in the table have shared provincial expectations across all four programmes. The [Required Areas Order][S1], sections 1-2, expressly includes Francophone students and prescribes the common subject guides; the [immersion policy][S2] also requires parallel curriculum content. Document language, classroom language, and the language suitability of a resource are different things.

### English-language program

Start with [English Language Arts](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared non-language subjects: **eight core records**. Add the language programme offered by the board; the [Languages landing](languages/README.md) is the navigator for the nine Grade 7 web-catalog choices.

Section 4(a) of [S1] requires a language other than English in Grades 5-8, subject to applicable exceptions. The [Language Education Policy][S3] describes board selection and Core French as the offering when the board does not provide an alternative. The course list is not a requirement to study all nine languages or a guarantee that each is locally offered.

### Early French Immersion

Use [regular French Immersion Language Arts 7](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts 7](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects: **nine core records**.

English Language Arts is provided from Grade 4 under [S1] and [S2]. The policy recommends 80 percent French and 20 percent English instruction for the relevant elementary years including Grade 7. This is provincial programme guidance, not a subject-by-subject school timetable. Core French does not replace immersion language arts or become a mandatory extra course simply because it appears in Languages.

### Late French Immersion

Use [French Immersion Language Arts 7T](french-immersion-language-arts/transition-7t/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts 7](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects: **nine core records**.

Grade 7 is normally the second year after entry in Grade 6. The official French compilation identifies `7T` as Grade 7 late immersion. It is a separate variant with actual grade `"7"` and course `transition-7t`, not another grade or the same record as regular 7.

Section 4(c) of [S1] provides for immersion English Language Arts in Grades 4-9; section 5 makes an exception only for **Late Immersion Grade 6**, not Grade 7. [S2] recommends fully French instruction in the first year; in the second year, English Language Arts and other English-taught courses should together take no more than 20 percent of instructional time. Do not carry the first-year arrangement into Grade 7.

### Francophone

Use the distinct [Francophone Language Arts 7](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects. The reviewed **CSF pathway also includes English Language Arts 7**, making **nine core records**; its provider basis is explained in the separate [CSF context](csf-context.md).

Section 4(b) of [S1] requires a language other than French for Francophone students in Grades 5-8. It does not itself name ELA7 or impose the immersion timetable. CSF's documented first-language English provision is provider delivery, not a new provincial requirement inferred from an English source page. Neither immersion language arts nor Core French replaces first-language Francophone language arts.

Each record retains the course's specific scope, such as ASL's signed modality and Deaf culture, Japanese kana and Indigenous peoples of Japan, Korean particles and formality, or Mandarin phonetic systems and characters. An English summary does not prescribe English-medium instruction or make the courses interchangeable.

The nine web choices do not inventory every approved or locally developed language programme. In particular, section 6 of [S1] permits a local Indigenous-language programme that does not meet a prescribed guide's outcomes when the board has the specified agreement with a band council or treaty First Nation. This exception must qualify the older policy's general statement about approved guides. The ministry's Languages Template is a curriculum-development framework, not a tenth Grade 7 course.

The individual exceptions in section 3 of [S1] concern specified learners unable to demonstrate the guide's outcomes under its IEP or English-language-learning provisions. They are not blanket exemptions for every learner receiving support. Confirm the applicable programme with the school rather than inferring it from a diagnosis, document language, or a catalog entry.

## Grade-specific boundaries

ADST and Career Education use Grades 6-7 standards; the other subject and language-arts records here have Grade 7 source scope. ADST requires experience with a minimum of three modules **in each grade**, not three across the two-year band. All twelve provincial ADST options are summarized, and locally developed modules may supplement or replace them while retaining the competencies.

Regular immersion 7 and late-immersion 7T both use youth novels and poetry, but their language progressions differ. First-language Francophone 7 emphasizes short stories, myths, and essays. These are distinct curricula, not language editions of one record.

First Peoples perspectives remain within their subjects. Examples include oral tradition in English Language Arts, cultural comparison in French language arts, knowledge of biodiversity and climate in Science, and cultural ownership and appropriation in Arts and ADST. Social Studies has separate requirements to include a religion and systems/structures indigenous to the Americas. Use specific community contexts and permissions; curriculum inclusion does not license cultural works.

## How to use the records

Choose the programme, open a landing, and read the record's Scope and use section before following its source locators. Big Ideas concern enduring understandings, Curricular Competencies concern skills and processes, and Content concerns knowledge. The summaries group related expectations without replacing the official sources.

Use descriptive references such as [Mathematics 7: C-04](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-04), whose canonical local key is `bc-g07-mathematics#c-04`. These are repository IDs, not official BC codes, assessment levels, or a prescribed teaching order. The [format guide](../../curriculum-format.md#linking-to-records-and-items) explains the convention.

There are no special prerequisites or materials for reading these references. Activities, when added separately, must identify their own learning objective, curriculum mapping, materials, accessibility, language suitability, and permissions.

## Coverage and limits

All **20 records** are source-checked: seven shared non-language subjects, English Language Arts, three distinct French language-arts entries including 7T, and nine Languages courses. Source checking means comparison with the primary standards and elaborations, not ministry endorsement or independent educator review. No requested primary source remains inaccessible.

Source curriculum revision dates were not established and remain `null`. **2026-09-14** is the actual review date, not a "2026 curriculum edition." Complete main HTML and, where needed, official DOCX sections were used rather than relying on simplified pages that expose only a tooltip.

All written summaries are English editions. The reviewed primary sources are English for seventeen records and French for the three French language-arts records; English summaries do not become French classroom materials.

**Remaining scope gaps:** Additional Languages-course applicability or placement in immersion and Francophone programmes is not established by the nine English-pathway records. Locally developed, Indigenous, and other guide-listed language curricula beyond the nine web choices, school-specific ADST module inventories, French-language repository editions, and classroom resources are not populated. These are distinct limits, not inaccessible-source blockers or omissions hidden behind a claim of complete BC-wide coverage.
