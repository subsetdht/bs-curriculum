# Physical and Health Education - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7](../README.md) > [Physical and Health Education](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. Immersion's parallel-content basis is in [S3]. The Required Areas Order [S4], sections 1-2, includes Francophone students under the same PHE guide, subject to the individual exceptions in section 3.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the source reviewed is English. This is separate from programme instructional language.

**Learning objective:** Develop movement confidence, informed healthy-living decisions, safety and relationship skills, and ways to support personal and community well-being during adolescence.

**Prerequisites:** Build from each learner's experiences and abilities. No athletic selection standard, fitness score, or health disclosure is required by this reference.

**Materials:** None are required for reading. Activities need suitable equipment, accessibility arrangements, supervision, and current safety guidance.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and its elaborations. Physical literacy, healthy and active living, social and community health, and mental well-being are all part of the subject. Local IDs are repository references, not medical advice, diagnoses, or official standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Varied daily activity builds physical literacy and contributes to personal health and fitness goals. Growing movement competence and fitness can also make participation more successful and enjoyable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily participation, physical literacy, fitness, success, and enjoyment.

### BI-02

Choices and life changes affect physical, emotional, and mental well-being and how people understand themselves and others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, healthy choices and changing views of self and others.

### BI-03

Understanding both shared experiences and differences among people contributes to the health of a community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, similarities/differences and community health.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Physical literacy:** Refine movement skills and apply concepts and strategies in varied activities and settings. Monitor and adjust exertion, participate safely and fairly with leadership, and identify activities that fit personal preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Physical literacy, movement skills/concepts/strategies, exertion, safety/fair play/leadership, and preferred activities.

### CC-02

**Healthy and active living:** Participate daily in activity that develops or maintains health-related fitness. Examine how activity across school, home, and community affects health, and investigate influences on food choices and other health decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Healthy and active living, daily participation, settings, eating habits, and influences on healthy choices.

### CC-03

Evaluate and communicate health information. Develop strategies toward personal healthy-living goals and examine their outcomes to judge how well the strategies worked.

**Source locator:** [S1], Healthy and active living, health information, goal pursuit, and reflection on outcomes/strategies.

### CC-04

**Social and community health:** Identify ways to avoid or respond to unsafe, abusive, or exploitative situations. Assess responses to bullying, stereotyping, and discrimination, practise skills for healthy relationships, and explore contributions to a healthier school and community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Social and community health, protection, discrimination/bullying, relationships, and school/community well-being.

### CC-05

**Mental well-being:** Assess ways to support well-being for oneself and others and to respond to problems involving mental health and substance use affecting others. Recognize appropriate help and support rather than taking on a clinician's role.

**Source locator:** [S1], Mental well-being, promoting well-being and managing problems related to mental well-being and substance use; support-resource elaboration.

### CC-06

Develop and assess strategies for managing the physical, emotional, and social changes of puberty and adolescence. Explore how transitions and changing relationships affect identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Mental well-being, puberty/adolescence and the impact of transition/change on identities.

## Content

### C-01

Learn effective techniques for movement in place, travel, and object control. Body and spatial awareness, effort, relationships with others, and game strategies support participation in individual, paired, rhythmic, and game activities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, fundamental movement skills, movement concepts/strategies, and activity types.

### C-02

Understand ways of noticing and adjusting exertion and how different activities affect the body. Fitness planning includes frequency, intensity, type, and time (FITT), adaptation to imposed demands (SAID), and specificity: the demands of an activity influence the adaptations it develops.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, exertion monitoring, training principles, and activity effects.

### C-03

Examine influences on eating, including personal preferences, culture, family, peers, media, and available choices. Know where to obtain health information and learn practices that reduce the risk of sexually transmitted infections and serious communicable diseases.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, eating choices, health-information sources, and reducing STI/life-threatening communicable-disease risks.

### C-04

Know basic emergency-response principles and ways to seek help. Understand protection from abuse, exploitation, and harm in physical and online settings, including personal boundaries, affirmative and ongoing consent, power imbalances, and coercion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, emergency response and protection from abuse/exploitation/harm; Social and community health elaborations on boundaries and consent.

### C-05

Understand the effects of bullying, stereotyping, and discrimination and the importance of relationships that respect identity, boundaries, and the right to decline. The source addresses discrimination related to gender identity/expression, sexuality, race, religion, and other characteristics.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, consequences of bullying/stereotyping/discrimination; Social and community health elaborations on discrimination and healthy relationships.

### C-06

Recognize possible signs of stress, anxiety, and depression, and consider how physical growth, changing feelings, and social changes influence identity and relationships during adolescence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content and elaborations, signs/symptoms of stress/anxiety/depression and physical/emotional/social changes.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Health, consent, safety, mental well-being, and substance-use learning are not optional extras to exercise. The source emphasizes respectful support for survivors, who are not responsible for abuse, and includes seeking trusted help. Learners must not be required to disclose private health information or personal experiences of abuse.

Daily participation does not establish an additional duration or fitness-performance threshold here. FITT, SAID, and exertion measures support understanding and personal planning; they do not constitute an individualized exercise prescription. Recognizing signs of distress is not self-diagnosis.

[S2] discusses inclusion, adaptations and modifications, and alternative delivery. Alternative delivery is an agreed way to address and assess the health standards, not permission to omit them. Programme arrangements belong with the school and family, not in this reference.

The source's activity elaborations include traditional First Peoples games among possible activities. [S2] situates First Peoples perspectives in well-being, identity, family, community, experiential learning, and local relationships. No particular game or cultural practice is compulsory here; obtain community-specific permission before using or translating cultural materials.
