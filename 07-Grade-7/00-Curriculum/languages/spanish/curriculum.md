# Spanish - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Spanish](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S3] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports Spanish as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), using the English official source. The subject of learning is Spanish; this reference does not prescribe English as the classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Make meaning from familiar Spanish messages and stories, exchange information and preferences, and explore cultural perspectives in Hispanic and other communities.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience with basic Spanish sounds, words, and interaction is useful, not a formal admission rule established by this record.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Spanish texts, recordings, images, and cultural works require separate checks for accessibility, suitability, and permissions.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1] and its elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. The Grade 7 standards are distinct from other grades in the Grades 5-10 downloads. See [Languages course-choice scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Listening and viewing attentively, together with reciprocal communication, support understanding of an expanding variety of Spanish messages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories link language learning with experiences and ideas that help learners interpret the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

A growing understanding of another language can reshape questions about identity and place, while knowledge of diverse communities supports cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Spanish letter patterns and pronunciation with meaning. Identify main information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other texts, and understand stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, letter patterns/pronunciation/meaning, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Use familiar language, prior knowledge, context, and non-verbal clues to support understanding. Use pitch, intonation, and tone to communicate meaning and intention.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and pitch, intonation, and tone.

### CC-03

Follow task instructions, answer questions, and exchange ideas orally and in writing. Ask for clarification or repetition and use rewording to resolve misunderstandings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, instructions/questions, exchanging ideas and information, and seeking clarification.

### CC-04

Choose presentation formats and supports that enable sharing for learners with different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, presentation format suited to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

### CC-05

Identify and compare linguistic and cultural information about Hispanic communities both within and outside Canada. Consider how cultural perspectives shape personal, shared, and other people's experiences and worldviews.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Hispanic communities in and outside Canada, and the cultural lens.

### CC-06

Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing without treating them as one uniform body of knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring Spanish spellings and their pronunciation relationships, including common word endings such as -ción and -mente.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Spanish letter patterns; letter patterns and pronunciation elaboration.

### C-02

Develop frequent vocabulary and sentence structures for varied questions, descriptions of people, and instructions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions of people, and instructions.

### C-03

Express opinions and preferences with reasons, and use simple comparisons to relate choices and experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, opinions/preferences and comparisons, including reason-giving elaborations.

### C-04

Recognize how story characters, setting, events, problems, and resolutions work together to communicate meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about Hispanic communities worldwide, their cultural practices, and their creative works, with attention to cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of Hispanic communities, communities around the world, and Hispanic works of art.

### C-06

Explore connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place in First Peoples contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 7 examples extend opinions and preferences with reasons; these should not be reduced to isolated likes and dislikes. The sound-pattern and sentence examples in [S1] illustrate the scope without establishing a compulsory word count or complete verb-tense syllabus.

Keep the slow, clear speech qualification when selecting comprehension material. “Text” includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms, not only printed passages.

The source names Hispanic communities and explicitly includes comparisons in and outside Canada. Do not collapse different communities, nationalities, or cultural experiences into one account, or use Spanish study as a substitute for the separate First Peoples expectations.

Stories and artworks need attribution, suitable cultural context, and permissions before being copied, retold, translated, or adapted. A curriculum example is not permission to use a community's knowledge or creative work.
