# American Sign Language (ASL) - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [American Sign Language](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, when ASL is the selected additional-language course. [S2] establishes the second-language policy and board choice of offerings; this record does not establish placement in other programs. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing an English official source. ASL is the language being learned, not a spoken or written form of English.

**Learning objective:** Understand and participate in ASL exchanges and visual storytelling while examining Deaf communities and the connections between language, culture, and experience.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with basic signs and attentive visual interaction is helpful, not an admission requirement imposed by this reference.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this document. Learning resources should make signing, facial expression, and signing space accessible; videos and community contributions require separate suitability and permission checks.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], including its elaborations. Local IDs support resource mappings and are not official BC standard codes. These are Grade 7 standards, even though the source also offers Grades 5-10 compilations; this is not a prescribed lesson sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding signed messages depends on attentive viewing and on noticing meaning carried beyond the hands, including facial expression and body movement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, viewing with intent and non-verbal cues.

### BI-02

Back-and-forth signing and stories provide ways to develop language while exploring experiences, ideas, and the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, reciprocal interactions and stories.

### BI-03

Learning about Deaf communities alongside other communities broadens cultural understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, Deaf and other diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret signed stories and identify a message's main information and supporting details. Notice how relationships between familiar handshapes and sign locations distinguish meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, handshape/location relationships, stories, and key information in texts and other communications.

### CC-02

Draw on context, prior knowledge, recognizable visual meanings, and compound signs to support understanding. Repair communication by asking for repetition, slower signing, or clarification.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and seeking clarification; elaborations on iconic and compound signs.

### CC-03

Coordinate signs with facial expression, other non-manual signals, movement, and location. Exchange information in complete ASL sentences, respond to questions, and follow directions to accomplish a task.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, effective ASL communication, complete ASL sentences, and following instructions/responding to questions.

### CC-04

Create simple stories organized around handshapes. Select visual or digital ways to share information that accommodate both the presenter's and the audience's abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, creating handshape stories and accessible presentation formats.

### CC-05

Investigate Deaf communities internationally and compare information about Deaf culture and experiences. Consider how cultural perspectives shape personal, shared, and other people's interpretations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Deaf communities worldwide, Deaf culture and experiences, and the cultural lens.

### CC-06

Recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing without treating them as one interchangeable tradition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Understand ASL as a complete natural language with its own visual organization. It is non-written; written English descriptions can discuss ASL but are not ASL sentences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ASL as a natural language and cultural aspects; complete ASL sentences elaboration.

### C-02

Learn how handshape, movement, palm orientation, location, and facial expression contribute to a sign. Use basic classifier handshapes to represent kinds of things and features such as size, shape, or movement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, five parameters of ASL and classifiers.

### C-03

Develop visual grammar through non-manual signals and basic ASL structure: questions and statements, topic and time, listing and ordering, and spatial contrasts. Facial expression, mouth movements, head movements, gaze, and shoulder shifts can convey grammatical and expressive information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-manual signals and basic ASL structure; elaborations on question expression, mouth morphemes, and spatial organization.

### C-04

Build a usable repertoire for communicating about oneself and others, interests, feelings and physical states, preferences, locations, directions, and simple comparisons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency signs, self and others, locations/directions, likes/dislikes/preferences, and comparisons.

### C-05

Explore handshape-based stories and cultural practices within Deaf and other communities. Distinguish identification with Deaf culture from hearing status; the source's term “D/deaf” includes people whose cultural identifications differ.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects, handshape stories, and D/deaf perspectives and points of view.

### C-06

Consider First Peoples connections among language, histories, identity, and place, including the importance of community protocols and local relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture; histories, identity, and place elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Grade 7 elaborations specify examples of classifier handshapes, mouth morphemes, question expressions, and contrastive signing space. Consult [S1] rather than treating this prose summary as a signing demonstration or an exhaustive sign list.

Do not convert ASL competencies into listening, pronunciation, or written-ASL assignments. The source's references to “tone” and louder or quieter signing describe visual expression, not a requirement to use voice. Complete ASL sentences and handshape storytelling must retain their signed modality.

Deaf cultural identity is not reducible to hearing status. Neither Deaf communities nor First Peoples communities should be portrayed as culturally uniform. Learning about a community does not confer permission to record, translate, adapt, or reproduce its stories or cultural work.
