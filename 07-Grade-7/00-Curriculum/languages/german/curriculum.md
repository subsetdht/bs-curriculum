# German - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [German](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S3] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports German as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), using the English official source. German is the language being studied; English documentation does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Build practical German comprehension and expression while connecting language patterns, stories, cultural perspectives, and communities.

**Prerequisites:** Prior encounters with German sounds, words, and simple interactions can help; no formal entry requirement is added here.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read the record. Selected German texts, audio, images, and cultural works need separate accessibility and rights checks.

**How to use:** Read the summary with [S1] and its elaborations. The IDs are local mapping references, not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. Grade 7 is the source scope; a Grades 5-10 download is a compilation, not one set of banded standards. See [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing, together with two-way interaction, enable learners to understand more varied messages in German.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories connect developing language with ideas, experiences, and ways of understanding the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning German can open new ways to consider identity and place; encountering diverse communities supports cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use relationships between German spelling patterns and pronunciation to support understanding. Identify main information and details in slow, clear speech and other texts, and make sense of stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, letter patterns/pronunciation, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Combine familiar words, prior knowledge, context, and non-verbal information to interpret messages. Use intonation and tone deliberately, and request or offer clarification to repair misunderstandings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, intonation/tone, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Follow directions and answer questions during tasks. Exchange ideas orally and in writing, and share information through formats that accommodate different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, instructions and questions, oral/written exchanges, and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Identify, share, and compare language and cultural information about German communities. Examine personal and other people's experiences and worldviews in relation to cultural values and practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, German community information and the cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge alongside local cultural knowledge and other ways of knowing, without collapsing their differences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring German letter and sound patterns, including vowel combinations and forms such as umlauted vowels and ß.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German letter patterns; letter patterns and pronunciation elaboration.

### C-02

Understand that German grammatical forms and endings vary with gender, case, and number. Connect these changes to meaning within the language being used.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German declination and gender, case, and number; grammatical forms/endings elaboration.

### C-03

Build frequent vocabulary, expressions, and sentence structures for different questions, descriptions of people, instructions, and comparisons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-04

Recognize how characters, setting, events, problems, and resolutions contribute to stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about German communities worldwide, including Canadian communities, and their cultural practices and creative works. Engagement with art includes attention to cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of German communities, communities around the world, and German works of art.

### C-06

Explore how First Peoples oral histories, identities, and relationships with place connect language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's grammatical scope names gender, case, and number; this summary does not add a complete declension syllabus or a fixed order for teaching particular cases. Letter-pattern examples remain at [S1], and the comprehension expectation retains the qualification of slow, clear speech.

German communities are not limited to Germany. The official examples include European, Canadian, and other international settings; they illustrate breadth rather than require study of every listed location.

Texts may combine oral, written, visual, and digital communication. Story elements and cultural works should support communication rather than become a compulsory book or arts list. The official elaborations are examples and clarifications, not additional blanket requirements.

First Peoples histories and community knowledge remain part of the course. Obtain appropriate guidance and permissions before using or adapting cultural works; neither attribution alone nor a curriculum reference grants reproduction rights.
