# Languages - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [7](../README.md) > Languages

**Content status:** Summary available for all nine named courses in the Grade 7 catalog entry. The course inventory is complete for that entry; it is not an inventory of every locally developed or provincially approved language curriculum.

**Official BC entry:** [Languages 7](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/languages/7/courses)

**Program coverage:** The nine records establish English-language program course-choice coverage (`english-language`). They do not establish an additional Languages-course requirement or course placement for Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, or the distinct first-language Francophone program. This limitation is not a claim that students in those programs cannot study another language.

**Document and source languages:** All nine summaries are English (`en-CA`), based on the English Grade 7 course pages (`en`). These are reference documents, not English-language teaching materials for every course. The language being learned is separate from the language of this documentation; ASL is a signed, non-written language.

## Course choice and policy scope

Read the [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order][areas-order], sections 1 and 3-6, together with the [Language Education Policy][policy]. The Order includes Francophone authorities, programs, and students. Section 4 distinguishes a language other than English for programs offered in English, a language other than French for Francophone students, and English Language Arts for French Immersion students in Grades 4-9. The first two provisions concern Grades 5-8. These are program-level distinctions, not evidence that all nine catalog courses apply to every program.

The Language Education Policy describes second-language study in Grades 5-8 and board choice of offerings; Core French is the offering when a board does not provide an alternative. Read its general statements with the Order's qualifications below. A catalog listing does **not** mean every learner takes all nine courses, that all nine are offered locally, or that this page determines an individual student's placement.

Section 3 of the Order exempts the specified students from sections 2, 4, 5, and 6: students who must have an IEP because they cannot demonstrate learning against the applicable guide's outcomes, or students receiving English language learning services who cannot demonstrate that learning because of limited English proficiency. These are conditional provisions, not blanket exemptions for every learner with a disability or receiving support.

Section 5 removes the board's section 4(c) obligation to offer English Language Arts to Late French Immersion students in **Grade 6**. It is not a Grade 7 exception or a basis for assigning a separate catalog Languages course to an immersion learner. Consult the [grade program pathways](../README.md#choose-a-program) and the [French Immersion policy][immersion-policy] for program context. Core French is not a replacement for immersion or Francophone language arts.

## Coverage beyond this inventory

The catalog's [Ministry-created Languages Template][template] is a curriculum-development entry point, not a tenth Grade 7 course. The catalog and [Language Education Policy][policy] describe a provincial review and approval route for locally developed language curricula. The normal section 4 route uses the applicable guide listed in the [Educational Program Guide Order M231/19][guide-order]; a locally offered or board-authorized course does not qualify merely by existing.

Section 2(a) of the Guide Order names Grades 5-9 guides for all nine web courses in this inventory. Section 2(b) separately lists French curriculum documents for Francophone and French Immersion programs. These listings do not make all nine courses compulsory or establish additional program applicability for the English summaries here. Other grades and entries in the Guide Order do not add courses to this Grade 7 web inventory.

**Local Indigenous-language exception:** Section 6 of the [Order][areas-order] permits a board to offer a Grades 5-8 second-language program without matching an educational program guide's outcomes when it is a local Indigenous-language program developed and offered by the board and the board has an agreement for its provision with an Indian Act band council or a treaty first nation. The Order calls this a “local Aboriginal language.” This specific agreement-based route must not be erased by a blanket claim that only guide-listed courses can qualify, or extended to every locally developed language course.

The policy encourages opportunities to learn Indigenous and other community languages. Indigenous language curriculum and resources should be developed collaboratively with the local Indigenous people and the board. This page does not inventory those curricula, establish their provincial approval or section 6 agreement status, summarize their learning standards, or supply community-specific cultural knowledge. Their absence from these nine summaries must not be presented as an absence of legitimate language-learning pathways.
