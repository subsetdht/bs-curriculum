# Japanese - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Japanese](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S3] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports Japanese as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), using the English official source. This is reference writing about Japanese learning, not a statement that Japanese instruction should be in English.

**Learning objective:** Connect Japanese scripts and sounds with meaning, participate in familiar exchanges, and explore stories and cultural perspectives in Japanese and other communities.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience with Japanese sounds, familiar expressions, and kana is helpful, not a formal entry requirement created by this summary.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read the reference. Japanese texts and recordings should present scripts and sounds accessibly; classroom selections need separate suitability and rights checks.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1] and its elaborations. Local IDs are repository mapping tools, not official BC codes. This is a Grade 7 summary, not the whole Grades 5-10 download or a prescribed teaching sequence. See [Languages course-choice scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful attention to what is heard and seen, together with reciprocal interaction, supports understanding of increasingly varied Japanese messages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories connect language development with experiences, knowledge, and ways of interpreting the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Growing language knowledge offers new ways to explore identity and place; learning about diverse communities strengthens cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate Japanese characters to sounds and use those connections in understanding language. Identify essential information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other texts, and interpret stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, character/sound relationships, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Use familiar language, contextual clues, prior knowledge, and non-verbal information to support comprehension. Attend to pitch, accent, and tone when expressing meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and pitch, accent, and tone.

### CC-03

Carry out instructed tasks and respond to questions. Exchange ideas and information orally and in writing, using familiar clarification requests when communication breaks down.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, instructions/questions, oral and written exchanges, and seeking clarification.

### CC-04

Present information in forms that suit different abilities, including combinations of visual, spoken, and digital supports where appropriate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, presentation format suited to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

### CC-05

Share and compare linguistic and cultural information about Japanese communities, and examine how culture shapes personal and other people's perspectives and experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Japanese community information and the cultural lens.

### CC-06

Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing without assuming they are interchangeable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn hiragana and katakana as the two kana systems named for this grade, connecting their written forms with sound.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, hiragana and katakana; Thinking and communicating, Japanese characters and sounds.

### C-02

Build frequent vocabulary and sentence patterns for questions, instructions, comparisons, and descriptions of people, including basic particle use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions, including basic particle usage.

### C-03

Recognize how a story's characters, setting, events, problem, and resolution contribute to its meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore Japanese creative works and cultural life in communities around the world, including communities outside Japan. Cultural appropriation and plagiarism matter when engaging with art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese works of art and cultural aspects of Japanese communities around the world.

### C-05

Learn about Indigenous peoples of Japan; the official elaboration names Ainu and Okinawan peoples.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, indigenous peoples of Japan and its Ainu/Okinawan elaboration.

### C-06

Explore connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place in First Peoples contexts, including the role of local traditions and protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Grade 7 writing-system entry specifies **hiragana and katakana**. Romanized examples in the elaborations do not replace those scripts, and this summary does not add a kanji quota or import another grade's script requirements. The character/sound elaboration highlights Japanese syllable structure and its five vowels.

The source's examples of classroom directions, opposites, and familiar descriptions illustrate the expected language use rather than a fixed vocabulary list. Comprehension retains the slow, clear speech qualification. “Text” includes oral, written, visual, and digital forms.

Keep the explicit study of Ainu and Okinawan peoples distinct from general descriptions of Japanese communities and from Canadian First Peoples expectations. Neither one cultural account nor one community can stand for all of these peoples.

Stories and artworks remain with their creators and communities. Curriculum references do not grant permission to copy, retell, translate, or adapt cultural materials; obtain appropriate guidance, attribution, and permissions.
