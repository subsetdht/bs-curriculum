# Korean - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Korean](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S4] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports Korean as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on English official sources. This is not an English-medium teaching requirement: the Korean introduction [S3] encourages Korean use throughout instruction while allowing support in the prevailing school language when needed.

**Learning objective:** Connect Korean sounds and script with meaning, communicate about familiar situations with attention to grammar and social context, and explore cultural perspectives and communities.

**Prerequisites:** Prior exposure to Korean sounds, writing, and basic interaction is helpful, not a formal admission condition imposed by this summary.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read the reference. Korean texts, sound models, and cultural resources need separate checks for accessibility, suitability, and rights.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] and its elaborations for Grade 7 expectations. Local IDs are repository mapping references, not official codes or a lesson order. The Grades 5-10 downloads contain separate grade standards; they do not broaden this record's grade scope. See [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending carefully to messages and participating in back-and-forth communication help learners understand a wider range of Korean.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories provide opportunities to develop language and explore experiences and understandings of the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning another language opens questions about identity and place; encountering different communities broadens cultural understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate pronunciation, sounds, phonetic representation, and Korean characters to meaning. Interpret key information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other texts, including the meaning of stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation/sounds/phonetic representation/characters, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Draw on context, gestures, familiar language, and prior knowledge to support understanding. Use pitch, accent, intonation, and tone to convey meaning, and ask for clarification when needed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies, pitch/accent/intonation/tone, and seeking clarification.

### CC-03

Follow instructions, complete tasks, and answer questions. Exchange information orally and in writing, choosing ways of presenting it that accommodate one's own and others' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, instructions/questions, exchanging ideas and information, and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Identify, share, and compare linguistic and cultural information about Korean communities. Examine experiences and worldviews, including one's own, in their cultural contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Korean community information and the cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing while respecting their differences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn particles as functional elements that establish grammatical relationships in Korean, including basic use of the subject and object markers illustrated in the source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, particles (functional words) and basic particle usage.

### C-02

Understand how speech endings and etiquette reflect the relationship and situation of an exchange. The elaborations distinguish formal-polite, informal-polite, and casual forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, language formality and etiquette, including its three basic ending types.

### C-03

Build frequent vocabulary and sentence patterns for questions, instructions, comparisons, and descriptions of people, using familiar everyday contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions; everyday observations elaboration.

### C-04

Recognize the characters, places, setting, and plot that organize a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about cultural life in Korean communities in Canada and internationally, and explore Korean creative work with attention to cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean works of art and cultural aspects of Korean communities in Canada and around the world.

### C-06

Explore how First Peoples oral histories, identities, and relationships with place connect language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Korean examples in [S1] use Hangul. Script–sound–meaning relationships remain part of the competencies even though Grade 7 Content foregrounds particles and formality. This summary does not add a character quota or treat phonetic notation as a replacement for Korean characters.

Formality and etiquette are communicative choices, not merely endings to memorize without context. The listed three types illustrate Grade 7 scope rather than supply a complete honorific grammar. [S3] explains the supporting role of grammar in purposeful communication and the flexibility of instruction.

The slow, clear speech qualification matters. Official examples are not a compulsory vocabulary list or fixed proficiency benchmark; [S3] describes CEFR as an optional supporting tool, not an assigned Grade 7 level.

Stories and cultural work require appropriate attribution and permission. [S3] calls for consultation with local First Peoples communities and authorization before using or translating their cultural materials or practices. Do not assume one community's protocols apply to all.
