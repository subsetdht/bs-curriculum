# Mandarin - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Mandarin](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S3] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports Mandarin as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English official source. The target language is Mandarin; documentation language does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Connect Mandarin pronunciation, phonetic notation, and Chinese characters with meaning while exchanging familiar information and exploring stories and cultural perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Previous experience with Mandarin sounds, tones, and familiar written forms is helpful background, not a formal entry criterion added by the repository.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read the reference. Any selected texts, pronunciation models, or cultural resources require separate accessibility, suitability, and rights checks.

**How to use:** Consult [S1], including elaborations, for official expectations. The IDs are local references for mapping resources, not ministry codes or a teaching order. Grade 7 standards are distinct from other grades in the downloadable Grades 5-10 collection. See [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing, combined with two-way communication, help learners understand increasingly varied messages in Mandarin.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories connect language learning with experiences, ideas, and ways of understanding the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Another language can offer new perspectives on identity and place; learning about different communities deepens cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize connections among sounds, tonal and intonation patterns, phonetic representation, Chinese characters, and meaning. Identify important information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other texts, and interpret stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sounds/phonetic representation/Chinese characters/meaning, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Use context, prior knowledge, non-verbal information, and word-building relationships to support comprehension. Seek clarification through familiar questions and statements, including requests for repetition or rewording.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and seeking clarification; word-building elaboration.

### CC-03

Follow directions and respond to questions during tasks. Exchange ideas orally and in writing, and present information in forms that accommodate different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, following instructions/responding to questions, exchanging ideas and information, and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Identify, share, and compare linguistic and cultural information about Chinese communities. Consider personal and other people's experiences and worldviews in relation to culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Chinese community information and the cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge while respecting their different contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how Mandarin pronunciation and tonal distinctions can be represented through phonetic systems. The source's explanations refer to Pinyin letters or Zhuyin symbols as pronunciation supports.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phonetic systems and tonal variations; phonetic representation elaboration.

### C-02

Develop knowledge of commonly used Chinese characters and their basic parts and radicals. The character competency's elaboration permits traditional or simplified forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, commonly used Chinese characters and basic parts/radicals; Chinese characters elaboration.

### C-03

Build frequent vocabulary and sentence structures for asking questions, giving instructions, making comparisons, and describing other people in familiar contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions.

### C-04

Recognize the characters, setting, events, problems, and resolutions through which stories communicate meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Explore Chinese communities worldwide, their cultural practices, and Chinese creative works. Consider the ethics of using cultural material and crediting creators.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Chinese works of art, cultural aspects of Chinese communities, and communities around the world.

### C-06

Explore how First Peoples oral histories, identities, and relationships with place connect language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Phonetic notation, tonal distinctions, and Chinese characters are related but different parts of the course. Do not replace character learning with romanization alone, conflate Pinyin and Zhuyin, or infer that both phonetic systems and both character forms must be mastered. The source supplies no fixed character or radical quota in this Grade 7 entry.

Word-building and familiar contexts support comprehension, whose speech condition remains slow and clear. The official question, comparison, and character examples illustrate possible teaching content, not an exhaustive vocabulary list or a prescribed progression through other grades.

The cultural standards use the term “Chinese communities.” This is not a claim that every member speaks Mandarin or shares one culture. Preserve the separate First Peoples expectations rather than treating them as interchangeable with these community studies.

Texts may be oral, written, visual, digital, or combined. Stories and artworks require appropriate cultural context, attribution, and permissions before reproduction, translation, or adaptation.
