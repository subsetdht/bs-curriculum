# Punjabi - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Punjabi](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, where Punjabi is the selected additional-language course. [S2] supports board choice of offerings, not a guarantee that Punjabi is available everywhere or a requirement to take all catalog courses. Other-program applicability is not established here.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English official source. Punjabi is the language being learned; an English reference does not set the language of classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange familiar information in Punjabi, attend to language patterns and forms of address, and compare cultural experiences within and beyond Punjabi communities.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with Punjabi sounds, writing, and simple interactions is useful background, not a formal entry rule created by this record.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read the reference. Texts, recordings, and cultural resources selected for instruction need separate accessibility, suitability, and permission checks.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] and its elaborations for the official expectations. Local IDs are repository references, not BC standard codes or a teaching order. These are Grade 7 standards, not a grade band inferred from a Grades 5-10 download. See [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful attention to spoken and visual messages, together with reciprocal interaction, helps learners understand a wider range of Punjabi.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories connect developing language with experiences, knowledge, and ways of making sense of the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Learning another language can expand understandings of identity and place; encountering diverse communities supports cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Identify main information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other texts, and interpret the meaning conveyed in stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, key information and supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

Use prior knowledge, familiar words, contextual clues, and non-verbal information to understand messages. Use intonation and tone to convey meaning, including differences between questions, statements, and emotional expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and intonation/tone of voice.

### CC-03

Complete tasks from instructions, respond to questions, and exchange ideas orally and in writing. Clarify understanding through common requests, questions, repetition, or rewording.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, following instructions/responding to questions, exchanging ideas, and seeking clarification.

### CC-04

Share information in presentation formats that support both one's own abilities and those of the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, presentation format suited to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

### CC-05

Compare one's own practices and traditions with those of Punjabi communities in different regions, including their purposes. Share and compare linguistic and cultural information, and examine experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, similarities/differences in practices and traditions, Punjabi community information, and the cultural lens.

### CC-06

Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing while respecting their distinct contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Develop connections between Punjabi letter patterns and pronunciation through sound units, blends, and rhyme. The elaboration also includes synonyms as part of this language knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, relationships between Punjabi letter patterns and pronunciation.

### C-02

Build frequent vocabulary and sentence patterns for asking questions, describing people and objects, making comparisons, and giving directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions, comparisons, and directions.

### C-03

Know contrasting formal and informal ways of addressing people, and attend to their use within social interaction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, formal and informal modes of address.

### C-04

Recognize how characters, place, setting, events, problems, and resolutions help organize stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about Punjabi communities worldwide, their cultural practices, and their creative works. Consider cultural appropriation and plagiarism when selecting and using art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi works of art, cultural aspects of Punjabi communities, and communities around the world.

### C-06

Explore how First Peoples oral histories, identities, and relationships with place connect language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Grade 7 elaborations illustrate Punjabi in Gurmukhi script. This record does not establish a parallel Shahmukhi-script progression or add a script quota. The letter–sound focus must remain connected to oral and written communication.

Formal/informal address is part of the course, not an optional cultural aside. Comparing regional practices includes considering their purposes, rather than merely listing festivals or treating all Punjabi communities as culturally identical.

The speech comprehension condition remains slow and clear. Texts include oral, written, visual, and digital communication, and the elaborations provide examples rather than a complete vocabulary, location, or activity list.

First Peoples perspectives and local knowledge remain within the course. Obtain appropriate community guidance and permissions before using, retelling, adapting, or translating cultural material; a source link or classroom purpose alone does not grant those rights.
