# Italian - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Italian](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S2] supports the English-language course-choice framework; [S3] explicitly designates the corresponding French guide for Francophone and French Immersion programs. This supports Italian as optional/additional study in those programs, not compulsory extra enrolment, a replacement for required language arts, or guaranteed local provision. See the [Languages policy scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope) for placement and exceptions.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing the English official source. The language being learned is Italian; this document is not an English-medium instructional requirement.

**Learning objective:** Communicate about familiar people, places, and needs in Italian, understand and retell stories, and consider cultural experience through Italian communities and creative expression.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with basic Italian communication is useful background, not a formal prerequisite added by the repository.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom stories, audio, images, and artworks require separate selection, accessibility, and permission checks.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and its elaborations. The local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes or an assessment sequence. Only Grade 7 standards are summarized; the Grades 5-10 downloads contain separate grades. See [Languages course-choice scope](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending closely to messages and taking part in two-way interaction help learners understand and use an expanding range of Italian.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening/viewing and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Stories offer a setting for developing language and making sense of thoughts, experiences, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories and understanding the world.

### BI-03

Developing another language can change how learners think about identity and place, while encounters with different communities extend cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and place, and diverse communities.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring Italian spelling patterns, including cases where differently written groups of letters have the same sound.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian letter patterns and the cu/qu elaboration.

### C-02

Develop frequent expressions and sentence patterns for questions, descriptions of people, locations and directions, needs and desires, and simple comparisons. The comparison examples include comparative and superlative adjective forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions; simple-comparisons elaboration.

### C-03

Know how characters, place, setting, and plot help organize a story and support its interpretation or retelling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories; Thinking and communicating, narrating or retelling stories.

### C-04

Explore Italian creative work across different art forms and the contributions of Italian Canadians to cultural, community, and economic life. Consider attribution and cultural appropriation when working with creative material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian creative works and contributions of Italian Canadians.

### C-05

Learn about Italian communities internationally while recognizing regional differences in language and culture, including dialects, traditions, and local products or industries.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of Italian communities around the world and regional differences.

### C-06

Explore relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place in First Peoples contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, including oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Italian 7 explicitly includes interpreting non-verbal cues and narrating or retelling stories. These should not disappear into a generic vocabulary summary. Gestures and tone support meaning, but do not replace oral and written communication.

The elaborations illustrate comparative and superlative language without prescribing an exhaustive grammar table. Likewise, examples of Italian-Canadian contributions, creative works, and regions are not a fixed research list. Preserve both Canadian connections and regional diversity rather than presenting one uniform Italian culture.

The comprehension standard refers to slow, clear speech and other texts; it does not set a universal fluency benchmark. Texts may be oral, written, visual, digital, or combinations of these.

First Peoples histories and perspectives belong within the course, but permission to use, retell, or translate community material must be established separately. Creative works need appropriate cultural context and attribution as well as copyright permission.
