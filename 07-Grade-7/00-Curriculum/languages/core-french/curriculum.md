# Core French - Grade 7

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 7 overview](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Core French](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language. Core French is an additional-language course, distinct from immersion and Francophone language arts. [S2] names Core French as the offering when a board does not offer an alternative second language; it does not require every learner to take all the catalog's language courses.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English official source. The learning described is French communication; an English summary does not establish English as the course's instructional language.

**Learning objective:** Participate in manageable French exchanges, understand simple messages and stories, and learn about Francophone communities and their relationships with language and culture.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with familiar French words and basic interactions is helpful, not a formal entry condition created by this record.

**Materials:** No special materials are required for the reference. Any selected French texts, recordings, or cultural resources need their own accessibility, suitability, and rights review.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a teaching order. This record covers Grade 7, not every grade in the downloadable Grades 5-10 collection. See the [Languages policy and program boundaries](../README.md#course-choice-and-policy-scope).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful listening and viewing, supported by a range of learning strategies, help learners make sense of French and extend what they can understand.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and strategies for understanding and acquiring language.

### BI-02

A limited French repertoire can still support meaningful back-and-forth interaction about interests.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, discussing interests with simple French and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-03

Stories support language development and exploration of experience; learning more about Francophone communities deepens cultural awareness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories and the world, and knowledge of Francophone communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect recurring French spellings with their pronunciation. Identify central information and supporting details in slow, clear speech and other simple texts, including the important events and information in simple stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, letter patterns/pronunciation, key information and supporting details, and simple stories.

### CC-02

Use strategies such as contextual clues, familiar language, and reformulation to understand and express meaning. Ask for clarification when needed, and use intonation and tone to make intentions clearer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to support communication, seeking clarification, and intonation/tone.

### CC-03

Carry out a task from instructions and exchange ideas in complete French sentences, both orally and in writing. Use presentation modes that enable understanding and expression for learners with different abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, following instructions, complete-sentence exchanges, and modes of presentation.

### CC-04

Explore and communicate information about Francophone communities in Canada and internationally, including descriptions of their cultural practices and contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Francophone communities across Canada and the world, and describing cultural aspects.

### CC-05

Investigate and share connections between French and First Peoples communities, recognizing that these connections include distinct First Nations, Métis, and Inuit experiences rather than a single cultural story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, connections between First Peoples communities and the French language, including its community examples.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring French letter combinations and how different spellings can represent the same sound. Attend to consistent pronunciation patterns, rhyme, and silent letters when connecting print and speech.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, French letter patterns; Thinking and communicating, letter patterns and pronunciation elaboration.

### C-02

Develop frequent vocabulary and sentence patterns for asking varied questions, describing other people, and explaining locations and directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, high-frequency vocabulary and sentence structures: questions, descriptions of others, and locations/directions.

### C-03

Extend statements of likes, dislikes, and preferences by giving reasons, and make simple comparisons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, reasons for likes/dislikes/preferences and simple comparisons.

### C-04

Recognize the people, places, setting, and sequence of events that help a story make sense.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about places where French is spoken in Canada and elsewhere, alongside cultural aspects of Francophone communities. These include diverse regional and First Peoples connections, not only France or Quebec.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, communities across Canada and around the world, and cultural aspects; community elaborations.

### C-06

Understand why cultural material needs appropriate permission and context, and why presenting another person's work as one's own is unacceptable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 7 combines **simple** texts and slow, clear speech with exchanges in **complete sentences**. It is not a requirement for unrestricted fluency, and it should not be reduced to isolated word lists. The source does not assign a mandatory vocabulary count or a standardized proficiency level here.

The official elaborations offer possible question forms, communities, strategies, and cultural topics; they are not a required itinerary or exhaustive list. “Text” includes oral, written, visual, and digital communication.

First Peoples–French connections belong within this course, but do not imply that all First Peoples communities speak French. Learning from stories and cultural practices requires attribution, context, and permissions; source examples do not authorize their reproduction or translation.

Core French, French Immersion Language Arts, and Francophone language arts serve different purposes. The [Languages overview](../README.md) records the limits of this record's program coverage rather than treating these courses as interchangeable.
