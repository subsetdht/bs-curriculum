# Mathematics - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject requirements under [S2].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English. The [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) distinguishes this from classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Extend number sense to larger whole numbers, fractions, and decimals, using relationships and representations to reason about operations, patterns, measurement, data, and chance.

**Prerequisites:** Prior work with place value, equal groups, and mathematical representations can help. These are useful connections, not formal entry requirements.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. It does not require a calculator, app, textbook, or practice program.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], especially its numerical limits and computational-fluency elaborations. Local IDs are stable repository references, not official BC codes, a teaching sequence, or a timed-performance standard.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Quantity can be represented with fractions and decimals as well as whole numbers. Understanding relationships within multiplication and division supports flexible calculation rather than dependence on one procedure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fractions/decimals as numbers and computational fluency through multiplicative patterns and relations.

### BI-02

Tables and other tools can make regular numerical change visible. Describing and comparing polygon features provides another way to investigate mathematical structure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, regular changes in patterns and measurable attributes of polygons.

### BI-03

Interpreting the results of chance experiments helps people understand probability.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, analysis and interpretation of probability experiments.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use reasoning, sensible estimates, and mental strategies to make sense of quantities. Model mathematics in meaningful situations and use technology to investigate when appropriate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and analyzing.

### CC-02

Build and apply understanding through inquiry, play, visualization, and more than one problem-solving strategy. Connect problems to place, stories, cultural practices, and perspectives from local First Peoples and other communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Understanding and solving, including contextual problem-solving elaborations.

### CC-03

Make thinking understandable through mathematical language, discussion, and concrete, visual, and symbolic representations. Explain why a strategy or conclusion makes sense.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and representing.

### CC-04

Reflect on approaches and results, connect concepts across subjects and interests, and incorporate First Peoples worldviews when exploring mathematical relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and reflecting.

## Content

### C-01

Develop place-value understanding and compare, order, and estimate whole numbers **to 10 000**. Use flexible strategies for addition and subtraction within that range, checking the reasonableness of results.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 10 000 and addition/subtraction to 10 000, with their place-value and estimation elaborations.

### C-02

Represent decimals **to hundredths** and connect them with fractions of a whole, set, or length. Compare and order fractions; the elaboration specifies **common denominators**, models, and benchmarks. Add and subtract decimals to hundredths.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, decimals to hundredths, ordering/comparing fractions, and decimal addition/subtraction; related elaborations.

### C-03

Multiply and divide **two- or three-digit numbers by one-digit numbers**, using relationships among operations and flexible computation. Continue developing addition/subtraction fluency **to 20** and introductory strategies for multiplication/division facts **to 100**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multidigit multiplication/division, addition/subtraction facts, and multiplication/division facts.

### C-04

Describe increasing and decreasing patterns through words, numbers, tables, and charts. Express relationships among quantities and solve **one-step equations with one unknown**, using any of the four operations; the unknown need not be the result.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, patterns, algebraic relationships, and one-step equations; start/change/result-unknown elaboration.

### C-05

Read analogue and digital time in **12- and 24-hour formats**, including a.m./p.m. and the relationship between minutes and hours. The elaborations extend reading from five-minute intervals to **the nearest minute**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, telling time, including clock-format and precision elaborations.

### C-06

Describe and sort regular and irregular polygons, investigate their boundaries through perimeter, and recognize or construct line symmetry.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, polygons, perimeter of regular/irregular shapes, and line symmetry.

### C-07

Interpret and create bar graphs and pictographs whose marks represent either single items or groups. Conduct chance experiments, predict individual outcomes, and record results to compare what happened with what was expected.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, one-to-one/many-to-one correspondence and probability experiments; related elaborations.

### C-08

Use monetary calculations, including change for amounts **to 100 dollars**, in making simple financial decisions. Connect calculations with choices about earning, spending, saving, giving, and fair exchange.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The multiplication/division-facts elaboration distinguishes introductory strategies from memorizing the entire set to 100. It specifically expects recall of the **2s, 5s, and 10s multiplication facts by the end of Grade 4**, while stating that general fact memorization is not intended at this level. Do not replace that qualification with universal instant recall, timed drills, or a later-grade fluency target.

Keep the different ranges separate: whole-number operations to 10 000, the stated multidigit-by-one-digit multiplication/division scope, decimals to hundredths, and financial calculations to 100 dollars. Fraction comparison does not establish a requirement for operations with unlike denominators.

Elaborations include First Peoples connections to time, seasons, place, design, and games. These are not a single prescribed cultural activity and do not grant permission to copy designs or reproduce community practices. Consult [S1] for precise definitions and examples; no activity or cultural work is supplied here.
