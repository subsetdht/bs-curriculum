# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the distinct first-language program. This is not French Immersion Language Arts or Core French; [S2] and [S3] establish the Francophone context.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative curriculum is in French. This is an original summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators are labelled English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Develop thoughtful reading, effective writing and speaking, and a sense of Francophone belonging while examining how texts, language choices, and stories convey meaning.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience using French in oral and written communication can help. This reference does not establish language-rights eligibility, admission, or placement criteria.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. No literary texts, speeches, worksheets, or assessment materials are supplied.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for official terminology and elaborations. The content particularly emphasizes **directive or instruction-giving texts, young readers' novels, and speeches**. Other genres and text types are also encouraged by the source. Consult the [Francophone pathway](../README.md#francophone) for the separate role of English Language Arts. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Textual clues support predictions and inference, while characters and their experiences give readers access to unfamiliar worlds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), hypotheses from clues and worlds encountered through characters and events.

### BI-02

Meaning depends on how a language works and how it is delivered: each language has its own rules, and voice and gesture influence spoken communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), distinctive language systems and effects of intonation/gesture.

### BI-03

Indigenous stories contribute to the history and cultural life of Canadian Francophonie.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), Indigenous narratives in Francophone Canadian history and culture.

### BI-04

Communicating in French can strengthen belonging within Francophone communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), French communication and community belonging.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Infer meaning by connecting experience and prior knowledge with textual clues and questions. Recognize a text's purpose and organization and use linking words to understand relationships among ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), inference, purpose/stages/organization, and relationship markers.

### CC-02

Explain characters' roles and connect their feelings with their actions. Use questioning to develop creative thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), character roles, feelings/actions, and creative questioning.

### CC-03

Recognize how stories within Indigenous cultures connect people to their environments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), Indigenous narratives and people/environment relationships.

### CC-04

Communicate precisely and fluently, adapting to listeners or readers with reasoned, expressive, and open discussion. Describe emotional responses to texts and restate their central ideas in one's own words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), expression, emotional response, audience and the art of discourse, and reformulation of the main idea.

### CC-05

Write in ways suited to the text types and language conventions being studied. Revisit written work and clarify spoken communication so the intended meaning is clearer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), writing with genre/convention awareness, revision, and clarification.

## Content

### C-01

Understand narrative development and the organization of young readers' novels and directive texts. Consider plot, description, setting, and chronology, distinguishing the author, narrator, and central character.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization and literary tools: narrative schema, novel/directive-text structures, and novel characteristics.

### C-02

Connect ideas using markers of **consequence and condition**. Use punctuation that helps organize and attribute meaning, including **colons, semicolons, and quotation marks**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), relationship markers and punctuation elaboration.

### C-03

Develop exclamatory and imperative sentence structures, use adverbs to qualify meaning, and expand vocabulary through synonyms and antonyms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: sentence structures, adverbs, and word relationships.

### C-04

Use verb forms in relation to the genres being studied. The elaboration identifies the **imperfect tense, imperative mood, and present infinitive**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), moods and tenses associated with studied genres.

### C-05

Develop strategies for communication and social interaction, purposeful reading, and the writing process. These include clarifying meaning, identifying important information and making inferences, and revising written communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication/socialization, reading, and writing strategies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source gives particular attention to the three stated text types or genres, while recommending a wider range as well. It does not prescribe named novels, a universal speech topic, or an exhaustive reading list.

Grammar belongs in meaningful communication, as explained in [S2]. The Grade 4 verb forms and sentence structures are not interchangeable with those in the immersion record. Keep these curricula separate even though both sources are in French.

Indigenous narratives have their own cultural contexts and permissions. Their presence within Francophone history and culture does not make them generic Francophone property or authorize translation, adaptation, or retelling. The source also explicitly connects Indigenous stories with people's environments.

All six language modes remain relevant: reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing [S2]. This English reference does not replace French-language instruction or provide a French edition of the summary.
