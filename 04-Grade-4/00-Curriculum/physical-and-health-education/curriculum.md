# Physical and Health Education - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Physical and Health Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject requirements under [S2].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for language-of-instruction distinctions.

**Learning objective:** Develop physical literacy and informed health choices while learning about safety, supportive relationships, mental well-being, and changes during puberty.

**Prerequisites:** Build from learners' existing movement, communication, and self-care experiences. This reference sets no fitness threshold or assumption about when puberty begins.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It does not prescribe equipment, exercise routines, or health treatments.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], retaining all four competency strands rather than equating this subject with sport alone. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or individual health assessments.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Regular activity at moderate-to-vigorous intensity supports well-being. Knowing which activities one enjoys and where participation is possible helps sustain an active life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily activity and knowledge of enjoyable activities and opportunities.

### BI-02

A balanced approach to health draws on self-understanding while recognizing that personal decisions and social or environmental conditions all affect well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, understanding health and the influences of choices and external factors.

### BI-03

Healthy relationships contribute to a sense of connection, support, and personal value.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, relationships and belonging.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Apply fundamental movements, concepts, and strategies across activities and environments. Monitor exertion and demonstrate safe participation, fair play, and leadership.

**Source locator:** [S1], Physical literacy, movement skills/concepts, exertion monitoring, safety, fair play, and leadership.

### CC-02

Participate daily in moderate-to-vigorous activity. Identify preferred activities, available opportunities, and barriers to taking part at home, school, or in the community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Physical literacy, preferred activities; Healthy and active living, daily participation and opportunities/challenges.

### CC-03

Explain connections between eating and well-being, examine influences on health choices and media messages, and use strategies to pursue personal healthy-living goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Healthy and active living, eating, choices, messaging, and goals.

### CC-04

Recognize unsafe or uncomfortable situations and describe ways to avoid them, assert boundaries, and seek help. Assess responses to stereotyping, discrimination, and bullying.

**Source locator:** [S1], Social and community health, avoidance/assertiveness and discrimination/stereotyping/bullying; safety and power-imbalance elaborations.

### CC-05

Develop and maintain positive relationships and contribute to safe, caring environments. The elaborations connect this work with respecting personal boundaries, obtaining affirmative consent, and recognizing others' right to refuse.

**Source locator:** [S1], Social and community health, relationships and safe/caring environments; consent elaborations.

### CC-06

Assess ways to support mental well-being and address problems involving mental health or substance use. Explore ways to manage puberty-related changes and identify influences that strengthen self-identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Mental well-being.

## Content

### C-01

Understand techniques for movements performed in place, travelling movements, and control of objects. Relate body and spatial awareness, effort, and relationships with others or objects to effective movement strategies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-locomotor/locomotor/manipulative skills and movement concepts/strategies.

### C-02

Know ways to gauge physical effort and participate in individual or paired activities, rhythmic movement, and games. Connect activity and exercise with physical and mental benefits.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, exertion monitoring, activity types, and benefits of physical activity/exercise.

### C-03

Understand how activity, sleep, eating, and illness-prevention practices contribute to health. Consider food amounts and dietary variety, and distinguish illnesses that can spread between people from those that cannot.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, health-promoting practices, food portions/servings, and communicable/non-communicable illnesses; food-guide elaboration.

### C-04

Examine the influence of media messages and social media on body image, choices, and self-identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, media messaging/body image and factors influencing self-identity.

### C-05

Know protective responses to hazards, abuse, bullying, discrimination, and violence, including recognizing manipulative approaches used by potential abusers. Understand potential effects of psychoactive substances and ways to reduce personal harm.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, hazardous/unsafe/abusive situations, lures or tricks, bullying/discrimination/violence, and psychoactive substances.

### C-06

Learn about the physical, emotional, and social changes associated with puberty, including developing sexual feelings and sexual identity, and the implications for self-understanding and relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, puberty, sexuality, and sexual identity; related elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The curriculum includes puberty, body image, substance-use prevention, personal safety, and mental well-being. These are not optional omissions from a sports-only summary. Use inclusive, developmentally appropriate explanations without asking learners to disclose personal health information, identity, or experiences of harm.

Consent and boundaries appear explicitly in the live elaborations. They include recognizing power imbalances and grooming, and understanding that boundaries can change and consent must continue. Safety education does not make a child responsible for another person's abusive behaviour.

The health-practice elaborations mention **60-90 minutes of daily moderate-to-vigorous activity** and **approximately 10-11 hours of sleep**. These are attributed details of this curriculum source, not a prescribed PHE class length or individualized medical advice. The core daily-participation expectation does not itself specify minutes.

Although the Content heading refers to food portions and servings, its elaboration says the newer Canada food guide no longer specifies daily serving counts and instead emphasizes varied foods. Do not turn that heading into a fixed serving-count requirement.

Activity elaborations include traditional First Peoples games among possible examples. They do not require a named game or authorize reproducing community practices. Full definitions and examples remain at [S1].
