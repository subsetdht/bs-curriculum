**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Written coverage:** All ten existing Grade 4 source-linked landings have source-checked core summaries. **Document language:** English (`en-CA`). **Alignment review date:** 2026-09-14.

These are original references to the provincial expectations, not official wording, lesson plans, an assessment checklist, or a prescribed timetable. The ten unique records support three program pathways, with shared records used where expectations genuinely overlap. All ten source entries were reviewed as core curricula; none is a course list requiring separate course folders.

## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

### English-language program

Use [English Language Arts 4](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) with the seven shared non-language-arts subjects in the [table below](#subjects-and-learning-standards): Mathematics, Science, Social Studies, Physical and Health Education, Arts Education, Career Education, and Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies. This pathway contains **eight records**.

The provincial second-language requirement begins in Grades 5-8, not Grade 4 ([P1], [P5]). Some districts offer earlier language instruction; that does not create a province-wide Grade 4 Core French requirement or make immersion language arts a substitute.

### Early French Immersion

Use both [French Immersion Language Arts 4](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) and [English Language Arts 4](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), together with the seven shared subjects. This pathway contains **nine records**.

English Language Arts is required for Early Immersion students **from Grade 4** under the provincial order and French Immersion policy ([P1], [P2]). Do not carry the Grade 1 exclusion forward. The two language-arts records are distinct curricula, not translations of each other.

The policy recommends **80% French and 20% English instruction** for the elementary period labelled "(3 or) 4-7." These are recommended overall allocations, not a subject-by-subject timetable. Shared curriculum expectations and English reference documents do not imply English delivery of the French-taught subjects.

### Francophone

Use [Francophone Language Arts 4](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts 4](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects. This pathway also contains **nine records**.

The provincial order explicitly includes Francophone students and education authorities in the common subject requirements [P1]. The province distinguishes the first-language Francophone program from immersion and describes French instruction except in English Language Arts [P3].

**Separate program-delivery evidence:** The Conseil scolaire francophone (CSF) states that it follows the provincial curriculum and provides first-language English to its students **from Grade 4** [P4]. That establishes this pathway's English Language Arts entry; it is not the immersion requirement being applied to Francophone students. CSF implementation and school scheduling remain separate from the provincial subject standards.

Late French Immersion begins at Grade 6 under provincial policy [P2]. There is no Grade 4 Late Immersion record or program pathway to populate.

## How to use these references

Select a program, then read a subject's Scope and use section. **Big Ideas** summarize enduring understandings, **Curricular Competencies** describe what learners do, and **Content** identifies knowledge and important limits. Follow the source locators for official wording and elaborations.

For a specific item, use a descriptive link such as [Mathematics 4: C-03](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-03). Its canonical repository reference is `bc-g04-mathematics#c-03`. These are local identifiers, not official BC codes or an assessment sequence. See the [linking convention](../../curriculum-format.md#linking-to-records-and-items).

No special materials or formal prerequisites are needed to read these references. The pathway counts describe ordinary curriculum coverage, not an individual learner's program. The provincial order contains specific exceptions for some learners unable to demonstrate the relevant outcomes who require an IEP or receive English-language-learning services ([P1], section 3); an IEP alone should not be treated as a blanket exemption.

## Learning across subjects

ADST and Career Education use **Grades 4-5** standards. ADST specifically combines that band's competencies with **Grade 4 content from other subjects**. Its fuller design cycle includes defining users' requirements, recording prototypes, and testing; it is not the K-3 process carried forward or a required list of technology modules.

The [BC curriculum model](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/overview) connects understanding, doing, and knowing. Cross-curricular competencies do not replace each subject's competencies, and a shared learning context does not erase either subject's expectations.

First Peoples knowledge and perspectives remain within the subjects in which they occur, including land and history, scientific inquiry, mathematics, arts, and language. Curriculum alignment does not grant permission to reproduce community-specific stories, designs, songs, or cultural knowledge.

## Coverage and limits

All **ten landings** now have **ten source-checked core summaries**. The program counts are eight English-language, nine Early Immersion, and nine Francophone records, reusing shared records rather than duplicating them. Eight primary curriculum sources are in English and two are in French.

No Grade 4 primary source or course inventory remains blocked in this pass. Source-checked means comparison with the primary source, not ministry endorsement or independent educator review. These are condensed references, not transcripts of all standards or elaborations. Unknown curriculum revision dates remain `null`; the review date is not a new edition.

French-language editions of the repository summaries remain unwritten. The existence of French official sources does not mean this collection supplies French classroom resources. All [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) categories remain unpopulated; no activities, assessments, or software tools are included in this pass.

School schedules, learning supports, local program availability, and instructional resources must be confirmed with the relevant authority. This overview separates the province's standards and policy from CSF's identified program-delivery commitment; it does not establish a district timetable.

## Sources and rights

The subject records link their primary sources and provide a locator for each summary item. All ten Grade 4 sources were read in full HTML, including embedded elaborations, on **2026-09-14**. The Career Education compilation's Grades 4-5 section was also inspected to establish its band.

- **P1:** [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order (PDF)][P1], Province of British Columbia. Sections 1-2 establish the shared subjects and Francophone inclusion; section 3 describes individual exceptions; sections 4-5 distinguish second-language provision and immersion English Language Arts.
- **P2:** [Provincial French Immersion policy][P2], program content, entry points, and Early Immersion language provision. Supports curriculum equivalence, Grade 4 English Language Arts, recommended language allocations, and Grade 6 Late Immersion entry.
- **P3:** [Provincial comparison of French education programs][P3], especially the Francophone and Immersion columns. Distinguishes program purpose and classroom language.
- **P4:** [CSF elementary program][P4], **Curriculum** and **Langues** sections, in French. The full HTML identifies provincial curriculum use and first-language English from Grade 4. This is explicitly a Francophone education authority's delivery source.
- **P5:** [Provincial Language Education Policy][P5], Grades 5-8 second-language requirement and local language offerings.

The five program sources above, the linked curriculum model, and the [BC copyright policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/copyright) were reviewed on **2026-09-14**. The original catalog links were captured on the same date, but catalog capture and alignment review are separate acts.

Curriculum source material remains subject to Crown copyright; the Open Government Licence is not assumed to cover it. This repository supplies original summaries and source links, not official documents, authorized translations, or copies of CSF material or cultural works.
