# Arts Education - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject requirements under [S2].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English. This does not determine classroom language; see the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Learning objective:** Make, interpret, and refine artistic work across dance, drama, music, and visual arts, connecting creative choices with identity, culture, and communication.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experiences of movement, listening, making, and responding offer entry points. This reference does not require specialist training or public-performance experience.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It does not supply artworks, scores, scripts, or a required equipment list.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], following the source locators for exact expectations and elaborations. This is one Grade 4 curriculum across four art forms, not a course-choice inventory. Local IDs are repository references, not official ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Creative expression allows people to consider who they are in relation to a community. Encountering others' work also opens access to different knowledge, values, and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity within a community and perspectives encountered through works of art.

### BI-02

Experimentation can reveal artistic possibilities that were not apparent at the outset.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artists' experimentation and new possibilities.

### BI-03

Dance, drama, music, and visual arts communicate through distinctive forms of expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the four arts as unique languages for creating and communicating.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Select and combine artistic elements, materials, movements, tools, technologies, and settings to suit a purpose. Create independently and with others, using imagination, questions, experimentation, and purposeful play.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, purposeful choices and individual/collaborative creation.

### CC-02

Investigate identity, belonging, place, and culture through the arts, including the relationships between artistic work and the societies in which it develops.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, identity/place/culture/belonging and relationships among cultures, societies, and arts.

### CC-03

Observe and question artists' choices, predict their effects, and refine one's own ideas and techniques. Reflect on the creative process and draw on other experiences and subject learning when planning, interpreting, and analysing work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting.

### CC-04

Adapt skills and ideas to new audiences or purposes, using symbolism and other creative choices to express meaning. Respond to artworks and artists' intentions, document and present creative work in different forms, and apply learning with increasing sophistication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting.

## Content

### C-01

In dance, relate body action, space, time, dynamic qualities, relationships, and form to movement principles such as balance and alignment. In drama, consider how character, time and place, and plot work with tension, mood, and focus.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance and drama elements; movement-principles elaboration.

### C-02

Understand musical patterns and duration in relation to a pulse, together with tempo, pitch, timbre, dynamics, form, and layered sound. For visual composition, work with line, shape, space, texture, colour, and form, using pattern/repetition, balance, contrast, emphasis, rhythm, and variety to create effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements and visual-art elements/principles of design; duration, timbre, and texture elaborations.

### C-03

Learn how processes and tools support art-making, including choreographic choices, different dramatic forms, and strategies for developing images. Use notation to represent artistic ideas, sounds, movement, and action, and explore how symbols and metaphors convey meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, processes/materials/technologies, choreographic devices, dramatic forms, notation, image development, and symbolism/metaphor.

### C-04

Encounter traditional and contemporary First Peoples arts and ways of making, alongside regional and national work from varied cultures, communities, historical periods, and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional/contemporary Aboriginal arts and arts-making processes, and regional/national works and traditions.

### C-05

Understand personal and collective responsibilities for physical and emotional safety when making, experiencing, or presenting art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, responsibility in a safe learning environment and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's elements and principles are explicitly non-exhaustive. Their elaborations clarify vocabulary and possible forms of work; they do not establish a required instrument, dance, play, artist list, or public performance. The curriculum spans all four art forms, not only visual art.

Grade 4 moves beyond simple exploration toward purposeful combinations, refinement, interpretation of artists' intentions, and adaptation to new contexts. Retain those competencies rather than treating the record as a collection of craft topics.

First Peoples arts belong within the subject's content. Access to a curriculum reference does not authorize copying designs, songs, dances, ceremonies, or other cultural expressions. Use the appropriate community's guidance and permissions; no cultural works are reproduced here.
