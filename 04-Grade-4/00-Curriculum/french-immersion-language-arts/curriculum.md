# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. The provincial Late Immersion pathway begins at Grade 6 [S3]; it is not a Grade 4 program. This is distinct from first-language Francophone language arts and Core French.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative curriculum is in French. This is an independent summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Understand and communicate increasingly nuanced meaning in French through inference, purposeful language choices, discussion, and structured writing.

**Prerequisites:** Prior French-language learning can provide a foundation. This reference does not set program admission or placement criteria.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It supplies no novels, poems, vocabulary drills, or teaching activities.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for official French terminology and elaborations. The Grade 4 content is situated in **young readers' novels and poetry**, alongside the broader text and communication skills described below. Use this record together with [English Language Arts 4](../english-language-arts/curriculum.md), which provincial policy also requires in Early Immersion at this grade [S3]. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

What people choose to say and how they say it reveal something of their identity. Texts also make visible the cultural diversity of the societies in which people communicate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), self-image through communication and cultural diversity in texts.

### BI-02

Meaning is not always stated directly: inference helps a reader notice subtleties, including the difference between a character's own identity and how others perceive that character.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), inference and characterization through others' perceptions.

### BI-03

Specific vocabulary and stylistic choices allow a communicator to create distinctive effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), stylistic tools and precise vocabulary.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Identify a text's central idea and details, form hypotheses from clues, and infer meaning that is not directly stated. Consider the roles played by characters in a narrative.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), main idea/details, text-based hypotheses, inference, and character roles.

### CC-02

Recognize cultural diversity within a society, including Indigenous cultures. Distinguish reference tools for definitions from those for translation and use them appropriately.

**Source locator:** [S1], Explore and reflect (English gloss), diverse cultural elements and definition/translation reference tools.

### CC-03

Contribute opinions while taking others' views into account, respond with spontaneous questions, and plan a message for its intended audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), discussion, questions in response to texts, and planning for an audience.

### CC-04

Read fluently with expressive intonation and appropriate links between spoken words. Shape communication through stylistic effects and organized paragraphs, applying the grammatical and lexical spelling conventions already studied in oral and written work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate (English gloss), fluent reading/liaisons, stylistic elements, structured paragraphs, and learned conventions.

## Content

### C-01

Use active listening, appropriate formal or informal address, and attention to another person's perspective as foundations for communication. Recognize cultural elements encountered through language and texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies and cultural elements; active-listening and forms-of-address elaborations.

### C-02

Understand how young readers' novels develop characters, settings, events, and chapters, and how poetry creates effects through features such as rhythm, rhyme, repetition, theme, and comparison.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: young readers' novels and poetry; introductory content-context instruction.

### C-03

Recognize the progression of a narrative and the organization and navigation features of an information text. Construct paragraphs whose sentences develop a main idea and connect through relationships such as time, space, or logic.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative schema, information-text structure/features, and paragraphs.

### C-04

Develop French questions using subject-verb inversion and interrogative pronouns. Expand expression through synonyms, antonyms, comparisons using adjectives, and adverbs formed from a regular feminine adjective with the ending **-ment**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: questions, word relationships, adverb formation, and comparative structures.

### C-05

Connect the genres being studied with their verb forms: the source specifies the **compound past of first-group verbs** and the **imperfect**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), verb moods and tenses associated with the genres; verb-form elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The novels-and-poetry focus does not remove information-text structure, oral communication, or visual texts from the curriculum. No particular published work or exhaustive vocabulary list is prescribed here.

The introduction [S2] connects grammar with text use and treats reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing as interrelated. Do not turn the grammatical topics into a free-standing replacement for communication or import a different grade's tense sequence.

The French source's cultural-diversity competency explicitly includes Indigenous cultures. Respect community-specific voices and permissions; no poems, stories, translations, or other cultural works are reproduced.

This is an additional-language immersion curriculum, not the [Francophone first-language curriculum](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md). English Language Arts is a companion subject at Grade 4, not a substitute for this record.
