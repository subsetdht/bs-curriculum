# Science - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Science

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject requirements under [S2].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English. Classroom-language distinctions appear in the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Learning objective:** Use observation, measurement, evidence, and explanation to investigate organisms' responses, matter and energy, and patterns caused by Earth and Moon motion.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with observing, comparing, and recording can help. This reference sets no formal prerequisite or equipment proficiency.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It does not provide experiments, equipment lists, or safety instructions for practical investigations.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the official standards and elaborations. Connect the inquiry competencies with the content rather than treating science as facts alone. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

An organism's relationship with its environment involves detecting conditions and responding to them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sensing and responding in living things.

### BI-02

Matter occupies space and has mass, even when its physical state changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, mass, space, and phase changes.

### BI-03

Energy can change from one form to another.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, energy transformation.

### BI-04

Patterns associated with Earth and Moon motion affect both organisms and their physical surroundings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, observable patterns from motions of Earth and the Moon.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Observe familiar phenomena with curiosity, identify questions that can be investigated scientifically, and use existing knowledge to make predictions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Questioning and predicting.

### CC-02

Suggest and conduct inquiries while considering ethical responsibilities. Use suitable tools safely, make formal measurements and use digital technology where appropriate, and collect simple data about the local environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Planning and conducting.

### CC-03

Interpret experiences of the local environment and recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives as sources of information. Organize and classify data, use tables or simple bar graphs to reveal patterns, and compare findings with predictions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Processing and analyzing data and information.

### CC-04

Draw simple inferences supported by observations and prior knowledge. Consider whether a test was fair, value evidence, and identify environmental implications of one's own and others' actions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Evaluating.

### CC-05

Apply learning beyond the original situation, develop or refine ideas with others, and contribute to care for people and shared surroundings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applying and innovating, care, co-operative design, transfer, and problem solving.

### CC-06

Communicate reasoning and findings in suitable forms, including diagrams and simple reports. Reflect on individual or shared experiences of place and the relationships these experiences reveal.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating, including the elaboration on place.

## Content

### C-01

Investigate how humans, other animals, and plants detect and respond to their surroundings. Recognize biomes as large regions with similar environmental conditions, including terrestrial and aquatic settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, sensing/responding and biomes; elaborations on responses and temperature/precipitation.

### C-02

Relate solid, liquid, and gas states to particle movement and temperature. Explore how heating and cooling can produce reversible physical changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phases of matter and effects of temperature on particle movement; physical-change elaboration.

### C-03

Recognize different forms of energy and the principle that energy is conserved rather than created or destroyed. Describe devices in terms of energy entering and leaving in different forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, forms/conservation of energy and devices that transform energy.

### C-04

Connect local daily and seasonal changes with Earth's axis, rotation, and orbit. Investigate effects of the relative positions of the Sun, Moon, and Earth, including Moon phases and tides, with local First Peoples perspectives on these relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, local changes from Earth's motion and relative Sun/Moon/Earth positions; associated astronomical and First Peoples elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The inquiry expectations include formal measurements, simple data displays, ethical decisions, and reflection on fair testing. Do not carry forward an assumption that all measurement is informal or non-standard from the early primary grades.

Temperature-driven phase changes are physical changes; they are not a claim that every change in matter is reversible. The source's energy examples illustrate different forms and transformations, not a requirement to build or handle each kind of device.

The astronomical elaborations also identify eclipses and ways seasons and tides affect organisms. Their examples and inquiry questions remain at [S1], not as a prescribed experiment sequence here.

First Peoples perspectives are part of both inquiry and astronomical content. Place-specific knowledge is not interchangeable between communities; access to the curriculum does not authorize reproducing teachings, stories, songs, or designs.
