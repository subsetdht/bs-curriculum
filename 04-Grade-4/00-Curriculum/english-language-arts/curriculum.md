# English Language Arts - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > English Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone, with the following distinct bases for inclusion. Provincial requirements add English Language Arts for immersion students from **Grade 4** ([S2], [S3]). The Conseil scolaire francophone (CSF) separately states that its students receive first-language English from **Grade 4** [S4]; that is the Francophone authority's delivery policy, not the immersion clause applied to another program.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and create varied texts thoughtfully, connecting language with identity, perspective, imagination, and informed participation in a community.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience reading, discussing, and composing texts offers useful connections. This reference imposes no commercial reading level or formal entry test.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It supplies no text selections, lessons, or assessment instruments.

**How to use:** Read [S1] with its elaborations. The ministry describes a common K-9 English Language Arts curriculum [S5], but the standards summarized here are **Grade 4-specific**. This record complements, rather than replaces, [French Immersion Language Arts](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) or [Francophone Language Arts](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) in the respective pathways. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Enjoyment and experimentation with words and texts can deepen both creativity and understanding of how language works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity/joy and creative, playful use of language.

### BI-02

Engaging with stories and other texts connects personal experience with the wider world, while allowing different readers to find different meanings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, connections through texts and different perspectives.

### BI-03

Questioning messages, rather than accepting them uncritically, supports informed citizenship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, questioning what is heard, read, and viewed.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Bring together information from several sources and prior knowledge. Use comprehension and thinking strategies before, during, and after reading, listening, or viewing, checking and deepening understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, accessing/integrating information, comprehension strategies, and thinking skills.

### CC-02

Consider a text's purpose, audience, context, perspective, and voice. Explain how organization and literary choices contribute to meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, purposes/audiences/perspectives, context and voice, literary elements/devices, and organization.

### CC-03

Recognize language's contribution to personal, social, and cultural identity. Connect texts with experience and knowledge, and develop personal or creative responses supported by reasons.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, language and identity, personal connections, and responses to text; response elaboration.

### CC-04

Recognize the place of oral tradition and the varied purposes of texts in First Peoples cultures, including how stories connect people with land.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples oral tradition, purposes of texts, and story/land relationships.

### CC-05

Develop shared understanding through respectful exchanges of ideas. Use writing and design processes, creative language choices, and oral storytelling to shape original communications for particular purposes and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchange of perspectives, writing/design processes, language play, storytelling, and transformation of ideas into original texts.

### CC-06

Communicate through sentences and paragraphs with the relevant Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation conventions. Expand word knowledge, including understanding meaningful word parts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, sentences/paragraphs and expanding word knowledge; morphology elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Understand relationships among a text's form, purpose, genre, and visible features. Recognize evidence as part of interpreting and supporting ideas about texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text: forms/functions/genres, text features, and evidence.

### C-02

Learn how characters, setting, plot, conflict, theme, and purpose contribute to a story, and how sensory detail and figurative language create effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text: literary elements and literary devices, with their elaborations.

### C-03

Use reading and oral-language strategies together with awareness of one's own learning. Understand writing as a process of developing, revisiting, and improving communication for a reader.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes: reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing processes.

### C-04

Consider how voice and gesture affect spoken messages. Organize written paragraphs around a central idea and supporting detail, with attention to sentence structure, parts of speech, tense, and subject-verb agreement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: oral features, paragraphs, sentence structure/grammar.

### C-05

Develop the written conventions that clarify meaning, including uses of commas, quotation marks for dialogue, and apostrophes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: conventions elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Text includes oral, written, visual, and digital communication, including combinations of those forms. The curriculum does not prescribe a novel list, a commercial literacy level, or a single correct format for responding.

The source introduction [S5] situates grammar and literary terminology within meaningful language use, rather than isolated terminology exercises. Its K-9 description does not make this a K-9-banded record; use the Grade 4 expectations and elaborations in [S1].

First Peoples oral traditions carry knowledge, histories, responsibilities, and relationships with land. They are not generic stories available for unrestricted reuse. The oral-storytelling elaboration explicitly calls for permission when using an existing story, and [S5] discusses authentic voices and community protocols.

Do not import the Grade 1 immersion exclusion into Grade 4. Both language-arts curricula appear in the Early Immersion pathway here. Francophone inclusion of English at Grade 4 is supported separately by CSF's policy; local timetables are not established by this record.
