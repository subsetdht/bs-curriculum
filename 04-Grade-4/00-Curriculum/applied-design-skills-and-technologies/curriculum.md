# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 4

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 4](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared subject requirements under [S2]. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for language-of-instruction distinctions.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed curriculum source is in English. This reference does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Develop a design from an identified need through planning, prototypes, feedback, improvement, and communication, selecting suitable skills and technologies.

**Prerequisites:** Experience exploring materials and explaining design choices can be helpful. This reference sets no formal entry requirement or prior coding requirement.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. It does not prescribe a device, software product, or making kit.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], including its elaborations. The Grade 4 page explicitly directs learners to combine **Grades 4-5 ADST competencies with grade-level content from other learning areas**. Map both sides of that relationship when citing an integrated learning opportunity. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

A design develops through evidence from successive trials rather than being finished at the first idea. Practice and sustained effort also improve the skills needed to realize it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, prototyping/testing and development of skills through practice, effort, and action.

### BI-02

Choosing tools is part of designing: the task determines which technologies will be useful.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, choice of technology and tools in relation to the task.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate who or what a design will serve. Turn information from potential users into a design opportunity, an objective, essential features, and practical or ethical limits.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Understanding context and Defining; elaborations on users and constraints.

### CC-02

Develop possible solutions with others, compare them against the objective and constraints, and choose a direction. Plan the work and its tools and materials, then build an initial version while recording how the prototype changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Use testing and peer responses to decide what needs improvement. Repeat the trial-and-revision process as needed, then make a final version that incorporates the planned changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Choose an audience and way to share the outcome, explaining both the product and the process. Judge how well it meets its purpose and benefits its users or surroundings; reflect on individual and group work, care of the shared workspace, and further design problems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Design, Sharing: demonstration, objective, contribution, reflection, co-operative workspace, and new issues.

### CC-05

Work safely for oneself and others in physical and digital settings. Identify skills that need development, use familiar technologies productively, and learn unfamiliar tools when a task calls for them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Applied Skills and Applied Technologies.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The integrated-content arrangement is source-checked for this grade, not inherited from the K-3 record. The Grades 4-5 process explicitly includes investigating users, defining constraints, planning, recording prototype iterations, and repeated testing. Do not replace it with the younger band's simpler process or add later-grade ADST modules.

A designed outcome need not be a physical object: the product elaboration also allows processes, systems, services, and environments. Likewise, technology is broader than digital equipment. These definitions are not a requirement to produce every kind of outcome.

The constraints elaboration includes environmental impact, expense, cultural appropriation, and sacred knowledge. Design work does not confer permission to use community-specific knowledge or cultural designs. Full elaborations remain at [S1]; this record supplies neither projects nor cultural works.
