# Physical and Health Education - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Physical and Health Education](../README.md) > Physical and Health Education 10

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations supported by the immersion and graduation provisions [S2], [S3].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed primary source is English. This does not set classroom language or provide French-language learning materials.

**Learning objective:** Develop movement competence, informed health decisions, and the personal and interpersonal understanding that supports active living and well-being.

**Prerequisites:** Previous experience with movement, health concepts, and respectful participation is useful; this reference does not impose an athletic ability or formal entry threshold.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Physical participation and emergency-care learning require appropriate instruction and arrangements, not equipment or procedures supplied by this summary.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for the complete standards and elaborations. Use the four competency strands together rather than treating physical education as the whole course. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or an assessment checklist.

This is the sole course in the official Grade 10 PHE list and a 4-credit Dogwood graduation requirement [S3]. Personal circumstances, accessibility, and delivery arrangements belong with the school; this record does not prescribe a local timetable.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Knowing one's preferences and current capabilities helps make goals purposeful. Experience with different forms of activity can help identify sustainable ways to remain active.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, self-understanding and goals, and variety of physical activities for lifelong participation.

### BI-02

Health-related choices and physical, emotional, and mental well-being affect one another. Regular activity provides an ongoing means of maintaining or developing fitness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, healthy choices and well-being, and regular participation for personal fitness.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Physical literacy:** Refine movement and tactical choices across activities and environments, adjusting exertion appropriately. Participate safely and fairly, demonstrate leadership, and examine how personal preferences and motivation affect participation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Physical literacy: movement, strategies, exertion, safety/fair play/leadership, preferences, and motivation.

### CC-02

**Healthy and active living:** Participate in activity that supports fitness and connect growing competence with confidence and continued participation. Address barriers, question health messages, and use reflection on health decisions and goal outcomes to improve personal strategies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Healthy and active living: fitness, confidence, barriers, health messages, goals, and decisions.

### CC-03

**Social and community health:** Develop responses to unsafe or exploitative situations, discrimination, stereotyping, and bullying. Build communication and conflict-management skills for healthy relationships, including respect for consent and boundaries, and examine social influences on health.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social and community health and elaborations on safety, consent, healthy relationships, discrimination, and social influences.

### CC-04

**Mental well-being:** Evaluate ways to support mental health and manage the physical, emotional, and social changes of adolescence. Investigate influences on substance use and personal identity, and explain connections among activity, mental well-being, and overall health.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Mental well-being: strategies, substance-use factors, adolescent changes, identities, and activity-health relationships.

## Content

### C-01

Develop knowledge of movement technique, movement concepts and strategies, and ways to monitor physical effort. Connect these with health benefits and a range of individual, shared, rhythmic, game-based, and outdoor activity settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, movement skills/concepts/strategies, exertion monitoring, health benefits, and activity categories.

### C-02

Understand how training choices relate to intended fitness changes. The source specifies FITT, SAID, and specificity: the frequency, intensity, type, and duration of activity, and the body's adaptation to the demands placed on it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, training principles including FITT, SAID, and specificity.

### C-03

Examine the immediate and longer-term consequences of decisions about activity, food, sleep, and technology, using an understanding of health-information sources. Connect goal-setting and self-motivation with maintaining health-related intentions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, consequences of health decisions, sources of health information, and strategies for goal-setting and self-motivation.

### C-04

Understand healthy sexual decision-making and ways to protect oneself and others from abuse, exploitation, and harm. Consent, communication, boundaries, power differences, and access to help are important parts of the source's elaborations; responsibility for abuse does not lie with the survivor.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, healthy sexual decision making and protection from abuse/exploitation/harm; related Social and community health elaborations on consent and survivors.

### C-05

Learn the basic principles of responding to emergencies, including CPR and AED learning. The official elaborations address recognizing an emergency, obtaining help, roles in the response, and development of practical skills.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, basic principles for responding to emergencies, including Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation and Automated External Defibrillator learning.

### C-06

Investigate how bullying, stereotypes, and discrimination affect people, and examine the physical, emotional, and social dimensions of psychoactive substance use and related behaviours.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, consequences of bullying/stereotyping/discrimination and aspects of psychoactive substance use.

### C-07

Recognize the source's signs and symptoms of stress, anxiety, and depression, and examine how adolescent physical, emotional, and social changes can affect identity and relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, signs and symptoms of stress/anxiety/depression and influences of changes on identities and relationships.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This reference describes expectations; it is not medical advice, a diagnostic tool, a CPR/AED training course, or certification. It supplies no emergency-care procedure. The official curriculum and appropriately qualified instruction remain necessary.

The [alternative-delivery policy][S4] permits school-agreed alternative instruction for specified reproduction and sexuality topics. It does **not** remove those learning standards and is not a general opt-out from PHE or other curriculum.

Named sports, games, and techniques in elaborations are examples, not a compulsory sports list. Where traditional Indigenous games are considered, their inclusion as an example does not establish permission to reproduce community cultural material.
