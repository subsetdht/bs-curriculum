# Études littéraires et artistiques + nouveaux médias - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Francophone Language Arts](../README.md) > Literary and artistic studies + new media

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone only; not French Immersion, Core French, or English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English; official title and source language: French. This is an original synthesis, not an authorized translation or French-language instructional material. Locators below are English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Interpret literature, art, and new media in Francophone contexts while developing responsible multimodal communication.

**Prerequisites:** Experience interpreting and producing French texts is useful preparation, not a formal admission rule.

**Materials:** The reference and linked sources; no particular media platform, account, or software is prescribed.

**How to use:** Read the grouped expectations with [S1] and its elaborations. Local IDs are not ministry codes. [S2] describes one **4-credit combined course**; the three Francophone options are alternatives, not three universal requirements.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Reflection and conversation about texts broaden understanding, while careful language supports creative and critical thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), reflection/exchanges and linguistic precision.

### BI-02

Form influences a work's meaning and aesthetic qualities. Literary and artistic works express perspectives on Francophone culture and history within particular times and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), textual form, Francophone culture/history, and time/space.

### BI-03

Digital citizenship requires awareness of how new media affect oneself, other people, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), digital citizenship and new-media impacts.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Explore and reflect:** Compare texts and connect their themes and cultural references. Identify central questions and interpret messages in light of diverse experiences and contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: themes/cultural markers, comparative study, textual problem, and diversity/context.

### CC-02

**Explore and reflect:** Appreciate Indigenous storytelling and oral and artistic traditions as expressions of perspectives, values, and beliefs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: Indigenous story, oral tradition, and artistic tradition.

### CC-03

**Explore and reflect:** Apply digital-citizenship principles, examine relationships between language and image, and explore symbolism and audience effects. Evaluate information's relevance and validity before adopting a position.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: digital citizenship, language/image, symbolism, language/structure/style effects, and information evaluation.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Plan and organize messages, summarize texts, incorporate different perspectives, and support arguments with evidence. Adapt formats to the communication situation and participants' abilities, including visual representation through new media.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: processes, presentation adaptation, visual representation, summary, perspectives, and arguments.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Respond personally, creatively, and critically; produce texts for specific audiences and purposes using stylistic choices and appropriate French conventions. Communicate ethically.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: responses, grammar/syntax/punctuation, stylistic production, and ethics.

## Content

### C-01

Know how genre, textual structure, graphical organization, and source acknowledgement support meaning and attribution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Textual organization.

### C-02

Understand rhetoric, style, and literary-analysis concepts, together with permission protocols for Indigenous stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Literary tools.

### C-03

Know strategies for taking a position, composing, recognizing implied and explicit messages, understanding the communication process, remembering information, and acknowledging sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Strategies.

### C-04

Study connotation and denotation, registers, discourse types, syntax, vocabulary, and the distinctive language of digital communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Language tools, including digital language.

### C-05

Understand imagery, context, relationships between images and statements, visual effects, and the traces people leave through digital activity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Enrichment tools: figurative language, image/message, context, digital footprint, and visual role.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This combined course retains literary and artistic study alongside multimodal, interactive, and digital texts. [S1]'s introductory scope sentence contains duplicated wording about written production; [S2]'s description and the specific new-media standards clarify the intended emphasis. This summary does not interpret that duplication as another course.

The variety elaboration refers to works from at least two different periods, places, and genres. Genre and artistic-movement examples do not prescribe individual works or platforms. Indigenous knowledge and cultural works require their holders' permission; public online availability is not a licence to reuse them.
