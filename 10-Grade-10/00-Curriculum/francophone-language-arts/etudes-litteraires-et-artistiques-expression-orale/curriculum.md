# Études littéraires et artistiques + expression orale - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Francophone Language Arts](../README.md) > Literary and artistic studies + oral expression

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone only; first-language French curriculum, distinct from French Immersion, Core French, and English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English; the official source and course title are French. This original synthesis is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Deepen interpretation of Francophone literature and art while developing persuasive, responsive oral expression.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading, discussing, and composing in French is helpful, not an official course-entry criterion.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; particular works require independent rights, accessibility, and age-suitability review.

**How to use:** Compare these thematic groups with the French standards and elaborations. Local IDs are not official BC codes. [S2] identifies this as one **4-credit combined course**, not two separate required subjects. See [program and choice guidance](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Discussion and reflection on texts can expand understanding of people and the world; precision in language supports both critical and creative thought.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), reflection/exchanges and linguistic precision.

### BI-02

A work's form contributes to its meaning and aesthetic effect. Literature and art offer an author's perspective on Francophone culture and history, situated in time and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), textual form, worldwide Francophone culture/history, and time/space.

### BI-03

The way a voice is used can give persuasion its force.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), persuasion and the power of voice.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Explore and reflect:** Connect themes and cultural references across Francophone texts, identify their central questions, and interpret them in light of diverse experiences and contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: themes/cultural markers, textual problem, and diversity/context.

### CC-02

**Explore and reflect:** Appreciate how Indigenous storytelling, oral traditions, and artistic expression communicate perspectives, values, and beliefs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: Indigenous story, oral tradition, and artistic tradition.

### CC-03

**Explore and reflect:** Investigate symbolism, rhetorical choices, and the effects of language, structure, technique, and style on an audience. Recognize prejudice and bias, and evaluate information before adopting a position.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: bias, art of discourse, symbolism, audience effects, and relevance/validity.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Organize ideas through writing and design processes, summarize messages, consider different perspectives, and support arguments with appropriate evidence and references.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: processes, summary, perspectives, and supported arguments.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Produce and respond to texts with personal, creative, and critical engagement. Use stylistic choices and French language conventions for the purpose and context, maintaining ethical communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: grammar/syntax/punctuation, stylistic effects, ethics, responses, and production.

## Content

### C-01

Know how textual structure and genre organize meaning, and how attribution and references identify others' contributions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Textual organization: structure/genre and attribution/sources.

### C-02

Study rhetoric, stylistic and narrative techniques, and concepts for literary analysis. Understand permission protocols for using Indigenous stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Literary tools: rhetoric, style, analysis, narrative techniques, and Indigenous-story permissions.

### C-03

Know strategies for taking a position, recognizing explicit and implied messages, organizing communication, and remembering material. Understand how presentation, delivery, and theatrical presence contribute to oral expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Strategies: position, writing techniques, implicit/explicit, communication model, memorization, presentation, discourse, theatrical presence, and sources.

### C-04

Understand connotation and denotation, registers, discourse types, French syntax, and vocabulary as tools for adapting a message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Language tools.

### C-05

Understand the contribution of imagery, social and cultural contexts, and visual support to richer expression and interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Enrichment tools: figurative language, contexts, and visual support.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Literary and artistic study remains part of this combined course; an oral emphasis does not reduce it to public speaking alone. [S1] frames learning through varied genres and artistic movements. Its variety elaboration refers to works from at least two different periods, places, and genres; named movements and genres are elaboration guidance, not a fixed reading list here.

The curriculum involves oral and written, digital and print, textual and visual material. Indigenous traditions are integral, not enrichment detached from the subject. Their inclusion does not authorize reproducing or performing community stories. The official French source remains authoritative for terminology and limits.
