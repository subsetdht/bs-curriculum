# Grade 10 curriculum

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > 10

**Browse:** [Programs](#choose-a-program) | [Area inventory](#subjects-and-learning-standards) | [Written course sections](#written-course-sections) | [Requirements and language](#program-and-course-requirements) | [Coverage limits](#coverage-and-limits)

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Document language:** English (`en-CA`). **Source languages:** 54 English (`en`) and 4 French (`fr`) primary sources. **Source review date:** 2026-09-14.

These are original, condensed curriculum references, not official wording, lesson activities, assessments, or a graduation plan. Each record distinguishes Big Ideas, Curricular Competencies, and Content and links to the complete primary source and elaborations.

**Coverage:** **58 source-checked records**: all **57 distinct curriculum sources** behind the ten ordinary Grade 10 catalog landings, plus **1 shared Career-Life Education record**. CLE uses Grade 10 as its administrative registry host, with flexible Grades 10-12 delivery. All course inventories and listed-source summaries are complete; no primary-course access blockers remain.

The 58 records are original core summaries, not a claim to reproduce every elaboration or inventory every locally authorized course. Seven included Arts names have alias landings pointing to their two shared music-family records; CLE's six ordinary reporting codes also share one record. Neither set of reporting names inflates the record count.

Program applicability concerns the provincial expectations represented by a record. It does not mean every listed course is required, available in every school, or delivered in the language of this summary.

The program counts below overlap because shared records serve multiple pathways. They count available references, **not a required number of enrolments**.

### English-language program

**54 records.** Start with the [nine English/English First Peoples choices](english-language-arts/README.md), then the shared subject and course areas below, including [Languages](languages/README.md) and [Career Education](career-education/README.md). Core French is included here as an additional-language choice, not a compulsory Grade 10 language or a replacement for English Language Arts.

### Early French Immersion

**54 records.** Use [Français langue seconde - immersion 10](french-immersion-language-arts/francais-langue-seconde-immersion/curriculum.md) with the separate [English Language Arts choices](english-language-arts/README.md), shared non-language courses, [CLE](career-education/career-life-education/curriculum.md), and the eight other [additional-language electives](languages/README.md). Core French is not mapped as the immersion language-arts component.

### Late French Immersion

**54 records.** The Grade 10 pathway contains the same source-checked course references as Early Immersion, including the distinct [immersion French](french-immersion-language-arts/francais-langue-seconde-immersion/curriculum.md) and [English language-arts](english-language-arts/README.md) components. This mapping uses the policy's specific Late Immersion secondary provisions, not an assumption that the earlier transition curricula or entry points are interchangeable.

### Francophone

**56 records.** Begin with the [three first-language French combined-course alternatives](francophone-language-arts/README.md) and the separate [English Language Arts component](english-language-arts/README.md). Shared non-language subjects, [CLE](career-education/career-life-education/curriculum.md), and eight other [additional-language electives](languages/README.md) are also mapped. Neither immersion French nor Core French is substituted for first-language French. Program eligibility and instruction language are not inferred from an English source edition.

## Program and course requirements

The [provincial graduation requirements][graduation] distinguish required credits from alternatives and electives. They require Language Arts 10, Mathematics 10, Science 10, Social Studies 10, and Physical and Health Education 10 credits. The Arts Education/ADST requirement is **4 credits across Grades 10, 11, or 12**, not a requirement to take every Grade 10 arts or technology course. The full Dogwood program also has senior-course, career, Indigenous-focused, elective, and provincial assessment requirements; this grade inventory is not a substitute for that policy.

All nine English/English First Peoples 10 catalog choices are **2-credit courses**, not nine standalone 4-credit requirements. The three Francophone combinations and the one immersion French course are **4-credit courses**. The language-arts landings cite their official course descriptions and policy evidence; retain their course-group and program distinctions when planning.

The [French Immersion policy][immersion-policy] requires a program parallel in structure and content to the regular curriculum. It separately describes Early and Late Immersion and directs Late Immersion Grades 8-12 to the secondary immersion provisions. English Language Arts is a component of secondary immersion, while French Immersion Language Arts serves a different language-learning purpose.

The [2026-27 Graduation Program Policy Guide][policy-guide], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that both immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content. This is the shared-content evidence used with actual course sources, not an inference from translated files or the K-9 Required Areas Order.

The [provincial program comparison][program-comparison] separately distinguishes Francophone education from immersion and Core French. Francophone subject areas are taught in French except English Language Arts. The policy guide's printed pp. 15-17 and the [graduation summary][graduation] set out the different French Immersion and Francophone dual-Dogwood requirements, including distinct French language-arts courses and French-delivered credits. None of these program mappings guarantees that every elective is offered locally.

All repository summaries in this pass are English `en-CA`. A course's target language, the language of its authoritative source, the summary language, and classroom instruction are different facts. School timetables, course offerings, resource choices, program entry, and any SD43-specific arrangements must be established separately with the relevant district; they are not provincial standards added by this collection.

## Other catalog entries

[CLE, CLC, and Additional Offerings](../../curriculum-special-entries.md) have cross-grade or named catalog entry points. The policy guide explicitly states that **CLE and CLC have no designated grade levels** (printed p. 21); students may begin this learning in Grades 10, 11, or 12.

The [ordinary CLE registry entry](https://www.bced.gov.bc.ca/datacollections/course_registry_web_search/run-details.php?courseCode=3201860) identifies administrative Grade 10, supporting the single [shared CLE record](career-education/career-life-education/curriculum.md) hosted here. The [reporting options](career-education/career-life-education/README.md#reporting-options) distinguish the complete 4-credit course from two 2-credit parts and exclude adult-only CLE 12. None of these administrative facts makes Grade 10 the required delivery year.

[CLC](../../curriculum-special-entries.md#career-life-connections) has its canonical registry host coordinated in Grade 12 rather than a duplicate record here; the shared navigation connects its flexible Grades 10-12 delivery.

The coordinated source review assigns **no further direct Additional Offerings curriculum to this Grade 10 pass**. The [central Additional Offerings inventory](../../curriculum-special-entries.md#additional-offerings) tracks the direct offerings at their published Grade 11 or 12 hosts and labels related credit schemes separately. Eligibility during Grade 10 does not turn those offerings into additional generic Grade 10 curricula or hidden requirements. BC Additional Offerings is not synonymous with this repository's unmapped enrichment category.

## How to use and maintain

Read a record's Scope and use before following its source locators. For an item citation, use a descriptive link such as [Social Studies 10: C-06](social-studies/curriculum.md#c-06), whose canonical repository key is `bc-g10-social-studies#c-06`. These IDs are not official BC standard codes.

The [format guide](../../curriculum-format.md) and [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page) govern updates. Preserve established landing URLs and record/item IDs. Update course landings, area inventories, this overview, and central written-summary navigation together when coverage changes.

## Coverage and limits

Records provide core summaries rather than transcripts of every standard and elaboration. Source review dates identify actual comparison work, not an invented curriculum revision or a guarantee of future accuracy. Source documents remain authoritative.

All 56 course-list sources, the Social Studies core source, and shared CLE have source-checked records. The [Francophone new-media record](francophone-language-arts/etudes-litteraires-et-artistiques-nouveaux-medias/curriculum.md#elaborations-and-boundaries) documents duplicated wording in its source introduction and how its official DOCX and course-specific standards resolve the emphasis. This is a source-text caveat, not an omitted course.

First Peoples perspectives remain within their curriculum subjects. Curriculum alignment does not grant permission to reproduce community-specific knowledge, stories, designs, or other cultural works.

French-language editions, learning activities, assessments, classroom resources, and tools are outside this pass. The neighboring [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) categories remain empty.
