# Foundations of Mathematics and Pre-calculus - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Mathematics](../README.md) > Foundations of Mathematics and Pre-calculus

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial mathematics expectations. The immersion policy and provincial graduation requirements support these pathways [S2], [S3]. Program applicability does not guarantee that a school offers this course.

**Document language:** English; the primary curriculum reviewed is in English. Neither language field prescribes classroom instruction or identifies a French-language learning resource.

**Learning objective:** Connect algebraic structure, linear models, and right-triangle reasoning to the interpretation and solution of mathematical and contextual problems.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with rational numbers, algebraic expressions, equations, graphs, and proportional reasoning is useful prior experience, not an entry requirement established by this reference.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this summary. The source discusses mathematical tools and technology without making this page a classroom equipment list.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and its elaborations. The local IDs identify this repository's grouped summaries, not official BC standard codes, a teaching order, or assessment levels.

This is one of the two courses in the [published Mathematics 10 inventory](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/mathematics/10/courses), not a generic record covering both. The Dogwood program requires Mathematics 10 credits [S3]; it does not require every listed mathematics option. Consult current course and admission guidance rather than assuming the two options have identical later pathways.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Algebra makes it possible to investigate a relationship beyond a single numerical example. Familiar operations acquire further meaning when applied to powers and polynomial expressions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, algebraic generalization and operations on powers and polynomials.

### BI-02

A steady rate of change connects the different ways a linear relationship can be expressed. Moving between a situation and its representations can reveal relationships that are otherwise difficult to notice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, constant rate of change in linear relations and representing and analysing situations.

### BI-03

Right-triangle ratios connect relative side lengths and angles, making some otherwise inaccessible measurements calculable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, proportional reasoning and indirect measurement in trigonometry.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Reasoning and modelling:** Investigate mathematical structure, develop and test strategies, and make defensible estimates. Select reasoning, technology, and other tools to turn a situation into a mathematical model, remaining open to different approaches and further questions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and modelling: strategies, mathematical ideas, estimation, contextual modelling, and creative thinking.

### CC-02

**Understanding and solving:** Build understanding through inquiry and visualization, choose suitable solution approaches, and persevere while testing whether a result fits its context. Connect problems with place, stories, cultural practices, and the perspectives of local First Peoples and other communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving: inquiry, visualization, strategic approaches, persistence, and community-connected experiences.

### CC-03

**Communicating and representing:** Make reasoning available to others through appropriate models, diagrams, symbols, and language. Explain and defend mathematical decisions, contribute to discussion, and offer tentative ideas as part of developing understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing: justification, representations, mathematical vocabulary, and classroom discourse.

### CC-04

**Connecting and reflecting:** Compare strategies and results, use errors to identify what needs reconsideration, and connect mathematics with other interests and disciplines. Make respectful mathematical connections with First Peoples knowledge, practices, perspectives, and worldviews.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting: reflection, connections, learning from mistakes, and First Peoples knowledge and practices.

## Content

### C-01

Use prime factorization to examine numerical structure, and extend calculations with powers to integral exponents, including negative exponents. The elaborations connect factors with greatest common factors and least common multiples.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, prime factorization and operations on powers with integral exponents; related factor and exponent elaborations.

### C-02

Interpret the relationship between data, graphs, and the situations they describe. Distinguish functions from other relations and work with domain, range, and function notation as developed in the source elaborations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, functions and relations: connecting data, graphs, and situations.

### C-03

Study linear relationships through slope and equations, connecting their graphical, tabular, and algebraic forms. Recognize arithmetic sequences as another expression of constant change and solve systems of linear equations while interpreting what a solution means.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, linear functions, arithmetic sequences, and systems of linear equations.

### C-04

Extend multiplication to polynomial expressions and investigate how factoring reverses their construction. Source elaborations include common-factor extraction, simpler trinomial cases, and differences of squares rather than an unrestricted inventory of factoring techniques.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multiplication of polynomial expressions and polynomial factoring, with factoring elaborations.

### C-05

Use the primary trigonometric ratios to reason about right triangles and determine unknown measurements. The elaborations connect sine, cosine, and tangent with the Pythagorean theorem and direct or indirect measurement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, primary trigonometric ratios and right-triangle elaborations.

### C-06

Distinguish income before deductions from the amount received after deductions, connecting different forms of earnings with income tax and other reductions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: gross and net pay.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's four competency strands apply across the course rather than forming four separate units. Examples in elaborations are not an additional prescribed activity list. In particular, arithmetic **series** appear as an extension; the listed Content expectation is arithmetic **sequences**.

Right-triangle trigonometry here does not establish a requirement for every later trigonometric topic. Likewise, the source's specified factoring scope should not be expanded into a complete advanced algebra syllabus.

First Peoples mathematical connections require locally appropriate, shareable knowledge and respectful relationships. A curriculum citation does not authorize reuse of cultural material. This record does not establish school timetables, post-secondary admission eligibility, or a required choice between Mathematics 10 options.
