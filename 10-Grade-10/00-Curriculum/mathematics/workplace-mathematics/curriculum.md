# Workplace Mathematics - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Mathematics](../README.md) > Workplace Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial mathematics expectations, supported by the immersion and graduation provisions [S2], [S3]. Local offerings must be confirmed separately.

**Document language:** English; source reviewed in English. These metadata do not prescribe classroom language or supply French learning resources.

**Learning objective:** Use number, proportional reasoning, measurement, data, and probability to interpret practical situations and make reasoned decisions.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with fractions, decimals, proportional comparisons, measurement, and simple graphs is helpful. This reference does not impose formal course-entry criteria.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to consult the reference. The source's use of measuring and mathematical tools is not a requirement to obtain a particular product.

**How to use:** Compare these grouped summaries with [S1], following the locators for exact expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a prescribed sequence.

Workplace Mathematics is one of the two [published Mathematics 10 options](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/mathematics/10/courses). The provincial requirement is for Mathematics 10 credits [S3], not completion of both options. This summary neither prescribes a student's pathway nor establishes post-secondary admission eligibility.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Multiplicative comparisons describe scale rather than simply how much two quantities differ. Flexible use of numbers makes such comparisons and other practical calculations more meaningful.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, proportional reasoning in multiplicative relationships and flexibility with number.

### BI-02

Direct measurements and calculations can be combined to investigate the dimensions, covering, and capacity of three-dimensional objects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, direct and indirect measurement of length, surface area, and volume.

### BI-03

Organizing and examining data helps reveal possible relationships and raises questions about what the information means.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, representing and analysing data.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Reasoning and modelling:** Develop strategies, investigate connections, and make estimates that can be defended. Choose reasoning, technology, and tools to model practical situations while considering alternative approaches and new questions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and modelling: thinking strategies, mathematical ideas, estimation, modelling, and curiosity.

### CC-02

**Understanding and solving:** Build concepts through inquiry and visualization, select workable strategies, and persist in resolving problems. Connect mathematical work with place, story, cultural practices, and local First Peoples and other community perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving: conceptual understanding, visualization, approaches, persistence, and community-connected problem solving.

### CC-03

**Communicating and representing:** Show relationships through concrete, pictorial, and symbolic forms and explain why a decision or solution is reasonable. Use mathematical vocabulary in discussion and contribute ideas even while understanding is still developing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing: explanation, representations, vocabulary, and mathematical discourse.

### CC-04

**Connecting and reflecting:** Revisit strategies and learn from errors, connecting ideas across mathematics, everyday contexts, and personal interests. Draw respectful connections with First Peoples worldviews, knowledge, and practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting: mathematical reflection, interdisciplinary connections, mistakes, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Produce and interpret graphical displays, considering whether a graph presents its data appropriately. Use measures of centre and range to analyse a data set and consider how unusual values affect its interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, creating/interpreting/critiquing graphs and central tendency; elaborations on graph forms, mean, median, mode, range, and outliers.

### C-02

Work within and between metric and imperial measurement systems, selecting suitable tools and units. Connect calculations of surface area and volume to three-dimensional objects, including the source's prism and cylinder contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, metric and imperial measurement and conversions, and surface area and volume.

### C-03

Apply sine, cosine, and tangent to measurements in single right triangles. This is the course's specified trigonometric setting, not a general treatment of every triangle.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, primary trigonometric ratios and the single-right-triangle elaboration.

### C-04

Use experimental results to investigate probability, examining the relationship with theoretical expectations where that comparison is possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, experimental probability and its elaboration on simulations and theoretical probability.

### C-05

Understand how different kinds of earnings become take-home pay after income tax and other deductions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: gross and net pay.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The four competency strands run through all content. Broad mathematical examples within competency elaborations do not automatically add the other Mathematics 10 course's algebra syllabus to this one. Conversely, Workplace Mathematics includes substantive reasoning and communication, not just workplace calculation procedures.

The source emphasizes length in measurement conversions and names single right triangles for trigonometry. Graph formats and simulations in elaborations illustrate the listed expectations; this summary does not prescribe particular games, occupations, or teaching materials.

Use local First Peoples knowledge only in appropriate contexts and with required permissions. The record does not license cultural material or establish a school-specific sequence, prerequisite, or admission promise.
