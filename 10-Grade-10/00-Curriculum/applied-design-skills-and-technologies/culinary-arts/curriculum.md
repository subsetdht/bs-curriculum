# Culinary Arts - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Culinary Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); official source language is English. This reference does not determine the teaching language.

**Learning objective:** Understand safe, professional culinary production and service, connecting diners' needs with cooking methods, kitchen organization, and responsible ingredient choices.

**Prerequisites:** Experience interpreting simple recipes, measurement, and cooperative work can help; these suggestions are not formal course-entry criteria.

**Materials:** Only the reference and access to [S1] are needed for reading. Kitchen access or equipment is not required to use this document.

**How to use:** Read the grouped items with the official source, particularly its safety and technical elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes. Culinary Arts is a named Grade 10 course choice, distinct from Food Studies; this document neither prescribes enrolment nor certifies food-handling competence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Culinary service responds to diners' needs and tastes while taking account of the social, ethical, and sustainability consequences of food decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, diner needs and tastes; social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Producing and serving food involves connected tasks whose different stages call for different tools and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Establish who will be served and how, then interpret recipes and service tasks. Order the work, anticipate unfamiliar terms or safety and equipment challenges, and plan around the teaching kitchen's physical and practical limits.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating (recipe comprehension).

### CC-02

Consider the wider consequences of culinary choices and the suitability of available or emerging technologies. Draw on appropriate inspiration to select and refine recipes, techniques, ingredients, and procedures, including opportunities to reuse materials or reduce disposal impacts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Use feedback and suitable tests to judge a recipe, method, or skill and revise it. Coordinate time, equipment, ingredients, and production processes; demonstrate safe cooking and equipment operation while minimizing waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Consider audiences and ways of sharing or promoting culinary work. Evaluate the quality of food products, identify improvements, and examine effectiveness when working alone or with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Practise culinary professionalism and recognize preventive and emergency safety procedures affecting oneself, co-workers, and kitchen users. Assess cookery and service capabilities and make specific plans for improvement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills, professionalism, safety, and skill-development plans.

### CC-06

Choose, adapt, and learn the technologies appropriate to culinary tasks, considering how land, resources, and culture influence ingredients, tools, and their use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand the practices that support safe and professional food service: personal safety, safe handling of food, kitchen roles and conduct, and appropriate use of service tools and equipment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, culinary best practices, safe food handling and personal safety, food service tools/equipment, and elements of professionalism.

### C-02

Connect ingredient classifications and culinary properties with suitable preparation methods. The source develops cooking methodology through areas such as stocks and sauces, moist and dry cooking, thickening, and baking; measurement includes both metric and imperial systems and weight and volume.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, units/types of measurement, culinary ingredients, and components of cooking methodology, including elaborations.

### C-03

Recognize locally available agricultural, fished, and foraged foods and their culinary properties. Understand that First Peoples food practices connect harvesting, stewardship, preparation, preservation, celebration, and cultural ownership.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, locally available food products and First Peoples food protocols.

### C-04

Examine cultural appropriation in culinary work, including the importance of permission and context when drawing on recipes, knowledge, or other cultural expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's cooking-method and ingredient examples clarify broad expectations; they do not mandate a fixed menu or every listed dish. FOODSAFE procedures appear as an elaboration of professionalism, not an independently established certification prerequisite in this record.

This curriculum summary is not operational kitchen-safety instruction. Actual work requires appropriate supervision and applicable food and equipment safety arrangements. Local First Peoples food protocols differ; the curriculum is not permission to gather food, use community recipes, or reproduce protected knowledge.
