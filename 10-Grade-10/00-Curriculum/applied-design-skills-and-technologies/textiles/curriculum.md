# Textiles - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Textiles](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English source; not a classroom-language requirement.

**Learning objective:** Connect users' needs with the design, construction, repair, and responsible selection of textiles, considering material properties and cultural and environmental consequences.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier design-process experience and familiarity with basic measurement or textile handling may help; these are not formal entry requirements.

**Materials:** The reference and access to [S1] are sufficient for reading; no sewing machine, pattern, or textile purchase is required.

**How to use:** Follow each source locator for the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository mapping aids, not ministry codes. This record describes a named Grade 10 course choice, not a universal enrolment requirement.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Textile design responds to what people need and value while taking account of social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs and interests; social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Complex textile work involves stages that require different tools and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research directly with potential users to identify opportunities and barriers. Determine consumers, likely effects of design choices, success criteria, and limits such as cost, resources, time, and environmental impact.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context and Defining.

### CC-02

Generate and extend ideas with openness to alternatives, examining how social, ethical, economic, and sustainability pressures affect textile availability. Use relevant inspiration and information to choose the prototype's form, scale, and detail, test materials and processes, and consider reuse, recycling, and biodegradability.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Identify what feedback is most useful and devise suitable prototype tests. Revise the design or process using users' responses, then produce textile items with appropriate tools and methods, accounting for time, cost, and the skills and knowledge required.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Share progress during production so feedback can still inform decisions. Choose audiences and ways to share finished work, reflect on design thinking and new goals, and assess independent and collaborative work, including shared workspace organization.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Recognize preventive and emergency safety procedures for oneself and others in physical and digital settings. Identify and develop the skills appropriate to a design interest or project.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose, adapt, and learn appropriate technologies. Examine their personal, social, and environmental effects, including unintended harms, and the influence of land, resources, and culture on their development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand the origins, properties, and care of natural and manufactured textiles, connecting raw materials and production contexts to suitability for a design opportunity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities and origins, characteristics, and care of natural/manufactured textiles.

### C-02

Understand hand and machine methods for making and repairing textile items. Relate pattern alteration and upcycling to changing an item's form or purpose, using principles such as balance, proportion, movement, harmony, and scale to guide design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, construction and repair techniques, altering patterns and upcycling, and principles of design.

### C-03

Recognize both traditional and present-day First Peoples textile knowledge and practices as part of textile study, rather than treating Indigenous textiles only as historical objects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples traditional and current textile knowledge and practices.

### C-04

Examine how textile decisions affect communities locally and globally. Relevant considerations include raw-material production, processing and disposal, workers' rights, and the ethics of using cultural designs or knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, environmental and ethical factors influencing textile choices, including cultural appropriation elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's prototype and test examples clarify possible approaches; they do not prescribe a garment, pattern, brand, or single construction technique. Upcycling changes an item's use or material into something with improved quality or environmental value; it is not simply another word for every form of recycling.

Respect community-specific permissions for First Peoples textile knowledge and cultural designs. This summary does not authorize reproducing patterns or works, or replace safe equipment instruction. Body measurements and personal information are not required to use this reference.
