# Metalwork - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Metalwork](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English source; not a prescription for classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Understand how metal properties, precision, fabrication methods, and safe work practices support responsible design and production for users.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with measurement, planning, and earlier ADST work can help; these suggestions are not formal entry requirements.

**Materials:** The reference and access to [S1]; no metal stock, tools, or workshop are needed to read it.

**How to use:** Compare grouped items with the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository mapping references, not ministry codes. This is one named Grade 10 course choice, not an enrolment requirement for all learners.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Metalwork design responds to users' needs and interests while considering social, ethical, and sustainability consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Complex metalwork tasks require tools and technologies suited to their different stages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research the design context and observe users' experiences. Define intended effects, criteria, limits, and whether work is independent or collaborative; generate ideas from appropriate inspiration and evaluate alternatives against practical constraints and wider social, ethical, and sustainability priorities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Plan prototypes and resources, considering material effectiveness and reuse or disposal options. Document iterations and use suitable tests and feedback to collect and interpret evidence, then decide on changes to tools, materials, or procedures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Use appropriate technologies, tools, materials, and processes in a sequenced production plan, adapting it as necessary and minimizing waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Choose how to share the product and process, demonstrate the result to users, judge its success, and establish further design goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Demonstrate and document awareness of preventive and emergency safety procedures. Build manual dexterity and proficiency in metalwork techniques, identifying individual and collaborative skill-development needs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose and adapt tools thoughtfully and learn unfamiliar technologies when needed. Evaluate intended and unintended effects on people and the environment, and the influence of land, natural resources, and culture on technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand material selection through metal size, shape, finish, and common gauges. Distinguish ferrous and non-ferrous materials and consider carbon content when identifying and choosing stock.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, metal selection, common gauges, and identification of ferrous/non-ferrous materials and carbon content.

### C-02

Connect precision measurement with reading and preparing drawings, plans, and cutting lists. Understand units, conversions, and tolerances in relation to accurate layout and fabrication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, precision measurement and reading/preparing drawings, plans, and cutting lists, including measurement elaborations.

### C-03

Understand methods for laying out, forming, and joining metal, including mechanical fastening and thread cutting. Recognize precision grinding and computer numerical control applications as parts of the available metalworking processes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cutting threads, fasteners/fastening methods, laying out/forming/joining metal, precision grinding, and CNC applications.

### C-04

Understand organized tool and equipment storage and the importance of correct compressed-gas-cylinder handling, start-up, and shutdown procedures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, tool/equipment storage and organization; compressed gas cylinder procedures.

### C-05

Recognize design opportunities while considering the ethics of cultural appropriation, including permission, context, and representation when drawing on cultural designs or knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities and ethics of cultural appropriation in the design process.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This source includes compressed gas, precision grinding, and CNC content, but the reference does not provide operational instructions or certify readiness to use equipment. Practical work requires appropriate supervision, training, and safety arrangements.

Official examples of tests, measurements, and threading methods clarify the content without mandating a single project or every listed technique. First Peoples perspectives in research and feedback require appropriate relationships and permissions; a design opportunity is not authorization to reproduce cultural works.
