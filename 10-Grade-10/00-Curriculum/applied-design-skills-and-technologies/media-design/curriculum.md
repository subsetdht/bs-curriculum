# Media Design - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Media Design](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing an English source rather than prescribing classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Understand how media choices construct stories, information, and viewpoints, and develop a reflective, ethical design process informed by communities, technologies, and feedback.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with communicating ideas, observing media, and earlier ADST processes can help; these are not formal entry criteria.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no camera, editing suite, personal media account, or production equipment is needed for reading.

**How to use:** Read the condensed groups alongside the official source and elaborations. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official standards. This is one Grade 10 course option; it is not a requirement to study every listed media form.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Personal media-design decisions grow through self-exploration and increasingly refined skills.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, personal design choices, self-exploration, and skill refinement.

### BI-02

Design choices have social, ethical, and sustainability dimensions, and the tools used to make media can affect people's lives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, social/ethical/sustainability considerations and impacts of tools and technology.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research and observe a design context empathetically while sustaining reciprocal relationships with people who contribute knowledge. Identify users, intended effects, success criteria, constraints, and possible unintended harms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, reciprocal relationships, and Defining.

### CC-02

Generate and extend ideas, compare them against criteria, and weigh social, ethical, and sustainability considerations in relation to community needs and long-term balanced futures. Use appropriate inspiration to plan prototypes and resources, examine material reuse, and document successive refinements.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Communicate with feedback sources and edit in response to critiques, revising or abandoning an idea when appropriate. Select technologies and processes, carry out an adaptable production plan, and minimize material waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Share work in progress for continuing feedback and choose how to share or promote the design. Evaluate its success and contribution, reflect on design thinking and future goals, and assess individual and collaborative work and shared workspace practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Recognize preventive and emergency procedures in physical and digital settings and develop the particular skills needed for media-design work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose and adapt technologies thoughtfully, evaluating personal, social, and environmental effects, including unintended harms. Consider the influence of land, natural resources, and culture on technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand media-design opportunities and technologies, and how pre-production, production, and post-production contribute to a finished work. Use standards-compliant technology appropriate to the medium.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities, media technologies, media production skills, and standards-compliant technology.

### C-02

Understand how organizing ideas and choosing technical and symbolic elements shape stories, information, images, and viewpoints. Representations can reflect genre, values, and the creator's point of view rather than being neutral accounts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, techniques for organizing ideas and creating points of view; technical/symbolic elements and representations.

### C-03

Consider media works from past and present through their features, purposes, and multiple viewpoints, including First Peoples perspectives. Connect digital and non-digital media's roles in documentation, reporting, communication, and self-expression with local, Indigenous, regional, and global design histories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, features/purposes of media artworks, influences of digital/non-digital media, and history of design.

### C-04

Understand responsible participation in media through digital literacy, etiquette, and citizenship. Consider ethical, moral, and legal issues in duplication, manipulation, and reuse, including copyright and the appropriation of cultural knowledge or expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethical/moral/legal considerations, ethics of cultural appropriation, and digital citizenship/etiquette/literacy.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source spans digital and non-digital forms; its examples do not mandate every production technology or a specific software package. The design-process emphasis distinguishes this ADST course from treating it as a generic arts-course substitute.

Reciprocity requires respectful relationships with knowledge holders, not simply extracting content. First Peoples perspectives and design histories remain integral to the subject, with appropriate community protocols and permissions. Studying media works does not grant reproduction rights or permission to publish other people's personal information.
