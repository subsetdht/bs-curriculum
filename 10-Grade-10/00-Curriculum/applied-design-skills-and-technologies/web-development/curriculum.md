# Web Development - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Web Development](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on an English source; this does not prescribe the language of instruction.

**Learning objective:** Understand the technologies and design decisions behind usable websites, integrating collaboration, accessibility, standards, security, privacy, and responsible content use.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with files, browsers, and communicating ideas can help; no formal programming or course prerequisite is established here.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]. Reading does not require a domain, hosting account, paid editor, or public website.

**How to use:** Compare these grouped expectations with the official source and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not BC standard codes. This is a named Grade 10 course choice rather than a required course for every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Web design responds to users' needs and interests while being shaped by ethical, social, and sustainability considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Complex design work needs tools suited to its stages, and collaboration can improve projects that develop over several stages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies/tools for complex tasks and collaborative work environments.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research and observe users' experiences to establish a design opportunity, relevant context, and societal effects. Define success, desired impact, constraints, and potential unintended consequences; generate and screen ideas collaboratively while remaining open to alternatives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Use relevant information and inspiration to plan prototypes, stages, and resources. Record revisions, obtain suitable feedback, and test under appropriate conditions; collect and interpret results to decide whether to refine the prototype or abandon the idea.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select technologies and production processes appropriate to the design, then carry out and revise a sequenced production plan.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Choose an audience and sharing method with attention to attribution. Demonstrate the result using appropriate terminology and explain decisions and modifications; reflect on design goals and individual and collaborative effectiveness, including cooperative workspace practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing, including Creative Commons attribution elaboration.

### CC-05

Recognize preventive and emergency safety procedures in physical and digital settings. Identify and strengthen the skills required by the project.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose, adapt, and learn technologies as needed, evaluating intended and unintended personal, social, and environmental consequences of their use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how a website's content and structure, visual presentation, and interactive behaviour relate. Connect HTML and CSS with these roles, compare website and content-management-system approaches, and understand editing tools and planning representations such as site maps and wireframes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, web structure/content/HTML, style/design/CSS, functionality/interactivity, websites and CMS, planning tools, and HTML editors.

### C-02

Connect interface functionality with the user's overall experience. Understand creative design, accessibility, and usability alongside web standards, responsive layouts, browser compatibility, and efficient loading and bandwidth use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, UI/UX, W3C standards, responsive and optimized design, accessibility/functionality, and creative web design.

### C-03

Understand writing suited to web audiences and navigation. Apply copyright, Creative Commons, and source-described fair-use protocols to media and content, while recognizing that cultural appropriation also raises questions of permission, context, and representation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, writing for the web; copyright, Creative Commons, fair use protocols, and ethics of cultural appropriation.

### C-04

Understand domains, hosting choices, and database creation and management. Consider how permissions, storage location, encryption, and related decisions affect privacy and security rather than treating deployment as only a technical matter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, domain/hosting options, security/privacy implications, and principles of database creation/management.

### C-05

Recognize web-development career roles and design opportunities, including the communication and collaborative skills needed to contribute successfully to project-based work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities and career options/interpersonal skills.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source names technology and language examples, but does not mandate a particular framework, editor, database product, or hosting provider. Its standards and privacy examples should be read with current authoritative technical and legal guidance; this curriculum reference is not deployment advice or a legal interpretation of reuse rights.

Accessibility is an explicit content expectation, not an optional visual enhancement. Collaboration is also a Big Idea, beyond simply dividing production tasks. First Peoples knowledge may inform research and inspiration under suitable protocols, without authorizing reproduction. No public publishing or collection of personal data is required to use this reference.
