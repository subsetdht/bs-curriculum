# Drafting - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Drafting](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English source; not a classroom-language requirement.

**Learning objective:** Understand how manual and digital drafting communicate design solutions through scale, drawing conventions, visual representations, and iterative development for users.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with measurement, spatial visualization, and earlier ADST processes may help; these suggestions are not formal course-entry criteria.

**Materials:** The reference and access to [S1]; no drafting equipment or software is needed for reading.

**How to use:** Follow the source locators to the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes. Drafting is a named Grade 10 choice, not a course every learner must take.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Drafting decisions respond to users' needs and interests while considering social, ethical, and sustainability effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Different stages of a complex design task call for different tools and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a context and observe users' experiences to define a design opportunity, relevant factors, desired effects, criteria, and constraints. Decide whether the work is independent or collaborative, and compare creatively generated ideas against both practical limits and social, ethical, and sustainability priorities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Visualize possible solutions and plan stages and resources. Consider material effectiveness and reuse, record prototype revisions, and gather feedback through suitable tests; use the collected data to decide what should change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select appropriate tools, technologies, materials, and processes, and follow an adaptable production sequence that minimizes waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Choose how to share the product and process, demonstrate the result to users, evaluate its success, and identify subsequent design goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Demonstrate and document awareness of preventive and emergency safety procedures. Develop manual dexterity and drafting proficiency, identifying the individual or shared skills needed for particular projects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose and adapt technologies deliberately, learning more when needed. Evaluate personal, social, and environmental effects, including unintended consequences, and how land, resources, and culture influence technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand drafting terminology, standards, and conventions as a common language for design communication. Relate drawing layout, line properties, and scale to the purpose and type of drawing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drafting terminology, drawing standards/conventions, and scales for different drawing types.

### C-02

Recognize design opportunities and communicate them through perspective, mechanical, and architectural drawing approaches, using appropriate manual and computer-aided tools and equipment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities, drafting styles, and equipment/tools for manual and computer-aided drafting.

### C-03

Understand modelling with computer-aided design and computer-aided manufacturing software, and the use of coding to create three-dimensional representations of design solutions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, CAD/CAM modelling and coding for 3D representations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The curriculum includes manual drafting as well as digital modelling; it is not exclusively a CAD software course. Source examples of terminology, line conventions, and drawing types are illustrative and do not prescribe a commercial package or one professional certification standard.

First Peoples and other cultural perspectives may inform contextual research and feedback under appropriate protocols. The course does not authorize reproducing cultural designs. This summary is not operational equipment instruction or certification that drawings are suitable for construction.
