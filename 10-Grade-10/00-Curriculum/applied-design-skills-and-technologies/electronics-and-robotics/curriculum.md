# Electronics and Robotics - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Electronics and Robotics](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on an English source; not a statement of classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Understand how circuit principles, physical construction, and programmed behaviour combine in electronic and robotic solutions developed for users through testing and refinement.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with basic measurement, logical sequences, and earlier design processes may help; these are not formal entry requirements.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no circuit components, robot kit, or powered equipment are required to read it.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the official standards and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes. Electronics and Robotics is one published Grade 10 course, not two separate records or a universal enrolment requirement.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Electronic and robotic design responds to users while taking account of ethical, social, and sustainability considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Complex tasks depend on applying skills in a suitable sequence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sequencing of skills.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a context and observe people's experiences to define users, relevant factors, intended effects, criteria, and constraints. Determine whether work is independent or collaborative; develop and compare ideas while weighing community needs and competing social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Plan prototype forms, stages, and resources, considering material effectiveness and reuse. Record revisions and use suitable feedback and tests to collect and interpret evidence before changing the design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select tools, technologies, materials, and processes appropriate to the design, and carry out a sequenced plan that can be revised while limiting waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Select audiences and sharing methods, demonstrate the result to users, critically judge its success, and identify new design goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Demonstrate and document awareness of preventive and emergency safety procedures. Develop manual dexterity and circuitry skills, identifying capabilities that need refinement for individual or collaborative work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose, adapt, and learn technologies as needed, considering intended and unintended consequences for people and the environment. Examine how land, natural resources, and culture influence technology.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand relationships among voltage, current, and resistance through Ohm's law, and electrical theory in series and parallel circuits. Connect the functions of electronic components with their applications in a circuit.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Ohm's law, electrical theory using parallel/series circuits, and function/application of components.

### C-02

Understand breadboard circuitry and the translation of schematic drawings into simple circuits. Relate construction sequences and diagnostic or testing instruments to obtaining and checking a working circuit.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, breadboards, circuits from schematic drawings, diagnostic/testing instruments, and circuit construction sequences.

### C-03

Understand the functions of hand tools and stationary equipment used in electronic construction, and the role of cases that enclose circuits within a designed product.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, function/use of hand tools, stationary equipment operation, and circuit enclosures.

### C-04

Understand the elements and assembly sequence of a functional robot. Connect input, output, control, and movement with flow-chart representations of behaviour, programming platforms, and block-based coding or logic-based programming.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, functional robot sequences, robot elements, robotics coding/programming platforms, and behaviour flow charts.

### C-05

Recognize design opportunities in which electronic or robotic systems can respond to a need, connecting the technical content with the course's user-centred design process.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The course includes both physical electronics and robotics programming. Component, tool, and enclosure examples do not require one kit, programming platform, or every listed machine. Block-based and logic-based programming are alternatives in the source, not a requirement to complete two separate programming courses.

This is a curriculum reference, not electrical, soldering, or machinery-safety instruction. Practical work requires appropriate supervision and safety arrangements. First Peoples and other cultural knowledge in contextual research or feedback requires suitable protocols and does not imply permission to reproduce designs.
