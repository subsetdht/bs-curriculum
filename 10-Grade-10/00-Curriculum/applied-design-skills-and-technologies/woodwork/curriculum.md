# Woodwork - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Woodwork](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on an English source; this does not prescribe instructional language.

**Learning objective:** Understand how wood properties, design communication, fabrication skills, and responsible material choices support useful products while respecting safety and cultural context.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with measuring, planning, and earlier ADST design processes may help; these suggestions are not formal entry criteria.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no wood stock, tools, or workshop are needed to read it.

**How to use:** Compare the grouped summaries with official expectations and elaborations using the locators. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes. Woodwork is a named Grade 10 course choice, not a requirement that all learners enrol.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Woodwork design responds to users' needs and interests while considering ethical, social, and sustainability effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

The stages of complex woodworking tasks call for different tools and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a design context and observe users' experiences. Define desired effects, success criteria, constraints, and independent or collaborative roles; develop ideas from appropriate inspiration and compare alternatives against requirements and competing social, ethical, and sustainability priorities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Plan a prototype's form, stages, and resources, considering material effectiveness and reuse or disposal options. Record revisions and use appropriate feedback and tests to collect and interpret evidence, then decide on changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select appropriate tools, technologies, materials, and processes, and follow an adaptable sequence of production while minimizing material waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Choose audiences and ways to share the product and process, demonstrate the result to users, critically evaluate its success, and identify new design goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Demonstrate and document awareness of preventive and emergency safety procedures. Develop manual dexterity and woodworking proficiency, identifying and refining skills needed for individual or collaborative projects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose and adapt technologies deliberately and learn unfamiliar tools as needed. Consider intended and unintended personal, social, and environmental effects and how land, resources, and culture influence technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Recognize woodworking design opportunities and the role of woodworking in historical and present-day First Nations, Métis, Inuit, and other cultural contexts. Consider the ethics of appropriation when drawing on cultural designs or knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, project design opportunities, cultural contexts of woodwork, and ethics of cultural appropriation.

### C-02

Understand how to identify woods from different species through their characteristics, properties, and uses. Connect material selection with choices about sustainable use of wood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, identification/characteristics/properties/uses of wood species and sustainable use of wood.

### C-03

Understand the creation and use of plans and drawings, and the techniques that turn stock into a woodworking product. The source's examples include shaping, laminating, turning, joining, and finishing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, plans and drawings; stock breakout and woodworking techniques, including elaborations.

### C-04

Understand the functions and uses of hand tools and the roles of portable and stationary power equipment in project creation, including equipment used for stock preparation and woodworking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, stock breakout with tools/equipment, portable/stationary power equipment, and hand tools.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source names many equipment examples, but does not mandate ownership of each tool or a particular project. This record describes learning expectations, not operating procedures, safety certification, or permission to use machinery without appropriate supervision and training.

First Nations, Métis, and Inuit woodworking contexts are both historical and current; avoid reducing them to one tradition or copying community designs. Research and feedback involving knowledge holders require appropriate protocols and permissions. The full elaborations remain at [S1].
