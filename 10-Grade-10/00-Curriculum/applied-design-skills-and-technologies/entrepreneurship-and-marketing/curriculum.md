# Entrepreneurship and Marketing - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Entrepreneurship and Marketing](README.md)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing an English source. Neither specifies classroom language or supplies French instructional materials.

**Learning objective:** Understand how entrepreneurial ideas develop into offerings, and use design thinking to weigh customer needs, ethical marketing, and social and environmental consequences.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with everyday buying decisions, communicating ideas, and earlier ADST design processes is helpful, not a formal entry requirement established by this reference.

**Materials:** A reader and access to [S1] are sufficient; no business software, money, or commercial venture is required to read this reference.

**How to use:** Compare the grouped expectations with [S1] and its elaborations. Local IDs support precise mappings; they are not ministry codes. This is one published Grade 10 course choice, not a requirement that every learner take all ADST courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Entrepreneurial design and decisions have social and environmental consequences; ethical marketing matters to the health of the wider marketplace.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, social/ethical/sustainability considerations and ethical marketing.

### BI-02

Creating an offering and communicating its value involve different stages, each needing suitable tools and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies and tools at stages of creation and communication.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate an opportunity through research and attentive observation of people's experiences. Identify prospective users, desired effects, success criteria, and practical or environmental limits before deciding what to design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context and Defining.

### CC-02

Generate and extend ideas while remaining open to alternatives; compare competing social, ethical, and sustainability priorities against community needs. Plan prototypes using relevant inspiration, stages, and resources; examine materials' reuse and disposal possibilities and document revisions to methods and tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Seek useful feedback and reconsider decisions, including whether to remake or abandon an idea. Organize production as an adaptable sequence using appropriate processes and technologies while reducing material waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Select an audience and sharing method, explain the solution and its changes using appropriate terminology, and evaluate its contribution to people or the environment. Reflect on design decisions, independent and group work, shared workspace practices, and goals emerging from feedback.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Recognize preventive and emergency procedures in physical and digital settings, and identify and strengthen the skills a particular project needs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Select and adapt tools deliberately, learning unfamiliar technologies when necessary. Evaluate intended and unintended personal, social, and environmental effects, and how land, resources, and culture shape technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Recognize entrepreneurial opportunities and the capabilities that help people pursue them. Examine barriers experienced by diverse entrepreneurs, alongside conditions that support success, rather than assuming opportunity is equally accessible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entrepreneurship opportunities, characteristics of entrepreneurs, and barriers and success factors for diverse groups.

### C-02

Distinguish generating an original invention from innovating on an existing idea, product, or service. Understand ways to add value and the development of an offering from invention or innovation to its arrival in a market.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, adding value, invention and innovation, and product life cycle to the marketplace.

### C-03

Relate marketing decisions to customer needs, wants, and demands. Compare forms of marketing that respond to, anticipate, or shape needs, including online approaches, while considering social responsibility and cultural sensitivity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethical marketing strategies, forms of marketing, online marketing concepts, and customer needs/wants/demands.

### C-04

Understand the ethical limits of drawing on another culture in a business idea or its promotion. Permission, context, and accurate representation matter when using cultural knowledge, designs, or expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This course is not a requirement to start a business or sell a product. In the source, a designed product can also be a service, process, system, or environment. The official examples of entrepreneur groups, characteristics, and online marketing are explanatory, not an exhaustive checklist.

First Peoples perspectives may inform research, inspiration, and feedback; that invitation does not authorize reuse of community knowledge or cultural works. Preserve community-specific protocols and permissions. The record condenses the full source without reproducing its elaborations.
