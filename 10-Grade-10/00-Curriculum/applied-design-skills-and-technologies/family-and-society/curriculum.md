# Family and Society - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Family and Society](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English course source; this is separate from language of instruction.

**Learning objective:** Understand diverse family lives and relationships, and apply respectful, consultative service design to needs affecting individuals, families, and communities.

**Prerequisites:** Listening, collaborative problem solving, and prior ADST experience may help; no formal entry requirement is established here.

**Materials:** Access to this reference and [S1] is sufficient. No family records, interviews, or personal disclosures are needed to read or use it.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not BC standard codes. This is a named Grade 10 course option, not a claim that every student must enrol or that one family structure is preferred.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Useful problem solving begins with people's needs and wants. Designing support for individuals, families, and groups also requires attention to ethics, social effects, and sustainability.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, needs and wants; social, ethical, and sustainability considerations in service design.

### BI-02

The tools appropriate for developing a service or product may differ from those used to communicate it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies and tools at different stages of creation and communication.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a challenge affecting families and listen empathetically to the people involved. Establish needs, wants, constraints, and success criteria through their perspectives, so the intended benefit reflects what they actually value.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context and Defining, including intended valued impact.

### CC-02

Develop and compare possible responses, building on others' thinking and considering competing social, ethical, and sustainability priorities. Use appropriate information and inspiration to plan a product or service, its stages and resources, and possible effects on individuals, families, and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Consult the people affected and other appropriate feedback sources. Use the resulting information to revise strategies, account for ethical and cultural concerns, and carry out an adaptable sequence for implementation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing, consultation, strategies, and implementation plan.

### CC-04

Choose how to share a proposed service or product and explain its rationale to potential users. Evaluate whether it meets its purpose and contributes positively, reflecting on planning, independent and group work, and the organization of collaborative workspaces.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Use safe, supportive interpersonal communication in person and digitally. Develop needed skills, reflect on cultural sensitivity, and apply interviewing and consultation etiquette, including confidentiality and informed consent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills, supportive strategies, cultural sensitivity, interviewing and consultation etiquette.

### CC-06

Adapt tools and technologies to the task while examining unintended effects on people and the environment. Consider how social, cultural, and environmental conditions influence technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how cultures define family and how these definitions and roles change. Examine social influences on family life, including the effects of displacement, economic pressures, and residential schools identified in the source's examples.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural factors defining family and societal influences and impacts on families, with elaborations.

### C-02

Explore family and relationship dynamics through responsibilities, resources, needs, communication, and healthy or ending relationships. Consider local and international challenges, special caregiving concerns, access to support, and strategies for action.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, family and relationship dynamics/challenges and social factors in interpersonal relationships.

### C-03

Understand children's roles and rights in families and society, both locally and globally. Recognize varied living arrangements and housing options without treating one arrangement as the standard for all families.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, role and rights of children; living arrangements and housing options.

### C-04

Connect problem-solving models with service-design opportunities and strategies across the lifespan. A response may support individuals, families, or groups through services, products, environments, or other forms appropriate to their needs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, service design opportunities, service strategies, and problem-solving models.

### C-05

Understand cultural sensitivity and etiquette as part of responsible service design, including the ethical limits of using cultural knowledge or expression without permission or adequate context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural sensitivity and etiquette, including ethics of cultural appropriation.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Service design here is a human-centred response to social needs, not a promise that learners can resolve complex family problems or provide professional counselling. The source places implementation under Testing; this summary preserves that scope rather than inventing a separate manufacturing strand.

The curriculum does not require learners to disclose private family experiences. Consultation requires appropriate consent, confidentiality, and respectful community protocols. First Nations, Métis, and Inuit family perspectives are part of the subject, not a single interchangeable family model. Examples of rights instruments, family challenges, and living arrangements remain explanatory at [S1].
