# Computer Studies - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Computer Studies](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on an English source; neither field specifies the teaching language.

**Learning objective:** Understand how computer systems operate and how to develop and evaluate digital solutions, connecting technical skills with security, citizenship, and the wider effects of computing.

**Prerequisites:** Basic file handling, digital communication, and earlier ADST experience may help. No formal course-entry requirement is established here.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no particular device, operating system, software licence, or programming environment is needed for reading.

**How to use:** Follow the source locators for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references rather than ministry codes. This named Grade 10 option is broader than programming alone and does not imply that all students take every ADST course.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a design context and observe people's experiences. Identify users, societal effects, success criteria, constraints, and possible unintended outcomes; compare candidate ideas against these considerations while remaining open to alternatives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

Use relevant information and inspiration to choose a prototype form and plan stages and resources. Revise tools and procedures as needed, document iterations, and use appropriately designed tests and feedback to collect and interpret data before revising or abandoning an idea.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select suitable production technologies and processes, then carry out a sequenced plan that can change as new information emerges.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

Recognize preventive and emergency safety procedures in physical and digital environments, and identify and develop skills needed for a particular project.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

## Content

### C-01

Understand computer hardware, peripherals, internal and external components, and standards. Distinguish system, utility, and application software and cloud-based versus desktop applications; use intermediate word-processing, spreadsheet, and presentation features to organize and communicate information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, hardware/components/standards, software types, cloud and desktop applications, and intermediate business-application features.

### C-02

Understand operating-system shortcuts and command-line operations, preventive hardware and software maintenance, and security risks. Troubleshooting involves identifying a problem, testing a likely cause, taking action, checking results, and communicating findings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, operating-system shortcuts and command line, preventive maintenance, computer security risks, and hardware/software troubleshooting.

### C-03

Understand wired and wireless networks and how evolving digital technologies change traditional computing. Evaluate both benefits and risks of large-scale data collection, connected devices, and the Internet of Things, including convenience, privacy, security, and personal information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, networking, evolution of digital technology, and risks/rewards of big data, multi-device connectivity, and Internet of Things.

### C-04

Use computational thinking to break down problems, recognize patterns, abstract relevant features, and express algorithms. Understand introductory program constructs such as data types, variables, expressions, operators, decisions, loops, classes, and objects, and apply planning and error diagnosis when writing simple programs, including games.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, computational thinking, introductory programming concepts/constructs, and planning and writing simple programs, including elaborations on syntax, logic, and run-time errors.

### C-05

Connect computing design opportunities with technology's effects on society, access, and human interaction. Consider responsible use of information and cultural expression, environmental sustainability, digital citizenship and literacy, and the effects of technology use on personal health and wellness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, design opportunities, societal impacts, ethical considerations/cultural appropriation/environmental sustainability, digital literacy/citizenship, and health/wellness.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This is a broad introductory computing course, not a mandate for one operating system, programming language, brand, or every hardware example in the elaborations. Classes and objects appear among introductory constructs, but the source does not prescribe an advanced software-engineering sequence.

Security risks and ethical questions are topics for understanding and responsible practice, not authorization to access others' systems or collect private information. Cultural references in research and design require appropriate permission and context. Official examples and detailed technical terms remain at [S1].
