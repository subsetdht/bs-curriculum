# Food Studies - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Food Studies](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), summarizing an English source; not a statement of classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Connect meal design and safe food preparation with people's circumstances, well-being, cultural protocols, and the wider systems that shape food choices.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience reading recipes, measuring, and discussing food choices may help; these are suggestions rather than formal course-entry criteria.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no ingredients or kitchen equipment are required to read it.

**How to use:** Read with the official expectations and elaborations using the source locators. Local IDs are not ministry standard codes. Food Studies is a distinct Grade 10 course choice, not interchangeable with Culinary Arts and not compulsory for every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Food production and preparation respond to consumers' needs and preferences, while design choices also carry social, ethical, and sustainability responsibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, consumer needs/preferences and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Complex food-preparation work requires tools and technologies suited to its different stages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, complex tasks and technologies at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research the context of a meal task and consider differing perspectives, potential consumers, and settings. Define intended effects, success criteria, dietary or practical constraints, and workspace capacities before selecting an approach.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context and Defining.

### CC-02

Respond creatively to challenges while weighing social, ethical, economic, and sustainability effects on food choices. Use appropriate inspiration to choose an idea and prototype it at a useful scale and level of detail, experimenting with ingredients and methods and considering efficient techniques and material reuse.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating and Prototyping.

### CC-03

Identify relevant feedback and suitable tests, then refine a food prototype. Plan production in steps, select appropriate processes and equipment, and work independently or cooperatively while adapting the plan and minimizing food waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Testing and Making.

### CC-04

Decide how and with whom to share prepared food. Evaluate a meal's success and its contribution to individuals, families, community, and environment, along with effectiveness in individual and collaborative work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Recognize preventive and emergency safety procedures for oneself and others, including food safety and sanitation. Assess current skills and plan how to strengthen them or learn new ones.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose or adapt food-preparation technologies, examining unintended personal, social, and environmental consequences and how land, resources, and culture influence their development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand meal-design opportunities and the relationships among planning, preparation, and eating practices. Practical decisions involve matters such as time, budget, quantities, access, and presentation alongside the social and cultural context of a meal.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, meal design opportunities and elements of meal preparation, including meal planning and eating practices.

### C-02

Understand how food-contamination outbreaks arise and their consequences. Examine relationships between eating practices and both physical and mental well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, causes and consequences of food contamination outbreaks; eating practices and mental/physical well-being.

### C-03

Recognize First Peoples food protocols connecting land stewardship, gathering and harvesting, preparation or preservation, celebration, and cultural ownership. Consider appropriation and the need for permission and context when drawing on cultural food knowledge or recipes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples food protocols and ethics of cultural appropriation.

### C-04

Examine food trends in relation to nutrition, marketing, and food systems. Compare simpler and more complex global systems, connecting food choices to production, movement, consumption, and disposal and their environmental, ethical, economic, and health effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, food trends and simple/complex global food systems and their impacts.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The official examples support interpretation rather than prescribe a menu, diet, or every possible food trend. The course connects practical meal design with broader food systems; it should not be reduced to recipes alone.

This record is not personal medical or nutrition advice or a substitute for food-safety instruction. First Peoples protocols vary by community, and their inclusion does not grant permission to reproduce recipes or knowledge. Keep access, dietary restrictions, and cultural context visible without requiring personal health disclosures.
