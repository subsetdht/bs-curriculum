# Technology Explorations - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10 overview](../../README.md) > [ADST courses](../README.md) > [Technology Explorations](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject content supported by [S2] and the [reviewed provincial program evidence](../README.md#program-coverage). Francophone delivery is in French; local course availability is not implied.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), based on the English source; not a classroom-language requirement.

**Learning objective:** Develop a coherent design-and-making process across selected technological areas, connecting users' needs with safe skills, suitable tools, evidence, and responsible decisions.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier ADST exploration, measurement, and collaborative planning may help; these are suggestions, not formal course-entry criteria.

**Materials:** This reference and access to [S1]; no workshop, robot kit, or software is required for reading.

**How to use:** Apply the source's selection rule: at least **six listed content entries from at least two curricular areas**. Use [S1] to identify and count those entries; the grouped local IDs below are not six compulsory units or equivalent to source-entry counts. IDs are repository references, not official BC codes.

This is a separately published Grade 10 course, not a direction to complete every source course. Its content draws selected entries from [Woodwork](../woodwork/README.md), [Metalwork](../metalwork/README.md), [Electronics and Robotics](../electronics-and-robotics/README.md), [Power Technology](../power-technology/README.md), and [Drafting](../drafting/README.md), with its own Big Ideas and competencies.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Design responds to users' needs and interests while taking social, ethical, and sustainability consequences into account.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, user needs/interests and social, ethical, and sustainability considerations.

### BI-02

Technologies support the accomplishment of many particular tasks in everyday life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies and specific tasks in our lives.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research a context and observe users' experiences to define a design opportunity, relevant factors, desired effects, criteria, and constraints. Decide whether to work independently or collaboratively; generate ideas from appropriate inspiration and compare them with requirements and wider social, ethical, and sustainability priorities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Choose a prototype form and plan stages and resources, considering material effectiveness and reuse or disposal. Record revisions and use feedback and suitable tests to collect and evaluate evidence before deciding what to change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Select tools, technologies, materials, and processes appropriate to the chosen focus, and carry out an adaptable sequence of work that minimizes waste.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making.

### CC-04

Select audiences and ways to share the product and its development, demonstrate it to users, critically judge its success, and identify new design goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing.

### CC-05

Demonstrate and document awareness of preventive and emergency safety procedures. Develop manual dexterity and proficiency within the selected specializations, identifying and refining skills needed for individual or collaborative work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Choose and adapt technologies and learn more as needed. Examine intended and unintended effects on people and the environment, and how land, resources, and culture influence technological development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

The content is a selection, not an all-topics syllabus: use at least six of the official listed entries across at least two of the five named curricular areas. The groups below summarize the available choices and their source attribution; they must not be used as a substitute for counting the source entries.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content introduction, minimum of six entries from at least two curricular areas.

### C-02

Woodwork choices connect project design opportunities with stock preparation and woodworking techniques, the roles of portable and stationary power equipment, and hand-tool use. Cultural appropriation in the design process is a listed choice attributed to both Woodwork and Metalwork.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entries labelled Woodwork 10 and shared Woodwork 10/Metalwork 10 cultural-appropriation entry.

### C-03

Metalwork choices address tool storage and organization, selecting metal for size, shape, and finish, compressed-gas-cylinder procedures, mechanical fasteners, and methods for laying out, forming, and joining metal, together with computer numerical control applications.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entries labelled Metalwork 10: storage, selection, gas cylinders, fastening, fabrication, and CNC.

### C-04

Electronics and Robotics choices connect series and parallel circuit theory with schematic-based construction, test instruments, assembly sequences, and tool or stationary-equipment use. Robotics choices cover functional assembly, robot elements, programming platforms, and block-based coding or logic-based programming.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entries labelled Electronics and Robotics 10: electrical theory, circuits, instruments, construction/tools, robot sequences/elements, coding, and platforms.

### C-05

Power Technology choices connect combustion, engine terminology, and disassembly or assembly with mechanical repair tools. They also include hydraulic and pneumatic systems, energy transfer and conversion, transmission systems, and alternate energy sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entries labelled Power Technology 10: combustion, sequences, terminology, hydraulics/pneumatics, energy systems, repair tools, and alternate sources.

### C-06

Drafting choices concern drawing standards and conventions, scale, perspective and mechanical or architectural drawing, CAD/CAM modelling, and coding that produces three-dimensional representations of design solutions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, entries labelled Drafting 10: drawing standards/conventions/scales/styles, CAD/CAM, and coded 3D representations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The parenthetical course names identify the origin of available content; they are not five additional enrolments, aliases, or permission to import each course's entire content list. For example, the full Electronics and Robotics record includes Ohm's law and robotics flow charts, but those are not separate listed choices on this Technology Explorations source. Use [S1], not the complete contributing-course records, to establish this course's selection.

The shared cultural-appropriation entry should not be silently counted twice merely because two source areas are named. Local implementation should preserve the source's minimum and actual selected entries. No particular pair of areas or set of six entries is prescribed here.

Equipment examples clarify available content but do not provide operational safety instruction. Practical work requires suitable supervision and safety arrangements. First Peoples perspectives in research and feedback require community-specific protocols and permissions; curriculum inclusion does not authorize reproduction of cultural knowledge or works.
