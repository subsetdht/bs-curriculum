# Core French - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Core French](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language. This optional additional-language course is distinct from French Immersion Language Arts and first-language Francophone Language Arts. It is not represented as satisfying either program's language-arts requirements; [S2] explains the separate purpose of immersion.

**Document language:** English, as is the reviewed official source; the language being learned is French. This is not a French-language teaching resource.

**Learning objective:** Communicate with increasing fluency about familiar matters, connect events across time, and understand how French language, Francophone communities, and personal identity interact.

**Prerequisites:** Previous Core French learning and experience with everyday conversation may help; these are practical suggestions, not official admission or proficiency criteria.

**Materials:** No special materials are necessary to read this reference. The course's texts and learning materials remain separate from this summary.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for precise expectations and examples. The local IDs support traceable references and are not ministry standard codes, assessment levels, or an ordered course plan.

This record covers the published 10 standards, not all grades in the downloadable 5-10 compilation. A course listing does not make Core French compulsory for every student in 10.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing support understanding French, while sustained participation in conversation involves taking risks and persisting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent, expression, and conversation.

### BI-02

Learning another language can change how people understand themselves and their world. Cultural expression is varied rather than limited to a single art form or custom.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspective and identity, and cultural expression.

### BI-03

French opens connections with the Francophone world and expands possibilities for learning, relationships, work, and other life experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, interaction with the Francophone world and diverse opportunities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret key meaning and supporting detail across varied texts. Use context and communication strategies, clarify unfamiliar meaning, and select words with attention to nuances such as pronoun choice, verb form, or placement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: meaning from texts, strategies, clarification, and word choices.

### CC-02

Converse about familiar topics and express ideas with growing oral and written fluency. Tell logically connected stories in speech and writing, using time expressions and transitions to relate past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: conversations, increasing fluency, and narration.

### CC-03

Explore regional French usage and Francophone cultural expression, connecting language choices with culture. Investigate Francophone Canadians' lives and contributions and engage with Francophone people and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: regional variation, cultural expression, language-culture connections, Canadian contributions, and community experiences.

### CC-04

Recognize how encounters between First Peoples and Francophone communities have influenced language and culture in Canada. Consider how stories convey knowledge and help sustain personal, family, and community identities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: First Peoples-Francophone interactions and the importance of story.

## Content

### C-01

Use commonly encountered French vocabulary and sentence patterns for different question forms, ordered events, degrees of preference, personal aspirations, and opinions on familiar subjects. Connect these functions with past, present, and future reference rather than learning tense labels in isolation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary and sentence structures for time frames, questions, event sequences, likes and dislikes, hopes and ambitions, and opinions.

### C-02

Understand how text format, audience, purpose, and register influence communication, and recognize how characters, setting, events, and resolution organize a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of different text types and common story elements.

### C-03

Explore idiomatic French across la francophonie and the lives and contributions of Francophone Canadians, including First Nations, Métis, Inuit, immigrant, and other diverse backgrounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, idiomatic expressions from la francophonie and Francophone Canadians' lives and contributions.

### C-04

Understand why cultural expression needs permission, context, and accurate representation, and distinguish responsible attribution from plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source expects increasing fluency on familiar topics, not native-like proficiency, a specified external language level, or mastery of every possible French tense. Its examples illustrate how forms change meaning; they are not an exhaustive compulsory grammar or vocabulary list.

The First Peoples-Francophone strand includes language contact and the effects of colonization; it should not be reduced to a celebratory account of cultural exchange. The source's examples include Michif, Chinook Jargon, and First Peoples writing in French. These are distinct histories and languages, not varieties that can all be labelled French.

Core French does not become immersion or first-language French because its source also has a French edition. Texts include oral, written, visual, and digital forms. Official elaborations remain at [S1]; this record does not reproduce literary works, oral histories, or community knowledge, and curricular inclusion is not permission to retell them.
