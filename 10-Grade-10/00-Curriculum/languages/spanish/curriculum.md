# Spanish - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Spanish](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] supplies common immersion secondary guidance; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. Together with Spanish 10 [S1], this supports shared content, not local availability, a prescribed classroom support language, or replacement of program language arts.

**Document language:** English, matching the reviewed source language. Spanish is the language being learned; this summary is not a Spanish-language classroom resource.

**Learning objective:** Understand and share increasingly complex meaning in Spanish, connecting language form with context and engaging with varied Hispanic community perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Prior Spanish learning and familiarity with everyday spoken and written patterns may help. These are practical suggestions, not official entry or placement requirements.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Textbooks, media, and other classroom materials are not prescribed here.

**How to use:** Compare the synthesis with the precise expectations and elaborations at [S1]. The local IDs are repository references, not official standard codes, proficiency ratings, or a teaching sequence.

The target is the published 10 course, not the full 5-10 download compilation. A listed course does not make Spanish mandatory for all students.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing support learning Spanish, and stories provide ways to explore meaning and reflect on experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and stories.

### BI-02

Expressing ideas and taking part in conversation develop through communicative risks and sustained effort.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, courage, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Language learning can connect people with diverse communities, whose experiences are represented through many kinds of cultural expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and access to diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Spanish spelling patterns with pronunciation and meaning, and recognize how wording affects a message. Interpret key information, details, and story viewpoints in speech and other texts, using non-verbal cues and communication strategies to aid understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: word choices, letter patterns/pronunciation/meaning, comprehension, story viewpoints, strategies, and non-verbal cues.

### CC-02

Exchange ideas orally and in writing, respond to questions and instructions, and clarify meaning when needed. Narrate connected events across time, and choose ways of presenting information that accommodate participants' varied abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narration, exchange, presentation formats, responses, and clarification/verification.

### CC-03

Analyze how regional, cultural, and linguistic practices shape identities across Hispanic communities, and engage with Hispanic people and communities. Recognize stories as important to personal, family, and community identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: regional practices, story and identity, and community experiences.

### CC-04

Consider personal and shared experiences through cultural perspectives, recognizing First Peoples knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural analysis and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Use increasingly complex Spanish vocabulary and sentence patterns for questions, activities, events, and opinions. Communicate in affirmative and negative forms and relate past, present, and future through regular and irregular verbs in context, preserving the distinction between pretérito and imperfecto.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary/sentence structures/expressions, questions, activities and opinions, and time frames with pretérito and imperfecto.

### C-02

Understand how format, purpose, audience, and register shape common texts, and how narrative components organize stories. Explore idiomatic expressions across the Hispanic world rather than relying only on literal word meanings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common texts, story elements, and idiomatic expressions from across the Hispanic world.

### C-03

Learn about Hispanic Canadians' contributions and artistic expression and cultural life in varied Hispanic communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Hispanic Canadians, Hispanic works of art, and cultural aspects of Hispanic communities.

### C-04

Explore First Peoples connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Consider the ethical limits of borrowing cultural expression and the responsibility to avoid plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The past-form distinction between pretérito and imperfecto is explicit in the source and should not be flattened into generic past-tense practice. Regular and irregular verbs are used in context; the source's examples are not an exhaustive required conjugation or vocabulary inventory.

The course connects Spanish written patterns and pronunciation without specifying a spelling test list or external proficiency level. Increasing complexity is not a native-like fluency requirement. The source's regional practices and idioms encompass more than Spain; no one community stands for the entire Hispanic world.

Full elaborations remain at [S1]. Their cultural examples are not mandatory activities or works. First Peoples stories and other community knowledge require appropriate permissions and context; inclusion in the curriculum does not authorize copying or adaptation.
