# German - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [German](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] supplies Early/Late immersion secondary guidance; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. Read with the actual German course [S1], this supports shared content, not a guarantee of local offerings, a classroom support language, or replacement of program language arts.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed official source is English. German is the target language, not the language of this summary.

**Learning objective:** Understand German texts and exchange connected ideas with increasing fluency, using grammatical distinctions purposefully and engaging with German-speaking communities.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with earlier German learning, everyday vocabulary, and basic sentence formation may be useful. These are helpful starting points, not official placement rules.

**Materials:** No special materials are required for reading the reference; it is not a textbook or classroom resource list.

**How to use:** Follow each source locator to [S1], including its elaborations. Local IDs identify this repository's summaries, not official BC standards or a prescribed teaching order.

The standards apply to 10, although official downloads also compile earlier grades. Taking German is a course choice, not a requirement for all students.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Careful listening and viewing help learners make sense of German. Narratives offer another way to examine meaning and experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening/viewing and stories.

### BI-02

Developing expression and conversation requires trying unfamiliar language and persisting through difficulty.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, courage, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Language learning makes connections with diverse communities possible, while cultural expression offers many ways to encounter their experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and access to diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Understand main information and supporting detail in spoken German and other texts, including the meaning and viewpoints of stories. Use contextual clues, rephrasing, and other strategies to improve comprehension and oral or written expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: strategies, key information and details, and meaning and viewpoints in stories.

### CC-02

Respond to questions and instructions, resolve uncertain meaning, and exchange information with growing spoken and written fluency. Tell stories using transitions and references to past, present, and future; select presentation formats accessible to oneself and others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narration, responses, clarification, exchange, and presentation formats.

### CC-03

Investigate regional, cultural, and linguistic differences in German-speaking communities, considering how they shape identity. Connect with German people and communities and recognize how stories sustain personal, family, and community identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity, regional variations, and community experiences.

### CC-04

Examine personal and shared experiences through differing cultural perspectives. Recognize First Peoples knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge rather than assuming one worldview explains everyone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural analysis and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Understand German declension and the roles of gender, case, and number. Connect prepositions with their corresponding cases, including distinctions involved in describing location and movement, and use language for directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German declination, gender/case/number, locations and directions, and prepositions with respective cases.

### C-02

Develop common vocabulary, questions, and affirmative or negative statements about activities, events, and opinions. Attend to formality, directness, modality, and verb forms when communicating across past, present, and future, including regular and irregular verbs in context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common vocabulary and sentence structures, questions, activities and opinions, formality/directness/tense/modality, and time frames.

### C-03

Recognize how format, purpose, audience, and register organize common texts, and how narrative elements work together. Explore idiomatic expressions found across German communities rather than treating literal word meanings as sufficient.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common texts, story elements, and idiomatic expressions throughout German communities.

### C-04

Learn about German Canadian contributions and German artistic expression. Connect First Peoples language and culture with oral histories, identity, and place, and understand the ethical issues in cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German Canadians, First Peoples perspectives, German works of art, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Declension and preposition/case relationships are explicit German content, not interchangeable with a generic additional-language grammar list. The source's grammatical examples illustrate meaning in context; this summary does not add a complete case-by-case syllabus or demand mastery of all irregular forms.

Growing fluency does not establish a specific external proficiency level, word count, or error-free performance. Regional variation and the range of German communities are curricular concerns; no single national variety is presented as the whole language.

Official elaborations remain at [S1]. Examples of cultural experiences and art are not compulsory activities or required works. First Peoples histories and local knowledge retain their own cultural protocols and permissions; curricular inclusion is not permission to reproduce them.
