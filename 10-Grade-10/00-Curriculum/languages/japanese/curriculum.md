# Japanese - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Japanese](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] provides common immersion secondary guidance; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. Together with Japanese 10 [S1], this supports shared content without establishing local offerings, classroom support language, or replacement of program language arts.

**Document language:** English, also the language of the reviewed source. Japanese is the language being learned, including its writing systems; this is not a Japanese-language resource.

**Learning objective:** Connect Japanese characters, sounds, and meaning to understand and communicate ideas, while investigating varied Japanese communities and cultural identities.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with prior Japanese learning and initial kana reading may help. These are practical suggestions, not official entry criteria or a required character test.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. It does not prescribe handwriting supplies, textbooks, or classroom equipment.

**How to use:** Use the topic locators to read the official standards and elaborations at [S1]. Local IDs are repository identifiers, not official codes or a mandated sequence.

The source standards apply to 10; the multi-grade download is a compilation rather than the scope of this record. Japanese is a course choice, not compulsory for all learners.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing support learning Japanese. Stories offer ways to explore meaning beyond isolated words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and stories.

### BI-02

Trying to express oneself and converse in an unfamiliar language involves courage and sustained effort.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Language learning enables relationships across diverse communities, and cultural expression provides many forms through which to encounter their experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and interaction with diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Japanese characters with sounds and meanings, and interpret key information and supporting details in speech and other texts. Understand viewpoints in stories and use Japanese non-verbal cues to help interpret situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: characters/sounds/meaning, comprehension, story viewpoints, and non-verbal cues.

### CC-02

Use strategies that build on familiar Japanese structures to understand and produce language. Exchange ideas orally and in writing, respond to questions and instructions, and clarify uncertain meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: strategies, exchange, responses, and clarification/verification.

### CC-03

Tell stories orally and in writing with connected time references and transitions. Select ways of presenting information that support the varied abilities of participants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narration across time frames and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Describe how regional, cultural, and linguistic practices shape Japanese community identities, and engage with Japanese people and communities. Examine worldviews through culture, recognize stories' role in identity, and attend to First Peoples knowledge and local ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: regional practices, community experiences, cultural analysis, story, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Use hiragana and katakana and extend the range of kanji encountered. Relate the phonetic functions of kana and the meanings of characters to communication rather than substituting a Roman-letter-only treatment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, hiragana, katakana, and an increasing range of kanji; Curricular Competencies, relationships between characters, sounds, and meaning.

### C-02

Develop increasingly complex Japanese vocabulary and sentence patterns for questions, activities, situations, events, and opinions. Express time relationships across past, present, and future, with context helping distinguish present and future uses of the forms illustrated in the source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary/sentence structures/expressions, questions, activities, opinions, and time frames.

### C-03

Understand how common texts vary with format, audience, and purpose, and how stories organize characters, setting, events, and resolution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common text elements and common story elements.

### C-04

Explore Japanese artistic expression, practices in different Japanese communities, and Japanese Canadian contributions. Include the Indigenous peoples of Japan named in the source's elaboration, Ainu and Okinawan peoples, without treating Japanese cultural identity as uniform.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese works of art, Indigenous peoples of Japan, cultural practices, and Japanese Canadians.

### C-05

Consider First Peoples relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Understand ethical boundaries concerning cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source calls for an increasing kanji range but does not set a numerical kanji list or an external proficiency examination level. Hiragana, katakana, and kanji should not be collapsed into one alphabet. Past/present/future reference does not imply Japanese has three separate verb forms corresponding directly to English tense labels.

The curriculum addresses First Peoples in the BC/Canadian context and separately includes Indigenous peoples of Japan. These are not interchangeable communities or traditions. The source's named story and celebration examples do not require copying, translating, or performing those works.

Full elaborations remain at [S1]. Cultural knowledge and stories need appropriate permission, attribution, and context; no community work is reproduced. Presentation choices should preserve the source's attention to diverse abilities.
