# Italian - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Italian](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, for this optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] provides common secondary guidance for the immersion entry pathways; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. With the actual Italian course [S1], this supports shared content, not local availability, a prescribed classroom support language, or replacement of program language arts.

**Document language:** English, matching the reviewed source language; Italian is the language being learned. This reference does not set the language of classroom support.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and share connected meaning in Italian, select language appropriate to context, and consider the identities and experiences represented in Italian-speaking communities and media.

**Prerequisites:** Previous Italian study and familiarity with common conversational and written patterns may help. They are suggestions for using the reference, not official admission requirements.

**Materials:** Nothing beyond access to this reference and its linked source is required for reading; no textbook or classroom materials are prescribed here.

**How to use:** Compare the synthesis with the official expectations and elaborations at [S1]. The local IDs provide stable repository references, not ministry codes or assessment criteria.

This record is for the published 10 course, not the entire 5-10 download compilation. It does not make Italian mandatory.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Listening and viewing with purpose support understanding Italian, and stories provide ways to interpret and reflect on experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening/viewing and stories.

### BI-02

Participation in a new language grows through trying, taking communicative risks, and sustaining effort.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, courage, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Learning Italian can connect people with diverse communities, whose experiences are represented in many forms of cultural expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and access to diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret key information and details in speech and other texts, and consider the viewpoints within stories. Recognize how word selection, formality, directness, and verb choices influence meaning; use strategies to extend understanding and spoken or written expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: word choices, comprehension, story viewpoints, and language-learning strategies.

### CC-02

Exchange ideas orally and in writing, and clarify or verify meaning when communication breaks down. Narrate logically connected events with time expressions and transitions, and choose presentation formats that suit the varied abilities of participants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narration, exchange, presentation formats, clarification, and verification.

### CC-03

Describe how practices, traditions, and attitudes contribute to cultural identity, including the role of stories in personal, family, and community life. Locate and explore Italian media and connect with Italian people and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural identity, story, Italian media, and community experiences.

### CC-04

Examine experiences and worldviews through cultural perspectives, including one's own assumptions. Recognize First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: analysis through a cultural lens and First Peoples knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Use Italian vocabulary, sentence patterns, and common expressions to ask questions, describe events, express degrees of preference, give opinions, and order events. Consider affirmative and negative forms and how tense choices serve these purposes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary, sentence structures, common expressions, questions, activities, likes/dislikes, opinions, and sequences.

### C-02

Express past, present, and future with regular and irregular verbs in context. Preserve the distinction between passato prossimo and imperfetto when referring to the past; the elaborations also illustrate conditional questions and direct and indirect objects in meaningful exchanges.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, time frames and elaborations on questions, activities, and degrees of likes and dislikes.

### C-03

Understand text organization in relation to format, audience, purpose, and register, alongside the organization of stories. Encounter Italian creative works and resources, and regional differences such as dialects, accents, and colloquial expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common texts, story elements, Italian creative works and resources, and regional variations.

### C-04

Learn about past and present contributions of Italians and Italian Canadians. Explore First Peoples connections among oral histories, identity, place, and language, and address cultural appropriation and plagiarism when engaging with others' expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, contributions of Italians and Italian Canadians, First Peoples perspectives, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source distinguishes two Italian past forms; replacing them with an undifferentiated requirement to "use the past tense" loses a meaningful boundary. Examples remain examples rather than a complete compulsory grammar inventory. No fixed word count, external proficiency level, or native-like performance standard is assigned.

Italian media, regional language variation, and the contributions of both Italians and Italian Canadians are part of the scope. They should not be reduced to a single region's traditions or to a required selection of famous works.

Read the complete elaborations at [S1] for context. First Peoples histories and knowledge require community-appropriate protocols; the curriculum does not grant permission to reproduce or adapt them. This reference contains no cultural works or prescribed activities.
