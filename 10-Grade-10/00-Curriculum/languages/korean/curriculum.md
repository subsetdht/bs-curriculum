# Korean - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Korean](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] supplies Early/Late immersion secondary guidance; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. Read with Korean 10 [S1], this supports shared content, not local availability, a prescribed classroom support language, or substitution for program language arts.

**Document language:** English; the primary source reviewed is English. Korean is the language being learned, in oral and written communication.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and express connected meaning in Korean, attending to formality and context while exploring regional practices and Korean communities in Canada and elsewhere.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier Korean learning and familiarity with common spoken and written patterns may help. These suggestions are not official admission or placement requirements.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom texts, equipment, and supports are not prescribed here.

**How to use:** Read the source topics at [S1] alongside this synthesis. Local IDs are stable repository references, not official BC codes, proficiency labels, or an assessment sequence.

The record covers 10 standards, not every grade in the official 5-10 compilation. Course availability and student choice remain separate from published curriculum coverage.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Intentional listening and viewing help learners understand Korean, while narratives give them ways to consider meaning and experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and stories.

### BI-02

Using an unfamiliar language with others calls for experimentation, courage, and continued effort.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Language learning opens relationships with diverse communities, and cultural expression represents experience in many forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and access to diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Recognize how wording and degrees of formality or directness influence meaning. Interpret main information, supporting details, and story viewpoints across speech and other texts, using Korean non-verbal cues and language-learning strategies to aid comprehension.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: word choices, comprehension, stories, strategies, and non-verbal cues.

### CC-02

Exchange ideas orally and in writing, respond to questions and instructions, and clarify or verify meaning. Tell stories using connected time references and transitions, and present information in forms suited to participants' diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narration, exchange, presentation, responses, and clarification.

### CC-03

Investigate regional cultural and linguistic practices and explain their part in identity. Engage with Korean people and communities, and identify and share information about Korean communities in Canada.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: regional practices, community experiences, and Korean communities in Canada.

### CC-04

Analyze one's own and others' experiences and worldviews through cultural perspectives, recognizing First Peoples knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural analysis and First Peoples perspectives and knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Use Korean vocabulary and sentence structures for questions, descriptions of activities or events, and opinions. Account for language etiquette and the differing levels of formality used in speech and writing rather than treating them as interchangeable styles.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary/sentence structures/expressions, types of questions, activities, opinions, and language formality and etiquette.

### C-02

Communicate about past, present, and future, attending to how Korean sentence endings mark when events occur. Use appropriate affirmative and negative forms in context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past/present/future time frames and elaborations on sentence endings and activities.

### C-03

Understand how common texts depend on audience, purpose, register, and format, and recognize how stories are organized. Learn common Korean expressions and differences among regional dialects and other language varieties.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common texts, story elements, dialects and regional variations, and common expressions.

### C-04

Explore Korean artistic expression and Korean Canadian contributions. Connect First Peoples languages and cultures with oral histories, identity, and place, and consider ethical issues involving cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean works of art, Korean Canadians, First Peoples perspectives, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source illustrates levels of Korean formality and time-marking endings; this summary neither removes those distinctions nor adds a comprehensive list of all speech levels or conjugations. Oral and written exchange is expected, but the 10 Content list does not introduce a separate prescribed alphabet inventory or writing-system quota.

Regional Korean practices and Korean communities in Canada both belong in this course. They do not imply a uniform culture or a single experience shared by all Korean speakers. The source does not assign an external proficiency level, fixed vocabulary count, or native-like fluency target.

The complete official elaborations remain at [S1]. Examples are not compulsory activities, texts, or performances. First Peoples oral histories and cultural knowledge require appropriate permission and context; no source or community work is reproduced here.
