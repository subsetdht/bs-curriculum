# Punjabi - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > [Punjabi](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, for students taking this optional additional-language course where offered. [S2] supplies common Early/Late immersion secondary guidance; [S3], printed pp. 40-41, establishes parallel regular-curriculum content for immersion and Francophone programs. Read with Punjabi 10 [S1], this supports shared content without establishing local offerings, classroom support language, or replacement of program language arts.

**Document language:** English, also the language of the reviewed source. Punjabi is the language being learned, including oral and written expression.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and communicate connected ideas in Punjabi, connecting written patterns with pronunciation and meaning while exploring language variation, media, and cultural identity.

**Prerequisites:** Prior Punjabi learning and familiarity with basic reading and conversation may help. These are practical suggestions, not official admission or placement criteria.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference; it is not a prescribed resource or equipment list.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to the official course and elaborations at [S1]. Local IDs identify repository items, not BC standard codes or an assessment sequence.

The record describes 10 standards, not the entire multi-grade download compilation. It does not make Punjabi compulsory for all students or assume home-language experience.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful listening and viewing help learners understand Punjabi, and stories offer ways to interpret and reflect on meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening/viewing with intent and stories.

### BI-02

Speaking and interacting in a new language involve courage, experimentation, and persistence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expression, conversation, risk taking, and perseverance.

### BI-03

Learning Punjabi creates opportunities to connect with diverse communities and encounter cultural expression in many forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cultural expression and interaction with diverse communities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Punjabi letter patterns, pronunciation, and meaning, including using written patterns to anticipate how words sound. Notice how word choices affect meaning, and understand key information, supporting details, and story viewpoints in spoken and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: letter patterns/pronunciation/meaning, word choices, comprehension, and story viewpoints.

### CC-02

Use strategies to extend understanding and oral or written expression, exchange ideas, and resolve uncertain meaning through clarification. Narrate logically connected events with time references and transitions, and choose presentations that accommodate varied abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: strategies, narration, exchange, presentation formats, and clarification/verification.

### CC-03

Describe how practices, traditions, attitudes, and language in Punjabi regions shape cultural identity. Appreciate regional language differences and engage with Punjabi people and communities rather than treating one dialect or place as representative of all.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: regional practices and identity, Punjabi variations, and community experiences.

### CC-04

Analyze personal and shared experiences through different cultural viewpoints, and recognize First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural analysis and First Peoples knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Use Punjabi vocabulary and sentence patterns for more complex questions and instructions, including questions that invite explanation or reflection. Describe people, objects, places, personal interests, activities, and situations, and organize events into sequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary/sentence structures/expressions, complex questions and instructions, activities, descriptions, and event sequences.

### C-02

Communicate across past, present, and future time frames with appropriate language forms. Recognize how format, audience, register, and purpose shape common texts and how narrative elements organize stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, time frames, common text elements, and common story elements.

### C-03

Explore Punjabi artistic expression and media alongside cultural life in varied Punjabi communities and Punjabi Canadians' contributions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi works of art, Punjabi media, cultural aspects of Punjabi communities, and Punjabi Canadians.

### C-04

Understand First Peoples connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place, and consider ethical questions of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Punjabi letter patterns and pronunciation are explicitly connected in the competencies. The source's 10 standards do not prescribe a new alphabet inventory, character quota, or transliteration-only approach. Complex questions include open-ended reasoning, not merely longer sentences.

The course combines oral and written exchange with regional language variation, media, and community experience. It does not require heritage-language knowledge, native-like fluency, or an externally defined proficiency level. Regional Punjabi identities and practices should not be flattened into one national or cultural description.

Complete elaborations remain at [S1]. Suggested media and cultural contexts are not a compulsory set of works or activities. First Peoples histories and community knowledge require permission, attribution, and appropriate context; no cultural works are reproduced.
