# Creative Writing - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > Creative Writing

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This is an English Language Arts option alongside, not instead of, applicable French language arts; see [program evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Develop a creative writing practice that draws on attentive observation, experiments with forms and voice, and improves original work through reflection and revision.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with reading and composing different kinds of texts is useful preparation, not an official admission criterion.

**Materials:** The reference and linked official curriculum; particular literary selections require separate accessibility and rights checks.

**How to use:** Compare these thematic groups with [S1]. IDs are local reference points, not ministry standards numbers. This is a **2-credit** choice in [S2], not a compulsory course for every learner.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Stories and other texts offer ways to investigate identity and human experience, while remaining shaped by the times, places, and communities in which they arise.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, text/story and identity; social, cultural, geographical, and historical construction.

### BI-02

A writer's language can alter how an audience thinks or feels.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language shaping ideas and influencing others.

### BI-03

Creative work grows through noticing the world carefully and continuing to experiment despite uncertainty or setbacks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, observation, risk-taking, and perseverance.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Read, listen, and view strategically to gather ideas for writing. Consider how choices of form, organization, and presentation influence what texts mean and how they affect an audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, information for writing, comprehension strategies, and text forms/features.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Connect personal experience with texts and the wider world; investigate how language forms identity. Think across texts and question bias, contradictions, and distortions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, critical/creative/reflective thinking, identity, personal connections, and bias.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Appreciate the perspectives and values expressed through First Peoples stories and oral traditions, recognizing diversity within and across societies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples story/oral tradition and diversity.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Exchange ideas respectfully, respond to others' texts in several ways, and communicate through purposeful speaking and listening. Explain opinions using supporting evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchange of viewpoints, responses, speaking/listening, and supported opinions.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Transform ideas into original texts through writing and design processes. Rework language for clarity and impact; use Canadian conventions and appropriate acknowledgement of other people's knowledge and work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, planning/developing, refining texts, original texts, conventions, and intellectual property.

## Content

### C-01

Know how genres, text features, and narrative structures create possibilities for storytelling, including structures represented in First Peoples texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Text forms and text genres; Text features and structures.

### C-02

Understand that First Peoples oral texts have ownership and sharing protocols that are not removed by a writer's creative purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, protocols related to ownership of First Peoples oral texts.

### C-03

Develop knowledge of reading and oral-language strategies, reflection on one's learning, and flexible processes for composing and revising.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-04

Explore how voice, style, literary devices, word usage, and conventions contribute to a writer's choices and effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: language features, style, voice, usage, literary elements/devices.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Creative writing does not mean disregarding evidence, intellectual property, or cultural permissions. A student's original expression should not impersonate an authorized holder of another community's story.

The source's illustrative genres and possible course emphases are not a required anthology or set of assignments. Oral, visual, digital, and written texts remain relevant. The Content list and competencies are related but not identical: acknowledgement is explicit in the competencies even though citation is not separately listed in this course's Content.
