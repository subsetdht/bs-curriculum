# English First Peoples Spoken Language - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > EFP Spoken Language

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; an English Language Arts choice, not a replacement for French language arts. See [program evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Develop oral communication while understanding the continuing roles of First Peoples oral traditions, relationships with place, and culturally responsible expression.

**Prerequisites:** Experience listening, discussing, and composing texts is helpful, not a formal entry criterion.

**Materials:** This reference and its official sources; no performance equipment or community story is supplied or prescribed.

**How to use:** Compare the thematic groups with [S1], including cultural-protocol elaborations. Local IDs are not official BC codes. [S2] labels this a **2-credit** catalog component; see the inventory for graduation-course distinctions.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Oral stories deepen understanding of identity and relationships, and reflect the circumstances of their creation and sharing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, oral text/story and identity; social/cultural/geographical/historical construction.

### BI-02

Voice can carry emotional force and influence how people experience a message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, powerful and evocative voice.

### BI-03

First Peoples oral texts can contribute to repairing relationships damaged by colonial policies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, First Peoples oral text and Reconciliation.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Appreciate diverse First Peoples perspectives and explain how oral traditions carry knowledge in historical and contemporary life. Recognize the influence of land and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, story/oral tradition, diversity, historical/contemporary roles, and land/place.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Use inquiry and comprehension strategies to explore ideas across oral and other texts. Consider the effects of context and identity on communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, strategies, critical/creative/reflective thinking, contexts, and language/identities.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Examine how structure, form, literary techniques, and presentation choices shape messages for particular purposes and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, forms/structures/features and literary elements/techniques/devices.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Exchange ideas respectfully and respond personally, creatively, and critically. Use effective speaking and listening in varied contexts and explain opinions with evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, viewpoints, responses, speaking/listening, and supported opinions.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Plan and develop oral and other texts through writing and creative processes, then improve their clarity and audience engagement. Apply appropriate First Peoples and other Canadian language conventions and respect intellectual property and community protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, creative processes, audience engagement, refinement, conventions, and rights/protocols.

## Content

### C-01

Know the forms, purposes, genres, and narrative organization of oral and other texts, including structures found in First Peoples communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Text forms and genres; Text features and structures.

### C-02

Understand recurring themes involving land, family, Elders, spirituality, community, identity, colonial harms, continuity, healing, and reconciliation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Common themes in First Peoples texts and Reconciliation in Canada.

### C-03

Understand the variety and purposes of First Peoples oral traditions. Know protocols for ownership and use, acknowledging territory, and introducing oneself through connections with people and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples oral traditions; Protocols, including territory and situating oneself.

### C-04

Know reading, oral-language, and metacognitive strategies, writing processes, and presentation/performance techniques.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-05

Understand stylistic, literary, and rhetorical effects, language conventions, and the role of citation and acknowledgement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Territorial acknowledgement and self-location are protocol-related content, not universal scripts supplied by this record. Individual Nations and knowledge holders determine appropriate forms and permissions.

The course is distinct from [Spoken Language](../spoken-language/curriculum.md). First Peoples oral knowledge is not merely performance material: it can carry histories, responsibilities, teachings, and relationships. [S2]'s examples are optional emphases, not permission to stage or record protected cultural works. No performances, activities, or stories are reproduced here.
