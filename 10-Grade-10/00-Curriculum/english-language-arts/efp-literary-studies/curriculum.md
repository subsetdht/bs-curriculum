# English First Peoples Literary Studies - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > EFP Literary Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. EFP is an English Language Arts pathway, not a separate language-of-instruction program; see [provincial selection and program evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Interpret authentic First Peoples literature across contexts and forms, connecting literary understanding with identities, histories, place, and responsible representation.

**Prerequisites:** Experience discussing and interpreting literature is helpful, not a formal entry criterion or a requirement for Indigenous ancestry.

**Materials:** This reference and its sources; selecting actual works requires separate cultural-permission, accessibility, and suitability review.

**How to use:** Compare the grouped expectations with [S1]. Local IDs are not ministry codes. [S2] labels this catalog component **2 credits**; see the parent inventory for the distinction between curriculum choices and graduation-course reporting.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Stories help people investigate identity and relationships while revealing the particular settings in which texts are made.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, text/story and identity; socially/culturally/geographically/historically constructed texts.

### BI-02

First Peoples literature offers perspectives essential to understanding Canada's history, current relationships, and possible futures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, First Peoples texts and Canada's past, present, and future.

### BI-03

When First Peoples represent their own experiences, literature can support justice and contribute to reconciliation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, authentic self-representation, justice, and Reconciliation.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Recognize how stories and oral traditions express First Peoples knowledge and perspectives, while appreciating differences within and across societies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, story/oral tradition and First Peoples diversity.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Use strategies for inquiry and comprehension to connect ideas across texts, personal experience, and the world. Consider how personal, social, and cultural identities affect First Peoples texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, strategies, self/text/world, thinking across texts, and identities.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Examine relationships between textual form, literary technique, audience, and meaning. Assess authenticity and identify biased, contradictory, or distorted representations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, forms/structures/features, literary elements/devices, authenticity, and bias.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Build understanding through respectful discussion, varied responses, and purposeful speaking and listening. Use evidence to support opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, viewpoints, personal/creative/critical responses, speaking/listening, and evidence.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Develop and refine texts through writing and design processes. Use First Peoples and other Canadian language conventions appropriately while applying intellectual-property rights and community protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, writing/design, refining, spelling/syntax/diction, and rights/protocols.

## Content

### C-01

Know forms, genres, and narrative structures that support understanding First Peoples texts, including their purposes and organization.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Text forms and genres; Text features and structures.

### C-02

Understand recurring concerns in First Peoples texts: relationships with land, family, Elders, community, and spirituality; the effects of colonization; and identity, continuity, healing, and reconciliation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Common themes in First Peoples texts and Reconciliation in Canada.

### C-03

Understand the purposes and continuing significance of oral traditions, together with protocols governing ownership and use of oral texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples oral traditions; Protocols.

### C-04

Know approaches to reading, oral communication, writing, and reflecting on one's own thinking that support increasingly independent learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-05

Study style, usage, conventions, literary devices, citation, and the distinction between directly stated and inferred meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Authenticity is not simply the presence of an Indigenous character or theme. [S1]'s elaboration emphasizes First Peoples voices, substantial contributions, respectful representation, and context-appropriate storytelling features. First Nations, Métis, and Inuit experiences must not be treated as interchangeable.

The source's possible literary emphases in [S2] are choices, not a prescribed reading list. Reconciliation is connected to colonial harms rather than a demand for a particular personal response. Neither curriculum alignment nor citation permits reproducing or retelling culturally held knowledge. This record remains distinct from [Literary Studies](../literary-studies/curriculum.md).
