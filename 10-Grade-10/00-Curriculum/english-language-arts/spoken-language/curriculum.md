# Spoken Language - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > Spoken Language

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This English Language Arts choice remains distinct from French language arts; see [program evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Develop purposeful oral expression and responsive listening by examining how spoken texts work and improving communication for different audiences.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with discussion and interpreting texts is useful preparation, not an official course-entry requirement.

**Materials:** This reference and linked provincial sources; no recording equipment, performance venue, or published script is prescribed.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the full expectations and elaborations. IDs are local, not ministry codes. [S2] describes a **2-credit** course choice, not a mandatory standalone graduation course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Stories and other texts can deepen understanding of human experience, while the perspectives of listeners and readers affect their interpretations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, text/story, identity, and worldviews.

### BI-02

Texts reflect their cultural, social, geographical, and historical settings; language can in turn shape people's thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, constructed texts and language's influence.

### BI-03

Voice can give communication emotional force and lasting impact.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, powerful and evocative voice.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Gather information for communication and use suitable comprehension strategies across media. Examine how textual forms, presentation, and structure shape meaning and effect.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, sources for writing, strategies, and forms/formats/structures/features.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Explore ideas and personal connections across texts. Consider contexts, values, perspectives, and identities, while noticing bias, contradiction, and distortion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, critical/creative/reflective thinking, contexts, identities, self/text/world, and bias.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Appreciate how First Peoples stories and oral traditions communicate beliefs and perspectives, recognizing cultural diversity rather than treating oral knowledge as one uniform tradition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples story/oral tradition and diversity in oral and other texts.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Use respectful dialogue, attentive listening, and purposeful speaking to build understanding. Select spoken formats for particular purposes and support opinions with evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchange of viewpoints, speaking/listening, spoken formats, and supported opinions.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Develop original spoken and other texts through writing and design processes, then refine them for clarity and impact. Use context-appropriate Canadian conventions and acknowledge intellectual property.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, planning/creating, refining, transforming ideas, conventions, and citations.

## Content

### C-01

Know a range of spoken and other genres, their purposes, and their organizational features. Consider how narrative patterns, including those found in First Peoples texts, shape communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, creative spoken forms; oral text features and narrative structures.

### C-02

Understand First Peoples oral traditions as continuing ways of carrying knowledge, histories, and values, together with protocols governing ownership and sharing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples oral traditions/oral texts and ownership protocols.

### C-03

Know reading, oral-language, and metacognitive strategies, writing processes, and techniques for preparing presentations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-04

Study oral-language features, rhetoric, persuasion, style, literary devices, literal and figurative meaning, usage, conventions, and citation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The oral emphasis does not exclude written, visual, or digital communication. Source examples of performances and presentation formats are not mandatory assessment tasks. No particular speaking style or public platform is prescribed by this summary.

This is distinct from [EFP Spoken Language](../efp-spoken-language/curriculum.md). Learning about First Peoples oral traditions does not confer authority to perform, record, or adapt a community's stories. Local protocols and individual accessibility needs require separate attention.
