# Composition - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > Composition

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; an English Language Arts choice, not a replacement for either French language-arts curriculum. See the [program and selection evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Develop purposeful written communication by considering how other writers work, investigating ideas, and repeatedly improving compositions for their readers.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading varied texts and drafting extended writing is helpful, not an official course-entry requirement.

**Materials:** This reference and access to the linked provincial sources; no textbook or special equipment is prescribed here.

**How to use:** Read the grouped expectations alongside [S1], including its elaborations. Local item IDs identify this repository's summaries, not official BC codes. [S2] describes this as a **2-credit** choice; the catalog is not a list of courses every learner must take.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Reading stories and other communications can complicate and deepen our understanding of identity, relationships, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, exploration of text and story and complex ideas about identity.

### BI-02

Writing emerges from particular communities, places, and times; its language also helps shape readers' ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, social/cultural/geographical/historical construction and language's influence.

### BI-03

Returning to a piece through different stages of writing can make it both more inventive and easier to understand.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, writing processes, creativity, and clarity.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Gather information suited to a writing purpose and question its reliability. Use comprehension strategies across written, oral, visual, and multimodal texts; consider how their organization and presentation affect meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, information sources, relevance/accuracy/reliability, comprehension strategies, and forms/features.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Explore ideas across texts, connect them with one's experience, and examine how language shapes identity. Notice bias, inconsistency, and misleading representations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, critical/creative/reflective thinking, identities, self/text/world, and bias.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Recognize the distinct perspectives conveyed through First Peoples stories and oral traditions, without flattening differences within or between societies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, First Peoples perspectives and diversity.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Build understanding through respectful discussion and attentive speaking and listening. Develop personal, imaginative, and critical responses, and support opinions with evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchanging viewpoints, responding to text, speaking/listening, and evidence.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Plan, draft, design, and revise original texts for different readers and purposes. Improve clarity and effect while using context-appropriate Canadian conventions and acknowledging others' intellectual property.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, writing/design processes, refining, transforming ideas, conventions, and citations.

## Content

### C-01

Know how genres, presentation features, and narrative organization support communication, including recurring and circular structures encountered in First Peoples texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Text forms and genres; Text features and structures.

### C-02

Understand ownership protocols for First Peoples oral texts, including restrictions on who may share a story and in what circumstances.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, protocols related to ownership of First Peoples oral texts.

### C-03

Know strategies for making sense of reading, communicating orally, reflecting on one's learning, and developing writing through revisiting earlier decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes: reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing.

### C-04

Develop knowledge of voice, style, literary techniques, usage, written conventions, and citation, so language choices serve the intended communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: style, voice, usage, literary devices, and citation.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This is a condensed reference, not a required sequence of essay types. The possible focus areas in [S2] illustrate choices rather than prescribe a universal assignment list. Text includes oral, visual, digital, and written communication even in a writing-focused course.

First Peoples oral knowledge is not freely reusable because it appears in a curriculum. Follow the relevant community's permissions as well as citation practices. This record provides neither literary works nor activities, and does not settle individual credit combinations or school timetables.
