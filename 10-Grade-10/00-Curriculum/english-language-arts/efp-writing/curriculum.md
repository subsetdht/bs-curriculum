# English First Peoples Writing - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [English Language Arts](../README.md) > EFP Writing

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. This English Language Arts choice does not replace French language arts; see [program and selection evidence](../README.md#programs-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English; official source language: English.

**Learning objective:** Strengthen written expression through engagement with First Peoples texts, personal and cultural identities, and purposeful processes of developing and revising writing.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading and creating texts is useful preparation, not a formal course-entry rule.

**Materials:** This reference and official sources; no purchased text, community interview, or cultural work is required to use the reference.

**How to use:** Read these groups with [S1] and follow the topic locators. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes. [S2] labels this catalog component **2 credits**; consult the parent inventory for course-reporting distinctions.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Stories and texts help people understand identity and the world while remaining connected with particular times, places, and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, text/story and identity; constructed texts.

### BI-02

Authentic First Peoples self-representation can help counter injustice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, self-representation through authentic First Peoples text and justice.

### BI-03

First Peoples writing and other texts can contribute to reconciliation by informing relationships affected by colonial harms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, First Peoples texts and Reconciliation.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Comprehend and connect:** Appreciate diverse First Peoples perspectives in stories and oral traditions, and explore how language reflects personal and cultural identities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, story/oral tradition, diversity, and language/identities.

### CC-02

**Comprehend and connect:** Gather information for writing and use inquiry and comprehension strategies to connect ideas across texts and lived experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, self/text/world, strategies, information sources, and critical/creative/reflective thinking.

### CC-03

**Comprehend and connect:** Examine how genre, structure, textual features, and literary techniques serve audiences and purposes. Identify bias, contradiction, and distortion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, forms/structures/features, literary elements/devices, and bias.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Exchange viewpoints respectfully, respond personally and critically as well as creatively, and communicate through purposeful speaking and listening. Support opinions with evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, viewpoints, responses, speaking/listening, and evidence.

### CC-05

**Create and communicate:** Develop engaging writing through planning, design, and revision. Improve clarity and effect while using context-appropriate First Peoples and other Canadian conventions and respecting intellectual-property rights and community protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, writing/design processes, refinement, conventions, and rights/protocols.

## Content

### C-01

Know genres and the relationships between a text's purpose, form, and organization, including narrative structures found in First Peoples texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Text forms and genres; Text features and structures.

### C-02

Understand recurring First Peoples concerns involving land, spirituality, family, Elders, community, identity, colonization and decolonization, cultural continuity, healing, and reconciliation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Common themes in First Peoples texts and Reconciliation in Canada.

### C-03

Understand the purposes of First Peoples oral texts and protocols governing their ownership and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples oral traditions; Protocols.

### C-04

Know reading, oral-language, and metacognitive strategies alongside flexible, iterative processes for writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes.

### C-05

Develop knowledge of style, literary devices, usage, conventions, citation, and acknowledgement to support responsible written communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This is not a requirement to write on behalf of a First Peoples community. Authentic self-representation and the diversity of First Nations, Métis, and Inuit experiences must remain visible. The official possible focus areas in [S2] do not license borrowing restricted knowledge or adopting an assumed cultural voice.

Writing-focused study still involves oral, visual, and digital texts. This core summary is neither a prescribed genre sequence nor an assignment list. Source elaborations supply the full examples; no official documents or creative works are reproduced.
