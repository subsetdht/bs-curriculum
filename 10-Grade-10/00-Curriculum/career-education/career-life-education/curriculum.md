# Career-Life Education - Grades 10-12 delivery, Grade 10 registry host

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared career-learning expectations. The current graduation policy guide establishes the career requirement and parallel secondary program content [S2].

**Document language:** English; the primary standards reviewed are English. The registry entry cited here describes an English reporting code, not a rule that immersion or Francophone instruction must be English.

**Learning objective:** Develop a flexible approach to career and life possibilities through self-understanding, reciprocal relationships, informed exploration, and revisable planning.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier reflection on interests, learning, relationships, and community roles provides useful experience. This record does not require a chosen occupation, paid employment, or a formal course prerequisite.

**Materials:** No special materials or disclosure of personal employment, family, or financial information is required to read this reference.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the actual standards and elaborations. Local IDs locate this repository's original grouped summaries; they are not official BC codes or a sequence of tasks.

**Administrative host versus delivery:** The ordinary CLE registry entry identifies Grade 10 and 4 allowable credits [S3]. That administrative grade explains `grade: "10"` and this record's repository location. It is **not a designated teaching year**: the policy guide states that learners may begin CLE and CLC in Grades 10, 11, or 12 and supports flexible delivery (printed p. 21) [S2]. Accordingly, `source.grade_scope` records all three delivery grades.

The complete CLE curriculum is shared across the 4-credit CLE reporting option and the two-part CLEA/CLEB option, not three independent sets of learning standards. The [course landing](README.md#reporting-options) inventories these alternatives and the French counterparts. Adult-only CLE 12 reporting is excluded. [Career-Life Connections](../../../../curriculum-special-entries.md#career-life-connections) is a separate shared course, not duplicated here.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Career-life planning remains open to revision as people learn, reflect, and encounter new possibilities. Continued learning helps sustain that adaptability beyond a single school or employment decision.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, recurring planning/reflection/adaptation/decision making and lifelong learning.

### BI-02

Personal strengths and circumstances interact with community conditions and wider trends. Understanding these influences helps explain why a preferred pathway may change and why choices differ between people.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, internal and external influences, including local and global trends.

### BI-03

Reciprocal relationships create opportunities to learn about possible roles and futures. Networks are more than routes to personal advantage: their value also comes from contributing to others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, networks and reciprocal relationships with family, peers, and community.

### BI-04

Well-being depends in part on how personal and working lives fit together, making balance an important consideration in career-life decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, balance between personal and work life.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Examine:** Investigate how personal and public representations affect opportunities, including benefits and risks of a digital presence. Consider the role of personal and employment networks in exploring possible futures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Examine: profiles, digital footprints, and networks.

### CC-02

**Interact:** Use a mentor's guidance and collaborate with supportive community members to investigate the factors surrounding career-life choices. Communicate strengths, abilities, and accomplishments purposefully to others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Interact: mentorship, reciprocal influences, and communication of strengths.

### CC-03

**Experience:** Participate respectfully, inclusively, and safely in different career-life environments. Respond to challenges, connect learning within and beyond school with possible pathways, and develop ways to maintain a workable school/work/life balance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Experience: interactions, challenges/opportunities, experiential learning, and balance.

### CC-04

**Initiate:** Reflect on personal growth and different life roles, using that understanding to develop initial profiles and flexible plans for further learning and exploration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Initiate: roles, personal growth, preliminary profiles, and flexible plans.

## Content

### C-01

**Career-life development:** Understand how mentorship, self-assessment, and reflection support knowledge of oneself as a learner. Consider different ways of knowing, including cultural and First Peoples perspectives, and connect personal development with educated-citizen, employment, leadership, and collaboration competencies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Career-life development: mentorship, preferred ways of knowing/learning, competencies and skills, and self-assessment.

### C-02

**Career-life development:** Examine well-being strategies, personal and public self-representation, appropriate workplace conduct, and workplace safety. The elaborations connect safety with employment standards, health-and-safety rights and responsibilities, and prevention of harassment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Career-life development: well-being, representation, workplace behaviour and safety, with related elaborations.

### C-03

**Connections with community:** Understand inclusion and contributions to society through attention to different worldviews and perspectives. Consider how personal relationships, environmental conditions, and land use both shape and are affected by career-life choices, including respectful attention to First Peoples rights and relationships with place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Connections with community: inclusive practices, factors informing choices, cultural influences, and land-use elaborations.

### C-04

**Connections with community:** Examine networking and employment-marketing strategies alongside the value of volunteerism for both individuals and communities. These are ways of understanding relationships and opportunities, not a requirement to disclose a private network or obtain a particular job.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Connections with community: personal networking, employment marketing, and volunteerism.

### C-05

**Career-life planning:** Research possible roles and pathways and learn to organize authentic evidence of career-life development. Use decision-making and innovative-thinking models to keep goals and plans responsive to changing circumstances.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Career-life planning: development research, evidence organization, and flexible decision-making/planning models.

### C-06

**Career-life planning:** Connect financial planning tools with opportunities before and after graduation and with local and global labour-market conditions. Consider possible futures in relation to both personal priorities and the conditions that make them feasible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Career-life planning: financial tools, pre-/post-graduation opportunities, and labour/market trends.

## Elaborations and boundaries

CLE supports exploration and initial flexible planning, not a requirement to settle on one occupation. The source's examples of profiles, planning tools, community involvement, and employment documents are not an added lesson or submission checklist.

CLE and CLC have distinct curricula. The CLC career-life exploration requirement and capstone must not be silently imported as separate CLE Content requirements. The source grade band and reporting alternatives likewise do not justify inventing different CLE standards for Grades 10, 11, and 12.

First Peoples perspectives, land relationships, inclusive practices, and reconciliation remain within career learning. Use community knowledge appropriately and respect permissions. Nothing here requests private student information or authorizes sharing personal profiles.

The registry's open date and its program-guide title are administrative information, not proof of a revision date for the current web standards. This record therefore retains `source.version: null`.
