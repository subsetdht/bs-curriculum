# Français langue seconde - immersion - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [French Immersion Language Arts](../README.md) > Français langue seconde - immersion

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion and Late French Immersion. [S2] separately identifies the entry pathways and directs Late Immersion in Grades 8-12 to the same secondary guidance. This is not first-language Francophone language arts, Core French, or English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English; official source and course title: French. This original synthesis is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators are English glosses.

**Learning objective:** Develop interpretation and expression in French by exploring short fiction, poetry, cultural variation, and the relationship between language choices and meaning.

**Prerequisites:** Prior immersion experience in reading, listening, and composing French texts is helpful; the reference sets no formal course-admission or proficiency threshold. Program entry rules are a separate matter in [S2].

**Materials:** This reference and the official source; no particular anthology, language software, or purchased text is prescribed.

**How to use:** Compare the grouped expectations with [S1] and its French elaborations. Local IDs are repository references. [S3] lists this course as **4 credits** in the French Immersion dual-diploma requirements; the course does not replace English Language Arts.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Variation in French carries cultural meaning and makes the diversity of Francophone communities visible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), language variation and Francophone diversity.

### BI-02

Looking closely at how texts communicate can reveal both their construction of meaning and the complexity of human experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), text analysis, linguistic/textual meaning, and human life.

### BI-03

Poetic choices enrich expression and influence an audience's response.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), poetic elements, writing, and intended effects.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Explore and reflect:** Analyze how communication strategies affect an audience. Recognize varieties of French and cultural expression, and examine the purposes of stories in Francophone and Indigenous cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: communication effects, story roles, and Francophone language/cultural variation.

### CC-02

**Explore and reflect:** Distinguish abstract from concrete ideas and situate works and authors within social, historical, and cultural contexts. Use themes and poetic features to interpret implied messages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: abstract/concrete, author/work context, themes/poetic elements, and implicit meaning.

### CC-03

**Explore and reflect:** Analyze plot and character development to understand a character's role in a literary short story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: plot, character development, and the nouvelle littéraire.

### CC-04

**Create and communicate:** Adapt expression, register, and presentation format to the situation and participants' abilities. Develop an individual writing style, use poetic effects purposefully, and make messages more precise through enrichment strategies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: context/formats, writing style, register, poetic effects, and precision.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how register, prosody, voice, and non-verbal expression shape communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Communication strategies: registers, prosody, verbal and non-verbal.

### C-02

Understand protocols governing Indigenous ownership of stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Cultural and historical elements: Indigenous story ownership.

### C-03

Know the features and organization of literary short fiction, along with poetic techniques, related word groups, and idiomatic or regional expressions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Literary tools and Textual organization: nouvelle features, poetry, lexical fields, expressions, narrative and actantial structures.

### C-04

Develop knowledge of possessive and demonstrative adjectives and pronouns, verb moods and tenses, and active and passive voice. The source elaborates past hypothetical constructions and the past conditional.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Language tools: adjectives/pronouns, modes/tenses, and active/passive voice.

### C-05

Know approaches to revising and enriching expression through more deliberate word choice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), Revision strategies and Enrichment tools: word choice.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's introductory context elaboration identifies literary short stories and poetry; these are not a requirement for a particular author or anthology. The French *nouvelle littéraire* is short fiction, not a news report. Examples of poetic devices and grammar clarify the standards rather than create an exhaustive practice list.

Both immersion pathways are supported at this grade by the provincial policy; this does not erase their different entry points or authorize copying transition expectations into Grade 10. Cultural variation is not a hierarchy of valid and invalid French. Permission to share Indigenous stories remains with the appropriate holders.
