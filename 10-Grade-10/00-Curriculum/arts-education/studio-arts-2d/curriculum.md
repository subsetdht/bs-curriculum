# Studio Arts 2D - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Studio Arts 2D

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Develop and refine two-dimensional artworks through expressive design, informed material choices, critical reflection, and cultural and social connections.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing images or experimenting with drawing and painting may help; this reference sets no formal portfolio or entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary alongside [S1]. Local IDs are stable repository references, not official BC codes or a sequence of projects. Studio Arts 2D is one course choice, not a requirement to take all Arts courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Intentional making transforms materials into art through the interaction of bodily action and thought.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intention/materials and body/mind.

### BI-02

Visual art can share traditions and worldviews while helping people understand identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, aesthetic experiences and identity.

### BI-03

Artistic development takes time, patience, and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth as an artist.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Design, create, and refine two-dimensional works using sensory experience, imagination, and inquiry. Consider audience, meaning, and emotion; experiment and develop technique across styles and movements while using tools, materials, and space safely and responsibly.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: inspiration, design/refinement, audience, risks, expression, technique, and safe use.

### CC-02

Address creative challenges by analyzing artists' use of materials, technology, processes, and environments. Evaluate design choices, develop personal responses to aesthetic questions, and reflect on contextual influences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: challenges, artists' methods, design choices, aesthetic questions, and contexts.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate two-dimensional works in different settings. Show respect for people and place and respond to social and environmental concerns through artistic expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation/sharing, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Create work reflecting personal, cultural, and historical contexts. Explore First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing, and investigate relationships between two-dimensional art, culture, and society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: contextual creation, First Peoples/local knowledge, and art/culture/society.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how visual elements and design principles organize two-dimensional work. Develop images from ideas through exploratory and transformative strategies, using symbols and metaphors to convey perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual elements, design principles, image development strategies, and symbols/metaphors.

### C-02

Learn materials, technologies, and processes suited to two-dimensional art. Drawing and painting methods can develop an image through marks, tonal shading, perspective, or layered colour; these illustrate possibilities rather than a requirement to use every method. Understand creative processes as ongoing exploration, selection, combination, and refinement rather than a single linear recipe.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, 2D materials/technologies/processes and creative processes.

### C-03

Consider artist and audience roles, visual culture's influence on identity and self-perception, and how two-dimensional works participate in social justice issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artist/audience, visual culture, and social justice.

### C-04

Explore traditional, innovative, and intercultural artists, including First Peoples worldviews, stories, and practices expressed through two-dimensional work. Understand cultural appropriation and plagiarism as ethical concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artists' contributions, First Peoples perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This course focuses on two-dimensional art, while Art Studio is broader and Studio Arts 3D has different material considerations. Source examples of pigments, tools, drawing or painting processes, and art movements illustrate possibilities rather than requiring every medium or technique. Technology includes ordinary art tools, not only electronics.

Safe material use includes environmental responsibility. Study of a community's visual traditions is not permission to copy designs, stories, or culturally restricted practices. Seek suitable guidance, context, attribution, and permissions rather than treating cultural work as a style to imitate.
