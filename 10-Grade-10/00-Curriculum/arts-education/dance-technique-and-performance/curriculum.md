# Dance Technique and Performance - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Dance Technique and Performance

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Build safe, expressive dance technique and performance skills while interpreting movement, collaborating, and connecting dance with cultural and community contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Prior movement practice and familiarity with dance vocabulary may help; this reference sets no formal training or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary with [S1]. Local IDs identify summaries, not official BC codes or a prescribed sequence. This is a course choice, not a requirement to take every Dance or Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Dancers develop through persistent practice, resilience, and informed creative risk, as well as collaboration and critical exchange.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth and collaboration.

### BI-02

Technique and performance knowledge become embodied through work with dance styles; creating, performing, and responding are connected aspects of dance as art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, embodied technique/skills and creating/performing/responding.

### BI-03

Experiencing dance can change perception, thought, and feeling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, transformative aesthetic experiences.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop expressive bodily control using sound anatomical and developmental principles. Explore historical and contemporary styles and how movement interacts with sound, imagery, and form. Combine dance elements and technical skills to communicate intention, and invent and perform phrases in solo and group settings while considering audience and venue.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: kinesthetic awareness, genres, movement principles, meaning, innovative phrases, performance contexts, and audience/venue.

### CC-02

Analyze and interpret dance using its terminology, refining concepts and technique through rehearsal reflection and constructive feedback. Consider social, cultural, historical, political, and personal influences, and responsibilities toward people, audiences, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: analysis, refinement, feedback, responsibilities, and context.

### CC-03

Use technical language to document and respond to performance. Communicate and interpret emotions, cultural identity, values, and perspectives through movement, including responses to local issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: vocabulary/documentation, interpretation, local issues, and cultural expression.

### CC-04

Make responsible movement, music, theme, and costume choices. Explore First Peoples knowledge, local cultural perspectives, and other ways of knowing; connect dance with issues and communities at local, regional, and national levels and investigate further learning and career possibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, First Peoples/local knowledge, opportunities, and community connections.

## Content

### C-01

Understand body, space, time, dynamics, and relationships in dance, together with techniques specific to a chosen genre or style. Learn the history and theory that give those techniques context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements, technical skills, and history/theory of a technique/genre/style.

### C-02

Relate bones, muscles, and joints to movement principles such as alignment, mobility, stability, and weight transfer. Develop kinesthetic and spatial awareness and understand protocols for preventing harm.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, movement principles, awareness, bones/muscles/joints, and safety protocols.

### C-03

Understand how technical accuracy, expressive presence, and cognitive preparation support rehearsal and performance. Learn notation and the relationships among dancers, choreographers, and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, rehearsal/performance skills and elaborations, notation, and participant roles.

### C-04

Study local and intercultural performers and influential dance innovators. Examine First Peoples and cross-cultural perspectives communicated in dance, including ethical responsibilities around appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, innovators/performers, First Peoples worldviews, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source allows exploration of a specific genre or a variety of genres; its examples are not a compulsory catalogue of techniques. This record does not turn anatomical knowledge into medical advice or impose a single body type, flexibility target, or professional training standard.

Creative risk means informed artistic experimentation, not unsafe movement. Cultural study and interpretation do not grant permission to reproduce dances, music, stories, or designs. Seek appropriate permissions and community context, including for costume and thematic choices.
