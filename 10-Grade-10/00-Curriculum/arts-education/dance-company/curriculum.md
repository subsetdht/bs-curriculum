# Dance Company - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Dance Company

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Contribute to a performance-based dance ensemble through expressive movement, cooperative rehearsal, interpretation of choreography, and responsible production practice.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience learning movement phrases or working in an ensemble may help; this reference sets no formal entry or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the Grade 10 standards and elaborations behind each local item. IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a prescribed sequence. This is one course option, not a requirement to take all Arts courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Developing as a dancer involves sustained effort, recovery from difficulties, informed creative chances, and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth through risk taking, perseverance, resilience, and reflection.

### BI-02

Participation in a dance company's rehearsal and performance processes can develop identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, dance company processes and identity.

### BI-03

Choreographic meaning emerges through artistic choices in movement and production, including sound, costume, lighting, and setting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, choreographic communication and the choreographer's intent.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create, remember, rehearse, refine, and perform dance works individually and within a company. Explore historical and contemporary styles, the interaction of movement with other production elements, and how context influences a technique. Use sound movement principles, work with different choreographers, and prepare and recover physically and mentally.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: repertoire, company processes, expressive body, movement principles, contexts, choreographers, and preparation/recovery.

### CC-02

Take creative chances to express choreographic intent while considering audience, venue, and place. Interpret work using dance terminology, collaborate in rehearsal, apply feedback, and reflect on performance with awareness of fellow dancers and production personnel.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: creative risks and audience/venue/place; Reason and reflect: dance language, collaboration, feedback, and awareness.

### CC-03

Use technical vocabulary to document and critically discuss the company's rehearsals, compositions, and performances. Communicate ideas and emotions in relation to the choreographer's intended meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: technical documentation and choreographic intent.

### CC-04

Make responsible movement, musical, thematic, and costume choices. Connect dance with community issues, First Peoples and local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing; explore future opportunities in dance while maintaining health and injury-prevention practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, opportunities, First Peoples/local knowledge, local issues, and safety.

## Content

### C-01

Understand company roles and responsibilities, stage etiquette, and the technical and expressive skills needed to learn, refine, and perform choreography with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, company roles, rehearsal/performance skills, technical/expressive skills, and stage etiquette.

### C-02

Learn how body, space, time, dynamics, and relationships shape dance. Relate technique and movement principles to kinesthetic and spatial awareness, and understand safety protocols that protect dancers and others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements, techniques/movement principles, awareness, and safety.

### C-03

Understand how composition skills, forms, structures, choreographic devices, and design principles organize a work. Learn notation as a means of representing its movement and form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, compositional skills/forms/structures, devices, design principles, and notation.

### C-04

Explore innovators in different dance traditions and contexts, including First Peoples and cross-cultural worldviews expressed in movement. Understand why attribution alone does not resolve plagiarism or inappropriate cultural borrowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance innovators, First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source defines a dance company as a performance-based ensemble. Its examples of choreographers include students and teachers as well as guest artists; it does not require a professional company or a particular audition system. Technical growth must remain anatomically and developmentally appropriate.

Styles, forms, and production examples are illustrative rather than a compulsory repertoire. Seek permission and suitable cultural context before using community-specific dances, stories, music, or designs. Creative risk does not override injury prevention or respectful treatment of performers.
