# Dance Foundations - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Dance Foundations

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Explore dance as an expressive and culturally situated art through movement technique, composition, performance, and reflection.

**Prerequisites:** Experience exploring movement and cooperating with others may help; this reference sets no formal training requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary with [S1] and consult its elaborations. Local IDs support traceability, not official BC coding or a fixed teaching sequence. This is a course option, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Dance connects personal identity and belonging with the histories, values, and cultural communities that shape individual and collective expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity/belonging and the roots of expression.

### BI-02

The moving body can express artistic ideas and offer experiences through which traditions, stories, and worldviews are shared.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the body as instrument and aesthetic experiences.

### BI-03

Developing as both a dancer and choreographer involves persistence, resilience, and thoughtful reconsideration of one's work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth as dancer/choreographer.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore dance elements and techniques across historical and contemporary styles. Develop expressive movement, combine it with sound, imagery, and form, and use choreographic devices to invent phrases. Create and perform individually and with others, considering audience and venue while trying unfamiliar possibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: genres, expressive body, movement phrases/devices, collaboration, creative risks, and audience/venue.

### CC-02

Describe and respond to movement using dance language. Reflect on rehearsal and performance, use constructive feedback, and respect oneself, fellow participants, audiences, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: dance language, reflection, feedback, and respect.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate dance experiences. Perform steps, combinations, and works in different settings, and develop ways to communicate and interpret emotions and ideas. Use individual and group compositions to express movement preferences, cultural perspectives, and responses to personal or social concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation, performance, communication strategies, issues, and identity/values.

### CC-04

Make socially responsible choices in dance and production. Explore performers and genres from local to international contexts, First Peoples and local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing. Consider dance's influence on societies and its potential for personal and community connection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, performers/genres, First Peoples perspectives, societal impact, and connections.

## Content

### C-01

Learn the relationships among body, space, time, dynamics, and partners or surroundings. Relate genre-specific techniques to anatomically and developmentally sound movement, bodily awareness, and safety protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements, technique/genre/style skills, movement principles, kinesthetic awareness, and safety.

### C-02

Understand the technical, expressive, and cognitive dimensions of rehearsal and performance. Learn how choreographic forms and devices organize or transform movement, and how vocabulary and notation represent it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, rehearsal/performance skills, forms/structures, devices, and notation/vocabulary.

### C-03

Investigate the roles of dancers, choreographers, and audiences, the history and theory of different genres, and the contributions of influential innovators.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, participant roles, dance innovators, and history/theory of genres.

### C-04

Understand that movement can communicate traditional and contemporary First Peoples and cross-cultural worldviews. Learn to distinguish respectful engagement from appropriation or plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives and ethics of appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Foundations combines creating, performing, and responding; it is not solely conditioning or learning one dance style. Technique and genre examples illustrate the source rather than setting a compulsory style list. Notation includes informal as well as formal systems.

Dance learning should respect bodily differences, development, and safe participation. Study of First Peoples perspectives does not authorize imitation of restricted dances, designs, music, or cultural roles. Seek appropriate guidance, permissions, and context for community knowledge.
