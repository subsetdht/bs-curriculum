# Photography - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Photography

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Create and interpret expressive photographs by connecting composition, light, image-making processes, cultural context, and responsible sharing.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing images or experimenting with a camera may help; this reference sets no formal equipment-ownership or entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary with [S1]. Follow locators for official expectations and process qualifications. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a project sequence. Photography is a course option, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Photography uses captured images for artistic expression, joining bodily perception and action with thought.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, photography as an art form and interplay of mind/body.

### BI-02

Photographic experiences can convey stories and worldviews and help people examine identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, traditions/perspectives/stories and identity.

### BI-03

Photographers develop through patient practice over time and reflection on their work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth as a photographer.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create images from sensory experience, imagination, and inquiry for an intended audience and purpose. Experiment, refine technique across styles and genres, and use materials, equipment, and workspace safely.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: inspiration, possibilities/risks, meaning, audience, skills/styles/genres, and safe use.

### CC-02

Analyze how photographers use materials, technologies, processes, and settings. Evaluate compositional choices and develop personal responses to questions about photographic art and aesthetic experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: photographers' methods, design choices, and aesthetic questions.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate photographic images, showing respect for people and place. Use photography to communicate and respond to social and environmental concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation/sharing, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Create photographs reflecting personal, cultural, social, environmental, and historical contexts. Explore First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing, and practise digital citizenship throughout making and sharing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: contexts, First Peoples knowledge, and digital citizenship.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how visual elements, including light, exposure, and contrast, interact with composition. Develop images through choices about framing, viewpoint, emphasis, and transformations such as cropping or layering.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual elements, principles of composition, and image development strategies.

### C-02

Learn materials, processes, techniques, and technologies for **one or more** of digital, darkroom, and alternative photography. Understand relevant creative processes; photographic chemistry applies when using film or chemical alternative processes, rather than becoming a requirement for every digital pathway.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, materials/processes/technologies and the one-or-more-types qualification; photo chemistry and creative processes.

### C-03

Understand how lenses and mirrors form images and how transparent, translucent, and opaque objects affect light. Learn how optical instruments sense light and how camera parts and functions contribute to image making.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, behaviours of light and ways of sensing light.

### C-04

Explore the history of photography, relationships between photographer and viewer, and the influence of visual culture in social and other media.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, photographer/viewer roles, visual culture, and history of photography.

### C-05

Study traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews, stories, and histories expressed through photography. Understand responsibilities involving cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source does not require learners to complete every photographic process or own a particular camera. Chemical-process examples are not handling instructions; appropriate safety guidance is essential when those processes are used.

Digital citizenship includes privacy laws and respectful information sharing. A photograph's availability online does not establish reuse permission, consent to publication, or authority to represent a community. Seek appropriate rights and cultural permissions, and avoid decontextualized use of First Peoples images or stories.
