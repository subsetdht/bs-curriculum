# Contemporary Music - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Contemporary Music

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Explore contemporary musical expression through performance, improvisation, combinations of styles, and informed responses to changing musical and cultural contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Prior music making or attentive listening may help; this reference sets no formal instrument, training, or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Compare this Grade 10 summary with [S1] and its elaborations. Local item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. This is one course option, not a requirement to take all Arts courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Personal and collective music making draws on histories, communities, and cultures and can help people explore identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, roots of expression and identity/belonging.

### BI-02

Musical development joins sensory experience with persistent practice, resilience, and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth and interplay of the senses.

### BI-03

Music's aesthetic impact can contribute to change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, aesthetic experiences and change.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Perform alone and with ensembles, developing expression and relevant technique. Improvise and combine genres or styles to explore musical ideas, personal stories, and values; investigate how place, time, and other contexts influence the music.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: solo/ensemble performance, improvisation, combining genres, contexts, technique, and personal voice.

### CC-02

Analyze musicians' choices in composition, arranging, and performance using musical terminology. Consider technique, technology, environment, style, and one's instrumental, vocal, or collaborative role; reflect on rehearsal experiences and growth to make informed decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: composition/arranging/performance, reflection, ensemble roles, and styles.

### CC-03

Document and share musical work, appreciate others' contributions, and apply constructive feedback. Use musical language and personal or cultural perspectives to communicate, including responses to social and global concerns, while respecting participants and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation, feedback, language, identity, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Connect with people locally and beyond through responsible musical participation. Combine technical understanding with contextual observation, maintain instruments and equipment appropriately, and use self-care to prevent performance-related injury.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, connections, decisions, equipment care, and injury prevention.

## Content

### C-01

Understand musical elements, principles, theory, terminology, and symbols. Relate these to techniques, technologies, and vocabulary appropriate to the particular musical idiom or genre.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, musical elements/principles/theory and genre-specific techniques/vocabulary/technologies/context.

### C-02

Learn how creative processes develop musical work and how movement, sound, imagery, and form interact. Understand the contributions of performer, audience, and venue.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, creative processes, movement/sound/image/form, and performer/audience/venue roles.

### C-03

Investigate changing trends, innovative musicians, and the histories of diverse genres and traditions. The source explicitly includes First Nations, Métis, Inuit, Canadian, and wider world contexts rather than treating contemporary music as a single popular genre.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, emerging/evolving trends, styles/genres/traditions and elaboration, innovative artists, and genre history.

### C-04

Understand the ethical difference between creative influence and appropriation or plagiarism, including when combining genres and drawing on cultural traditions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Contemporary Music is a distinct curriculum, not merely an alias for Instrumental Music or Choral Music. Ensembles can vary in size, instrumentation, and voicing; no specific band format, instrument, software, or commercial repertoire is prescribed here.

Blending styles does not remove rights in songs, recordings, or community knowledge. Use appropriate permissions, attribution, and cultural context. Safe practice includes attention to hearing, vocal strain, and repetitive-use injuries; this reference is not medical or equipment-repair instruction.
