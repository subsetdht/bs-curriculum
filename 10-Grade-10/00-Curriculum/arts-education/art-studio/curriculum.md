# Art Studio - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Art Studio

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Develop visual expression through purposeful material and design choices, artistic experimentation, critical interpretation, and cultural and social inquiry.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience exploring images and art materials may help; this reference sets no formal portfolio or entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 summary with [S1] and consult the official elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a prescribed project sequence. Art Studio is one course choice, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Artistic purpose gives materials expressive meaning through the interaction of thought and bodily making.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intention/materials and interplay of mind/body.

### BI-02

Visual experiences can communicate stories and worldviews while providing ways to explore identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, traditions/perspectives/stories and identity.

### BI-03

Artists develop through time, patient practice, and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artistic growth.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create work from sensory experience, imagination, and inquiry, considering audience and intended meaning. Explore possibilities, take informed artistic risks, and develop skills across styles and movements while using materials, tools, and space safely and responsibly.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: inspiration, risks, audience, expression, skills/styles/movements, and safe use.

### CC-02

Analyze artists' use of materials, technologies, processes, and environments, and evaluate design decisions. Develop personal responses to aesthetic questions and reflect on how different contexts influence works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: artists' methods, design choices, aesthetic questions, and contexts.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate visual work with respect for people and place. Communicate ideas and respond to social and environmental concerns through art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation/sharing, respect, ideas, and issues.

### CC-04

Explore First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing through visual art. Create works that reflect personal, cultural, and historical contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: First Peoples/local knowledge and contextual creation.

## Content

### C-01

Understand visual elements and design principles as tools for organizing an image or artwork. Learn strategies that transform ideas into images and use symbols and metaphors to represent perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual elements, design principles, image development, and symbols/metaphors.

### C-02

Learn how materials, techniques, and image-making technologies support creative processes from exploration and selection through refinement and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, materials/techniques/technologies and creative processes.

### C-03

Consider artists' and audiences' roles, the influence of visual culture on identity and self-perception, and visual art's relationship to social justice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artist/audience, visual culture, and social justice.

### C-04

Study traditional, innovative, and intercultural artists alongside First Peoples worldviews, stories, and histories. Understand why cultural appropriation and plagiarism raise responsibilities beyond acknowledging an influence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artists' contributions, First Peoples perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Art Studio is a broad visual-art course, distinct from the more specifically two- or three-dimensional Studio Arts courses. The source treats available materials as open-ended and technologies as including ordinary image-making tools; it does not require digital equipment or a fixed list of media.

The listed styles, movements, and image strategies illustrate possibilities rather than a compulsory art-history survey or project plan. Safe use includes environmental responsibility. Learning about First Peoples work does not grant permission to copy community designs or stories; use appropriate context, guidance, and permissions.
