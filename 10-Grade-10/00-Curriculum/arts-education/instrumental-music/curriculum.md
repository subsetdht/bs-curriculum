# Instrumental Music - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Instrumental Music

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Develop musical interpretation and expressive instrumental technique through solo and ensemble performance, improvisation, contextual inquiry, and safe practice.

**Prerequisites:** Prior instrument exploration, ensemble participation, or music reading may help; this reference sets no formal proficiency or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 family record with [S1]. The official course inventory [S5] includes **Concert Band 10, Orchestra 10, Jazz Band 10, and Guitar 10** under Instrumental Music. These options share this published curriculum; they are not four additional sets of standards. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. Not every learner takes every option.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

History, culture, and community shape musical expression, which can develop understanding of identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, roots of expression and identity/belonging.

### BI-02

Musicianship develops through perseverance, resilience, and reflection, with the senses working together in musical experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth and interplay of the senses.

### BI-03

Experiencing music can reshape thoughts and feelings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, transformative aesthetic experiences.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Perform varied styles in solo, small-ensemble, and large-ensemble settings. Improve technical control and expression, improvise and experiment, and investigate music that reflects personal stories and values alongside influences of place, time, and other contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: performance contexts, expression, improvisation, styles, contexts, technique, and personal voice.

### CC-02

Use musical terminology to analyze technique, technology, and environment in composition and performance. Reflect on rehearsal, growth, and the function of one's musical contribution in an ensemble, using stylistic analysis to guide decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: musicians' choices, reflection, voice within ensemble, and styles.

### CC-03

Document and share musical experiences, use constructive feedback, and communicate in discipline-specific language. Bring personal and cultural perspectives to performance, respond to social and global issues, and respect other participants and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation, feedback, language, identity/perspective, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Participate responsibly and connect with others locally, regionally, and globally. Make musical decisions using technical and contextual knowledge, care for instruments and equipment, and practise self-care to prevent performance-related harm.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, connections, decisions, safe maintenance, and injury prevention.

## Content

### C-01

Understand musical structure and expression through elements, principles, theory, vocabulary, and symbols, including rhythm, pitch, harmony, tone colour, notation, and expressive directions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements/principles/vocabulary/symbols/theory of music and elaboration.

### C-02

Learn techniques relevant to the chosen instrument or family. For winds, the source addresses breath, embouchure, fingering or slide control, articulation, and tone; percussion emphasizes controlled use of hands and implements, including pitched instruments. Keyboard technique connects touch and pedalling with fingering, chords, accompaniment, and balance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, instrument-specific techniques: winds, percussion, and keyboard.

### C-03

For orchestral strings, technique joins left-hand control with bowing or plucking, intonation, and tone. Guitar technique includes chordal and single-note playing, picking or strumming, articulation, accompaniment, notation or tablature, and differences between acoustic and electric playing. Learners study techniques for their **instrument(s) of choice**, not all listed instruments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, instrument-specific techniques: orchestral strings, guitar, and explicit instrument-choice note.

### C-04

Understand technical strategies and technologies, the development of creative work, and relationships among movement, sound, image, and form. Consider performer, audience, and venue roles.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, technical skills/strategies/technologies, creative processes, movement/sound/image/form, and participant/venue roles.

### C-05

Study varied genres in historical and cultural context, including traditional and contemporary First Peoples and cross-cultural worldviews expressed in music. Understand responsibilities concerning cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, genre history, First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The four named options in [S5] share one official curriculum; no independent option-specific standards were found in that inventory. The source's instrument-choice limitation is essential: do not convert its broad technique list into a requirement to master all instrument families. Ensemble examples do not prescribe local staffing or repertoire.

Safe care covers instruments, equipment, and the musician, including hearing and repetitive-use risks. No repertoire or cultural tradition is free to copy because it appears in curriculum study. Obtain performance and reproduction rights where needed, plus appropriate community permissions and context.
