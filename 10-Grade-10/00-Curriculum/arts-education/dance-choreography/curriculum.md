# Dance Choreography - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Dance Choreography

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Turn movement ideas into purposeful dance compositions, using choreographic structures, collaboration, reflection, and culturally responsible choices.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with movement exploration and describing dance may help; this reference sets no formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary alongside [S1] and its elaborations. Local item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. Choreography is one course choice, not a requirement to take every Dance or Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Choreographers organize dance elements and composition skills to give abstract movement ideas a bodily form that communicates.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, foundations of choreography, translating abstract ideas, and creative expression.

### BI-02

Creating dance provides a way to explore identity and relationships with communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and sense of belonging.

### BI-03

Choreography can develop through the exchange of ideas, cooperative experimentation, and critical reflection with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, choreographers' collaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Design and explore compositions using bodily awareness and varied starting points. Combine dance elements into motifs and phrases, use forms and structures to convey intent, and create and perform in solo, small-group, and large-group settings. Consider how production choices, venue, and audience support meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: kinesthetic awareness, stimuli, movement phrases, production elements, forms/structures, performance contexts, and audience/venue.

### CC-02

Use dance language to explain interpretations and develop repeatable choreography that communicates an idea. Reflect on rehearsal and performance, apply constructive feedback, and examine social, cultural, historical, political, and personal influences with respect for people and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: interpretation, composition skills, reflection/feedback, contexts, and respect.

### CC-03

Document and discuss choreography and rehearsal using technical vocabulary. Communicate movement preferences, cultural identity, values, and emotions; respond to local issues and anticipate how design decisions may affect an audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: technical vocabulary, personal expression, local issues, and audience impact/design choices.

### CC-04

Apply dance knowledge to personally meaningful work and community connections. Investigate innovators across genres and cultures, First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing. Plan, rehearse, and perform with attention to physical health and injury prevention.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: application, meaningful work, innovators, First Peoples/local knowledge, connections, and safety.

## Content

### C-01

Understand body, space, time, dynamics, and relationships as interacting dance elements. Learn genre- or style-specific skills and how kinesthetic and spatial awareness support movement choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of dance, technique/genre/style skills, and kinesthetic/spatial awareness.

### C-02

Learn how choreographic forms organize a whole work, devices transform movement, and design principles shape its progression. Use dance notation to represent movement and form rather than relying only on memory.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, choreographic forms/structures, devices, principles of design, and notation.

### C-03

Understand the relationships among dancers, choreographers, and audiences in different settings. Explore local and intercultural performers, movements, and genres, including traditional and contemporary First Peoples and cross-cultural perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, participant roles, local/intercultural performers, and First Peoples worldviews.

### C-04

Recognize ethical responsibilities concerning cultural appropriation and plagiarism, alongside safety procedures appropriate to bodies, environments, clothing, and footwear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, appropriation/plagiarism and safety protocols.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This course emphasizes composing dance, not simply learning a set sequence. Examples of stimuli, choreographic forms, styles, and production elements explain possibilities; they are not a mandatory repertoire or requirement for an elaborate stage production.

The source's dance literacy includes communication through movement vocabulary, symbols, and notation. Respect differing bodies and development when interpreting technique. A dance or story's cultural significance does not confer permission to reproduce it: seek appropriate community guidance and permissions, and avoid decontextualized or misrepresentative uses.
