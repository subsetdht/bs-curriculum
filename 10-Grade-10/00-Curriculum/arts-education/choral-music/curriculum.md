# Choral Music - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Choral Music

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Develop expressive, healthy singing and musical understanding through solo and ensemble performance, improvisation, listening, and contextual reflection.

**Prerequisites:** Experience singing, listening attentively, or working in an ensemble may help; this reference sets no formal entry or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 family record alongside [S1]. Local item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a rehearsal sequence. The official inventory [S5] includes **Concert Choir 10, Chamber Choir 10, and Vocal Jazz 10** under Choral Music. They share this published curriculum, not invented separate standards. These are options, not courses every learner must take.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Musical expression grows from history, culture, and community, providing ways to investigate identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, roots of expression and identity/belonging.

### BI-02

Musicians develop through sustained effort, resilience, and reflection, drawing on interacting sensory experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth and interplay of the senses.

### BI-03

Encounters with music can change thought and feeling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, transformative aesthetic experiences.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Study and perform varied styles in solo, small-ensemble, and large-ensemble contexts. Refine vocal technique and expression, improvise, and try unfamiliar possibilities while exploring personal voice, values, and the influences of place, time, and other contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: performance contexts, expression, improvisation, styles, contexts, technique, and personal voice.

### CC-02

Use musical language to analyze how technique, technology, and environment shape composition and performance. Reflect on rehearsal, performance, and growth, including the function of one's singing voice in the ensemble, and use stylistic analysis to inform choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: musical analysis, reflection, ensemble voice, and styles.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate musical experiences, using constructive feedback and appropriate terminology. Contribute personal and cultural perspectives, respond musically to social and global concerns, and show respect for fellow participants and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation, feedback, language, identity/perspective, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Take responsibility for musical participation, connect with others from local to international contexts, and combine technical knowledge with contextual observation when making decisions. Care for and use the voice safely to prevent performance-related harm.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, connections, musical decisions, and safe voice care.

## Content

### C-01

Understand musical organization through elements, principles, theory, vocabulary, and notation, including relationships among rhythm, pitch, harmony, timbre, texture, and expressive markings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements/principles/vocabulary/symbols/theory of music and elaboration.

### C-02

Learn singing techniques suited to individual voices and repertoire. These include coordinating breath and diction, shaping tone and intonation, negotiating vocal registers and range, and using articulation, ornamentation, blend, and balance expressively.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, singing techniques specific to individual voices and elaboration.

### C-03

Understand technical strategies and technologies, creative-development processes, and relationships among movement, sound, image, and form. Consider how performers, audiences, and venues influence a musical experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, technical skills/strategies/technologies, creative processes, movement/sound/image/form, and participant/venue roles.

### C-04

Study musical genres in their historical and cultural settings, including traditional and contemporary First Peoples and cross-cultural worldviews. Understand ethical limits on appropriation and plagiarism in repertoire and performance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, genre history, First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The three included names in [S5] are navigated through this shared curriculum; the source does not assign separate Big Ideas or standards to each. Ensemble-size examples explain musical roles rather than imposing school staffing or casting arrangements. The source includes solo as well as ensemble contexts.

Individual vocal technique does not imply a prescribed voice type or range. Protect vocal and aural health. Repertoire examples and cultural study do not authorize performance, recording, adaptation, or copying of songs. Seek appropriate rights and community permissions, and retain cultural context.
