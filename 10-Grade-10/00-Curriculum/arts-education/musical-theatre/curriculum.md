# Musical Theatre - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Musical Theatre

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Integrate dramatic, musical, and movement choices in expressive performances, developing collaboration, interpretation, and responsible artistic practice.

**Prerequisites:** Prior singing, movement, or drama experience can be helpful; this reference sets no formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 course summary with [S1]. Follow item locators for official expectations and elaborations. Repository IDs are not official BC codes or a teaching sequence. This is a course choice, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Musical theatre brings art forms together to communicate beliefs and create experiences capable of changing how audiences respond to the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, aesthetic experience/change and ideas/beliefs in production; four-discipline elaboration.

### BI-02

Interpreting an existing work can express cultural and personal identity rather than merely repeating a previous performance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, interpretation of existing work and identity/culture.

### BI-03

Artists develop through sustained effort, recovery from setbacks, informed experimentation, and reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perseverance, resilience, risk taking, and reflection.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create, rehearse, and perform musical theatre for an intended audience. Combine conventions from drama, music, and dance deliberately, experiment with props and production technologies, and take creative chances to strengthen expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: theatrical works, combining conventions, creative risks, props/processes/technologies, and audience.

### CC-02

Use appropriate artistic terminology to interpret and respond to performances. Give and use feedback, reflect on rehearsal experiences, and consider how self-awareness, audience awareness, and a work's place, time, and context inform interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: feedback, performance analysis, self/audience, and contextual reflection.

### CC-03

Document, share, and respond to creative work in different settings, attending to how artistic decisions affect oneself, fellow participants, and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: sharing/responding and awareness of self/others/audience.

### CC-04

Work responsibly and safely during creation, rehearsal, and performance. Explore First Peoples and local cultural knowledge, the influence of musical theatre on society, professional practice habits, and personal, educational, and career possibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, First Peoples/other ways of knowing, opportunities, professional habits, social impact, and safe environments.

## Content

### C-01

Understand audition, rehearsal, and performance practices involving acting, singing, and dance. Learn the stylistic conventions, elements, principles, terminology, symbols, techniques, and tools used to bring those disciplines together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, audition/rehearsal/performance techniques and musical theatre styles/elements/principles/tools/vocabulary/symbols.

### C-02

Learn creative-development strategies that link interpretation to performance choices, such as relationships among blocking, movement, speech, and characterization. Understand how performers, crew, and audience contribute in different contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, creative-process strategies/techniques and roles of performers/crew/audiences.

### C-03

Consider how innovative artists across periods, genres, and cultures have shaped musical theatre, including First Nations, Métis, and Inuit contributions. Examine traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews alongside cross-cultural perspectives communicated in works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, innovative artists and First Peoples/cross-cultural worldviews.

### C-04

Understand how plagiarism and cultural appropriation can misrepresent people or remove expressive work from its rightful context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Big Ideas draw on all four Arts disciplines; performance content centres on drama, music, and dance and may draw supplementary content from those curricula. The source includes different presentation formats, so it does not prescribe one full-scale commercial musical, production budget, or audition outcome.

Interpreting a work does not grant performance, recording, adaptation, or cultural permissions. Respect rights in scripts, songs, movement, and designs, and seek guidance for community-specific cultural knowledge. Safe creative risk must not be confused with physical danger or pressure to disclose personal experiences.
