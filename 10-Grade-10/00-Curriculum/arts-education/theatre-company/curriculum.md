# Theatre Company - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Theatre Company

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Contribute to the creation of a theatre production through dramatic interpretation, collaborative rehearsal, characterization, and reflective performance.

**Prerequisites:** Experience in dramatic exploration or ensemble work may help; this reference sets no formal entry or audition requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Read the Grade 10 summary with [S1] and follow each source locator. IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a rehearsal schedule. This is one course option, not a requirement to take all Arts courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Theatre creates aesthetic experiences through coordinated movement, sound, imagery, and language, communicating ideas and insights into human experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, aesthetic communication and personal/cultural connections.

### BI-02

Both making theatre and encountering its finished production can shape identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, process/product and identity.

### BI-03

A theatre company develops through perseverance, collaboration, and shared reflection.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, growth as a theatre company.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use imagination, observation, and inquiry to explore theatrical work and a range of theatre experiences. Develop performance vocabulary and skills, combine conventions for an intended audience, and take informed creative chances to communicate ideas and emotions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: repertoire, inquiry, conventions/audience, creative risks, and theatre experiences.

### CC-02

Refine rehearsal and performance through self-reflection, director feedback, and audience response. Describe work with dramatic terminology, connect it to personal experience, and develop awareness of oneself, the company, the audience, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: feedback, dramatic language, awareness, refinement, and personal connections.

### CC-03

Share and respond to creative work, communicate through a theatre production, and develop characters as a means of expressing personal voice and perspective.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: sharing, production communication, and character development.

### CC-04

Act responsibly when making, performing, or responding to theatre. Explore how drama affects cultures and society, including understanding developed through First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility, First Peoples knowledge, and cultural/social impact.

## Content

### C-01

Understand the elements, principles, terminology, symbols, and techniques of drama, and how movement, sound, images, and form work together. Learn conventions and creative-development strategies across dramatic forms, genres, styles, and movements.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dramatic elements/principles/techniques, forms, creative strategies, conventions/movements, genres/styles, and movement/sound/image/form.

### C-02

Learn characterization as a process of representing another person's voice or worldview. Understand company roles and responsibilities and the etiquette associated with auditions, rehearsals, and performances.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, characterization, stage etiquette, and company roles/responsibilities.

### C-03

Investigate performers and innovative artists across genres, alongside traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives communicated through drama.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, performers, innovative artists, and First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives.

### C-04

Understand the ethical responsibilities involved in cultural borrowing and attribution, including the risks of appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source explicitly intends Theatre Company to support creating a theatre production. Its reference to Grades 10, 11, and 12 describes that shared course purpose, not a grade-banded set of standards; this record summarizes only the Grade 10 page.

Company roles and stage etiquette do not establish a provincial audition or casting policy. This summary does not prescribe a particular play or production scale. Scripts, character portrayals, music, and cultural narratives require appropriate rights, permissions, and context; study is not authorization to reproduce or stereotype them.
