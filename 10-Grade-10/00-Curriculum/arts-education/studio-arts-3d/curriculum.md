# Studio Arts 3D - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Studio Arts 3D

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Create and interpret three-dimensional art by connecting expressive form, material properties, design decisions, and cultural and social meaning.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing objects or making with varied materials may help; this reference sets no formal portfolio or entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference; it is not a workshop or equipment-operation manual.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 summary alongside [S1] and its elaborations. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes or a prescribed project sequence. Studio Arts 3D is one course choice, not a requirement to take all Arts courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Three-dimensional form can express ideas, emotions, and viewpoints through the interaction of bodily making and thought.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, communication through form and mind/body.

### BI-02

Experiencing visual art can communicate cultural stories and worldviews and deepen understanding of identity and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, aesthetic experiences and identity.

### BI-03

Artists develop through patient work over time and reflection on experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artistic growth.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create three-dimensional works through sensory experience, imagination, and inquiry. Consider audience and expressive intent, explore unfamiliar possibilities, and refine technique across styles and movements with safe and responsible use of tools, materials, and space.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: inspiration, risks, audience, expression, skills/styles/movements, and safe use.

### CC-02

Find ways to resolve creative problems by analyzing how artists use materials, technology, processes, and environments. Develop personal responses to aesthetic questions and consider the contexts that influence artistic work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: challenges, artists' methods, aesthetic questions, and contexts.

### CC-03

Document, share, and appreciate three-dimensional work in different settings. Respect people and place while using art to respond to social and environmental issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation/sharing, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Make work reflecting personal, cultural, and historical contexts. Explore First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing, and examine the relationships among three-dimensional art, cultures, and society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: contextual creation, First Peoples/local knowledge, and art/culture/society.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how visual elements, design principles, and image-development strategies organize three-dimensional form. Use symbols and metaphors to communicate ideas and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual elements, design principles, image development, and symbols/metaphors.

### C-02

Learn about materials, tools, technologies, and processes for three-dimensional work, including building up, removing, modelling, and treating surfaces. Connect these with creative-development processes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, materials/technologies/processes and creative processes; additive/subtractive/modelling/surface-treatment elaborations.

### C-03

Understand how material properties affect possible forms and working decisions. Changes during drying, setting, or firing, differences in hardness, and relationships among weight, strength, and balance can influence tools, timing, sequence, and design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, physical properties and impacts of materials and elaborations.

### C-04

Consider the roles of artist and audience, the influence of visual culture in media, and how three-dimensional works participate in social justice concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artist/audience, visual culture, and social justice.

### C-05

Study traditional, innovative, and intercultural artists, including First Peoples worldviews, stories, and practices expressed through three-dimensional art. Understand appropriation and plagiarism as ethical issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, artists' contributions, First Peoples perspectives, and appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Material behaviour is a distinctive part of this course; it should not be replaced with a generic two-dimensional design summary. The source includes ceramics and other materials, not a requirement to use every material, kiln, carving tool, or process. Examples explain artistic considerations and do not authorize unsupervised hazardous work.

Responsible use includes environmental impacts. Study of First Peoples form, stories, or practices does not grant permission to copy cultural works or use restricted knowledge. Seek appropriate cultural guidance and permissions and preserve the meaning and context of works.
