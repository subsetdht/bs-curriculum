# Theatre Production - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Theatre Production

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Explore technical theatre and production design as collaborative means of communicating stories, perspectives, and emotions.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with visual design, collaborative making, or theatre may help; this reference sets no formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference; it is not an equipment-operation guide.

**How to use:** Read this Grade 10 summary alongside [S1] and its elaborations. Item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a production checklist. This is one elective course choice, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Production choices in movement, sound, imagery, and language shape theatre's aesthetic impact and how stories, traditions, and worldviews reach audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, enhanced aesthetic experience and communication of traditions/perspectives/stories.

### BI-02

Participation in production can connect personal and cultural experience with wider understanding of people.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, personal/cultural connections and human experience.

### BI-03

Artistic development requires persistence and reflection, while technical production challenges invite inventive and cooperative solutions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artistic growth and creativity/collaboration in production.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore and create productions through imagination, observation, and inquiry. Select production elements for a particular effect or audience, investigate props, processes, and technologies, and explore team roles while developing innovative designs.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: inquiry, production elements, technologies, team roles, and design innovation.

### CC-02

Plan and refine production work using feedback and discipline-specific discussion of artistic choices. Reflect on development processes and connect them with other experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: planning/refinement, feedback, response, and reflection.

### CC-03

Document and respond to creative work with respect for participants and audiences. Use design to communicate emotions, cultural identity, and perspectives, including responses to social and environmental concerns, and fulfil personal and social responsibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: responsibility, documentation, design communication, identity, respect, and issues.

### CC-04

Use design to strengthen a story's relationship to place, time, and context. Explore First Peoples and local cultural knowledge, other ways of knowing, and how theatre production can influence cultures and society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: contextual storytelling, First Peoples knowledge, and cultural/social impact.

## Content

### C-01

Distinguish production design, the technical work that implements it, and theatre management. Understand production roles, hierarchy, and crew responsibilities as well as relationships among performers, crew, and audiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, production design/technical theatre/management, production roles, and performer/crew/audience roles.

### C-02

Learn dramatic vocabulary, elements, skills, techniques, and symbols alongside visual design elements, design principles, and image-development strategies. Relate these to creative processes and the coordination of movement, sound, image, and form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements/techniques, design elements/principles/strategies, creative processes, and movement/sound/image/form.

### C-03

Understand how time and place influence dramatic forms, and how theatre expresses traditional and contemporary First Peoples and cross-cultural perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, time/place and drama forms; First Peoples/cross-cultural worldviews.

### C-04

Recognize hazards in theatre and classroom spaces, as well as ethical concerns involving plagiarism and cultural appropriation in designs, narratives, and other expressive choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, potential hazards and ethics of appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This course concentrates on technical theatre: design and realization of scenery, lighting, sound, costumes, props, makeup, and management are examples of its scope. It is not simply acting under another title. The examples do not require every learner to operate every tool or carry out hazardous rigging; appropriate supervision and local safety procedures remain essential.

Social and cultural interpretation does not authorize copying protected designs, scripts, music, or community knowledge. Obtain relevant permissions and preserve cultural context. This record provides neither a technical safety manual nor a prescribed production budget.
