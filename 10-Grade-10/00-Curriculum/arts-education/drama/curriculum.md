# Drama - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Arts Education](../README.md) > Drama

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. [S4], printed pp. 40-41, explicitly states that French Immersion and Francophone programs parallel the regular English program in structure and content; together with [S1], this supports shared Arts expectations. [S2] supplies Early/Late Immersion guidance. This does not establish local course availability.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is English. Neither establishes classroom language or local course availability.

**Learning objective:** Create and interpret drama through purposeful performance choices, characterization, collaborative inquiry, and reflection on human and cultural experience.

**Prerequisites:** Prior imaginative play, performance, or cooperative creative work may help; this reference sets no formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference.

**How to use:** Compare this Grade 10 course summary with [S1] and its elaborations. Item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a prescribed sequence. Drama is a course choice, not a requirement to take every Arts course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Drama brings movement, sound, images, and language together to express meaning, feelings, and viewpoints.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, dramatic communication.

### BI-02

Participation in drama can deepen understanding of human experience while connecting personal and cultural identity with belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, personal/cultural connections and identity/belonging.

### BI-03

Artistic growth depends on engagement, informed experimentation, and reflection; collaborative drama also requires cooperative creation and exchange of ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artistic growth and collaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create and present dramatic work to express ideas and emotions. Build a repertoire of skills across performance settings, purposefully combine conventions for an audience, and experiment with improvisation, props, processes, and technologies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and create: dramatic works, repertoire/performance skills, conventions, props/processes/technologies, audience, and improvisation.

### CC-02

Describe and analyze drama in discipline-specific language and use feedback to refine ideas. Relate dramatic experience to personal experience and examine social, cultural, historical, environmental, and personal influences, including the significance of place and time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reason and reflect: analysis, feedback, personal connections, and contexts/place/time.

### CC-03

Document and respond to dramatic experiences in different settings. Use symbols and imagery to compose, interpret, and extend ideas, expressing an individual perspective on social and environmental concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicate and document: documentation, symbolism/imagery, and personal voice/issues.

### CC-04

Take responsibility for respectful creation, performance, and response. Build family and community connections through drama and explore First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connect and expand: responsibility/respect, First Peoples knowledge, and family/community connections.

## Content

### C-01

Understand dramatic elements and principles, with vocabulary and symbols for describing choices. Learn creative-process strategies that coordinate movement, sound, image, and form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements/principles/vocabulary/symbols, strategies/techniques, and movement/sound/image/form.

### C-02

Learn how characterization represents another person's perspective and can be refined through dramatic choices. Distinguish dramatic forms, conventions, genres, and styles, developing relevant skills.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, character development, drama forms/conventions, and genre/style skills.

### C-03

Understand how performer, audience, and venue relate, and how dramatic works emerge from particular places and periods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, performer/audience/venue and influences of time/place.

### C-04

Study traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives expressed through drama. Recognize ethical limits on borrowing cultural voices or works, including appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples/cross-cultural perspectives and ethics of appropriation/plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Drama 10 includes exploration and presentation as well as performance; it is not interchangeable with the production-focused Theatre Company or technical Theatre Production courses. The source's examples of genres, character techniques, and dramatic elements are not a mandatory script or repertoire list.

Representing a perspective requires care rather than stereotyping or impersonating a community's cultural authority. Obtain appropriate permissions for scripts, stories, recordings, and cultural knowledge. Informed creative risk does not require unsafe participation or public disclosure of private experiences.
