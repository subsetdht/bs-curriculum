# Science - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../../README.md) > [Science](../README.md) > Science 10

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial science expectations. The immersion policy and provincial graduation requirements support the program mapping [S2], [S3].

**Document language:** English, using an English primary source. Classroom instruction and resource languages are separate; this is not a French-language teaching resource.

**Learning objective:** Use scientific inquiry and evidence to connect inheritance, chemical and energy changes, and the development of the universe with their practical and ethical implications.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience with scientific investigation, matter, energy, living systems, and interpreting data is helpful, not a formal entry requirement established here.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. It is not a laboratory procedure, equipment specification, or authorization to handle chemicals or radiation.

**How to use:** Follow [S1] for complete learning standards and elaborations. Read the source's six competency strands across the content areas. Local item IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a prescribed sequence.

The Grade 10 Science course-list entry contains this one course. Its standards are reached through the official `core` route; the local named course folder preserves the course-list structure. Science 10 is a 4-credit requirement in the Dogwood graduation program [S3], not a substitute for the separately required senior science credits.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

The organization and transmission of genetic information help explain both continuity and variation among living things.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, DNA and diversity of living things.

### BI-02

Chemical change rearranges atoms while involving energy. Understanding both aspects helps explain what happens during a reaction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, energy change and the rearrangement of atoms in chemical processes.

### BI-03

Energy can change form without being created or destroyed, but those transformations can still have important consequences for organisms and environments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, energy conservation, transformation, and impacts.

### BI-04

The big bang theory provides an evidence-based account of the universe's formation that can be examined through observations and models.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, formation of the universe and big bang theory.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Questioning and predicting:** Sustain an investigation-worthy interest, use observations to frame increasingly complex questions, and consider multiple hypotheses and possible outcomes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Questioning and predicting.

### CC-02

**Planning and conducting:** Choose methods that can produce dependable qualitative and quantitative evidence, working individually and collaboratively. Assess risks and ethical, cultural, and environmental concerns, and use appropriate equipment to collect accurate records under safety and ethical guidelines.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Planning and conducting: investigation methods, risks, equipment, data, and safety.

### CC-03

**Processing and analysing:** Interpret local environments and draw on First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing. Examine variable relationships, inconsistencies, graphs, and models to develop evidence-consistent conclusions and distinguish possible causes and effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Processing and analyzing data and information: place, sources of knowledge, patterns, graphical analysis, conclusions, and causation.

### CC-04

**Evaluating:** Examine uncertainty, confounding variables, alternative explanations, and the limits of models, then identify ways to improve an investigation. Question assumptions and bias in one's own and others' work, consider the effects of changing tools on knowledge, and evaluate scientific claims alongside social, ethical, environmental, and career connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Evaluating: methods, models, assumptions, changes in knowledge, careers, secondary sources, and implications.

### CC-05

**Applying and innovating:** Transfer scientific understanding to new problems and refine ideas collaboratively or independently. Consider how inquiry and scientific innovation can contribute to the well-being of people, communities, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applying and innovating.

### CC-06

**Communicating:** Use models, appropriate scientific representations, and evidence-based arguments to explain a phenomenon or proposed response to an identified audience. Reflect on how experiences, perspectives, and relationships with place inform understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating: theoretical models, scientific arguments, audiences, and place.

## Content

### C-01

Connect DNA's structure and function with genes, chromosomes, gene expression, and inheritance patterns. The elaborations develop Mendelian models while also recognizing patterns such as incomplete dominance, co-dominance, and sex-linked inheritance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, DNA structure and function and patterns of inheritance, with their elaborations.

### C-02

Examine how mutation and natural or artificial selection contribute to biological diversity. Consider applications of genetics together with their health, environmental, social, political, and ethical implications.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, mechanisms for diversity of life and applied genetics and ethical considerations.

### C-03

Explain chemical reactions in terms of rearranged atoms, conservation of mass, and energy changes. The elaborations connect reaction types and acid-base chemistry with exothermic/endothermic change and activation energy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, rearrangement of atoms in chemical reactions and energy change during chemical reactions.

### C-04

Investigate the uses and consequences of chemical processes, including First Peoples knowledge. Connect chemical understanding with practical contexts without treating the elaboration examples as instructions or an unrestricted right to use community knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, practical applications and implications of chemical processes, including First Peoples knowledge.

### C-05

Distinguish nuclear energy processes and forms of radiation, considering their uses and impacts. The elaborations distinguish fission from fusion and ionizing from non-ionizing radiation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, nuclear energy and radiation and their elaborations.

### C-06

Account for potential and kinetic energy and for transfers and transformations consistent with conservation of energy. The source develops quantitative relationships involving mass, height, speed, and temperature change, and connects energy technologies with local and global environmental effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, conservation of energy, potential and kinetic energy, energy transformation, and local/global impacts; related energy and heat elaborations.

### C-07

Investigate the universe's formation and changing composition through big bang theory. Connect the evidence used in astronomical explanations with the instruments and methods through which the data are gathered.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, formation of the universe: big bang theory, components over time, and astronomical data and collection methods.

## Elaborations and boundaries

These are integrated Grade 10 standards, not separate Biology, Chemistry, Physics, and Astronomy graduation courses. The summary condenses the source's examples and inquiry questions without making every example compulsory.

Scientific inquiry requires attention to evidence, uncertainty, risk, and ethics. This reference does not replace qualified supervision, health guidance, laboratory procedures, or local safety requirements.

First Peoples knowledge belongs within the science expectations. Community-specific medicines, practices, and cultural knowledge require appropriate relationships and permissions; their appearance in an elaboration is not permission to reproduce them or attempt a preparation.
