# Social Studies - Grade 10

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 10](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial Social Studies expectations. Program mapping is supported by the immersion policy and graduation requirements [S2], [S3].

**Document language:** English, using the English primary source. This does not prescribe classroom language or provide a French-language teaching resource.

**Learning objective:** Investigate Canada in relation to the wider world from 1914 to the present, using evidence and historical thinking to examine governance, identities, conflict, injustice, and responses to it.

**Prerequisites:** Experience asking historical questions, interpreting sources, and considering Canadian and world contexts is helpful prior learning, not a formal admission requirement set here.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to consult this reference. Historical documents, images, testimony, and cultural works retain their own rights and contextual requirements.

**How to use:** Read this original synthesis beside [S1], following the locators for precise expectations and optional elaboration examples. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry standard codes or a teaching sequence.

The catalog gives Social Studies 10 a **core entry**, so its record is adjacent to the stable subject landing rather than placed in an invented course-choice hierarchy. The source's historical period is not a school-year schedule. Social Studies 10 is a 4-credit Dogwood requirement, with additional senior Social Studies credits required separately [S3].

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Conflicts at different scales have helped produce contemporary societies and identities. Political institutions also develop through the interaction of social conditions, economic interests, ideas, and geography.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, global/regional conflicts and influences on political institutions.

### BI-02

Understandings of Canadian developments differ with people's worldviews and experiences. Attending to those differences makes a single national account open to examination rather than treating it as self-evident.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, worldviews and perspectives on developments in Canadian society.

### BI-03

Historical and continuing injustice complicates claims that Canada has always been inclusive. Studying these tensions is central to understanding national narratives and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, injustices and Canada's inclusive, multicultural narrative.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Inquiry:** Frame questions, gather and interpret information, analyse competing ideas, and communicate supported findings and decisions. The source elaborations develop the use of data, maps, visual representations, and accurate source citation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and skills, with key-skills elaborations.

### CC-02

**Significance and evidence:** Consider why a person, place, event, or development matters to different groups in different contexts. Compare contested accounts by examining source reliability, disputed points, and whether the available evidence supports the claim.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance and evidence.

### CC-03

**Continuity and change:** Compare what changes and what persists for particular groups, taking account of time and place rather than assuming everyone experiences the same historical development.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, continuity and change.

### CC-04

**Cause and consequence:** Investigate how conditions and the choices of people or groups interact to shape an event or decision, and analyse its multiple consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, cause and consequence.

### CC-05

**Perspective:** Explain differing views of past or present issues in relation to the beliefs, values, and prevailing norms of the people concerned.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-06

**Ethical judgment:** Support judgments about past and present actions with reasons, and consider appropriate ways to remember, acknowledge, and respond to them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Examine government and political institutions alongside First Peoples governance and political ideologies. The elaborations distinguish Indigenous governance and self-government from structures imposed through the Indian Act, and connect institutions with rights, representation, and decision making.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, government, First Peoples governance, political institutions, and ideologies.

### C-02

Study environmental, political, and economic policy as choices involving interests, values, and consequences. Consider how different participants, including First Peoples, influence public decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, environmental, political, and economic policies, including stakeholder and policy-development elaborations.

### C-03

Trace Canadian autonomy in relationships within Canada and with other countries, and investigate the multiple identities through which people understand belonging. First Peoples and Francophone identities remain visible within this inquiry rather than being absorbed into one uniform national identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Canadian autonomy and Canadian identities, with their elaborations.

### C-04

Examine residential schools and other discriminatory policies and injustices affecting First Peoples in BC and Canada. Attend to the different experiences and continuing effects described in the source rather than treating colonial harm as a single completed event.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, residential schools and other discriminatory policies and injustices against First Peoples; related discrimination elaborations.

### C-05

Investigate discrimination in Canada and internationally, retaining the source's named Canadian cases: the Chinese head tax, the Komagata Maru incident, racial segregation and the destruction of Hogan's Alley, and the internment of Japanese Canadians. Their distinct histories illuminate exclusion, dispossession, community responses, and challenges to national narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, discriminatory policies and injustices in Canada and the world, including the explicitly named head-tax, Komagata Maru, Hogan's Alley, and Japanese Canadian cases.

### C-06

Study the Holocaust as a distinct historical event and examine the concept of genocide. The elaborations situate the Nazi genocide of Jewish people within escalating antisemitism, persecution, mass murder, resistance, and aftermath, while distinguishing broader inquiry into genocide from that particular history.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, The Holocaust and the concept of genocide, with Holocaust/Nazi-atrocity and genocide elaborations.

### C-07

Examine advocacy for human rights, including the findings and recommendations of the Truth and Reconciliation Commission. Connect recognition of injustice with efforts toward accountability, redress, rights, and change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, advocacy for human rights, including Truth and Reconciliation Commission findings and recommendations.

### C-08

Investigate domestic and international conflict alongside forms of cooperation. Consider Canadian participation, non-participation, negotiation, and relationships with other peoples and institutions rather than viewing this period only as a sequence of wars.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, domestic conflicts and co-operation and international conflicts and co-operation.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The official Content explicitly names the Holocaust and several Canadian injustices. They must not disappear into a vague reference to diversity or human rights. At the same time, the numerous **sample topics** beneath the standards are examples, not a direction to reproduce or teach every listed event as a separate required unit.

The source period begins in 1914; background needed to understand a development does not turn this into a different grade's historical survey. Historical thinking involves evidence and reasoned interpretation, not assuming every account is equally supported.

First Peoples governance, identities, experiences of colonialism, and rights advocacy are part of the subject's expectations, not optional enrichment. Use survivor accounts, community knowledge, and cultural works with appropriate care, context, and permission. No such works are reproduced here.
