**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Document language:** English (`en-CA`). **Source review date:** 2026-09-14.

**Coverage:** All **11 main catalog landings** are populated and retained at their established URLs. There are **24 source-checked summaries**: nine core-entry records, all five Arts choices, and all ten Languages choices. Both course inventories are complete for the reviewed Grade 9 catalog.

These are original, condensed references to provincial expectations, not official wording, a school timetable, lessons, or an assessment checklist. Course counts describe available references, not the number of courses a learner must take.

### Late French Immersion

Use the same regular Grade 9 [French Immersion Language Arts](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) and [English Language Arts](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) records, plus shared core subjects, selected Arts study, and conditionally mapped Languages options: **23 source-checked references**, not a required course load. Applicability follows the provincial policy's secondary provisions and program-guide designations, not an assumption that earlier Late Immersion transition years equal Early Immersion. Core French remains distinct from the required immersion language-arts route.

### Francophone

Start with the distinct [Francophone Language Arts 9](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md), the six shared non-language core records, and the Arts options. The ten Languages choices also have explicit French-program guide designations, conditional on local selection and suitability: **22 source-checked references**, not a required course load. The K-9 required-areas order establishes shared-area coverage; the separate program-guide order supports the additional-course mappings.

Province-wide Francophone applicability for the English Language Arts 9 record has **not been established in this pass**. This is a mapping gap, not a claim that Francophone schools do not teach English; see the separately labelled [CSF provider context](#provider-context-csf). Do not substitute either Core French or immersion language arts for the first-language French record.

### Program policy and course selection

The [Required Areas of Learning Order][required-areas], sections 1-2, includes Francophone authorities, programs, and students. It requires boards to offer K-9 programs meeting the applicable standards in language arts and seven shared non-language areas: Mathematics, Science, Social Studies, Physical and Health Education, Arts Education, Career Education, and ADST. Section 2(a) distinguishes French language arts for immersion and Francophone students from English Language Arts for the regular English program.

The order's section 3 provides **specific individual exceptions**, not blanket exemptions for every learner with an IEP or English-language-learning services. Consult the actual conditions rather than inferring an exemption from a program label.

Section 4(c) additionally specifies English Language Arts for immersion in Grades 4-9; section 5's Late Immersion exception concerns Grade 6, not 9. The [French Immersion policy][immersion-policy] requires parallel curriculum content and directs Late Immersion in Grades 8-12 to the common secondary provisions. Shared standards do not specify which subjects a particular school teaches in French.

The ordinary additional-language requirement in section 4(a-b) concerns **Grades 5-8**, not a compulsory ten-course Languages program in Grade 9. The [Language Education Policy][language-policy] describes school-board language choices and elective or additional language study. A catalog entry does not guarantee local availability or establish a graduation requirement.

The [Educational Program Guide Order, M231/19][program-guides], sections 1-2, designates the K-9 guides, including French documents for French Immersion and Programme francophone. Its French-program schedule explicitly contains the nine non-Core-French language guides and the French Core French 5-12 guide. This supports conditional guide-level mappings, not compulsory enrolment, individual suitability, or replacement of either distinct French-language-arts curriculum. The source's legacy filename is not its current order number; guide publication years do not establish revisions of the live Grade 9 standards.

[ADST 9](applied-design-skills-and-technologies/curriculum.md#scope-and-use) has its own module-selection rule: a **full-year course equivalent**, using **one or more** of ten provincial modules or locally developed content with the Grade 9 competencies. These ten modules are summarized within one record, not counted as ten compulsory courses.

Arts Education 9 is a **five-choice course list**. Its [provincial introduction][arts-introduction] permits unified and discipline-specific learning; the five records below preserve the alternatives rather than replacing them with one generic parent record or requiring all five.

School timetables, selected modules or courses, resource choices, and classroom language are implementation questions. No SD43-specific schedule is imported into these provincial references.

## Provider context: CSF

This is **Conseil scolaire francophone implementation context, not an additional province-wide requirement**. The [CSF elementary program page](https://www.csf.bc.ca/nos-programmes/elementaire/), under Langues, states that all CSF students receive first-language English courses from Grade 4. Its [secondary program page](https://www.csf.bc.ca/nos-programmes/secondaire/), under Curriculum, confirms use of the provincial curriculum. Both French-language pages were reviewed **2026-09-14**.

CSF families can consult the [English Language Arts 9 reference](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) alongside their school's program information. These provider statements do not establish an additional statutory province-wide Francophone ELA 9 requirement and are not used to relabel every Francophone program. The sources' other elementary interventions and senior offerings are not imported into Grade 9 expectations.

## Using the references

Read each record's Scope and use before mapping a resource. For example, [Mathematics 9: C-01](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-01) has the canonical repository key `bc-g09-mathematics#c-01`. A bare item ID is ambiguous outside its record, and none of these IDs is an official BC standard code.

No special materials are needed to read the references. Any future activity must state its own prerequisites, materials, accessibility provisions, and program/language suitability. The cross-curricular [Core Competencies](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies) complement, rather than replace, subject-specific Curricular Competencies.

First Peoples perspectives remain within their curriculum subjects. Use community-appropriate sources, relationships, attribution, and permissions; curriculum alignment is not permission to reproduce stories, songs, designs, performances, or other cultural works.

## Coverage and limits

The 24 written records are **core summaries**, not transcripts of all standards and elaborations. Their source content was reviewed on 2026-09-14; curriculum revision dates were not established, and the review date is not a new curriculum edition. `Source-checked` does not mean ministry endorsement or independent educator review.

**Written-summary and source-access gaps:** None for the 11 main Grade 9 entries and their 15 inventoried course choices. Complete source content and elaborations were accessible for all 24 records.

**Program-mapping gap:** Province-wide Francophone applicability for English Language Arts 9 remains unverified. This is not a missing curriculum record, evidence of ineligibility, or proof that a school does not teach English. The other recorded mappings distinguish recognized curriculum guides from compulsory study and individual course suitability.

All 24 summaries are in English. The two French-language-arts sources are French; the other 22 primary course sources are English, sometimes with target-language examples. French-language editions of repository summaries and classroom learning resources are not supplied by this pass. Source language, document language, and classroom language are separate.

Locally developed ADST modules, local Indigenous-language alternatives, school course availability, and offerings outside the main Grade 9 catalog have not been inventoried. The ten Languages choices are not an exhaustive inventory of such opportunities. This is not a complete directory of every school or board-authorized course, nor a graduation plan.

The [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) folders remain empty. Follow the [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page) when extending or maintaining the curriculum references.
