# Physical and Health Education - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Physical and Health Education](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), based on an English source. This does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Develop movement competence and sustainable healthy-living habits while making informed decisions about relationships, safety, mental well-being, and care for others.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience with varied movement and health learning is helpful. The record sets no athletic performance or body-size entry standard.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Activities require accessible equipment, suitable supervision, and adaptations appropriate to learners; a particular sport or fitness device is not prescribed here.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for complete expectations and elaborations across physical literacy, healthy and active living, social and community health, and mental well-being. Local IDs identify grouped reference items, not official BC codes or a personal medical plan.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Varied daily physical activity develops physical literacy and supports personal health and fitness goals. Enjoyable participation can become a lasting part of a healthy life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily participation in different activities; benefits of lifelong physical activity.

### BI-02

Health choices and relationships affect more than physical condition: they also shape emotional and mental well-being and the quality of one's life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, healthy choices and physical/emotional/mental well-being; healthy relationships.

### BI-03

Supporting other people's health is part of participating in a community, not only an individual concern.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, advocacy for others' health and community connection.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Refine and combine fundamental movement skills, concepts, and strategies across activities and environments. Monitor and adjust exertion, identify preferred ways to be active, and demonstrate safety, fair play, and leadership.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Physical literacy.

### CC-02

Participate **daily** in activity that maintains or improves health-related fitness. Examine how activity at school, at home, and in the community contributes to health, then pursue personal healthy-living goals and evaluate the strategies and outcomes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Healthy and active living: daily activity, participation contexts, goals, strategies, and reflection.

### CC-03

Propose choices that support lasting well-being and investigate how health messages influence behaviour. Consider the interests and assumptions behind information rather than accepting every health claim at face value.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Healthy and active living: lifelong healthy choices; factors influencing health messages.

### CC-04

Develop responses to unsafe, abusive, or exploitative situations and to bullying, stereotyping, and discrimination. Build healthy relationships through respected boundaries and consent, and create strategies that support the well-being of the school and wider community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Social and community health, including consent, boundaries, help-seeking, and abuse-prevention elaborations.

### CC-05

Analyze ways to support mental well-being for oneself and others, and evaluate support strategies for others facing mental-health or substance-use problems. Develop and assess responses to the physical, emotional, and social changes of adolescence, considering the social and cultural influences on identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Mental well-being.

## Content

### C-01

Understand sound technique for movement performed in place, movement that travels, and movement that controls objects. Connect body and spatial awareness, effort, and relationships with movement strategies across individual or dual activities, rhythmic activity, and games.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, fundamental non-locomotor/locomotor/manipulative skills; movement concepts and strategies; types of physical activities.

### C-02

Use knowledge of physical exertion and the body's responses to activity to plan and adjust fitness work. The training content includes **FITT** (frequency, intensity, type, time), **SAID** (specific adaptation to imposed demand), and **specificity**; these concern matching activity and goals, not applying one routine to everyone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, monitoring/adjusting exertion, training principles, and effects of different physical activities, with elaborations.

### C-03

Examine health information and the possible immediate and longer-term effects of decisions about nutrition, sleep, and protection from sexually transmitted infections. Healthy sexual decision making includes consent, knowing and communicating boundaries, and responding to risk.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, healthy sexual decision making; consequences of health decisions; sources of health information.

### C-04

Know basic emergency-response principles and strategies for protection from abuse, exploitation, and harm in different settings, including online environments. Understand the consequences of bullying, stereotyping, and discrimination and the importance of consent, safe help-seeking, and respect for boundaries.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, emergencies; protecting self and others; consequences of bullying/stereotyping/discrimination and related elaborations.

### C-05

Examine the physical, emotional, and social dimensions of psychoactive substance use and potentially addictive behaviours. Recognize signs associated with stress, anxiety, and depression and connect this knowledge with appropriate support rather than diagnosing oneself or others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, psychoactive substance use and potentially addictive behaviours; signs and symptoms of stress, anxiety, and depression.

### C-06

Understand how physical development, changing feelings, and social relationships during adolescence can influence identity and relationships. Personal boundaries can change, so respect for others and consent are ongoing, not one-time assumptions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, physical, emotional, and social changes and their influence on identities and relationships; associated elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Daily participation is explicit; this source does not set a universal exercise-duration quota or performance target in these standards. Particular activities, monitoring devices, and games in elaborations are examples rather than compulsory purchases or sports. Traditional First Peoples games appear among those examples and require respectful local context and permissions.

Consent and abuse-prevention content should not be reduced to telling learners to avoid risk. The elaborations address power imbalances, affirmative and ongoing consent, respect for refusal, trusted support, and the fact that survivors are not to blame. Learners should not be required to disclose personal experiences to demonstrate curriculum learning.

This is a curriculum reference, not clinical advice, an emergency-response certification, or a replacement for qualified help. The [Science 9 record](../science/curriculum.md) separately covers reproductive biology; that does not replace the health and relationship learning here.
