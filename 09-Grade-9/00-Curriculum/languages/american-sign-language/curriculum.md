# American Sign Language (ASL) - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > American Sign Language (ASL)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; [S1] is also in English. ASL is the language being learned, not spoken English represented on the hands. Document language does not determine instructional language or accessibility supports.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange increasingly varied signed messages, organize experiences into stories, and examine language and identity with attention to Deaf communities and First Peoples perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with basic signs and short signed exchanges may help. This reference sets no admission or hearing-status requirement; the school determines placement and appropriate supports.

**Materials:** The linked source is sufficient for reference use. Learning activities need suitably accessible signed and visual materials, with permissions and recording arrangements considered separately; no commercial resource is prescribed here.

**How to use:** Follow each grouped item's locator to [S1], including its elaborations, before aligning an activity. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes or a teaching sequence.

These are Grade 9 standards, even though downloads collect several grades. The provincial second-language requirement described in [S2] concerns Grades 5-8; the ten Grade 9 language choices are not ten compulsory subjects.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful observation and conversations that matter to the learner work together to build understanding of ASL.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, viewing with intent and conversing about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Learning another language can change how we understand our own cultural position. Deaf creative expression offers perspectives through which to appreciate cultural differences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and culture through language acquisition; creative works and Deaf culture.

### BI-03

Stories make experiences and viewpoints available to others while carrying knowledge, history, and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories, including the stories elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret visual language by relating a sign's handshape, movement, and location to meaning. Combine these features with contextual knowledge, facial information, and other strategies to identify important information and supporting details in varied texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: relationships between sign features, deriving meaning from texts, and strategies for understanding and communication.

### CC-02

Sustain brief, straightforward exchanges, communicating ideas in complete ASL sentences and in writing. Check shared understanding and repair uncertainty through clarification, including visual feedback.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: short and simple conversations, exchanging ideas and information, and seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Tell a logically connected story using temporal expressions and transitions across past, present, and future. Choose presentation forms that make information accessible to the communicator and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories and its time-frame elaboration; presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Investigate how Deaf communities' practices, social movements, and attitudes contribute to identity. Compare local Deaf cultural experiences with one's own, consider differing worldviews, and explore opportunities for respectful cultural participation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: Deaf practices, traditions, social movements and attitudes; local cultural comparisons; Deaf cultural experiences; examining perspectives through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Connect storytelling with personal and collective identity while recognizing First Peoples knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story in personal, family, and community identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge and other ways of knowing.

## Content

### C-01

ASL meaning depends on more than the hands. Learn how facial and mouth movements, head and shoulder positions, and eye gaze contribute to questions, description, contrast, degree, and distance; coordinate gaze with pointing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-manual signals and its elaboration on facial expression, mouth morphemes, WH-face, contrastive structure, eye gaze, and distance signals.

### C-02

Expand sentence-building resources for describing, comparing, questioning, sequencing, and expressing personal concerns. Include topic-comment and subject-verb-object patterns, the source's question structures, and ASL ways of locating events in past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, increasing vocabulary and ASL sentence structures, including question forms and the ASL timeline elaboration.

### C-03

Recognize the organizing features of common texts and stories so that a message or narrative is understandable as a whole.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Study Deaf creative work and community life locally and globally, including changing societal perceptions of Deaf people. Distinguish cultural identification as Deaf from the broader grouping expressed by D/deaf, without treating individuals as culturally interchangeable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Deaf practices and traditions, creative works, worldwide communities, society's perceptions over time, cultural aspects, and D/deaf perspectives and their elaborations.

### C-05

Consider First Peoples accounts of how language carries histories and connects identity with place. Community protocols and local context matter to those connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture through histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This is a signed-language curriculum, not a spoken-language template. Non-manual signals have linguistic functions; they are not merely emotional decoration. The source's sentence-structure elaboration includes end-position WH signs, and its temporal elaboration uses signing space and duration, not an imported spoken-language conjugation system.

The writing expectation accompanies complete ASL sentences; it does not establish a prescribed ASL writing system or equate ASL with signed English. Presentation choices explicitly account for diverse abilities.

The source discusses collectivist aspects of Deaf culture and differences in D/deaf identification. These support inquiry, not assumptions about every Deaf or deaf person's identity, education, or communication choices. First Peoples and Deaf community knowledge require appropriate context and permission; the example of speaking with an Elder is not permission to collect or reproduce cultural knowledge.

This condensed reference does not reproduce the full non-manual-signal examples, prescribe cultural events, or establish a proficiency certification. Consult the complete elaborations in [S1].
