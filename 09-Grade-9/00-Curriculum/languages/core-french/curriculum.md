# Core French - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Core French

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as additional-course study where locally offered, selected, and appropriate to the learner. The program-guide order explicitly includes the French Core French guide in its French Immersion/Programme francophone schedule [S4]. This is guide-level applicability, not a claim that Core French is a normal or required language-arts route in those programs. It replaces neither French Immersion Language Arts nor Francophone first-language arts [S2], [S3].

**Document language:** English; the reviewed official course source is English, with French-language examples. This metadata does not itself prescribe classroom language. Separately, the official introduction encourages French as the language of instruction and use at every opportunity, while allowing some discussion or examples in the school's prevailing language [S5].

**Learning objective:** Communicate about familiar concerns in French, understand and narrate connected experiences, and explore how Francophone and Indigenous language experiences relate to culture and identity.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier Core French experience can support this work. This is not a provincial entry rule or a substitute for school placement advice.

**Materials:** Use the linked curriculum to consult this reference. Any classroom texts, recordings, or community contacts need their own suitability, accessibility, and permission review; no particular resource is required here.

**How to use:** Use the local items to find relevant groups of expectations, then consult the locators and complete elaborations in [S1]. These IDs are repository mapping aids, not official BC codes or an assessment checklist.

The standards apply specifically to Grade 9, not the full grade range in a compiled download. [S2] describes a second-language requirement for Grades 5-8; it does not make Core French or every language option compulsory in Grade 9.

The broader guide title in [S4] does not change this record's Grade 9 source scope. Its French-program designation is not an individual placement rule or evidence that a learner already proficient in French needs this additional-language course.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending closely to what is heard and seen supports meaningful French exchanges about learners' interests and concerns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; meaningful conversations about important topics.

### BI-02

Stories communicate experience and perspective, while Francophone creative expression gives access to the cultures from which it emerges.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories and Francophone creative works, including their elaborations.

### BI-03

Learning French can offer a different standpoint from which to examine one's own cultural identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, acquiring French and exploring cultural identity.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use French spelling-sound relationships to anticipate and interpret pronunciation. Read and listen for a text's main information, details, and setting, drawing on context and other comprehension strategies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: French letter patterns and pronunciation, deriving meaning from varied texts, and communication strategies.

### CC-02

Exchange ideas in complete spoken and written sentences about familiar topics. Keep communication workable by requesting clarification and adapting wording or other strategies to the situation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: engaging in conversations, exchanging ideas and information in complete sentences, seeking clarification, and strategies to support communication.

### CC-03

Organize a narrative so that a listener or reader can follow events and their timing. Use transitions and expressions appropriate to past, present, and future experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories and its logical-progression and time-frame elaboration.

### CC-04

Investigate connections between Indigenous communities and French, including how Indigenous people use French to express their own perspectives. Explore how stories contribute to identity in families and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: Indigenous communities and the French language; importance of story in personal, family, and community identity.

### CC-05

Learn from Francophone creative expression and community experiences. Compare cultural practices across regions and with one's own experiences, considering their purposes and their role in identity rather than reducing culture to a list of celebrations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: Francophone creative works, regional practices and traditions, cultural similarities and differences, and experiences with Francophone communities and people.

## Content

### C-01

Recognize recurring French spelling patterns and how groups of letters connect to sound.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, French letter patterns and its pronunciation-pattern elaboration.

### C-02

Develop sentence resources for questions, description, comparison, simple needs, and opinions. Connect events in sequence and alter wording to communicate whether they occur in the past, present, or future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, commonly used vocabulary and sentence structures across past, present, and future: question types, descriptions, comparisons and contrasts, sequences, needs, and opinions.

### C-03

Understand how text form, audience, and purpose influence communication, and how narrative elements organize a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common types of texts and common elements of stories, including their elaborations.

### C-04

Examine community cultural life and differing Francophone regional practices. Learn why using another community's knowledge or creative expression requires attention to context, permission, accurate representation, and authorship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of communities, traditions and other practices in Francophone regions, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The pronunciation elaboration includes silent letters, liaisons, and elisions, not simply pronouncing every written letter. Question forms include intonation, *est-ce que*, inversion, and different interrogative words. The three time frames are communication expectations; this summary does not create an exhaustive tense-conjugation syllabus.

In [S1], texts may be spoken, written, visual, or digital, and authentic or adapted. Familiar conversation topics may extend beyond the learner's immediate surroundings; they are not restricted to classroom routines.

The Indigenous-French connections elaboration includes communities in Canada and elsewhere and notes Indigenous authors' use of French to address effects of colonization. It does not imply that French replaces Indigenous languages or that Indigenous communities have one shared language history.

Stories and cultural works require attribution and appropriate permissions. The source's examples of festivals, artists' media, and community engagement are not a compulsory cultural checklist. This English summary is neither a French teaching resource nor a translation of the complete source.

The Core French guide's inclusion in the French-program schedule of [S4] must not collapse the three different French curricula into one. The [Required Areas of Learning Order][S6], section 2(a), and the immersion policy [S3] still govern the applicable language-arts route; a catalogued additional-course guide does not replace it.
