# Japanese - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Japanese

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English, matching the reviewed source's explanatory language. Japanese is the language being learned. Source and document metadata do not determine classroom instruction arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Connect Japanese sounds, writing, and meaning in purposeful communication, develop connected narratives, and explore varied Japanese and First Peoples cultural perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with some Japanese words, exchanges, and kana is helpful preparation, not an official admission rule imposed by this reference. Ask the school about appropriate placement and support.

**Materials:** [S1] is sufficient to consult this reference. Classroom reading, listening, and viewing resources require separate accessibility and permission checks; no particular text or character-learning product is prescribed.

**How to use:** Consult the source locators and full elaborations before mapping learning resources to these condensed items. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a prescribed script-learning sequence.

This record covers Grade 9 standards, not all grades found in a compiled download. [S2] describes a second-language requirement for Grades 5-8; it does not make every Grade 9 language option compulsory.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Intentional listening and viewing help learners make sense of Japanese, while personally meaningful conversations give a reason to keep learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; conversing about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Language learning can reveal new ways of understanding one's identity. Encountering creative expression allows cultural experiences to be considered beyond one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language acquisition and identity; creative works and appreciation of cultural diversity.

### BI-03

Stories carry experiences and viewpoints between people and can connect knowledge, history, and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories and its elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Japanese characters with sound and meaning, then use those relationships and contextual strategies to interpret messages. Understand significant information, supporting details, and the meaning of stories across speech and other text forms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: Japanese characters, sounds, and meaning; deriving meaning from speech and texts; comprehending stories; strategies for oral and written language.

### CC-02

Exchange ideas through speech and writing, adapting communication when meaning is uncertain. Request clarification and verify that the intended information has been understood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: exchanging ideas and information, seeking clarification, and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Create spoken and written narratives whose transitions and time expressions make the sequence clear. Present information through formats that suit the abilities of both the learner and others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories with past/present/future time frames and logical progression; presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Compare the purposes of cultural practices in Japanese communities with one's own experience, and engage with Japanese people and communities. Consider how cultural viewpoints shape personal and shared experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural similarities and differences, community experiences, and examining experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize storytelling's contribution to personal, family, and community identity. Include First Peoples and local cultural knowledge and acknowledge different ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn Japanese writing conventions involving hiragana, katakana, and kanji. The two kana systems and kanji are all part of the stated writing scope.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese writing conventions with hiragana, katakana, and kanji, including the kana elaboration.

### C-02

Develop frequently used language for asking questions, describing, sequencing, and expressing personal interests, needs, and opinions. Communicate events in past, present, and future without assuming that each time frame requires a separate verb form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions; past/present/future elaboration showing a shared present/future form and a past form.

### C-03

Understand how audience, purpose, and form influence a text and how narrative components connect to make a story understandable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore Japanese creative expression and diverse community practices, including the source's distinct attention to Indigenous peoples of Japan. Consider cultural permission and authorship when studying or using creative material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese works of art, Indigenous peoples of Japan and its Ainu/Okinawan elaboration, cultural practices in Japanese communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Study First Peoples connections among oral histories, language, identity, and place, recognizing the role of local community knowledge and protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The writing content is not a romanization-only or kana-only curriculum. [S1] specifies hiragana, katakana, and kanji but does not establish a compulsory character count here. Its temporal example uses *tabemasu* for present or future and *tabemashita* for past; do not replace this with an assumed three-tense conjugation model.

The source names **Ainu and Okinawan peoples** in its Indigenous-peoples-of-Japan elaboration. Retain that explicit scope and distinguish it from the separate First Peoples content and local Canadian knowledge. Neither strand makes communities interchangeable or authorizes retelling protected knowledge.

Texts can be oral, written, visual, digital, or combined, and presentations should account for diverse abilities. Particular festivals, art forms, and community-contact activities in the elaborations are examples rather than a required itinerary.

Use appropriate attribution and community permissions for cultural material. This reference condenses the standards; it does not reproduce works, provide a required reading list, or establish an external proficiency credential.
