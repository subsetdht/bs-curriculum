# Punjabi - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Punjabi

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; the official source's explanatory language is English and its Punjabi examples use Gurmukhi. Punjabi is the language being learned. Neither metadata language field prescribes classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Use Punjabi sound-pattern and text knowledge to understand and exchange ideas, develop connected narratives, and explore cultural experience and identity across communities.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with familiar Punjabi words, writing, and conversation can support this learning. This is not a first-language-proficiency requirement or an official admission rule; schools determine suitable placement.

**Materials:** Access to [S1] is sufficient for reference use. Choose any additional texts, recordings, or community activities with separate attention to accessibility, age appropriateness, attribution, and permission.

**How to use:** Follow local item locators to the full standards and elaborations when aligning resources. The IDs are repository references, not official BC codes, a spelling inventory, or a teaching sequence.

These standards apply to Grade 9 rather than the whole range of a compiled download. [S2]'s second-language requirement concerns Grades 5-8; it does not make Punjabi or all Grade 9 language choices compulsory.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending to spoken and visual communication helps learners understand Punjabi, and meaningful conversation connects that learning to their interests.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; conversing about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Learning another language can provide a fresh perspective on cultural identity. Creative expression makes varied cultural experiences available for consideration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language acquisition and identity; creative works and cultural diversity.

### BI-03

Stories help people communicate experiences and viewpoints while carrying knowledge and connections to history.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories and its elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use Punjabi letter patterns to anticipate pronunciation and connect written language with meaning. Bring contextual strategies to understanding key information, details, and stories in spoken and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: Punjabi letter patterns, pronunciation, and meaning; deriving meaning from speech and other texts; comprehending stories; language-learning strategies.

### CC-02

Participate in brief, simple conversations and communicate ideas orally and in writing. Request clarification and check meaning, adjusting a message or its wording when necessary.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: short and simple conversations, exchanging ideas and information, and seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Tell stories in speech and writing with clear transitions and time relationships. Choose ways of presenting information that work for the learner's and others' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories, sequence and past/present/future elaborations, and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Compare Punjabi community practices and their purposes with one's own cultural experiences. Engage with Punjabi people and communities, and consider how culture shapes personal and shared viewpoints.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural similarities and differences, experiences with Punjabi people and communities, and examining experiences through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize how storytelling contributes to family, community, and personal identity. Include First Peoples perspectives and local knowledge, recognizing multiple ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Develop knowledge of Punjabi letter-pattern relationships, including patterns that share sounds and support rhyme.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi letter patterns and its same-sound groupings and rhyming-words elaboration.

### C-02

Build a repertoire for description, personal concerns, and questions that can invite explanation and reflection. Connect events in sequence and express their past, present, or future timing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: question types with the open-ended-question elaboration, descriptions, sequences, personal interests/needs/opinions, and time frames.

### C-03

Recognize how audience, purpose, register, and format shape a text, and how narrative elements help a reader or listener follow a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore Punjabi artistic expression and cultural practices across communities. Consider how to acknowledge authorship and avoid using cultural knowledge or creative material without suitable permission, context, or representation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi works of art, cultural aspects of various Punjabi communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Examine First Peoples connections among oral histories, language, place, and identity, including the importance of community traditions and protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The reviewed Punjabi examples are in **Gurmukhi**. The Grade 9 standard describes letter patterns and their relationship to pronunciation and meaning; this record does not invent a character quota, supply a Shahmukhi edition, or infer a second script requirement.

The questions elaboration includes open-ended reasoning and reflection, not only yes/no questions. At the same time, the interaction competency specifies short, simple conversations; open-ended topics do not imply an unsupported advanced-proficiency standard.

Past, present, and future appear in the content and narrative elaborations. The summary does not turn these into an exhaustive grammar inventory or a requirement to memorize the example sentences.

Punjabi communities and cultural practices should not be reduced to one national or religious identity. First Peoples knowledge remains situated in its own communities and places. Cultural and oral-history material needs appropriate context, attribution, and permission.

The official text definition includes oral, written, visual, digital, and combined communication. Presentation accessibility is an explicit competency; particular activities and art forms remain examples in [S1], not a compulsory resource list.
