# Mandarin - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Mandarin

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed source explains its expectations in English and includes Mandarin examples. Mandarin is the learning target. The metadata does not prescribe classroom language arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Coordinate tone, phonetic representation, and Chinese characters to understand and create Mandarin messages and narratives, while examining cultural experience, community, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** Previous exposure to Mandarin sounds, familiar characters, and everyday exchanges may help; this is preparation advice, not a provincial entrance condition. Schools determine placement and support.

**Materials:** Use [S1] for reference. Any selected character, audio, video, or community-learning resources need their own accessibility, attribution, and permission information; no product or character list is prescribed here.

**How to use:** Read the grouped items with their source locators and consult the complete elaborations before mapping a resource. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes, character levels, or a lesson sequence.

This record covers Grade 9, not all grades contained in a downloadable compilation. The second-language requirement in [S2] concerns Grades 5-8; the Grade 9 list presents choices rather than ten compulsory courses.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending deliberately to language supports comprehension, while conversation about matters of personal interest gives Mandarin learning a meaningful purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; conversations about things learners care about.

### BI-02

A new language can offer a different view of one's cultural identity. Creative expression makes it possible to encounter and appreciate other cultural experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language acquisition and identity; creative works and cultural diversity.

### BI-03

Stories communicate experience and viewpoint, with the capacity to carry knowledge, history, and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories and its elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Mandarin sounds and tones with phonetic notation, Chinese characters, and meaning. Recognize and produce characters relevant to the content being communicated.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: sound/phonetic representation/character/meaning relationships, including tonal variation, Zhuyin, and Pinyin elaborations; recognizing and producing content-related characters.

### CC-02

Interpret important information and supporting details across speech and other text forms. Use context and language-learning strategies in both understanding and expression, and check meaning through clarification when exchanging ideas orally and in writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: deriving meaning from texts, communication strategies, oral and written exchanges, and seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Construct spoken and written narratives with a clear event sequence and appropriate time references. Choose presentation forms that support the communicator's and the audience's diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories, transitions and past/present/future elaborations, and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Compare cultural practices in Chinese communities with one's own, considering the purposes behind them. Engage with Mandarin-speaking people and Chinese communities, and reflect on cultural influences on experience and worldview.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural similarities and differences, experiences with Mandarin-speaking people and Chinese communities, and examining experiences through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize storytelling's role in personal and collective identity, and attend to First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and different ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Study phonetic systems and tonal distinctions alongside Chinese character structure. Connect a character's sounds and meanings with its component parts and radicals rather than treating it only as an isolated visual shape.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phonetic systems; Chinese characters, meaning, and structure, including parts and radicals; tonal variations.

### C-02

Develop common sentence resources for questions, descriptions, personal concerns, and connected events. Express past, present, and future situations using Mandarin ways of signalling time and experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, commonly used vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions, sequences, needs and opinions, and time frames, including the past-experience elaboration.

### C-03

Understand how text form, audience, register, and purpose relate, and how story components organize narrative information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore Chinese artistic expression and cultural practices across communities. Learn the responsibilities attached to using cultural material, including appropriate context, permission, accurate representation, and acknowledgement of authorship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Chinese works of art, cultural practices in various Chinese communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Consider how First Peoples oral histories and relationships to identity and place connect language with cultural knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The phonetic-representation elaboration names **both Zhuyin and Pinyin**. Do not silently substitute Pinyin-only coverage for the source or treat phonetic notation as a replacement for the explicit character-production competency. The source gives no compulsory character count in this Grade 9 page.

Tonal variation includes the source's unmarked *ma* example as well as marked tones. Time-frame learning is not a mandate to conjugate Mandarin verbs like those of another language: [S1] illustrates a past experience using `过`.

Mandarin is the target language; references to Chinese communities do not mean that all members share one language or culture. First Peoples histories and local knowledge remain distinct and require appropriate context and permissions.

Texts may combine spoken, written, visual, and digital elements. Accessible presentation formats are part of the competencies, while the particular art forms and engagement activities in elaborations are examples. This summary establishes neither a standardized proficiency level nor a required vocabulary or resource list.
