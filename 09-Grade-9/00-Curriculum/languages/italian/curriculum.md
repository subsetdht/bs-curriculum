# Italian - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Italian

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed official source is English with Italian examples. Italian is the language being learned. These metadata fields do not determine the language of classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Understand and communicate experiences and opinions in Italian, build connected narratives, and investigate the cultural lives and contributions of Italian and Italian Canadian communities.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier Italian learning can help with familiar vocabulary and exchanges; this reference does not impose an official prerequisite or proficiency threshold. Placement is a school decision.

**Materials:** Consult the linked official source to use this reference. Learning resources, recordings, and community activities should state their own materials, accessibility provisions, and permissions.

**How to use:** Map resources to a grouped local item, then check its source locator and elaborations for precise expectations. Local IDs are not official BC codes or an ordered teaching plan.

This is a Grade 9 record, even when linked downloads contain other grades. [S2]'s second-language requirement is for Grades 5-8, not a blanket requirement to take Italian or every Grade 9 language choice.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Listening and viewing attentively help learners understand Italian, while conversation about personally significant topics gives that learning purpose.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening and viewing; conversations about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Encounters with another language and its creative expression can broaden cultural understanding and prompt reflection on one's own identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity and culture through language acquisition; creative works and cultural diversity.

### BI-03

By shaping experiences into stories, people communicate perspectives, preserve knowledge, and contribute to shared identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories and its elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate Italian spelling to pronunciation and use intonation and tone meaningfully. Combine language patterns, context, and other strategies to understand significant information, details, and stories in speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: letter patterns and pronunciation, intonation and tone, meaning from speech and texts, story comprehension, and language-learning strategies.

### CC-02

Take part in brief, simple conversations and exchange ideas in both speech and writing. Clarify uncertain meanings and verify understanding through appropriate changes to a message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: short and simple conversations, oral and written exchanges, and seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Tell stories orally and in writing, keeping events and time relationships understandable. Select ways of presenting information that work for the learner's and others' abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories with transitions and past/present/future time frames; presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Investigate the purposes of cultural practices in Italian communities and compare them with personal experience. Engage with Italian people and communities, and analyze how a cultural standpoint shapes experiences and worldviews.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural similarities and differences across communities and regions, experiences with Italian people, and analyzing experiences through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize how stories contribute to individual, family, and community identity. Give attention to First Peoples perspectives, local cultural knowledge, and different ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Build knowledge of Italian spelling patterns and a practical repertoire for questions, descriptions, and expressing needs, wishes, feelings, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian letter patterns; common vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions, including questions, descriptions, needs, desires, emotions, and opinions.

### C-02

Use temporal language and sequencing to connect events rather than presenting them as unrelated statements. Learn ways to express past, present, and future situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, sequence of events and past, present, and future time frames, including the Italian tense examples.

### C-03

Recognize how a text's form and intended audience affect its language, and how story components work together to make a narrative.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Study Italian creative expression, diverse community practices, and contributions by Italians and Italian Canadians in both historical and contemporary life. Consider cultural permission, representation, and authorship when using this material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian creative works, contributions of Italians and Italian Canadians past and present, cultural practices in various Italian communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Explore how First Peoples oral histories, identities, and relationships to place connect language with culture and community knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Italian's time-frame elaboration names *passato prossimo*, *imperfetto*, *presente*, and *futuro semplice*. Preserve that guidance when planning; it is not an exhaustive conjugation syllabus or a requirement to reproduce the sample sentences. Intonation and tone are explicit communicative competencies.

The contribution strand extends beyond arts to wider social and cultural contributions, including language and inventions. It includes Italian Canadians and must not be reduced to a study of Italy alone. Regional practices, everyday routines, and idiomatic language are examples of cultural study, not a mandatory festival or food list.

Texts include oral, written, visual, digital, and combined forms. The source includes accessible presentation choices; it does not prescribe a textbook, vocabulary count, or standardized proficiency level.

First Peoples histories and community-specific cultural works require respectful context and permission. An example involving an Elder does not authorize collecting stories, and studying creative works does not grant redistribution rights. The complete official elaborations remain at [S1].
