# Spanish Entry - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Spanish Entry

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; the reviewed official source is English with Spanish examples. Spanish is the learning target. The source and document tags do not determine the language of classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Develop an entry-level repertoire for understanding supported Spanish messages, responding and exchanging information, and investigating language and culture across Hispanic communities.

**Prerequisites:** This is the separately named entry pathway, not the continuing [Spanish 9 course](../spanish/README.md). The record adds no prior-course or home-language requirement; the school determines entry placement and appropriate support.

**Materials:** Consult the linked source for reference use. Learning resources should provide clear, appropriately paced language and state their own materials, accessibility provisions, attribution, and permissions.

**How to use:** Use the local items to locate related expectations, then check the complete source and elaborations for their conditions. IDs are repository references, not official BC codes, a proficiency scale, or an ordered lesson plan.

The source is specifically Spanish Entry 9. [S2]'s second-language requirement covers Grades 5-8; this Grade 9 option is neither a compulsory language for every learner nor interchangeable with continuing Spanish.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Deliberate listening and viewing support language acquisition, and a repertoire of frequently used words and patterns can already make back-and-forth communication possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; reciprocal communication using high-frequency words and patterns.

### BI-02

Stories provide a way into language while helping learners make sense of experience, culture, and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories, language acquisition, and understanding the world, including their elaborations.

### BI-03

Creative expression connects language with culture. Learning about another language and culture can also deepen understanding of one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as language and cultural expression; understanding one's own language and culture through acquiring another.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Spanish pronunciation, letters, punctuation, and intonation with meaning. Understand key information and supporting details in **slow, clear speech** and other texts, drawing on language-learning strategies and non-verbal information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communication: pronunciation/letters/punctuation/intonation relationships, comprehension in slow clear speech and other texts, language-learning strategies, and non-verbal cues.

### CC-02

Respond to questions and straightforward commands or instructions, and exchange ideas orally and in writing. Use familiar clarification statements and questions to keep an exchange understandable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communication: responding to questions, simple commands, and instructions; exchanging ideas and information; seeking clarification using common statements and questions.

### CC-03

Select a way of presenting information that supports the learner's own abilities and those of the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communication: presentation formats best suited to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Identify and compare linguistic and cultural information from Hispanic communities around the world. Relate cultural practices to one's own and consider how cultural perspectives affect experiences and worldviews.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: linguistic and cultural information about worldwide Hispanic communities, cultural-lens reflection, and similarities and differences in cultural practices.

### CC-05

Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, local cultural knowledge, and different ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring Spanish spelling-sound patterns and distinctions of grammatical gender and number that support basic sentence construction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Spanish letter patterns; gender and number and its masculine/feminine and singular/plural elaboration.

### C-02

Use high-frequency language to ask questions, describe, give instructions, compare, and express personal interests, needs, and opinions. Link events in sequence and use common verbs in context to refer to past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, descriptions, instructions and comparisons, sequences, personal concerns, and time-frame elaboration.

### C-03

Recognize how form, audience, register, and purpose shape familiar texts, and how story elements provide a framework for narrative meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore cultural practices across Hispanic regions and their artistic expression. Consider permission, representation, and authorship when engaging with cultural works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural practices in Hispanic regions; Hispanic works of art and its elaboration on cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Understand that First Peoples relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place are cultural connections, not just geographical facts. Place can carry learning, memory, history, and belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and the expanded place elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Keep the supported conditions in this entry curriculum: frequent language patterns, slow and clear speech, non-verbal cues, and responses to simple instructions. Do not replace them with an unsupported expectation of unrestricted spontaneous fluency.

**Past, present, and future remain explicit Content**, even in this entry course. The source does not separately list the continuing [Spanish 9](../spanish/curriculum.md) competency to narrate stories orally and in writing. Retain both distinctions rather than making Entry present-only or silently adding the continuing course's production requirement.

Gender and number, instructions, and comparisons are explicit here. The source describes grammatical categories, not learner gender identities, and does not prescribe an exhaustive grammar or vocabulary inventory.

The source's *Hispanic* community scope is international. Its text definition includes multimodal communication, and its art elaboration explicitly raises appropriation and plagiarism even though these are not a separate top-level Content bullet.

First Peoples place-based knowledge and oral histories require appropriate local context and permission. The listed cultural activities and artistic media are examples, not permission to reproduce works or a compulsory itinerary. Consult the full elaborations at [S1].
