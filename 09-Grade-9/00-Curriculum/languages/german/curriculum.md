# German - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > German

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English, as is the official source reviewed. German is the language of the learning expectations; these metadata fields do not specify the classroom's language arrangements.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and exchange German messages, use grammatical relationships and temporal language to tell connected stories, and investigate cultural perspectives in German-speaking communities and one's own life.

**Prerequisites:** Previous experience with familiar German words and exchanges is useful preparation, not an entry requirement established by this reference. Consult the school for placement.

**Materials:** Access to [S1] is sufficient for reference use. Select and attribute any classroom texts, recordings, or cultural resources separately, with appropriate accessibility and permissions.

**How to use:** Start with a relevant local item and follow its locator to the complete standards and elaborations. IDs support repository resource mapping; they are not official BC codes, a grammar syllabus, or a lesson sequence.

The record summarizes the Grade 9 source standards, not the whole multi-grade download. [S2]'s second-language requirement concerns Grades 5-8; this Grade 9 catalog is a set of course choices, not a requirement to take all of them.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding grows through deliberate listening and viewing, especially when learners use German to discuss matters they find meaningful.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; conversations about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Learning German can make familiar cultural assumptions visible in a new way. Creative expression gives learners opportunities to encounter experiences different from their own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language acquisition and identity; creative works and cultural diversity.

### BI-03

Stories are a means of communicating experience and perspective as well as sharing knowledge and a sense of history.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing experiences and perspectives through stories and the stories elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect recurring German spellings with pronunciation and use that knowledge in understanding texts. Draw together central information, details, and narrative meaning, using contextual and language-learning strategies rather than decoding words in isolation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: German letter patterns and pronunciation, meaning from speech and other texts, understanding stories, and strategies for oral and written language.

### CC-02

Communicate ideas in speech and writing, checking that participants understand one another. Repair a message through clarification, repetition, or revised wording as appropriate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: exchanging ideas and information, seeking clarification, and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Develop spoken and written narratives with an intelligible progression through events and time. Present information in a form that suits both the learner's and the audience's abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories, past/present/future and transition elaborations, and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Compare cultural practices in German communities with one's own and investigate the purposes behind them. Participate in experiences involving German-speaking people and reflect on how cultural perspectives shape individual and shared experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: cultural similarities and differences, experiences with German communities, and examining experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize storytelling as a contributor to personal and collective identity. Include First Peoples perspectives and local knowledge while recognizing that knowledge can be developed and expressed in different ways.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Understand German declension as changes in grammatical forms and endings associated with gender, case, and number.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German declination and gender, case, and number, including the grammatical-endings elaboration.

### C-02

Build a usable repertoire for asking questions, making descriptions and comparisons, and expressing personal concerns. Connect events with sequencing language and place common actions in past, present, and future contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions; question inversion, comparisons, sequences, personal interests/needs/opinions, and time-frame elaborations.

### C-03

Learn how audience, purpose, and form shape a text, alongside the relationships among setting, characters, events, and problems in stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-04

Explore artistic expression and cultural life across German-speaking countries and communities. Consider the responsibilities involved in using cultural material, including avoiding plagiarism and misleading or unauthorized appropriation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German works of art, cultural aspects of various German countries and communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-05

Study connections between language and culture through First Peoples oral histories, relationships to place, and identity, with attention to local protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The pronunciation elaboration includes patterns such as `ie` and `ei` and vowels with umlauts. These are examples supporting sound-pattern recognition, not an exhaustive phonics checklist. The distinct declension content must not disappear into a generic vocabulary summary.

Past, present, and future refer to common verbs used in context; the source does not establish mastery of every German tense or case paradigm here. Spoken and written narration is expected, but the summary adds no standardized proficiency level or compulsory vocabulary count.

The official notion of text includes oral, written, visual, digital, and combined forms. Accessible presentation choices are part of the competencies, not an optional replacement added by this reference.

German cultural coverage is not confined to one country or one set of traditions. The source's event, art, and community-contact examples remain examples. First Peoples histories and other cultural works need appropriate context, attribution, and permission; an elaboration mentioning an Elder does not authorize collecting or reproducing knowledge.
