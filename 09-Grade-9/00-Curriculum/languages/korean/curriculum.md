# Korean - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Korean

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; additional-language study where offered and selected. The immersion policy [S3] and the course's explicit French-program guide designation [S4] support this mapping, not compulsory enrolment or identical local offerings. This record reviews the English course source, not a French-language edition.

**Document language:** English; the official source reviewed is English with Korean examples. Korean is the language being learned. Neither language tag prescribes the classroom's instructional language.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange Korean messages with attention to writing, sentence relationships, and social formality, while using stories and cultural encounters to consider identity and perspective.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with some Korean characters and everyday exchanges is helpful preparation, not a course-entry condition established here. The school determines placement and supports.

**Materials:** Use the linked official curriculum for reference. Any additional Korean texts, audio, visual resources, or community activities need their own accessibility, suitability, and permission checks.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to [S1] before connecting a resource with a local item. IDs group expectations for reference; they are not official BC codes, a prescribed lesson sequence, or a proficiency scale.

The record is limited to the Grade 9 standards, irrespective of the grades collected in downloads. [S2] sets out a Grades 5-8 second-language requirement; it does not require every Grade 9 learner to select Korean or all ten languages.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing support language learning, and personally meaningful Korean conversations can sustain that learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, listening and viewing with intent; conversing about things learners care about.

### BI-02

Encountering another language and its creative works can broaden cultural understanding and change how learners see their own identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language acquisition and identity; creative works and cultural diversity.

### BI-03

Stories allow experiences and perspectives to travel between people while carrying knowledge and a sense of belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, stories as a way to share experiences and perspectives, including its elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Korean pronunciation and sounds with phonetic representation, characters, and meaning. Use those relationships and contextual strategies to understand central information, supporting details, and stories in spoken and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: pronunciation/sound/phonetic representation/characters/meaning relationships, meaning from speech and texts, story comprehension, and language-learning strategies.

### CC-02

Participate in brief, simple conversations and exchange ideas in speech and writing. Clarify uncertain meanings and check shared understanding rather than allowing a breakdown in communication to go unnoticed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: exchanging ideas and information, short and simple conversations, and seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Create spoken and written stories with a clear progression through events and time. Share information in presentation formats that suit the abilities of the learner and the audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Thinking and communicating: narrating stories, transition and past/present/future elaborations, and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Analyze similarities and differences between personal cultural practices and those of Korean communities, and engage in experiences with Korean people. Examine how a cultural standpoint shapes experiences and worldviews.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: analysis of cultural practices, experiences with Korean people and communities, and examining experiences through a cultural lens.

### CC-05

Recognize how stories express perspectives and contribute to family, community, and personal identity. Include First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Personal and social awareness: story and identity and the Korean-story elaboration; First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how Korean particles identify relationships within a sentence, including the subject, object, and location roles illustrated in the source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, particles (functional words) and its subject/object/place-particle elaboration.

### C-02

Connect language choices with social formality and etiquette. Distinguish the source's formal-polite, informal-polite, and casual endings, including their use in questions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, language formality and etiquette and the three-ending elaboration; question types and degrees of formality.

### C-03

Use everyday sentence resources to describe people and situations, arrange events in sequence, and express interests, needs, and opinions. Understand that Korean sentence endings also mark past, present, and future contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, vocabulary, sentence structures, and common expressions; descriptions, sequences, personal concerns, and time frames with their sentence-ending elaboration.

### C-04

Learn how purpose, audience, register, and format organize a text, and how setting, characters, and plot help build narrative meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of common texts and common elements of stories.

### C-05

Explore Korean artistic expression and cultural practices across communities. Understand why cultural material needs accurate context, appropriate permission, and acknowledgement of authorship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean works of art, cultural practices in various Korean communities, and ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

### C-06

Examine First Peoples connections between language and culture through oral histories, identity, and relationships to place, with respect for community protocols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture: oral histories, identity, and place.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's Korean examples use Hangul. Recognizing sound-character-meaning relationships cannot be replaced by a romanization-only account. This Grade 9 page does not establish a character quota or an exhaustive spelling syllabus.

Keep **formality** and **time** distinct: both affect the sentence endings illustrated in [S1], but they do different communicative work. The particle examples distinguish grammatical roles; this summary does not invent a complete particle inventory or assume that all questions use the same social register.

Stories, artistic work, and cultural practices are not evidence that every Korean community shares the same experiences. First Peoples knowledge and local cultural perspectives remain distinct, with community-specific protocols and permissions.

The source recognizes multimodal texts and presentation choices suited to diverse abilities. Its cultural activities and language examples are not a compulsory resource list. Consult the full elaborations rather than treating this condensed record as a complete grammar or assessment specification.
