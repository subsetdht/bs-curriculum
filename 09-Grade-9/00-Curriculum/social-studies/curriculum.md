# Social Studies - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Social Studies](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed source is English. This does not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Investigate how ideas, power, geography, and collective identities shaped change in Canada and the world during **1750-1919**, using evidence and historical thinking to interpret different experiences and consequences.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with timelines, maps, source comparison, and distinguishing observation from interpretation is helpful, not a formal course-entry condition.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. Learning tasks need appropriately contextualized sources and accessible maps or other representations; no particular textbook is prescribed here.

**How to use:** Read the grouped summaries alongside [S1]. Source locators identify the official standards and their examples; local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a mandatory chronological teaching plan.

The official source is titled Social Studies 9, 1750-1919. This is a subject-level record, not a list of senior history electives. Examples that reach beyond the period can support comparison without changing the course's central historical scope.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

New ideas and ideologies can reshape institutions and people's possibilities. Their effects are bound up with unequal power and changing relationships within and between societies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, emerging ideas and ideologies; disparities in power.

### BI-02

Physical environments shape the opportunities and constraints within which political, economic, and social changes occur.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, physical environment and change.

### BI-03

Collective identities are made and remade through historical experience rather than remaining fixed across time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, construction and change of collective identity.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Frame historical and geographical inquiries, organize and interpret evidence, and communicate defensible findings or decisions. Use sources accurately, including citations and appropriate map, graph, and table conventions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Social Studies inquiry processes and skills; inquiry elaboration.

### CC-02

Judge why a development matters while comparing how its significance changes across groups, times, and places. Investigate disagreements between historical accounts by weighing the reliability of sources and the adequacy of their evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, significance and evidence.

### CC-03

Compare continuity and change in the experiences of different groups. Explain how wider conditions and the choices of individuals or groups contribute to events and their consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, continuity and change; cause and consequence.

### CC-04

Interpret past and present perspectives in relation to the norms, values, beliefs, and worldviews of their settings. Avoid assuming that one account represents everyone who experienced an event.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, perspective and its primary-source/context elaboration.

### CC-05

Identify stated and unstated ethical judgments in sources, then develop reasoned judgments of one's own. Consider responsible ways to remember past actions and respond to their consequences in the present.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, both ethical judgment standards.

## Content

### C-01

Investigate revolutions in political organization, social relations, economies, and technology alongside the rise of nationalism and modern nation-states, including Canada. Consider how new forms of production, governance, and national belonging changed people's lives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, political/social/economic/technological revolutions; nationalism and modern nation-states, including Canada.

### C-02

Examine imperialism and colonialism through their effects on Indigenous peoples in Canada and elsewhere. The source's elaborations connect this study to treaties, the Indian Act, reserves, and residential schooling as well as to other colonial settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, imperialism and colonialism on Indigenous peoples; treaties and Indian Act elaboration.

### C-03

Trace changing population patterns, migration, and population growth, relating movements of people to their causes, barriers, contributions, and effects on identity. Study conflict at local, regional, and global scales, including the period culminating in the First World War.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, global demographic shifts; local, regional, and global conflicts and elaborations.

### C-04

Examine discriminatory policies and injustices, who experienced them, and responses to unequal treatment. The core standard explicitly identifies the **Head Tax, Komagata Maru incident, residential schools, and First World War internment**; do not omit them in favour of only general discussion of prejudice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, discriminatory policies and injustices in Canada and the world.

### C-05

Relate Canada's physiographic features and geological processes to regional environments and human activity. Consider how access to natural resources and different perspectives on their use connect geography with economic activity and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, physiographic features of Canada and geological processes; associated resource and regional-identity elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source distinguishes broad standards from **sample topics, activities, and questions**. Its lengthy revolution and conflict examples are not a requirement to teach every listed event. Conversely, the named injustices in [C-04](#c-04) appear in the core standard itself and must not disappear when condensing the source.

The **1750-1919** frame remains visible even where an elaboration makes a later comparison. Do not silently turn this into a 20th-century survey or present an out-of-period example as the main period's event.

Teach colonialism and residential schooling with historical accuracy, care for learners, and attention to First Peoples agency, diversity, and continuing perspectives. The inclusion of a subject in the curriculum is not permission to reproduce community testimony, stories, images, or other cultural works. No re-enactment of traumatic experiences is prescribed by this reference.
