# English Language Arts - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [English Language Arts](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Late French Immersion. The secondary immersion pathway includes English Language Arts alongside, not instead of, [French Immersion Language Arts](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md). See [program policy and course selection](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the official source is in English. Here English is also the language being studied, not evidence that all subjects in these programs are taught in English.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and create increasingly purposeful texts, evaluating evidence, language choices, and perspectives while developing an informed personal voice and awareness of cultural context.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience reading, discussing, writing, and representing ideas is useful preparation. This reference sets no formal reading-level threshold or prescribed prior book list.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Learning draws on appropriately selected oral, written, visual, and digital texts; no particular novel, anthology, or device is mandated here.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and its elaborations. Work across reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing, both individually and collaboratively. The local IDs identify grouped reference items, not official BC standard codes or a required order of instruction.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Language and stories offer creative pleasure as well as ways to understand experience. Encounters with different texts can deepen knowledge of oneself and connections with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, language/story as creativity and joy; stories and texts connecting self, others, and world.

### BI-02

Texts emerge from particular social, cultural, and historical settings, and readers bring their own perspectives to them. Meaning is shaped by both these contexts rather than being received identically by everyone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, worldviews and interpretation; social, cultural, and historical construction of texts.

### BI-03

Questioning messages encountered through listening, reading, and viewing supports informed participation in society.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, questioning texts and educated, engaged citizenship.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Find and select information for a purpose, judging relevance, accuracy, reliability, and source credibility. Use comprehension and inquiry strategies across media and synthesize ideas from several sources to develop understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Comprehend and connect: accessing/evaluating information, comprehension/inquiry strategies, and synthesis.

### CC-02

Interpret connections within and between texts using critical, creative, and reflective thinking. Explain how genre, form, structure, and literary techniques work for particular purposes and audiences, and support thoughtful personal or critical responses with reasoning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Comprehend and connect: features/forms/genres, thinking across texts, responses, literary devices, and text structures.

### CC-03

Examine how language and context shape identity and meaning. Attend to First Peoples narrative and oral traditions, the diversity within and across First Peoples societies, and the influence of place in First Peoples and other Canadian texts rather than treating one work as representative of all communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Comprehend and connect: contexts/values/perspectives, language and identity, self-text-world connections, First Peoples story/oral tradition/diversity, and place.

### CC-04

Exchange viewpoints through attentive listening and respectful discussion, building on others' ideas to extend shared understanding. Use oral storytelling processes, including expressive delivery, with appropriate permission for an existing story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Create and communicate: exchange of ideas/viewpoints; oral storytelling processes and elaboration.

### CC-05

Plan, develop, and refine literary and informational texts through writing and design processes. Choose forms and media for the intended audience, purpose, and message; transform information into original work and substantiate opinions with credible evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Create and communicate: planning/design, refinement, features/forms/genres, original texts, and evidence-supported opinions.

### CC-06

Apply a growing repertoire of Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation conventions to make communication clearer and more effective.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Create and communicate: conventions of Canadian spelling, grammar, and punctuation.

## Content

### C-01

Study how text forms, purposes, genres, and features organize meaning. Relate characterization, narrative structure, and setting to literary devices, and consider how visual or graphic elements such as layout and images contribute to a text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text: forms/functions/genres, text features, literary elements/devices, and visual/graphic elements.

### C-02

Develop strategies for reading, oral interaction, and thinking about one's own learning. Understand writing as a process that includes planning for an audience, revising, and editing rather than only producing a final draft.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes: reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing processes.

### C-03

Build coherent **multi-paragraph** compositions, paying attention to development, unity, and connections between ideas. Use varied sentence structures and transitions, appropriate usage and agreement, and Canadian conventions for spelling, punctuation, capitalization, and quotation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: multi-paragraphing, usage, syntax/sentence fluency, and conventions.

### C-04

Examine how language changes and how choices of words, tone, formality, and inclusive expression shape style. Distinguish a word's literal denotation from its connotations and investigate how rhetorical devices influence an audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: language change, elements of style, rhetorical devices, and connotation/denotation.

### C-05

Understand the effects of voice, pace, emphasis, and gesture in oral language. Select and organize presentation techniques and media with care for the purpose and audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: features of oral language and presentation techniques.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source uses **text** broadly: spoken and performed works, writing, images, digital works, and combinations of these all belong. Do not reduce the curriculum to print literature or essay writing alone. Multi-paragraph composition is explicit, but a fixed essay length or a compulsory named novel is not established by this source.

First Peoples perspectives and oral traditions belong within the competencies, not as an optional substitute for the main literacy work. Respect the permission of storytellers and knowledge holders; a curricular invitation to explore stories does not authorize reproducing or retelling every cultural work.

Digital-source elaborations include credibility, privacy, ownership, and communication conventions. Examples of platforms, text types, or classroom routines are illustrations, not prescribed products or assessment methods.

This record does not replace either French-language-arts curriculum or a second-language course. Program membership and local scheduling should be read from the linked policy notes, not inferred from the language of this summary.
