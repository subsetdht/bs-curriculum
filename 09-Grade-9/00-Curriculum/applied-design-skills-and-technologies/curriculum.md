# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations, with local module selection. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), from an English source. The summary language does not specify classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Develop and evaluate designed solutions for people and communities by combining iterative design, practical skills, and responsible choices of materials and technologies.

**Prerequisites:** Previous collaborative design and tool-safety experience can help. This reference sets no entry qualification; practical tasks require the skills, supervision, and safety preparation appropriate to the tools used.

**Materials:** None are required to read the reference. Equipment and materials depend on locally selected modules and must be specified by the learning activity, not inferred from an example in this summary.

**How to use:** Start with the common Big Ideas and competencies, then consult the Content items for the selected module or modules. Read [S1] for complete wording, safety expectations, and elaborations. The local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC module codes.

Unlike this grade's Arts and Languages catalogs, the official entry is a **core framework with module choices**, not ten separately required courses. The source requires schools to provide a **full-year course equivalent**, assembled from **one or more modules**. Schools may use the ten provincial modules below or locally developed content with the Grade 9 ADST competencies, in addition to or instead of provincial modules.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Design decisions affect people and environments. A workable solution must therefore be considered in relation to social responsibility, ethics, and sustainability, not only technical performance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, social, ethical, and sustainability considerations in design.

### BI-02

Complex projects develop through connected stages. Planning the sequence of skills and choosing different tools or technologies for different stages supports successful completion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sequencing skills; different technologies and tools at different stages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Research and observe empathetically to understand a design opportunity. Identify the people a solution is for, its context and intended effect, and the criteria and constraints that will guide it, including cultural and environmental considerations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Design: Understanding context and Defining; research, observation, and constraints elaborations.

### CC-02

Generate possibilities collaboratively and take creative risks, then compare them against the problem's constraints. Weigh competing social, ethical, and sustainability concerns in choosing a direction while keeping alternatives available.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Design: Ideating.

### CC-03

Use research and appropriate inspiration to plan and build prototypes, recording successive changes. Assess materials for effective use and their potential for reuse, recycling, or biodegradation. Obtain relevant feedback, design tests, analyze the resulting data, and decide whether to revise or abandon an approach.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Design: Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-04

Organize production into achievable steps, select suitable tools and processes, and adapt the plan as needed while minimizing waste. Share a product and the reasoning behind it with an appropriate audience, evaluate its effects and success, and reflect on individual work, collaboration, and new design questions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Design: Making and Sharing, including product and shared-work-space elaborations.

### CC-05

Identify and develop the individual and group skills a project needs. Demonstrate awareness of precautions and emergency procedures in both physical and digital work environments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Learn, choose, or adapt technologies to suit a task, and evaluate their personal, social, and environmental effects, including unintended harm. Examine how land, resources, and culture influence technology's development and use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

**Module selection.** Grade 9 provides ten provincial content modules within the common design framework. A full-year equivalent may combine modules or use locally developed content with the same competencies; listing all ten here documents the provincial choices, not a ten-module requirement for each learner.

**Source locator:** [S1], introductory curriculum note before the learning standards, module/course lengths, full-year equivalent, and local-content provision.

### C-02

**Drafting.** Communicate designs through appropriately dimensioned drawings, models, and virtual representations. Connect perspective, mechanical, and architectural drafting with computer-aided design and manufacturing, using basic code and digital output processes to move from a design toward fabrication, including computer numerical control (CNC) and 3D printing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Drafting: drawing standards/styles, CADD/CAM, CNC, models, code, output devices, and virtual creation.

### C-03

**Electronics and Robotics.** Connect circuit components and the voltage-current-resistance relationship with useful electronic systems and printed circuit boards. Develop robotic responses using inputs, outputs, sensors, microcontrollers, and code; relate mechanisms that transfer energy to mechanical advantage, friction, force, torque, and efficiency.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Electronics and Robotics: circuits and Ohm's law, PCB production, robot behaviours, mechanisms, efficiency, and programming.

### C-04

**Entrepreneurship and Marketing.** Consider how a venture can meet changing needs, including social entrepreneurship in First Nations communities. Relate production and distribution costs, financing, and financial results to business decisions. Examine branding and market segmentation alongside the four marketing considerations of product, price, promotion, and placement, including how online access changes markets.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Entrepreneurship and Marketing: venture risks/benefits, First Nations social entrepreneurship, production, distribution, branding, four Ps, segmentation, technology, finance, and success/failure.

### C-05

**Food Studies.** Connect safe preparation and adaptation of ingredients, techniques, and equipment with understanding food-borne pathogens. Investigate how health, affordability, environments, and ethical choices shape food systems from personal to global scales. Include First Peoples food knowledge spanning harvesting or gathering, preparation, storage, and preservation, with appropriate community permissions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Food Studies: pathogenic microbes, food preparation, food availability and choice, food-system ethics, and First Peoples traditional food use.

### C-06

**Information and Communications Technologies.** Connect binary representations of text, sound, images, and video with how computers process information. Work with text-based coding, drag-and-drop mobile development, and programmable modular components. Relate operating systems to wired and wireless network hardware, topology, and client-server relationships. Use web and cloud environments to create and collaborate while managing personal content and learning networks. Examine privacy, access, and wider social change associated with evolving technologies; keyboarding technique remains part of the module.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Information and Communications Technologies: text-based coding, binary data, mobile/modular development, cloud collaboration, networks, operating systems, web design, digital curation, social change, learning networks, and keyboarding.

### C-07

**Media Arts.** Plan and produce media by combining technical and symbolic choices with conventions for organizing information and stories. Compare digital and non-digital media, their histories and social effects, and standards for production. Consider ownership, appropriation, and legal or ethical limits, including First Peoples viewpoints in media works and their use of media for social advocacy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Media Arts: technologies, conventions, production, compliance, rights, storytelling, First Peoples works and advocacy, and societal influence.

### C-08

**Metalwork.** Relate properties of metals to intended uses and fabrication decisions. Develop understanding of welding, fabrication with hand tools and stationary equipment, and foundry work from patterns and moulds to casting; consider how materials can be recycled or repurposed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Metalwork: metallurgy, uses, welding, fabrication, foundry processes, and material recovery.

### C-09

**Power Technology.** Follow energy conversion and transmission through devices, using thermodynamics to consider heat losses and efficiency alongside fuels and alternative energy sources. Connect small-engine systems and the effects of forces to practical work with measurement devices, hand tools, and technical manuals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Power Technology: transmission, efficiency, thermodynamics, fuels, alternative energy, engines, measurement, tools, forces, and manuals.

### C-10

**Textiles.** Use knowledge of natural and manufactured fibres to make choices about design, use, and care. Modify simple patterns and apply design elements, while examining the social and community effects of textile choices and the roles of textiles in First Peoples cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Textiles: fibres, patterns, design, social factors, and First Peoples cultures.

### C-11

**Woodwork.** Connect the properties of different woods, revised plans, and traditional and non-traditional joinery with a project's purpose, using appropriate tools including stationary power equipment. Investigate woodwork's historical and cultural significance and sustainable wood use, including relationships between First Peoples culturally modified trees and resource stewardship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Woodwork: history/culture, species properties, plans, techniques and joinery, culturally modified trees, and sustainability.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The ten module summaries preserve their distinct content but condense examples rather than reproducing an equipment or software checklist. Product brands, programming platforms, machines, and specific fabrication methods in elaborations are not a direction to purchase particular products or let learners operate equipment without appropriate training.

The local-content option does not remove the common Grade 9 competencies or the source's full-year-equivalent provision. Do not import the Grade 8 module-count rules or the K-3 integrated-content model into this record.

Cultural permission is a design constraint. The source explicitly includes appropriation and sacred knowledge among possible limits, and identifies First Peoples knowledge holders as potential sources of expertise and feedback. Community-specific designs, foods, stories, practices, and knowledge are not freely reproducible simply because a module mentions them.
