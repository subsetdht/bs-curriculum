# Mathematics - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Mathematics](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial mathematics expectations. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA). The reviewed source is in English; neither field prescribes classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Extend numerical reasoning to algebra, use linear and proportional models to solve problems, and judge quantitative information and financial choices critically.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with fractions, integers, basic equations, graphing, and proportional reasoning is helpful preparation, not an admission requirement set by this reference.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Representations and technology for learning should be selected accessibly for the particular task; the curriculum does not prescribe a calculator brand.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the complete expectations and elaborations. These grouped summaries support planning and resource mapping, not a fixed unit sequence or assessment checklist. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Arithmetic and algebra are connected ways of reasoning about quantities and relationships. Flexible calculation with rational numbers provides a foundation for understanding why algebraic operations work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, operations with numbers and algebraic situations; computational fluency with rational numbers.

### BI-02

A continuous linear relationship can be represented in different forms that describe the same regularity. Moving between those forms supports generalization and prediction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, continuous linear relationships and connected representations.

### BI-03

Similarity describes a proportional relationship between shapes, making it possible to compare their dimensions and reason about changes in scale.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, similar shapes and proportional relationships.

### BI-04

A convincing display of data is not necessarily trustworthy. Interpretation depends on how information was obtained, how reliably it represents a situation, and how it is presented.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, validity, reliability, and representation of data.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use patterns, logic, estimation, and mental strategies to investigate mathematical situations. Develop and test conjectures with suitable tools, including technology, and model relationships in meaningful contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Reasoning and analyzing: logic, reasoning, estimation, mental mathematics, tools, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Choose and compare strategies for abstract and contextual problems, using inquiry and visualization to build understanding. Connect problems to place, stories, cultural practices, and perspectives of local First Peoples, the wider community, and other cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Understanding and solving: multiple strategies, inquiry, visualization, and contextual connections.

### CC-03

Make reasoning understandable through mathematical discussion and through concrete, pictorial, symbolic, oral, and written representations. Explain decisions and justify conclusions with mathematical arguments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Communicating and representing: vocabulary, explanation, justification, and representations.

### CC-04

Evaluate one's own and others' approaches, connect concepts across subjects and interests, and use mathematics to support personal decisions. Include First Peoples worldviews and perspectives in making mathematical connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Connecting and reflecting: reflection, connections, personal choices, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Calculate with rational numbers using all four operations and the order of operations, including brackets and exponents. Extend exponent reasoning to numerical and variable bases. The source limits exponent laws to **whole-number exponents**, with **whole-number exponent outcomes after simplification**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, operations with rational numbers; exponents and exponent laws, including their elaborations.

### C-02

Use polynomial structure to carry out operations, interpreting variables, terms, coefficients, and degree. The specified polynomials have **degree no greater than 2**; concrete models can help connect their operations with arithmetic.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, operations with polynomials of degree less than or equal to 2 and elaboration.

### C-03

Represent continuous two-variable linear relations graphically, including rational coordinates and horizontal or vertical lines. Estimate values by interpolation and extrapolation. Solve and verify multi-step one-variable linear equations, including distribution, like terms, variables on both sides, and rational coefficients, constants, and solutions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, two-variable linear relations; multi-step one-variable linear equations and elaborations.

### C-04

Reason proportionally with scale diagrams and similar triangles and polygons, relating an enlargement or reduction to its original dimensions. The associated linear unit conversions are **limited to metric units**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, spatial proportional reasoning and elaboration.

### C-05

Investigate the use of statistics in society by distinguishing a population from a sample and examining sampling, bias, and misleading presentations. Consider privacy, ethics, language, cultural sensitivity, and practical constraints when judging data and the conclusions drawn from it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, statistics in society and elaboration.

### C-06

Use simple budgets and transactions to reason about money decisions. The elaborations connect this work to banking, simple interest, saving, and planning purchases rather than prescribing a particular financial product.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: simple budgets and transactions and elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Do not replace the whole-number-exponent limit with unrestricted negative or fractional exponent work, extend polynomial degree beyond the stated scope, or assume a non-metric conversion requirement. Extrapolated values are estimates from a model, not guaranteed outcomes. This grade's finance scope concerns simple budgets and transactions, not a senior financial-planning course.

[S1] includes inquiry questions and examples connecting mathematics with First Peoples designs, journeys, community events, and data. These illustrate contexts; they do not require reproducing a particular design or carrying out a named cultural activity. Local knowledge holders' permissions and protocols remain necessary.

The English summary can describe shared expectations without supplying French classroom materials. Program implementation and language of instruction are separate from curriculum alignment.
