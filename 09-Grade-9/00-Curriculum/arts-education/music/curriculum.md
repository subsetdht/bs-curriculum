# Music - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Arts Education choices](../README.md) > Music

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The immersion policy [S3] and K-9 required-areas order [S5] support shared curricular applicability. This is conditional on locally selected Arts study, not a requirement to enrol in every option.

**Document language:** Canadian English (`en-CA`); the reviewed official curriculum is in English. Neither establishes classroom language or requires English-language repertoire. This is not a French summary edition or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Develop musicianship through performing, making musical choices, listening critically, and refining work, while understanding music's connections with identity, culture, and shared experience.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with making, listening to, and responding to music is helpful. This reference does not establish an audition, private tuition, a required instrument, or a formal course prerequisite.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the summary. Particular learning activities should identify their instruments or other sound-making tools, accessible participation options, safe listening arrangements, and music-use permissions.

**How to use:** Compare this summary with [S1] and follow the item locators for the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes, performance grades, or a prescribed sequence of repertoire.

Music 9 is one of the [five Arts Education 9 course choices][S4]. It is not compulsory for every learner merely because Arts Education is a required area; see the [grade overview](../../README.md) for required-area and local course-selection context. The provincial introduction supports flexible programming and distinguishes discipline-specific study from the [unified course](../arts-education/README.md) [S2]. This record uses Grade 9 standards only, not a multi-grade curriculum inferred from the K-9 download.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Musical participation can shape how people understand themselves. Making music with others can also create relationships and a shared sense of community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through music experiences and community/relationships through collaborative music.

### BI-02

Music provides access to perspectives and experiences situated in different cultures, locations, and historical periods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspectives and experiences across times, places, and cultures.

### BI-03

Music communicates through its own sensory language. The way sounds and silences are shaped matters to the meaning people make from them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, music's unique sensory language for creating and communicating.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Participate in collaborative music-making in both solo and ensemble contexts. Contribute through independent study as well as shared creative work, developing the vocabulary, skills, and techniques needed for musical participation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, collaborative performance in solo/ensemble contexts, musical vocabulary/skills/techniques, and collaborative/independent musical study.

### CC-02

Make musical choices that express an idea, establish a style, or produce an intended mood or effect. Use varied musical experiences to demonstrate awareness of personal, social, cultural, historical, and environmental contexts, and take artistic chances that support growth.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, contextual understanding, selection/combination of musical elements and techniques, and musical risk for self-growth.

### CC-03

Explain and interpret how musicians' choices of techniques, technology, and environment affect composition and performance. Consider the relationship between musical intentions and the means used to realize them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, musicians' use of techniques, technology, and environments in composition and performance.

### CC-04

Improve musicianship by revisiting musical ideas, working methods, and technical skills, recording development and critically assessing the results. Exchange constructive feedback and put it to use in further work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, development/refinement/documentation/critical appraisal and receiving/offering/applying constructive feedback.

### CC-05

Transfer musical understanding and technique to unfamiliar settings, purposes, and audiences. Analyse, revise, and document musical experiences so that reflection informs subsequent learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, application in new contexts and refinement/analysis/documentation of musical experiences.

### CC-06

Connect performance experiences with personal learning. Explore musical risks that help ensemble members and audiences experience a shared responsiveness, while showing respect and increasingly sophisticated engagement with the course's content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, personal reflection, musical risk and synchronicity, respect, and increasingly sophisticated curricular engagement.

## Content

### C-01

Understand the organization of music in time: a beat or pulse, its speed as tempo, metre's grouping of stronger and weaker beats, the duration of sounds and silences, and their rhythmic arrangement. These related concepts describe different musical decisions rather than interchangeable names for speed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, musical elements including beat/pulse, metre, duration, rhythm, and tempo, with their elaborations.

### C-02

Distinguish pitch from tone colour or timbre, and both from changing levels of volume or dynamics. Form describes the work's structure, while texture concerns simultaneous layers of sound. Musical techniques, vocabulary, notation, symbols, and organizing principles help musicians use these elements to communicate ideas and style.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements/principles/techniques/vocabulary/notation/symbols, including pitch, timbre, dynamics, form, and texture, with their elaborations.

### C-03

Know that interpretation affects how music is performed, even when the starting musical material is shared. Understand how performers' and audiences' roles differ across musical settings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, interpretation and musical choices affecting performance and roles of performers/audiences.

### C-04

Recognize song as a way of communicating traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives. Consider contributions from innovative musicians and composers across communities, genres, periods, and places, including people who develop technologies for making music.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Aboriginal worldviews/cross-cultural perspectives through song and innovative musicians/composers, including the music-technology elaboration.

### C-05

Understand personal and shared responsibilities in making, performing, and responding to music. Distinguish legitimate use of another person's music or cultural expression from plagiarism or appropriation, taking permission, cultural context, and accurate representation into account.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal/social responsibility and ethics of cultural appropriation/plagiarism, including the permission/context elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full course page and all its elaborations were reviewed. Its musical-element list is expressly non-exhaustive. Notation may be traditional or non-traditional; the source's examples do not prescribe one notation system, instrument, ensemble type, genre, or list of works.

The competencies include **both solo and ensemble contexts**, independent study, and collaborative work. They are not a direction to assess every learner only through a public solo. The provincial introduction calls for attention to emotional safety and alternative strategies where performance activities cause distress, alongside hearing conservation, vocal health, safe equipment, and hygiene when instruments are shared [S2].

First Peoples song is part of curricular learning, not an unrestricted repertoire collection. Seek appropriate community guidance and authorization before using or translating cultural materials or practices [S2]. A recording being publicly accessible does not establish permission to transcribe, arrange, perform, record, or redistribute the song; attribution alone does not settle cultural or copyright responsibilities.

Documentation is intended to support learning. Audio/video recording is among the elaboration's possible approaches, not a requirement to record students or publish performances. Respect relevant permissions when choosing any documentation method.

These are Grade 9 course expectations, not external music-examination levels or guarantees that a particular course is offered locally. The four stated programs share content conditionally; French-language summary editions remain unwritten.
