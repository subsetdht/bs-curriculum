# Visual Arts - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Arts Education choices](../README.md) > Visual Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The immersion policy [S3] and K-9 required-areas order [S5] support shared curricular applicability. This is conditional on locally selected Arts study, not a requirement to enrol in every option.

**Document language:** Canadian English (`en-CA`); the reviewed official source is in English. Source and summary languages do not specify classroom language. This is not a French-language teaching resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Develop an intentional visual practice by investigating materials and ideas, designing and revising images, and interpreting how visual art relates to identity, other people, and wider contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier opportunities to make, discuss, and respond to art can help. No portfolio admission standard, private art lessons, or formal course prerequisite is established by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the summary. Activities should state their own media, tools, safe handling requirements, accessibility options, and any permissions; the reference does not require a particular software package or studio setup.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following source locators for official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository reference points, not BC standard codes, a prescribed sequence of projects, or assessment levels.

Visual Arts 9 is a discipline-specific choice within the [five-course Arts Education 9 inventory][S4]. The [unified Arts Education course](../arts-education/README.md) is a separate option. Flexible programming is described in [S2]; an Arts area requirement does not require every student to take this particular course or every listed choice. See the [grade overview](../../README.md) for required-area and local selection context. The record summarizes Grade 9 standards, not every grade included in the K-9 download.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Making and experiencing visual art can affect how a person understands identity. Art experiences can also connect people and contribute to community relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through visual arts experiences and community/relationships through art experiences.

### BI-02

Visual works offer ways of encountering experiences shaped by different places, cultures, and historical periods, broadening the contexts from which viewers interpret the world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspectives and experiences across times, places, and cultures.

### BI-03

Visual art has its own sensory language for making meaning. The design of an image or object matters to the ideas it enables an artist to communicate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, visual arts' unique sensory language for creating and communicating.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop work independently and collaboratively through imagination, questions, and purposeful play. Investigate materials, technologies, processes, and environments by trying different arrangements of visual elements and approaches to image design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, individual/collaborative creation and exploration of materials/technologies/processes/environments with elements, principles, and image design strategies.

### CC-02

Make deliberate design choices to communicate a mood or meaning. Sustain engagement with the work and resolve creative difficulties through disciplined artistic practice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, intentional selection/combination of elements and principles and active engagement/discipline in resolving creative challenges.

### CC-03

Investigate the relationships among identity, place, culture, society, and belonging. Bring awareness of personal, social, historical, cultural, and environmental circumstances to artistic experiences and decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, contextual understanding and identity/place/culture/society/belonging.

### CC-04

Interpret and evaluate how artists communicate through materials, technologies, processes, and settings. Critically examine and document one's own developing ideas and techniques, reflecting on both the art-making process and growth as an artist.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, interpretation/evaluation of artists' practices, development/refinement/documentation/critical appraisal, and reflection on artistic development.

### CC-05

Create for different purposes and audiences, choosing suitable processes and media. Develop and interpret ideas through symbolism, metaphor, and image design, then revise, analyse, document, and present or share personal work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, works for varied purposes/audiences, symbolism/metaphor/design strategies, refinement/documentation, and presentation/sharing.

### CC-06

Connect creative work with personal learning, try expressive approaches that extend current practice, and collaborate reciprocally and respectfully. Create personally meaningful art that demonstrates contextual understanding and increasingly sophisticated engagement with course content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, personal reflection, creative risk, respect, reciprocal collaboration, meaningful contextualized works, and increasingly sophisticated curricular engagement.

## Content

### C-01

Know the visual elements used to build an image or object: line, shape, space, texture, colour, form, and value. Value concerns lightness and darkness, not an artwork's price or merit.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of design and the value elaboration.

### C-02

Organize a composition using relationships such as balance and proportion, unity and harmony, or contrast and variety. Pattern, repetition, rhythm, movement, and emphasis help establish visual direction and interest. Design principles describe purposeful relationships among elements, not simply a list of separate effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, principles of design, including pattern/repetition/balance/contrast/emphasis/rhythm/movement/unity/variety/proportion/harmony and their elaborations.

### C-03

Understand ways of transforming an image idea through elaboration or simplification, magnification or reversal, and fragmentation or distortion. Personal narrative offers a way to connect image-making with self-perception and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, image design strategies and personal narrative as a representation of self-perception/identity.

### C-04

Know how artists' and audiences' roles vary across settings. Consider the contributions of innovative artists working within or changing different styles, genres, contexts, and movements, including changes made possible through technology.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, roles of artists/audiences and contributions of innovative artists, with the innovation elaboration.

### C-05

Recognize visual art as an expression of traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives. Interpret cultural meaning with attention to the communities and contexts from which the work comes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Aboriginal worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives communicated through visual arts.

### C-06

Understand responsibilities toward oneself and others when creating, experiencing, or responding to visual art. Recognize plagiarism and cultural appropriation, including uses that lack permission, remove appropriate context, or misrepresent a community's experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal/social responsibility and ethics of cultural appropriation/plagiarism, including the permission/context/misrepresentation elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full source and all its elaborations were reviewed. Its design-element and principle lists are non-exhaustive. The elaborations explain concepts and offer examples; this summary does not convert them into a required project list or a demand to use every medium.

Keep discipline-specific meanings clear: visual **value** is lightness/darkness, **movement** can direct a viewer's attention across a work, and **proportion** concerns size relationships. Those meanings are different from similarly named concepts in music or dance.

Personal narrative and sharing work are curricular topics, but this does not prescribe public disclosure of private experiences. The provincial introduction emphasizes emotional as well as physical safety and alternatives when activities cause distress [S2]. Documentation is for reflection and making learning visible; online publication or photographing students is not mandatory.

First Peoples visual arts require culturally informed interpretation, not imitation of a generic “Indigenous style.” Seek local community guidance and permission before using or translating cultural material or practices [S2]. Availability of an image, an artist's acknowledgement, or a classroom purpose does not authorize copying, adaptation, or redistribution of protected designs.

Apply appropriate safety precautions to materials, equipment, technologies, and facilities [S2]. Artistic experimentation does not override safe handling or accessible participation. Shared curriculum applicability does not establish local offerings, classroom language, or a French summary edition.
