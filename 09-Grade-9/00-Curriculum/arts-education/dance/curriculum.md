# Dance - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Arts Education choices](../README.md) > Dance

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The immersion policy [S3] and K-9 required-areas order [S5] support shared curricular applicability. This is conditional on locally selected Arts study, not a requirement to enrol in every option.

**Document language:** Canadian English (`en-CA`); the reviewed source is in English. These describe the documents, not a requirement to teach Dance in English. No French summary edition is provided.

**Learning objective:** Use movement with increasing technical and expressive intention, develop and refine choreography, and connect dance-making and interpretation with personal, cultural, and community experience.

**Prerequisites:** Previous opportunities to explore movement, respond to dance, and work with others may help. No audition, private dance training, or formal course prerequisite is established by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed for reading. Learning activities need suitable space, safe equipment where used, and accessible movement and participation options; they should specify their own requirements rather than treating a particular costume, device, or venue as compulsory.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and follow the source locators for official wording and elaborations. Use local IDs for traceable resource mappings, not as official BC codes or a prescribed sequence of performances.

Dance 9 is one discipline-specific choice in the [five-course Arts Education 9 inventory][S4]. The [unified option](../arts-education/README.md) is separate. The provincial curriculum allows flexible Arts programming [S2]; an area requirement does not mean that every learner must take this particular course or all five choices. See the [grade overview](../../README.md) for required-area and local selection context. This record covers Grade 9 standards, not the entire K-9 downloadable compilation.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Moving and creating with others can influence a person's sense of self while building connections within a group. Dance experiences have both personal and communal significance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through dance experiences and community/relationships through collaborative dance.

### BI-02

Dance can offer insight into how people live and understand experience in different places, historical periods, and cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspectives and experiences across times, places, and cultures.

### BI-03

The body in motion is an expressive medium. Dance makes and communicates meaning through a sensory language that is not interchangeable with spoken explanation or another art form.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, dance's unique sensory language for creating and communicating.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Turn imaginative questions and purposeful play into movement phrases, working alone and with others. Choose and combine movement elements so that the resulting dance has a deliberate expressive intention.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, selection of dance elements and individual/collaborative movement phrases inspired by imagination, inquiry, and purposeful play.

### CC-02

Investigate identity and belonging through movement, considering the relationships among place, culture, and society. Develop personally meaningful work that responds to social, historical, cultural, and environmental contexts, and try creative approaches that extend one's expressive range.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, contextual understanding, identity/place/culture/society/belonging, personally meaningful bodies of work, and creative risk.

### CC-03

Explain how choreographers and dancers organize movement to communicate. Interpret and evaluate the effect of their choices of dance elements and choreographic structures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, describing/interpreting/evaluating dancers' and choreographers' use of elements and structures.

### CC-04

Improve a performance piece by developing its movement ideas and technical execution together. Exchange constructive feedback and use it to guide further refinement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, refinement of ideas/movement/technical skills and receiving/offering/applying constructive feedback.

### CC-05

Use movement elements, imagery, and symbols to develop or interpret ideas. Rework familiar processes for different settings, audiences, and purposes, and document and analyse creative experiences to improve their presentation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, transfer to new contexts, symbolism/imagery/elements, and revision/refinement/analysis/documentation.

### CC-06

Relate dance and the process of making it to personal learning. Build reciprocal working relationships, show respect for participants and audiences, and engage with the course's movement knowledge and practices at increasing levels of sophistication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, reflection, respect, reciprocal collaboration, and increasingly sophisticated curricular engagement.

## Content

### C-01

Understand the movement choices involved in dance: the actions of the body; its use of space; dynamics of energy and flow; timing, pulse, and rhythm; and relationships with people, objects, or surroundings. Form concerns how movement is structured. These elements and their associated techniques and vocabulary help make an intention perceptible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements/techniques/vocabulary, including body, space, dynamics, time, relationships, and form, with their elaborations.

### C-02

Know movement principles that support control and effective movement, including alignment, weight transfer, flexibility, strength, balance, and coordination. Alignment includes attention to mobility, stability, and the body's plumb line.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, movement principles and the alignment/mobility/stability elaboration.

### C-03

Know how choreographic devices develop movement motifs and phrases, how compositional forms and structures organize a dance, and how these choices affect clarity of purpose. Formal or informal notation can represent positions and movement so that choreographic ideas can be recorded and revisited.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, choreographic devices and notation, choreographic choices affecting clarity of intent, and compositional devices/forms/structures.

### C-04

Understand the processes, materials, movements, strategies, techniques, and manual or digital technologies that can support creative work. Know how dancers' and audiences' roles vary with the performance context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, supports for creative works and technology elaboration; roles of performers and audiences.

### C-05

Recognize movement and dance as ways of communicating traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives. Learn about artists whose innovations have contributed to different dance genres and communities across places and periods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Aboriginal worldviews/cross-cultural perspectives through movement and dance and contributions of innovative artists.

### C-06

Understand responsibility for physical and emotional safety when creating, performing, and responding. Consider the ethics of authorship and cultural use: plagiarism and appropriation are not resolved simply by finding a dance online or naming its source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal/social responsibility with its safety elaboration, and cultural appropriation/plagiarism with the cultural-permission/context elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full course page and its elaborations were reviewed. The element list is expressly non-exhaustive. Devices and forms offered as examples are ways to develop dance, not an additional checklist of required choreography, named dances, or genres.

The source distinguishes the **five movement elements** from related learning about form, movement principles, and choreography. Do not drop those additional topics because the competency elaboration names five elements. Notation may be informal as well as formal; the source does not prescribe a single notation system or recording technology.

For First Peoples and other culture-specific dance, seek appropriate community guidance. The provincial introduction calls for permission before the use or translation of cultural material or practices [S2]. Neither curriculum inclusion nor an audience's access to a performance grants permission to imitate, record, adapt, or circulate it.

Creative risk concerns artistic exploration, not unsafe movement or compelled physical contact. The provincial introduction addresses appropriate preparation, facilities, supervision, physical and emotional safety, and alternatives where activities cause distress [S2]. Accessible implementation should not make personal disclosure or public solo performance the only way to participate.

This reference condenses Grade 9 learning; it does not establish a conservatory training level, a single dance tradition, or equivalence to the other four Arts course choices. Shared program applicability does not establish a school's offerings or language of instruction.
