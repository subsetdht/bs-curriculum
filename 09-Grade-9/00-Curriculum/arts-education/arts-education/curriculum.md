# Arts Education - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Arts Education choices](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The immersion policy [S3] and K-9 required-areas order [S5] support shared curricular applicability. This is conditional on locally selected Arts study, not a requirement to enrol in every option.

**Document language:** Canadian English (`en-CA`); the reviewed curriculum source is in English. Neither language field prescribes classroom language. This is not a French teaching resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Develop an intentional artistic practice: investigate ideas, make and interpret works, and explain how artistic decisions connect personal expression with other people and their contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience exploring and responding to the arts can support this learning. This reference does not establish an audition, private training, or formal course prerequisite.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Teaching activities should identify their own materials, accessible participation options, safety arrangements, and permissions.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], following each source locator for official expectations and elaborations. The local IDs identify this repository's summaries, not ministry standard codes, assessment levels, or a fixed project sequence.

This record covers the **unified Arts Education 9 option**, not a generic replacement for every Grade 9 Arts course. The [five-course inventory][S4] also offers Dance, Drama, Music, and Visual Arts. The provincial introduction permits learning in one discipline or combinations of disciplines and preserves their different expressive languages [S2]. It does not require five course registrations or equal instructional time in all four disciplines. See the [grade overview](../../README.md) for required-area and course-selection context.

The standards summarized here are specifically for Grade 9. A downloadable K-9 compilation does not make this a grade-banded record.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Arts participation can change how people understand themselves while also strengthening relationships. Personal expression and community formation are connected dimensions of artistic experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through arts experiences and community/relationships through creative arts experiences.

### BI-02

Encountering art opens ways of understanding lives shaped by different historical moments, places, and cultures; one's own experience is not the only starting point for interpretation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspectives and experiences across times, places, and cultures.

### BI-03

A movement phrase, dramatic action, musical passage, and visual image offer different sensory ways of making meaning. Understanding an art form involves learning how its particular expressive language works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the distinct sensory languages of dance, drama, music, and visual arts.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Generate work through imaginative investigation and purposeful play, both independently and with collaborators. Test arrangements of materials, tools, processes, and artistic elements, making deliberate choices about the mood or meaning the work should communicate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, individual/collaborative creation, purposeful play, exploration of materials/environments/tools, and intentional selection of elements and principles.

### CC-02

Use artistic experiences to investigate how identity and belonging relate to place, culture, and society. Bring an informed awareness of personal, historical, social, cultural, and environmental circumstances to the work rather than treating it as separate from its setting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, contextual understanding and relationships among identity, place, culture, society, and belonging.

### CC-03

Explain and evaluate the decisions artists make with their tools, materials, technologies, environments, and processes. Apply that reflective attention to one's own developing ideas and techniques, documenting and critically assessing changes that improve artistic quality.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, interpreting artists' practices and developing/refining/documenting/critically appraising ideas, processes, and technical skills.

### CC-04

Make and interpret meaning through symbols, imagery, and artistic elements. Adjust familiar approaches for a different purpose, setting, or audience, and use analysis, revision, and documentation to strengthen the presentation or performance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, transfer to new contexts, symbolism/imagery/elements, and refinement/documentation for presentation or performance.

### CC-05

Connect creative experiences with personal learning, and try unfamiliar expressive possibilities. Work respectfully with oneself, fellow participants, and audiences, treating collaboration as a reciprocal exchange rather than only a division of tasks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, reflection, creative risk, respect, and reciprocal relationships.

### CC-06

Develop a personally meaningful collection of works that shows engagement with social, cultural, environmental, and historical contexts. Across that work, apply the course's knowledge and processes with increasing sophistication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, personally meaningful bodies of work and increasingly sophisticated engagement with curricular content.

## Content

### C-01

Know how a discipline's elements, organizing principles, techniques, vocabulary, and symbols contribute to mood and meaning. Dance, drama, music, and visual arts have distinct technical content: the unified source explicitly directs readers to their Grade 9 discipline documents rather than supplying one interchangeable vocabulary.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, discipline-specific elements/principles/techniques/vocabulary/symbols and its elaboration referring to the four discipline-specific curriculum documents.

### C-02

Understand how the roles of performers and audiences depend on the setting. Connect those roles with personal and shared responsibilities when making, performing, and responding to art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, performer/audience roles and personal/social responsibility in creating, performing, and responding.

### C-03

Recognize artistic works as expressions of First Peoples worldviews, including both longstanding and contemporary practices. Consider cross-cultural perspectives without treating distinct peoples, communities, or traditions as a single artistic tradition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional and contemporary Aboriginal worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives communicated through artistic works.

### C-04

Learn how innovative artists have contributed to artistic practice in different genres, communities, periods, and places. Innovation is considered in its context, not only as a matter of an artist's fame.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, contributions of innovative artists across genres, communities, times, and places.

### C-05

Distinguish one's own creative contribution from the work and cultural knowledge of others. Understand plagiarism and cultural appropriation as ethical issues; giving credit alone does not establish permission or an appropriate context for cultural material.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism; [S2], Working with the First Peoples communities and Working with the arts community.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full course page, including its elaborations, was reviewed. This is a condensed reference, not an exhaustive set of definitions, examples, or assessment criteria. Purposeful play supports inquiry and reasoning; it is not confined to unstructured recreation.

For the discipline knowledge signposted by [S1], consult [Dance 9: Content](../dance/curriculum.md#content), [Drama 9: Content](../drama/curriculum.md#content), [Music 9: Content](../music/curriculum.md#content), and [Visual Arts 9: Content](../visual-arts/curriculum.md#content), each with its own official source locators. These links do not make all four separate courses prerequisites or compulsory enrolments. The unified curriculum preserves each discipline's character while allowing flexible program design [S2].

First Peoples perspectives belong within the arts curriculum. The provincial introduction calls for local community guidance and **authorization before using or translating cultural materials or practices**, through the appropriate individuals, families, or community contacts [S2]. Public availability, educational intent, or acknowledgement does not authorize copying a design, retelling a protected story, or performing a song or dance.

Artistic risk is not permission to compromise physical or emotional safety. The introduction addresses safe facilities, tools, movement, voice and hearing, as appropriate to the activity, and alternatives for experiences that may cause distress [S2]. Public performance, body contact, or disclosure of personal history should not be imposed as the only route to learning.

The four stated programs share these expectations, subject to course selection and the order's individual exceptions. French-language summary editions remain unwritten; an English source or resource does not determine the local language of instruction.
