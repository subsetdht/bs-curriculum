# Drama - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../../README.md) > [Arts Education choices](../README.md) > Drama

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone. The immersion policy [S3] and K-9 required-areas order [S5] support shared curricular applicability. This is conditional on locally selected Arts study, not a requirement to enrol in every option.

**Document language:** Canadian English (`en-CA`); the reviewed provincial source is English. This is not a direction to teach Drama in English, a French teaching resource, or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Create, interpret, and refine dramatic work through purposeful artistic choices, technical development, collaboration, and reflection on the relationship between performance and human experience.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with imaginative play, storytelling, and collaborative arts work is useful preparation. The reference establishes no audition, private acting lessons, or formal course prerequisite.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this document. Activities should identify safe spaces, any scripts or production materials, accessibility arrangements, and necessary use or performance permissions.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] alongside the summary, using the source locators for the official learning standards and elaborations. The local IDs support curriculum mappings; they are not ministry codes, a rehearsal schedule, or a prescribed production sequence.

Drama 9 is a discipline-specific alternative within the [five-course Grade 9 Arts inventory][S4], not a course that every student must take alongside all the others. The [unified Arts Education option](../arts-education/README.md) remains distinct. Flexible course design is described in [S2]; see the [grade overview](../../README.md) for required-area and local course-selection context. Only Grade 9 expectations are summarized here, even when a linked download contains other grades.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Exploring dramatic roles and experiences can influence one's understanding of identity. Making drama with others also creates opportunities to develop relationships and a sense of community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identity through drama experiences and community/relationships through collaborative drama.

### BI-02

Drama offers ways to encounter experiences beyond one's own, including lives situated in different cultures, places, and historical periods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, perspectives and experiences across times, places, and cultures.

### BI-03

Dramatic meaning is made through a distinctive sensory language. Choices in how a dramatic event is embodied and presented matter to what it communicates.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, drama's unique sensory language for creating and communicating.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop dramatic work from questions, imagination, and purposeful play, both independently and collaboratively. Arrange dramatic elements and principles intentionally so that the work produces a chosen mood, effect, or meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, intentional combination of elements/principles and individual/collaborative creation through imagination, inquiry, and purposeful play.

### CC-02

Investigate connections among identity, place, culture, society, and belonging through drama. Bring contextual understanding to the work and try unfamiliar expressive possibilities, rather than relying only on familiar ideas or techniques.

**Source locator:** [S1], Exploring and creating, relationships among identity/place/culture/society/belonging, personal/social/cultural/historical/environmental context, and creative risk.

### CC-03

Analyse the choices of performers and playwrights: explain and evaluate how dramatic structures, elements, and techniques help an audience understand an idea.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, describing/interpreting/evaluating how performers and playwrights use dramatic structures, elements, and techniques.

### CC-04

Develop a performance piece by improving its ideas and technical execution. Offer useful feedback, consider feedback from others, and make purposeful revisions in response.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and reflecting, refinement of ideas and technical skills and receiving/offering/applying constructive feedback.

### CC-05

Use symbols, imagery, and dramatic elements to extend and interpret meaning. Transfer familiar skills to new purposes and audiences, and analyse, revise, and document performance work to improve how it is presented.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and documenting, transfer to new contexts, symbolism/imagery/elements, and refinement/analysis/documentation of performance pieces and experiences.

### CC-06

Connect the creative process with personal learning, working respectfully and reciprocally with collaborators and audiences. Build a personally meaningful body of work that shows contextual awareness and increasingly sophisticated use of the course's content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and expanding, reflection, respect, reciprocal collaboration, contextualized bodies of work, and increasingly sophisticated curricular engagement.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how character, time, place, and plot establish a dramatic situation. Tension, mood, focus, contrast, and balance influence how that situation is experienced. Character work involves considering another person's perceptions, feelings, thoughts, and beliefs, not simply adopting an outward appearance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements/techniques/vocabulary and the character elaboration.

### C-02

Distinguish dramatic forms, which provide different ways of presenting dramatic meaning, from conventions, the established techniques used to investigate that meaning. Work with a variety of forms and conventions; these may also bring different media or arts disciplines together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, a variety of drama forms and conventions, including their definitions and cross-media elaboration.

### C-03

Know how performers' and audiences' roles change with the setting, and understand the personal and shared responsibilities involved in creating, performing, and responding to drama.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, roles of performers/audiences and personal/social responsibility in drama.

### C-04

Recognize storytelling and drama as expressions of both traditional and contemporary First Peoples worldviews and cross-cultural perspectives. Learn how innovative artists have contributed to drama across different genres, communities, times, and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Aboriginal worldviews/cross-cultural perspectives through storytelling and drama and contributions of innovative artists.

### C-05

Understand the ethical difference between developing original work and taking another creator's work or a community's cultural expressions without appropriate permission or context. Attend to plagiarism, cultural appropriation, and the risk of misrepresenting the people whose experiences are portrayed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism, including the cultural-permission/context/misrepresentation elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete course page and all its elaborations were reviewed. Its element list is non-exhaustive. Examples of forms, such as improvisation, tableau, or readers theatre, illustrate possibilities rather than requiring a production in every named form. The course is broader than staging a scripted play.

Drama's exploration of other viewpoints does not license stereotyping or claiming authority over another community's stories. The provincial introduction calls for consultation with local First Peoples communities and authorization **before** cultural materials or practices are used or translated [S2]. Credit alone does not secure rights to a script, story, ceremony, recording, or adaptation.

Keep artistic risk separate from physical and emotional danger. The introduction addresses safety in the use of spaces, materials, costumes, voice, and performance activities, as applicable, and alternatives for activities that may cause distress [S2]. Improvisation need not demand personal disclosure, humiliation, unwanted contact, or public solo performance as the only way to participate.

Documentation supports reflection and development; the source's examples do not require filming students or posting their work publicly. This summary is not a required play list, an audition standard, or an assessment rubric.

The record covers a Grade 9 course choice and the four stated programs, not all possible Arts programming. Local course availability and classroom language remain separate from source and summary language; French summary editions have not been written.
