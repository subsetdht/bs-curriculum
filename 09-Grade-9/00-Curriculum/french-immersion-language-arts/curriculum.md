# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion and Late French Immersion. The provincial policy distinguishes their entry models but directs Late Immersion in Grades 8-12 to the same secondary-program section [S2]. This record covers the regular Grade 9 immersion entry, not a transition course, Core French, English Language Arts, or Francophone first-language arts.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative curriculum is in French (`fr`). This original summary is not an authorized translation, French classroom material, or a requirement to teach through English. Source locators are labelled English glosses of the French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Use increasingly flexible French to interpret literature in cultural and historical contexts, reconsider perspectives, and create coherent communication with a deliberate emotional effect.

**Prerequisites:** Experience understanding connected French texts, sustaining conversations, and revising writing is helpful. These are practical supports, not new admission conditions or proficiency thresholds; program placement follows the applicable policies.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. Access to [S1] supports checking the official expectations. Chosen classroom texts and media need their own program/language suitability, attribution, permissions, and accessibility provisions.

**How to use:** Read alongside the French source and its elaborations, linking the Big Ideas, skills, and content rather than using them as separate checklists. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes or a prescribed sequence.

**Grade and text scope:** The source standards are specifically for Grade 9, even though the linked downloads compile other grades. Content is developed in varied contexts, with **novels and fables** identified in the source's context elaboration. Oral, written, and visual communication uses print, digital, and multimedia forms [S1]. The subject introduction [S3] connects reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Greater expressive range helps people articulate who they are and what they think. Language also carries a community's knowledge and values, making communication both a personal and a cultural activity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), communication and self-definition, and language as a cultural carrier of knowledge and values.

### BI-02

A text can sustain more than one level of meaning. Looking beyond an initial reading helps reveal how these meanings work together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), different levels of text study and the meanings they reveal.

### BI-03

Literature offers a way to explore a society's circumstances and the questions people were asking at the time a work emerged.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), literature's relationship to the reality and concerns of its period.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret communication as evidence of the linguistic and cultural variety of the Francophone world. Examine cultural symbols and values in texts, including Indigenous texts, rather than assuming that a single cultural reading fits every work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: Francophone linguistic/cultural variety and analysis of symbols and values, including Indigenous texts.

### CC-02

Analyse and synthesize meaning, comparing what authors communicate as well as their viewpoints and purposes. Use that inquiry to question and revise one's own knowledge and assumptions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: analysis and synthesis, critical reconsideration, and comparison of messages, viewpoints, and intentions.

### CC-03

Use the features of a text to deepen understanding of a society. Distinguish literal from figurative meaning and examine the clues that establish a character's nature.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: text characteristics and society, literal/figurative meaning, and characterization.

### CC-04

Respond spontaneously and effectively in spoken French, both in proposed situations and in authentic interactions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: spontaneous, effective oral communication in proposed and authentic situations.

### CC-05

Create texts with the intention of evoking feelings. Use the structures being studied and choose verb tenses with an understanding of the meanings they convey.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: emotional effects, studied text structures, and appropriate use of tense values.

### CC-06

Strengthen a text through enrichment and revision, attending to how its ideas connect, how smoothly it communicates, and the quality of its expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: enrichment for coherence, fluency, and text quality.

## Content

### C-01

Know how communication changes with register, distinguishing familiar, everyday standard, and more formal or literary French. Prepare a contribution with a clear purpose and organization, and use clarification and elaboration to make its meaning accessible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies: language registers, taking the floor, clarification, and elaboration; register and speaking elaborations.

### C-02

Connect cultural and historical knowledge with literary form. For a novel, examine narration, viewpoint, the role of characters, plot, and setting; for a fable, consider the moral and the relationship between literal events, figurative meaning, and social customs. Figurative and sound devices, including personification, metaphor, simile, hyperbole, and alliteration, help shape expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), cultural/historical elements and literary tools: characteristics of novels and fables, and figures of speech; corresponding elaborations.

### C-03

Understand narrative progression and the organization of a descriptive passage. Distinguish a physical portrait from a psychological portrait concerned with feelings and values. Descriptive organization develops a central subject through connected aspects and sub-aspects, framed by an introduction and conclusion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative structure, physical/psychological portrait, and descriptive sequence; corresponding elaborations.

### C-04

Coordinate past-time expression using the **passé composé, imparfait, and plus-que-parfait**. Connect verb forms and their meanings with the genres being studied; the source includes the plus-que-parfait and specifically calls for **recognition of the passé simple**, not an equivalent production requirement for that tense.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: coordination of past tenses and genre-related moods/tenses; past-tense and verb-form elaborations.

### C-05

Work with hypothetical sentences and with pronouns replacing direct and indirect objects. Preserve the distinction between these grammatical roles; the source's object-pronoun elaborations include **y** and **en**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: hypothetical sentences and direct-/indirect-object pronouns; corresponding pronoun elaborations.

### C-06

Know ways to expand expression through varied vocabulary and sentence types, and to review a text using approaches such as rereading, reference tools, peer feedback, and revision guides.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: revision strategies and their elaboration; enrichment tools: vocabulary variety and sentence types.

## Elaborations and boundaries

- **Preserve the grammar distinctions.** The source names three past tenses in its coordination elaboration and distinguishes recognizing the passé simple from using other studied forms. Its reference to hypothetical sentences does not establish a new exhaustive list of conditional constructions. Do not replace these Grade 9 targets with generic “correct grammar” or import a neighbouring grade's inventory.
- **Genres support contextual language learning.** Novels and fables are the identified Grade 9 contexts, not a prescribed list of titles or authors. The content goes beyond recalling a plot: it includes character portraits, descriptive organization, cultural meaning, and literary techniques. The subject introduction [S3], “A language continuum” (English gloss), connects grammatical progression with text genres and communication.
- **Oral and visual work matters too.** “Text” in [S1] includes oral, written, and visual forms. Spontaneous oral interaction and the creation of emotional effects are not reducible to an English reading report.
- **Program and instruction language are separate from this document's language.** [S2] supports the Early/Late secondary applicability recorded here, but does not make immersion and Francophone language arts interchangeable or turn this English summary into suitable French instructional material. It also provides for English Language Arts within immersion; this French-language subject does not replace that area.
- **Retain First Peoples perspectives in interpretation.** The curriculum explicitly includes Indigenous texts when studying symbols and cultural values. [S3] also relates French-language learning to Francophone and Indigenous cultures and perspectives. Use community-appropriate sources and permissions; do not treat distinct traditions as interchangeable or reproduce protected cultural works.
- The complete French elaborations remain at [S1]. Their illustrative pronoun forms, literary devices, and revision approaches are not an exhaustive compulsory exercise list. This is a condensed reference, not a lesson, assessment rubric, authorized translation, or ministry-endorsed record.
