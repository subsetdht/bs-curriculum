# Science - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Science](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial science expectations. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), from an English source. This reference does not determine the language of classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Use evidence and models to explain reproduction, chemical behaviour, electric circuits, and interacting Earth systems, while considering the consequences of scientific knowledge and human decisions.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing, measuring, interpreting simple graphs, and discussing cells, matter, and energy is useful preparation, not a formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No equipment is needed to read this reference. Investigations require separately planned supervision, safety procedures, accessible equipment, and relevant ethical and cultural permissions.

**How to use:** Read [S1] with the source locators below. The local IDs identify grouped summaries for mapping resources, not official BC codes, laboratory instructions, or a prescribed teaching sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Cellular reproduction links new cells to existing life. Understanding how cells are produced helps explain similarities and differences among offspring.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, cells derived from cells; reproduction inquiry elaboration.

### BI-02

An atom's arrangement of electrons helps explain its chemical behaviour and how it combines with other atoms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, electron arrangement and chemical nature.

### BI-03

Electric circuits can be understood by following moving charge and examining the conditions that allow or restrict its flow.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, electric current as flow of electric charge.

### BI-04

Living systems, land, water, and air interact rather than operating independently. Matter cycling and energy transfer connect these Earth systems across scales.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, interconnected biosphere, geosphere, hydrosphere, and atmosphere.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Sustain an inquiry into a question of interest, making observations that lead to increasingly complex questions. Develop more than one hypothesis and predict possible outcomes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Questioning and predicting.

### CC-02

Plan and carry out individual and collaborative investigations, selecting field or laboratory methods suited to collecting reliable qualitative and quantitative evidence. Use appropriate equipment systematically and accurately, assess risks, and address safety, ethics, cultural considerations, and environmental impacts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Planning and conducting.

### CC-03

Interpret local environments and use First Peoples perspectives, local knowledge, and other ways of knowing as information sources. Analyze patterns, inconsistencies, independent/dependent variables, and cause-and-effect relationships; use graphs, interpolation, extrapolation, models, and diagrams to reach evidence-consistent conclusions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Processing and analyzing data and information, including ways-of-knowing elaboration.

### CC-04

Critique methods, data quality, models, and explanations by considering uncertainty, confounding variables, assumptions, and alternatives. Propose improvements, evaluate secondary-source credibility and bias, and examine how changing technologies, careers, and social or environmental consequences relate to scientific work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Evaluating: error, uncertainty, model limitations, skepticism, sources, careers, and implications.

### CC-05

Apply learning to unfamiliar situations and develop or refine possible solutions to local and global problems. Work independently and with others to support well-being and care for communities and environments, considering scientists' roles in innovation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Applying and innovating.

### CC-06

Construct theoretical models and communicate scientific explanations or proposed actions for a particular audience. Support claims with evidence and appropriate conventions, and reflect on how experiences of place and different worldviews shape inquiry and communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, Communicating, including place elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Compare asexual and sexual reproduction through their cellular processes and outcomes. Connect mitosis with the production of matching cells and asexual forms, and meiosis with the formation of sex cells; human sexual reproduction involves offspring that are not genetically identical to either parent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, asexual reproduction: mitosis and different forms; sexual reproduction: meiosis and human sexual reproduction.

### C-02

Use periodic-table organization to relate atomic number and element properties. Connect electron arrangement to the compounds that elements form, including the naming and formulas of ionic and covalent compounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, element properties in the periodic table; electron arrangement and compounds, with elaborations.

### C-03

Explain why an electric circuit needs a complete path, and investigate how its components and layout affect charge flow. The elaborations include series, parallel, and short circuits, alternating and direct current, and the relationship among voltage, current, and resistance expressed by Ohm's law. Electrical safety requires attention to the hazards associated with both current and voltage.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, complete circuits; voltage, current, and resistance and their elaborations.

### C-04

Trace how solar energy drives processes connecting air, water, land, and living things. Follow matter through living and non-living parts of ecosystems, considering human changes to sources and sinks and the accumulation and concentration of substances in organisms and food chains.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, effects of solar radiation; matter cycles in biotic and abiotic components, including bioaccumulation and biomagnification elaboration.

### C-05

Consider sustainability as a systems question: changing one part can affect others. Include First Peoples knowledge of interconnectedness and the sustainability of systems when examining relationships from local to global scales.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, sustainability of systems; First Peoples knowledge of interconnectedness and sustainability.

## Elaborations and boundaries

[S1] supplies examples of reproductive forms and matter cycles; the summary does not turn each example into a separate compulsory project. Human reproduction belongs within the biology content here; the separate [Physical and Health Education record](../physical-and-health-education/curriculum.md) addresses health decisions and relationships.

This is not a set of unsupervised electrical, biological, or chemical experiments. Risk assessment, safe equipment choices, and ethical practice remain part of the competencies, not optional additions after an activity is designed.

First Peoples knowledge is included in both inquiry and systems content, not treated as an enrichment sidebar or as one uniform worldview. Use place-appropriate sources and community protocols; a curriculum link gives no permission to collect, reproduce, or disclose community knowledge.
