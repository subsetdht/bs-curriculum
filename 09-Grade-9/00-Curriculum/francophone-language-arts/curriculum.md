# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the French first-language program. These are not the expectations for French Immersion Language Arts or Core French. The provincial required-areas order includes Francophone students and identifies French Language Arts as their K-9 language-arts area [S3].

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative curriculum is in French (`fr`). This is original reference writing, not an authorized translation or an English-language teaching requirement. Source locators are labelled English glosses of the French headings and topics.

**Learning objective:** Develop informed literary interpretation and effective expression in French by examining social criticism, character construction, and performance, comparing works in context, and strengthening participation in Francophone cultural life.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading French literature, discussing interpretations, and revising connected writing is helpful. These are learning supports, not official admission or language-proficiency conditions; program eligibility is a separate matter.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. Consult [S1] for the official expectations. Any literary works or performance materials used in teaching need their own attribution, accessibility provisions, and permissions.

**How to use:** Read the three curriculum sections together and follow the locators to the French source and its elaborations. The local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC standard codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and text scope:** This is the regular Grade 9 core entry, not a K-9 grade band or a named senior course. The source gives particular attention to **fables, plays, and comparative essays**, while strongly recommending engagement with other text types and genres. Reading, listening, viewing, writing, speaking, and representing work together across oral, written, visual, print, and digital forms [S1] and [S2].

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Literary works can challenge the society that produces them. Some also carry values beyond their original setting, making it useful to distinguish a work's immediate social criticism from its longer-lived significance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), authors' critical treatment of social issues and values carried by enduring texts.

### BI-02

Interpretation involves construction: readers build an impression of a character from stated and unstated clues, while a stage adaptation can make a familiar work appear different.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), stage adaptation and the explicit/implicit basis of the character effect.

### BI-03

Expressive control depends on how vocabulary, linguistic conventions, and the organization of a text work together, not simply on having something to say.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), conventions, vocabulary richness, and textual organization.

### BI-04

French connects people with Francophone communities. In Canada, relationships between First Peoples and Francophone communities also shape both groups' languages and cultures; influence is not only in one direction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), participation through French and reciprocal Indigenous-Francophone linguistic and cultural influences.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Build an interpretation from specific evidence rather than relying only on a literal retelling. Infer the author's underlying message or outlook, and investigate the social criticism in a fable together with the audience it addresses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: implicit authorial messages, precise clues, and fables' criticism and intended audiences.

### CC-02

Use an author's biography and sociohistorical clues in the work to understand its context. Compare texts from different settings and consider how a message's significance and reception change across societies and periods.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: author biography and context, reception of messages, and comparison across sociohistorical contexts.

### CC-03

Explain how characterization shapes a reader's impression of a character. Examine how choices made in adapting or staging a work affect interpretation, and recognize the role of dramatization in the genres being studied.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: character effect, stage-adaptation choices and their effects, and dramatization.

### CC-04

Recognize the range of literary expression within the Francophone world and within Indigenous communities, rather than treating either as a single literary tradition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: literary diversity in Francophone and Indigenous communities.

### CC-05

Share responses to a work's themes and make deliberate stylistic choices to affect an audience. Prepare and present staged interpretations of an excerpt or a complete text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: thematic responses, stylistic effects, and staging portions or complete versions of texts.

### CC-06

Produce texts that suit the genres being studied and use their relevant linguistic conventions. Rework form and expression to improve the quality of the text, rather than treating the first version as finished.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: genre characteristics, linguistic conventions, and improvement of form and expression.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how narrative movement organizes events from an initial situation through disruption and development to an outcome. Distinguish the compact setup, action, and resolution of a fable. A comparative essay organizes similarities and differences between elements **within one work or between two works**.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative structure, fable structure, and comparative essay; corresponding elaborations.

### C-02

Recognize how literary and dramatic tools shape meaning. Character construction includes the contrast between hero and antihero, stereotypes, direct and indirect characterization, and symbolic details. A fable combines a brief narrative with poetic features and a moral; a play uses stage directions and forms of speech such as dialogue, monologue, and the aside. Allegory and personification are specifically identified among the figures of speech.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: character, characteristics of fables and plays, and figures of speech; corresponding elaborations.

### C-03

Distinguish direct from indirect discourse and recognize language registers as choices that shape how a voice or message is presented.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: discourse types and language registers; direct/indirect discourse elaboration.

### C-04

Know strategies for making meaning while reading, including drawing on prior knowledge, questioning, inference, visualization, and tracking important ideas and references. Communication and social interaction also involve physical presence: posture, gaze, and gesture contribute to theatrical expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), strategies: reading, communication, and socialization; comprehension and presence/theatricality elaborations.

### C-05

Understand writing as a process of planning, drafting, checking, revising, and bringing a text to a form that can be shared or published.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), writing strategies and the writing-process elaboration.

### C-06

Expand expressive choices through varied, precise vocabulary, idiomatic and figurative language, and lexical fields: groups of words connected by a subject or idea.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), enrichment tools: figurative language, idiomatic expressions, vocabulary richness and variety, and lexical fields.

## Elaborations and boundaries

- **Genre emphasis is not an exclusive reading list.** The source singles out fables, plays, and comparative essays and recommends other genres too. It does not prescribe particular authors, titles, performance lengths, or numbers of texts. A comparative essay need not always compare two separate works, and staging may use an excerpt rather than a whole work.
- **Character effect is an interpretation, not simply a character description.** The source's *effet-personnage* concerns the reader's constructed impression, supported by explicit and implicit evidence. Its theatrical focus includes both analysing adaptations and creating performances.
- **Keep language study contextual.** The Grade 9 content identifies discourse types, registers, and enrichment tools; it does not supply the same grammar inventory as Grade 9 French Immersion. The subject introduction [S2], “Grammar, usage and conventions” (English gloss), places grammar in meaningful language use rather than isolated drills.
- **Text is broader than print literature.** The source includes oral and visual meaning-making as well as written texts. The six language-arts modes support one another; reading summaries cannot replace listening, speaking, viewing, writing, or representing.
- **First Peoples perspectives belong within this subject.** Retain the mutual cultural and linguistic relationships and the diversity of Indigenous literary expression. Follow community-specific protocols and permissions for stories, cultural works, and adaptations; a curriculum reference is not permission to reproduce or perform them.
- This condensed record does not reproduce the complete elaboration inventory. Consult [S1] for precise terminology and [S2] for the subject's approach. Examples and clarifications are not an additional compulsory activity list. `source-checked` records source comparison, not ministry endorsement or independent educator review.
