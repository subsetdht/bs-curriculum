# Career Education - Grade 9

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 9](../README.md) > [Career Education](README.md)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program policy and course-selection notes](../README.md#program-policy-and-course-selection).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), from English sources. This does not determine classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Use growing self-knowledge, community connections, and research to explore possible learning and work pathways, make informed decisions, and develop safe and adaptable working habits.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier reflection on interests, collaboration, and community roles may help. This reference does not require employment, volunteering experience, or a settled career choice.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. Activities should identify their own information sources, permissions, and safety arrangements; private student career profiles are not part of this public record.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for the Grade 9 presentation and [S2] for the official **Grades 8-9** grouping. Local IDs support resource mapping; they are not official BC standard codes or a course-completion checklist.

The target repository grade is 9, but the source standards cover **8-9 together**. Do not invent a second set of expectations unique to Grade 9. This record is not Career Life Education, Career Life Connections, or a graduation plan; those have [separate catalog entry points](../../../curriculum-special-entries.md).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding personal interests and strengths helps turn aspirations into manageable learning steps. Progress requires sustained effort and an ability to keep working through difficulty.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, preferences and skills in career goals; effort and perseverance.

### BI-02

Work has personal and collective meanings. The pathways people pursue reflect community, educational, and individual choices, and different perspectives on what work contributes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, value of work; personal, community, and educational choices in career paths.

### BI-03

Economic and labour-market conditions change, so career development includes adjusting plans and learning rather than relying on one fixed route.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, flexibility in response to economic and labour-market change.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Reflect on strengths, preferences, and skills to explore possibilities and set realistic learning goals. Pursue those goals with resilience, and consider how subject choices and experiences beyond classes can shape future pathways.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, self-assessment, realistic learning goals, and curriculum/co-curricular choices.

### CC-02

Investigate connections between personal purposes and community needs, questioning assumptions about work and possible careers. Examine how family expectations, traditions, and differing social perspectives influence choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, purposes/passions and local/global needs; diverse views of work; family traditions and community needs.

### CC-03

Present oneself responsibly in public and digital settings, and solve problems with respect, collaboration, and inclusion. Demonstrate safety skills and recognize why safe work practices matter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, personal public identity and elaboration; respectful collaboration; workplace safety.

### CC-04

Use varied research methods and networks to investigate career possibilities and clusters. Recognize the value of mentors and sources such as family, community members, Elders, teachers, and peers; the source also recognizes learning through memory, history, and story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, resources and mentors; research skills and career clusters, with elaborations.

### CC-05

Explore volunteering and unfamiliar learning experiences as ways to develop initiative and improve ideas. Apply decision-making strategies to real life, work, or community problems, adapting the approach when circumstances change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competency, volunteer/new learning experiences and entrepreneurial/innovative thinking; adaptive decision making.

## Content

### C-01

**Personal development.** Connect self-assessment and reflection with goal setting and career research. Use project management to take an idea through planning, resource and timeline decisions, action, and review.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Personal Development: goal-setting strategies, self-assessment, reflection, and project management.

### C-02

**Community connections.** Examine local and global needs alongside the factors shaping jobs in a community. Cultural and social awareness and the career value of volunteering help relate personal possibilities to the communities in which people live and work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Connections to Community.

### C-03

**Learning and career planning.** Learn about graduation requirements and the ways technology affects learning and work. Understand how school, family, community, personal networks, and mentorship can inform decisions and connect learners with opportunities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Life and Career Plan: graduation requirements, technology, decision-making networks, and career planning.

### C-04

**Workplace safety.** Understand the evaluation and control of hazards, workers' rights and responsibilities, and emergency procedures. Safety knowledge supports the practical safety skills in the competencies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Life and Career Plan: workplace safety and its three specified subtopics.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source teaches exploration, not an expectation that a learner select a lifelong occupation or complete a particular work placement. It does not specify a volunteer-hour quota, employment prerequisite, or professional certification. Learning about graduation requirements here is not itself fulfilment of the later requirements.

The official compilation [S2] explicitly identifies **Grades 8-9**. Its K-9 filename describes the whole download, not this record's source grade scope. School sequencing of the shared standards is a local implementation question.

Where Elders, family histories, and community stories inform research, respect consent, cultural protocols, and privacy. An invitation to consult a mentor is not permission to publish their knowledge or a student's personal information.
