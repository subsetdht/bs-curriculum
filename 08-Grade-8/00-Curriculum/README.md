# Grade 8 curriculum

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > Grade 8

**Browse:** [Programs](#choose-a-program) | [Subjects](#subjects-and-learning-standards) | [Language courses](#language-courses) | [Language-study provisions](#language-study-and-course-choices) | [Coverage](#coverage-and-limits)

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Written coverage:** 19 source-checked records: ten core entries and nine language-course options. **Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, with the distinctions below. **Summary language:** en-CA. **Alignment review:** 2026-09-14.

These original, condensed references help families and educators locate learning expectations. They are not official wording, a timetable, an assessment checklist, or a claim that a learner must take all 19 entries. Course and program suitability are separate from the language of these English summaries.

## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Late French Immersion](#late-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

The seven non-language subjects in the table below share provincial expectations across all four programs. This applicability follows sections 1-2 of the [Required Areas of Learning Order][P1], which explicitly includes Francophone authorities, programs, and students; it is not inferred from a translated document.

### English-language program

Start with [English Language Arts 8](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects. Use the [language inventory](languages/README.md) for the board's appropriate second-language choice, subject to the [language-study provisions](#language-study-and-course-choices).

This pathway has **17 applicable written records**, counting all nine language alternatives. That means eight core references plus nine choices, not 17 compulsory courses.

### Early French Immersion

Start with [French Immersion Language Arts 8](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts 8](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects. Unlike the early primary years, Grade 8 immersion includes English Language Arts under [P1], section 4(c).

There are **nine core references** for this pathway and **eight approved non-French additional-language options** in the language table, for **17 applicable records**. Those eight options are not an extra compulsory language requirement and do not replace either language-arts subject.

The [provincial immersion policy][P2] distinguishes curriculum equivalence from classroom language and gives recommended instructional-language allocations. A shared subject record or an English summary does not establish which subjects a particular school teaches in French.

### Late French Immersion

Use [French Immersion Language Arts 8](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), [English Language Arts 8](english-language-arts/curriculum.md), and the seven shared subjects, with the same eight additional non-French language options when offered: **17 applicable records**, not 17 required courses.

The immersion record reviews the separate **6T and 7T late-entry context** in the official French compilation before the Grade 8 standards. [P2] expressly directs Late Immersion Grades 8-12 to the common secondary provisions. This supports a shared Grade 8 record without treating the earlier regular and late-entry standards as identical or requiring schools to merge cohorts.

[P1], section 5, makes a Late Immersion English Language Arts exception for **Grade 6 only**; it is not a Grade 8 exemption.

### Francophone

Start with [Francophone Language Arts 8](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects. This is the distinct first-language French program, not immersion or Core French.

[P1], section 4(b), requires a language **other than French** in Grades 5-8. [English Language Arts 8](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) is a supported option: the [provincial Programme Francophone glossary][P4] explicitly permits English to meet that requirement. The order does not uniquely mandate English as the Grade 8 choice.

The eight approved non-French courses in the [language inventory](languages/README.md) are also course options when appropriately offered. Together with the eight core references and the English option, this gives **17 applicable records**, not a requirement to take English and all eight other languages. Core French cannot satisfy the non-French-language requirement or replace Francophone language arts.

## Language study and course choices

[P1], sections 2 and 4, distinguishes a program's language arts from its additional-language provision. At Grade 8, the English-language program has English language arts and a language other than English; immersion has French language arts and English Language Arts; Francophone has French language arts and a language other than French. The distinct French language-arts records must not be merged merely because both sources are in French.

The [Language Education Policy][P5] describes board choice of offerings and Core French as the default where an alternative is not offered. Read that general policy with the program-specific order rather than assigning Core French to every program.

The [Educational Program Guide Order][P3], sections 2(a)-(b), lists the named language guides and French-language guides for Francophone and immersion programs. This is explicit evidence for approved non-French course options, not an inference from the language of a web page. Its collective French list also includes distinct first-language and immersion language-arts guides and Core French; it is not an every-guide-for-every-program assignment. Core French is therefore mapped here to the English-language pathway, without asserting that enrolment from another program is prohibited.

[P1], section 3, provides specific exceptions involving learners who require an Individual Education Plan or receive English Language Learner services and cannot demonstrate the applicable learning outcomes in the circumstances described there. These are **not blanket exemptions for all IEP or ELL learners**. Section 5's Late Immersion exception concerns Grade 6, not Grade 8.

[P1], section 6, also permits qualifying locally developed Indigenous-language programs under the specified band-council or treaty-first-nation agreement. The nine-course catalog is therefore not an inventory of every eligible Indigenous or locally developed language program. The existence of an unlisted local course does not by itself establish that it meets the order's conditions.

## How to use these references

Start with the program and course scope, then follow a record's locators to the official Big Ideas, Curricular Competencies, Content, and elaborations. The 19 records contain **293 stable, source-located summary items**; grouping related standards does not make these a transcript of every official item.

For example, [Mathematics 8: C-02](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-02) has the complete local key `bc-g08-mathematics#c-02`. These are repository identifiers, not ministry standard codes or achievement levels. Follow the [reference convention](../../curriculum-format.md#linking-to-records-and-items).

There are no formal prerequisites or special materials for reading the references. Lessons and activities, if added later, must establish their own learning objectives, materials, language suitability, accessibility, and permissions.

First Peoples perspectives remain within their curriculum subjects, including local geological knowledge, historical perspectives, arts, design, language, and oral traditions. Neither a source locator nor a curriculum mention authorizes reproducing cultural works or community-specific knowledge.

## District implementation

These are provincial references, not an SD43 or other district timetable. Course availability, module rotations, language of instruction for particular subjects, student placement, and grouping of immersion cohorts must be confirmed with the relevant school or district. No district delivery pattern is presented as a province-wide expectation.

## Coverage and limits

All existing Grade 8 main-catalog entries and all nine courses in its Languages list have source-checked core summaries. Career Education preserves its **8-9** source band; the other records summarize Grade 8 standards even when a download compiles several grades. ADST includes all twelve provincial module options without requiring them all, and Arts Education retains its unified curriculum.

All 19 summaries are in English. The two French language-arts records use **French** primary sources; the other 17 use **English** primary sources. Neither English source availability nor program alignment establishes the suitability of an English classroom resource for French instruction.

French-language repository editions, local language-course inventories beyond the main catalog, lessons, assessments, resources, and tools are not part of this pass. The [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) categories remain empty.

No required primary source remains inaccessible. The immersion record documents a conflicting webpage genre popover and the official DOCX evidence used to resolve it. The Mandarin record preserves its source's distinct Content and narration time-frame statements and explains its phonetic-example ambiguity. These are source-presentation qualifications, not silently invented expectations.

The records provide core summaries, not every elaboration or an independently certified curriculum interpretation. Source revision dates were not established; **2026-09-14** is the actual review date, not a curriculum edition. Maintain the records and navigation using the [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page).
