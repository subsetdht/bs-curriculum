# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the distinct first-language French program. This is Français langue première, not [French Immersion Language Arts 8](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md), Core French, or English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the primary source is French (fr). This original reference is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Locators below are English glosses of recognizable French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and create French texts with critical attention to perspective, cultural identity, and persuasive effect, using literature, description, and increasingly deliberate language choices.

**Prerequisites:** Experience reading, discussing, and composing varied French texts is useful preparation. This reference does not set admission or proficiency requirements for Francophone education; program eligibility and placement are separate matters.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Any literary selection, performance, or learning resource requires a separate suitability, accessibility, attribution, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the official French expectations and elaborations and [S2] for the curriculum's broader approach. Use local IDs for resource mapping; they are not official BC codes, an assessment checklist, or a prescribed teaching sequence.

**Grade and program scope:** These are Grade 8 standards, with particular attention to poetry, novels, and argumentative texts; the source also encourages other genres. The first-language Francophone curriculum supports participation in Francophone communities and cultural development [S2]. Provincial policy distinguishes this program's French language of instruction and minority-language rights [S3]; required French language arts remain distinct from the additional-language provision in [S4].

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Persuasion depends on how a case is built. The arrangement of ideas, strength of arguments, and use of varied sources help a communicator defend a position and influence an audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), effectiveness of argumentation; organization, sources, and influence on the recipient.

### BI-02

Texts carry traces of their creators' surroundings, identities, and views of the world. Through them, readers can encounter a culture and an era as well as an individual perspective.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), environment and perception; authors sharing identity, culture, worldview, and a portrait of an era.

### BI-03

Precise expression brings together purposeful vocabulary, sound language conventions, and an organized text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), linguistic conventions, vocabulary richness, and textual organization.

### BI-04

The histories of First Peoples and Canadian Francophone communities are connected: their interactions have affected one another's languages and cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), mutual linguistic and cultural influence through Indigenous–Francophone interaction.

### BI-05

Using French provides a way to participate in Francophone communities, from close personal settings to wider networks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), communicating in French and participation in the Francophone community; community elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore and reflect by examining what literary and descriptive choices contribute to a text. Use textual clues to distinguish an author's objectivity from subjectivity and to explain how description clarifies context and intent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: literary elements and their roles, objectivity/subjectivity, and descriptive subtleties.

### CC-02

Challenge a text's assumptions, values, and viewpoints with relevant arguments. Consider how the message functions in society and how its reception depends on the time and social setting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: questioning assumptions/values/viewpoints and the social role and reception of a message.

### CC-03

Compare First Peoples and other texts through themes, perspectives, and authorial intentions. Connect the Francophone cultural reference points in texts with one's own and those of others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: comparing texts, including Indigenous texts, and connecting Francophone cultural reference points.

### CC-04

Create and communicate with attention to the audience's emotional response. Notice listeners' non-verbal reactions and adjust speech to improve understanding and persuasiveness.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: evoking emotion and adjusting discourse to non-verbal reactions.

### CC-05

Compose convincing, well-structured arguments and other texts using the genre characteristics and language conventions studied. Strengthen expression through description and revision, attending to both form and quality of language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: genre/convention choices, argumentative writing, descriptive enrichment, and improving form/expression.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how narrative structure develops a story and how argumentative structure introduces a position, develops reasons and explanations, and brings the case to a considered conclusion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative scheme and structure of an argumentative text.

### C-02

Study the distinctive workings of novels and poetry, including narrative progression, descriptive passages, viewpoint, theme, and literary effects. Understand how description and narration connect, and how stylistic choices such as euphemism shape meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: novel, poetry, descriptive tools, point of view, and figures of speech; euphemism elaboration.

### C-03

Explore what complex sentences contribute through coordinated and juxtaposed clauses. Attend also to the overall impression a text creates.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: contribution of complex sentences, coordinated/juxtaposed clauses, and overall impression.

### C-04

Know strategies for interaction and reading: use verbal and non-verbal communication to convey a position, and draw on prior knowledge, questions, inference, key information, and textual references to build understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication/socialization strategies and reading strategies.

### C-05

Develop writing through planning, drafting, correction, revision, and preparation for publication. These processes support improvement rather than making the first version the finished text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), writing strategies and the writing-process elaboration.

### C-06

Enrich expression through imagery and careful word choice. Consider literal and associated meanings, fine differences in sense, related groups of vocabulary, and variety of wording.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), enrichment tools: figurative language, connotation/denotation, nuance, lexical fields, and vocabulary choice/variety.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Poetry, novels, and argumentative texts are the Grade 8 focal genres, not a prescribed list of books or the only acceptable texts. The source explicitly encourages other genres and treats text broadly, including oral, written, visual, digital, and print forms.

The two competency strands integrate interpreting and producing language. Literary study, critical literacy, Francophone identity, and First Peoples perspectives belong within this learning, rather than being optional cultural additions. [S2] explains that grammar and language conventions should be learned in context, not isolated from meaningful reading and writing.

The content references to complex sentences and overall impression do not provide an exhaustive grammar syllabus. Follow the French terminology and elaborations in [S1]; do not substitute English conventions, import another grade's verb requirements, or turn illustrative examples into mandatory exercises.

The Francophone program and French Immersion have different purposes and language-arts records. [S4], sections 2(a) and 4(b), requires boards to offer French language arts and a language other than French to Grade 8 Francophone students, subject to the specific exceptions in section 3. [S3] confirms that English can meet the Grades 5–8 second-language requirement; the order does not uniquely require English for Francophone Grade 8. Core French cannot replace this first-language curriculum or meet the requirement for a language other than French.

This summary does not determine a family's eligibility, a district's timetable, or which additional language a learner studies. Shared provincial subjects and separate language-arts choices are navigated from the [Grade 8 overview](../README.md).

First Peoples and Francophone communities are diverse. Study of cultural relationships or texts does not grant permission to reproduce, perform, adapt, or retell a community's cultural work. Select appropriate, attributed sources and respect any community-specific restrictions.
