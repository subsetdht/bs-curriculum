# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program context](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), separate from classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Respond to needs through iterative design, developing the practical skills and technological judgment required to make and evaluate useful solutions.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience designing, making, and collaborating is helpful, not a formal course-entry requirement. Specialized tools require appropriate preparation and supervision.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Materials, equipment, access arrangements, and safety instruction depend on the actual module and project.

**How to use:** Read the module-delivery instruction and standards in [S1]. Local IDs identify summaries, not official codes, certified tool-use instructions, or a mandatory project sequence.

**Required area, selectable content:** Schools must provide a **full-year-equivalent ADST course**, which may consist of **one or more modules**. They may select from the twelve provincial modules summarized below or develop local modules using the Grade 8 Curricular Competencies and locally developed content. Local modules may supplement or replace provincial ones [S1]. This is not a requirement for every student to complete all twelve modules.

The introduction [S2] places this work within the Grades 6-9 exploration years, with some Big Ideas and processes continuing across grades. The module content here is specifically Grade 8; do not substitute the Grades 6-7 options or the K-5 integrated-content arrangement.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding a need gives design a purpose and helps a proposed solution respond to the people or other users it is intended to support.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, designs responding to identified needs.

### BI-02

More demanding tasks can call for new skills and a combination of tools and technologies, rather than relying only on familiar techniques.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, additional skills and multiple tools/technologies for complex tasks.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate users' experiences with empathy, choose a design opportunity, and define requirements, success criteria, and constraints. Generate and build on ideas, then evaluate possible solutions for feasibility and personal, social, environmental, and ethical effects.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design, Understanding context, Defining, and Ideating.

### CC-02

Use information and expertise to plan stages and resources, explore materials, and construct a prototype or first version. Record iterations, gather feedback, troubleshoot, and test changes rather than treating the first design as final.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design, Prototyping and Testing.

### CC-03

Plan and carry out production using appropriate tools and materials, adjusting the plan and limiting waste. Choose how to share the result, explain design decisions and changes, and evaluate the product against the criteria and its contribution to others or the environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design, Making and Sharing.

### CC-04

Reflect on design thinking, independent and collaborative work, and the management of a shared workspace. Use the experience to identify further design issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design, Sharing, reflection/co-operation and new design issues.

### CC-05

Recognize precautionary and emergency procedures in physical and digital environments. Assess the individual and group skills a task requires and develop them where needed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-06

Select and learn to use technologies that extend capability. Examine intended and unintended impacts and how land, resources, and culture influence the development and use of tools.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

The following items summarize **provincial module options**, not twelve courses required of every learner. The common design, skills, and technology competencies apply to the module choices.

### C-01

**Computational Thinking:** Represent repeatable instructions as algorithms and programs, break problems apart to debug them, and relate binary data representation to programming. Explore connections among visual and text-based languages and programmable modular components.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Computational Thinking.

### C-02

**Computers and Communications Devices:** Connect the design of digital infrastructure with communication from personal devices to larger networks and the Internet of Things. Consider mobile technologies' social and economic effects, ways information is shared, and effective keyboarding practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Computers and Communications Devices.

### C-03

**Digital Literacy:** Manage digital content and learning connections responsibly. Investigate digital citizenship, legal and ethical implications of technology, and how search tools select and rank information so that results can be evaluated rather than accepted uncritically.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Digital Literacy.

### C-04

**Drafting:** Communicate designs through technical plans and manual or computer-aided drawings. Relate drafting conventions and vector files to the virtual creation and development of a design.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Drafting.

### C-05

**Entrepreneurship and Marketing:** Develop an idea in relation to a market need, target users, product or service benefits, and financing. Compare wants and needs, examine persuasive marketing, and include social entrepreneurship in First Nations communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Entrepreneurship and Marketing.

### C-06

**Food Studies:** Connect food preparation and cross-contamination prevention with the effects of changing ingredients. Examine social influences on eating, varied eating practices, local food systems, and changes over time in First Peoples food use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Food Studies.

### C-07

**Media Arts:** Create meaning using digital and non-digital media, considering story, genre, point of view, and the arrangement of image, sound, text, movement, space, time, and lighting. Manipulate and test digital media data, considering the effects of digital media on expression alongside copyright, privacy, and cultural-appropriation issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Media Arts.

### C-08

**Metalwork:** Relate properties of ferrous and non-ferrous metals to design choices, plans, shaping processes, and fastening, including basic welding and fabrication. Consider how hand tools and powered equipment support the work and how metals can be reclaimed or repurposed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Metalwork.

### C-09

**Power Technology:** Investigate devices through energy sources, conversion, and transmission, distinguishing renewable and non-renewable sources and kinetic and potential energy. Relate motion and performance to mass, inertia, aerodynamics, and forces.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Power Technology.

### C-10

**Robotics:** Connect local uses of robots with sensors, end effectors, user-operated and autonomous control, and movement- or sensor-based responses. Use schematics and circuit components to understand assembly and relate program flow to available programming platforms.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Robotics.

### C-11

**Textiles:** Investigate textile sources and use patterns or instructions with hand and machine techniques to make or repair items. Relate colour, culture, and personal expression to textile choices and identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Textiles.

### C-12

**Woodwork:** Connect historical and current woodworking with the properties of natural and manufactured wood. Interpret plans, use hand and powered tools with traditional and non-traditional joinery, and consider reuse and reclamation of wood products.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Woodwork.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This summary inventories all twelve module headings in the Grade 8 source without turning their example tools, software, devices, or techniques into a universal purchasing or activity list. Locally designed modules are permitted curriculum delivery, but their locally chosen content is not an additional provincial standard.

Design includes products, processes, services, systems, and environments. It is iterative rather than a rule that every project follow one fixed sequence. Safety, ethics, waste reduction, and consequences of technological choices belong throughout the work.

First Peoples perspectives appear in design constraints and expertise, food systems, entrepreneurship, and relationships among land, resources, culture, and technologies. Sacred knowledge and cultural ownership can constrain a design; consultation does not itself grant reproduction rights. Follow the community-permission guidance in [S2].
