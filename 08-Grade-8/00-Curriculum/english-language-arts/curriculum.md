# English Language Arts - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > English Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone at Grade 8, with different language-study provisions. See the [sourced program pathways](../README.md#choose-a-program). English Language Arts is required in immersion at this grade; English can meet the Francophone Grades 5-8 requirement for a language other than French, without being that requirement's only possible language.

**Document language:** English (en-CA). This record describes English Language Arts; it does not replace [French Immersion Language Arts](../french-immersion-language-arts/README.md), [Francophone Language Arts](../francophone-language-arts/README.md), or a second-language course.

**Learning objective:** Read, listen, view, write, speak, and represent with increasing independence, using evidence and awareness of context to interpret and create texts.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier experience with varied texts, discussion, and composing is useful background, not a formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No prescribed book or equipment is required to use this reference. Learning texts need separate selection for age, accessibility, program, and rights.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for the official wording and elaborations. Here, a text can be oral, written, visual, digital, or a combination, not only a printed story. Local IDs are repository references, not official standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Language and stories can be enjoyable and creatively rewarding while helping people understand themselves and connect with others and the wider world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity/joy and connections through stories and other texts.

### BI-02

Texts arise in particular social, cultural, and historical circumstances. Readers' and creators' perspectives and worldviews shape the meanings made from them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, different understandings of text and the construction of texts in context.

### BI-03

Questioning messages across media supports thoughtful participation in society rather than passive acceptance of information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, questioning what is heard, read, and viewed.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Find information for varied purposes, evaluate its relevance, accuracy, and reliability, and combine ideas from several sources. Use comprehension and inquiry strategies to extend rather than merely retrieve information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Comprehend and connect, information access/evaluation, comprehension strategies, and synthesis.

### CC-02

Analyze how forms, genres, structures, literary choices, and other features help texts address audiences and communicate meaning. Think critically, creatively, and reflectively within and across texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Comprehend and connect, features/forms/genres, critical thinking, literary techniques, and text structures.

### CC-03

Connect texts with personal experience and the world while examining the contexts, values, and perspectives they express. Recognize language's role in identity and develop personal, creative, and critical responses with reasons and evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Comprehend and connect, contexts/perspectives, identity, personal connections, and responses.

### CC-04

Recognize the importance of stories, narrative, and oral tradition in expressing First Peoples perspectives and values. Develop awareness of ownership and the protocols governing when, where, and by whom particular texts may be shared.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Comprehend and connect, First Peoples oral traditions and protocols/ownership.

### CC-05

Exchange viewpoints to develop shared understanding and extend thinking. Experiment with oral storytelling, selecting expressive approaches and respecting permission to use an existing story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate, exchanging ideas and oral storytelling processes.

### CC-06

Plan, compose, assess, and revise literary and informational texts for different purposes and audiences. Make deliberate choices of form and genre, strengthen clarity and impact, apply developing Canadian language conventions, and transform information into original work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate, writing/design processes, refinement, conventions, form selection, and original texts.

## Content

### C-01

Understand relationships among text forms, purposes, and genres. Examine how story structures, characterization, setting, sensory detail, and figurative language contribute to literary meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text, forms/functions/genres, literary elements, and literary devices.

### C-02

Recognize how textual and visual or graphic features organize and communicate messages. Use relevance, accuracy, and reliability as distinct considerations when judging information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story/text, text features, visual/graphic texts, and relevance/accuracy/reliability.

### C-03

Build a repertoire of reading and oral-language strategies, together with ways to notice and reflect on one's learning. Understand writing as a process that includes revising and editing in relation to audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes, reading, oral language, metacognition, and writing.

### C-04

Develop coherent multi-paragraph compositions and fluent sentence combinations. Connect syntax, grammar, punctuation, quotation, capitalization, and Canadian spelling with purposeful choices of diction, tone, inclusive language, and formality for a context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions, multi-paragraphing, usage/context, style, syntax, and conventions.

### C-05

Consider how spoken features such as pace, emphasis, and gesture affect communication. Choose and organize a presentation medium to suit its purpose and audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions, features of oral language and presentation techniques.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The two competency strands bring together **reading, listening, and viewing** and **writing, speaking, and representing**. Work is both individual and collaborative. Do not reduce this curriculum to written grammar exercises or a novel-reading list.

The source's suggested genres, activities, and revision techniques illustrate the expectations; they do not establish a compulsory set of books, compositions, or presentation formats. Context matters when choosing formal and informal English.

First Peoples texts are not unrestricted material simply because oral tradition is a curriculum topic. Seek the relevant permission and cultural guidance before recording, retelling, adapting, or sharing community-specific stories.
