# Arts Education - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program context](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), separate from classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Make, interpret, and communicate through the arts, developing creative choices and technical skills in relation to identity, culture, place, and audience.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier creative experiences are useful. Auditions, private lessons, or a particular level of performance are not entry requirements established by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this summary. Creative work needs separately selected, safe, accessible tools and materials suitable for the discipline.

**How to use:** Read [S1] alongside the unified-curriculum explanation in [S2]. The local IDs are mapping references, not ministry codes or a required production schedule.

This is the **Grade 8 unified curriculum**, not a collection of four compulsory separate courses. Within the K-8 unified design, teachers can work in an individual discipline or combine disciplines [S2]. The Grade 8 content retains the distinct languages of dance, drama, music, and visual arts; its grade scope is not a single set of K-8 standards.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Creative development involves persistence, a readiness to experiment, and enough patience to try unfamiliar approaches.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative growth and risk-taking.

### BI-02

The arts offer both individual and collective expression. Each discipline provides distinctive ways of creating and communicating rather than a single interchangeable artistic language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, individual/collective expression and the unique languages of the four arts disciplines.

### BI-03

Art can question familiar assumptions and established practices, creating opportunities to encounter different perspectives and experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, artists challenging the status quo.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Create individually and collaboratively through imagination, inquiry, experimentation, and purposeful play. Select and combine artistic elements, materials, movement, tools, technologies, and environments deliberately, while exploring connections among identity, belonging, culture, society, and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating.

### CC-02

Interpret how artists make meaning through their choices and processes, including historical and environmental contexts. Critically appraise and refine ideas and technical skills, drawing on knowledge from other learning and personal experience to understand and respond to works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and reflecting.

### CC-03

Adapt skills and processes for new purposes, audiences, and contexts. Use symbols and discipline-specific elements to express ideas, take creative risks, and communicate responses to artistic works.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting, adaptation, symbols, expression, and responses.

### CC-04

Document, choreograph, perform, and share creative work in varied ways, showing increasingly sophisticated use of the content. Use artistic expression to engage with environmental and global issues as well as personal experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting, experience/document/performance/sharing, issues, and growing sophistication.

## Content

### C-01

In dance, organize the moving body in space, time, and relationships, shaping energy and form to convey mood and ideas. Connect choreography and notation with movement principles such as alignment, weight transfer, balance, coordination, strength, and flexibility.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, dance elements and movement principles; choreographic devices; notation.

### C-02

In drama, build meaning through character, setting in time and place, and plot. Use tension, mood, focus, contrast, and balance together with dramatic forms, conventions, and notation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements; drama forms and conventions; notation.

### C-03

In music, organize sounds and silences through pulse, metre, duration, rhythm, and tempo. Connect pitch and tone quality with changing volume, musical structure, layered sound, and notation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements from beat/pulse through texture and notation.

### C-04

In visual arts, develop images by organizing line, shape, form, space, colour, value, and texture. Make relationships among repetition, pattern, contrast, emphasis, balance, proportion, variety, rhythm, and movement contribute to unity and harmony.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual-art elements and principles of design; image-development strategies.

### C-05

Use discipline-appropriate processes, technologies, tools, materials, and techniques, including symbolism and metaphor. Study artistic works and traditions from different cultures and places, including traditional and contemporary First Peoples arts and ways of making.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, creative processes/tools; symbolism/metaphor; Aboriginal arts and arts-making processes; national/international works and traditions.

### C-06

Understand ethical responsibility in the arts through inclusion, ownership, copyright, and cultural appropriation. Connect personal and collective responsibility with physical and emotional safety when creating, encountering, and presenting work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethical considerations and cultural appropriation; responsibility and a safe learning environment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's detailed terminology distinguishes concepts that share a name across disciplines: for example, musical dynamics concern sound volume, while dance dynamics concern how movement energy is directed. Use the relevant elaborations rather than assuming identical meanings.

The curriculum is flexible about discipline combinations and creative processes. It does not make every example technique, medium, instrument, genre, or presentation format compulsory. Safety includes emotional as well as physical well-being; creative risk-taking does not mean unsafe exposure or forced disclosure.

First Peoples arts remain part of the subject, with their own contexts, ownership, and protocols. The introduction [S2] calls for consultation and permission before using or translating cultural material or practices. A curricular mention does not authorize copying designs, songs, stories, dances, or ceremonial knowledge.
