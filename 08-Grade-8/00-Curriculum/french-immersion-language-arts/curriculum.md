# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion and Late French Immersion. This additional-language immersion curriculum is distinct from English Language Arts, Core French, and [Francophone Language Arts 8](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the primary curriculum source is French (fr). This is an original reference summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use English glosses of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Interpret French texts critically, including legends and theatre, and communicate coherent, evidence-supported viewpoints with growing control of language and awareness of cultural perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Previous immersion reading, discussion, and writing provide useful foundations. This reference sets no entry or proficiency threshold; districts determine placement under provincial program policy.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Selected texts, performances, and other resources require their own language-suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1] and the Grade 8 pages of [S2], following the locators to the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs support precise references; they are not official BC standard codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and program scope:** [S2] separately identifies Grade 6 and Grade 7 late-entry standards, followed by the Grade 8 standards summarized here. [S3], under Late French Immersion in the Secondary School, directs Grades 8–12 to the same secondary provisions as Early Immersion; [S4] describes the FILA curriculum's K–12 scope. Together these support both program labels at Grade 8, not equivalence of the earlier regular and late-entry standards. They do not prescribe merging particular classes or a school's subject timetable.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Meaning depends on more than the words spoken: choices of voice and non-verbal expression help reveal what a speaker is trying to accomplish.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), verbal/non-verbal language and the speaker's intentions.

### BI-02

The values within a text and the circumstances surrounding a literary work offer ways to understand a culture or society. Reading in context can broaden that understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), values and cultural content; literature in context and perceptions of society.

### BI-03

Interpretation requires attention both to what a text states directly and to what a reader must infer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), deepening understanding through explicit and implicit information.

### BI-04

A viewpoint becomes more persuasive when ideas are organized and supported by varied sources; these choices influence the audience's response.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), organization, sources, point of view, and influence on the recipient.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore and reflect by testing different interpretations against a text's explicit and implicit information. Question information sources to judge the reliability and truthfulness of what is presented.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: interpretations, explicit/implicit meaning, and evaluating information sources.

### CC-02

Investigate how legends and theatre convey meaning. Examine fantastic elements in legends, including those of First Peoples nations, and consider how a play's social and historical setting and non-verbal performance support its meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: fantastic elements in legends, sociohistorical context of theatre, and the non-verbal.

### CC-03

Compare the beliefs and viewpoints represented in First Peoples and other texts, making differences in perspective visible rather than treating all texts as expressions of one culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: comparing perspectives, beliefs, and points of view, including Indigenous texts.

### CC-04

Create and communicate critical responses to texts. Synthesize main and supporting ideas in one's own words, and build a character portrait from both directly stated and inferred evidence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: critical responses, summaries, and character portraits.

### CC-05

Develop a case that addresses opposing arguments as well as one's own position. Choose relevant quotations and integrate them using appropriate citation and attribution conventions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: arguments/counter-arguments and quotations with conventions of use.

### CC-06

Arrange connected ideas into coherent texts using a suitable structure. Select revision approaches deliberately to improve the work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: logical, fluent organization and appropriate revision strategies.

## Content

### C-01

Understand how voice, gesture, and language register support communication and the expression of a position. Distinguish familiar, everyday standard, and formal registers, with attention to cultural and historical context.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies: verbal/non-verbal, registers, taking a position, and cultural/historical elements.

### C-02

Study how legends develop imaginative explanations through characters and literary effects, and how theatre develops action through character relationships, dialogue, and stage directions situated in time and place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: characteristics of legends and theatre; [S2], Grade 8 Content elaborations, genre context and characteristics.

### C-03

Recognize elements of oral tradition in First Peoples texts. The source's elaborations include oral narrative, song, and circular thinking as points of study, not a claim that every nation's traditions are the same.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: elements of oral tradition in Indigenous texts.

### C-04

Understand how narrative and legend structures develop events, how information texts develop a topic, and how an argument connects a position, supporting reasons, counter-arguments, and a conclusion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative scheme and structures of legends, informational texts, and argumentative texts.

### C-05

Develop control of relative subordinate clauses and the grammatical functions of complements. Use written conventions that clarify meaning and quotation, including semicolons and quotation marks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), relative subordinate clauses, grammatical functions of complements, and punctuation.

### C-06

Study agreement involving subjects, verbs, and direct objects in passé composé constructions with être and avoir. Work with the verb forms associated with the genres studied, including the present conditional and simple future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), agreement with être/avoir in the passé composé and verb moods/tenses; verb elaboration names conditionnel présent and futur simple.

### C-07

Know ways to review and improve a text, drawing on rereading, reference tools, feedback, and revision guides rather than relying only on an initial draft.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), revision strategies and their elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Grade 8 context elaboration in the French DOCX [S2] specifies **legends and theatre**, consistent with the explicit competencies and content in [S1]. On the review date, the French webpage's introductory context popover instead referred to picture books and youth novels. This summary follows the Grade 8 DOCX clarification; it does not add that conflicting popover wording as another genre requirement.

Texts include oral, written, and visual communication across digital, print, and multimedia supports. Francophone cultural perspectives, social and historical context, and First Peoples legends and oral traditions belong within this subject, not in separate enrichment. The introduction [S4] explains the broader relationship between French-language learning, identity, and culture.

Official elaborations provide the detailed literary and grammatical terminology. This record does not reproduce a complete grammar guide, turn illustrative examples into compulsory exercises, or prescribe a book list, performance, or citation style. Agreement and verb forms must be read in their French grammatical context, not replaced with English conventions.

Early and Late Immersion remain distinct entry pathways. Their inclusion here does not replace the separate late-entry curricula or determine an individual learner's placement. [S5], sections 2(a) and 4(c), requires boards to offer French language arts and English Language Arts to Grade 8 immersion students, subject to the specific exceptions in section 3. Section 5's Late Immersion English Language Arts exception applies only to Grade 6. English Language Arts is a separate subject, not a translation of this one; Core French does not replace the immersion language-arts curriculum.

First Peoples and other cultural works require appropriate context, attribution, and permissions. A curriculum reference to a legend or oral tradition does not authorize retelling, performing, adapting, or reproducing a community's work.
