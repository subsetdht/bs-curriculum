# Social Studies - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program context](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Document language:** English (en-CA), not a requirement for the classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Investigate societies from the **7th century to 1750**, using historical evidence and perspectives to explain interactions, changes, and unequal consequences.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with inquiry, maps, chronology, and comparing accounts is useful background, not a formal entry requirement established here.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. Historical texts, maps, and cultural materials used in learning must be selected, attributed, and made accessible separately.

**How to use:** Follow the source locators to [S1] for the complete expectations and examples. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a required sequence of civilizations.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Interactions between peoples can transform societies. Encounters involving conflict, exploration, expansion, and colonization produce different consequences for different groups.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, contacts/conflicts and the consequences of exploration, expansion, and colonization.

### BI-02

Population and living conditions change through the interaction of human decisions and environmental circumstances.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, human and environmental influences on population and living standards.

### BI-03

New ways of understanding the world can challenge established traditions, creating disagreements about which ideas and practices to preserve or change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, tensions between new ideas and established traditions.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Frame an inquiry, gather and analyze information, and communicate findings and decisions. Select appropriate representations, interpret maps and other graphics, represent information fairly, and acknowledge sources.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and skills, including key-skills elaboration.

### CC-02

Judge historical significance in relation to a particular time and place. Also examine the choices made by creators of narratives, maps, and accounts about what deserves attention.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, the two significance expectations.

### CC-03

Compare sources for credibility and decide whether the evidence is sufficient for a conclusion. Consider origins, purposes, bias, and the distinction between primary and secondary accounts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, evidence and its source-evaluation elaboration.

### CC-04

Describe periods of continuity, change, progress, or decline and identify turning points. Weigh the relative influence of causes and distinguish short-term from long-term consequences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, continuity and change; cause and consequence.

### CC-05

Explain differing perspectives on people, events, places, and issues by comparing the values, beliefs, and worldviews of societies in their historical and geographical contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-06

Make reasoned ethical judgments about past actions and decisions while recognizing the limits of transferring lessons directly from the past to the present.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Compare how societies organize political authority, social relationships, and economic life. The study must include the systems and structures of **at least one Indigenous civilization**, not only European societies.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, social, political, and economic systems and structures, including the Indigenous-civilization requirement.

### C-02

Examine how scientific and technological developments interact with changing philosophies and cultural expression, including changes in how knowledge and ideas circulate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, scientific and technological innovations; philosophical and cultural shifts.

### C-03

Study exchanges of resources, ideas, arts, and cultures within and between civilizations. Connect these interactions with exploration, territorial expansion, and colonization rather than treating contact only as travel or discovery.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, interactions and exchanges among civilizations; exploration, expansion, and colonization.

### C-04

Investigate changes in population and living standards, considering movements of people, disease, settlement growth, and environmental pressures as contexts for explaining change.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, changes in population and living standards and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source specifies a period, broad topics, and a minimum Indigenous-civilization inclusion; it does not prescribe a single civilization list or a Europe-only narrative. Its many named events, societies, people, and sample questions are possible ways to investigate the topics, not an additional compulsory inventory.

Some official elaboration questions make comparisons with later developments. They do not replace the stated **7th-century-to-1750** course scope with a later historical survey. Preserve source context when using those questions.

Colonization and cultural exchange must be examined from more than an expanding state's perspective, including effects on Indigenous peoples. Community-specific knowledge, oral histories, and cultural works require appropriate permissions and context. This summary does not reproduce them or dictate how a community should be represented.
