# Mathematics - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program context](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and classroom-language distinctions.

**Document language:** English (en-CA), independently of the language of instruction.

**Learning objective:** Use proportional and algebraic reasoning, spatial relationships, and data to model situations, justify decisions, and connect mathematical representations.

**Prerequisites:** Earlier work with fractions, integers, equations, measurement, and graphs is useful background, not a formal admission requirement established by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this summary. Any future learning activity should specify its own tools, representations, and accessibility supports.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1] and its elaborations. The local IDs identify condensed concepts for resource mapping; they are not official BC standard codes, a teaching sequence, or a speed-test specification.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Ratios, rates, and percents express comparisons between quantities. Flexible fraction calculation supports reasoning about those quantities rather than relying on a single procedure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, number through ratios/rates/percents and computational fluency with fractions.

### BI-02

A discrete linear pattern can be described in connected forms; moving among those forms helps reveal and generalize the relationship.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, representations of discrete linear relationships.

### BI-03

Considering both the surface and the space enclosed by a solid makes it possible to compare three-dimensional objects mathematically.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, relationships between surface area and volume.

### BI-04

An average is one way to summarize a large data set and support comparison, but its meaning depends on the data it represents.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, interpreting data through averages.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate mathematical relationships using patterns, logic, estimation, and mental strategies. Use models, appropriate tools, and technology to explore situations and test conjectures, including through puzzles and games.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and analyzing.

### CC-02

Develop understanding through inquiry, visualization, play, and problem solving in abstract and practical settings. Compare strategies and connect problems to place, stories, cultural practices, and local First Peoples and other community perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving.

### CC-03

Explain and justify decisions with mathematical language and arguments. Make thinking accessible through discussion and connected concrete, pictorial, and symbolic representations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing.

### CC-04

Reflect on the reasoning of oneself and others, connect concepts across subjects and interests, and use mathematical arguments when making personal choices. Relate mathematical ideas to First Peoples worldviews and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting.

## Content

### C-01

Connect perfect squares and cubes with their roots, using representations and estimates to make sense of square and cube roots.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, perfect squares and cubes; square and cube roots.

### C-02

Reason proportionally with ratios, rates, proportions, and percents, including percents below 1 and above 100 and decimal or fractional percents. Apply comparisons of price and quantity to financial decisions about best buys.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, percents; numerical proportional reasoning; financial literacy - best buys.

### C-03

Calculate with fractions using all four operations and the order of operations. The elaboration includes brackets but excludes exponents from this fraction-operation expectation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, operations with fractions and its scope elaboration.

### C-04

Connect expressions, tables, and graphs for discrete linear relationships, with the relationship work limited to integers. Write expressions and evaluate them by substitution; solve and verify two-step equations whose coefficients, constants, and solutions are integers.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, discrete linear relations; expressions; two-step equations.

### C-05

Represent solids with constructions, nets, and different views. Determine surface area and volume for regular solids, including cylinders and triangular and other right prisms, and relate these measurements to the object's structure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, surface area and volume of regular solids; construction, views, and nets of 3D objects.

### C-06

Use and explain the Pythagorean relationship in right triangles, including finding a missing side.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Pythagorean theorem and its modelling elaboration.

### C-07

Interpret mean, median, and mode as measures of central tendency. Represent the possibilities for two independent events and determine their theoretical probabilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, central tendency; theoretical probability with two independent events.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 8 does not extend every mathematical domain without limit. The integer restriction belongs to the discrete linear relationship and specified equation work; it does not exclude fractions from other topics. The probability expectation is specifically about **two independent events**, and the Pythagorean relationship applies to right triangles. Preserve the distinction between surface area and volume.

Official elaborations give examples of representations, practical problems, and First Peoples connections. They are not a compulsory exercise list or permission to reproduce cultural designs, songs, or knowledge. Work with appropriate local guidance and permissions rather than treating a cultural example as a generic craft.
