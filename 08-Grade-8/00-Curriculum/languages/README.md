## Language choices and requirements

The [provincial Language Education Policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/education-training/k-12/administration/legislation-policy/public-schools/language-education-policy) describes board-selected offerings, with Core French the default where an alternative is not offered for the English-language pathway. Read it with the [Required Areas of Learning Order](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m295_95.pdf), sections 2-6: immersion Grade 8 requires English Language Arts alongside its French language arts, while Francophone Grade 8 has a non-French-language provision. English can meet that Francophone requirement; Core French cannot. The [grade overview](../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices) explains the program distinctions and specific exceptions, which are not blanket exemptions for all IEP or ELL learners.

The [Educational Program Guide Order M231/19](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/education/administration/legislation-policy/legislation/schoollaw/e/m333_99.pdf), sections 2(a)-(b), lists the named guides and French-language guides for Francophone and immersion programs. That listing supports the approved non-French course options; document language alone would not. The collective French list also includes distinct language-arts guides and Core French, and does not assign every guide to every program. The English-language mapping of Core French is not a prohibition on another program's learner enrolling.

Core French, French Immersion Language Arts, and Francophone Language Arts are distinct curricula. ASL is a signed language, not spoken English with gestures. The English language of these summaries does not prescribe an English-speaking classroom or make them French-language learning resources.

The catalog also links the [Languages Template](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/languages-template) for development of other language curricula. Section 6 of the Required Areas of Learning Order permits qualifying locally developed Indigenous-language programs under specified community agreements. The nine-course inventory here therefore does not claim to inventory every eligible local or provincially approved language program. Local development, the applicable approval or agreement conditions, and actual school availability are separate questions.

## Using and maintaining the inventory

Follow a course landing to its source-checked record and source locators. For an item reference, use a descriptive link such as [Core French 8: C-03](core-french/curriculum.md#c-03); its complete local key is `bc-g08-languages-core-french#c-03`, not an official BC code.

**To fill or maintain:** Maintain the named course landings, stable record/item IDs, program scope, grade overview, and central written-summary coverage together. Keep this parent as the course navigator rather than creating a generic Languages learning-standard record.

Follow the [landing-page workflow](../../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page). This page is navigation, not a set of learning standards.
