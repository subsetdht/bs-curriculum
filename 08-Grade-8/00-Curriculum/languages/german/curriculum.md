# German - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > German

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make German compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed provincial source is English. German is the language being learned, distinct from the source and summary languages.

**Learning objective:** Understand and communicate connected information in German, tell stories across time, and examine relationships among language, cultural experience, and perspective.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry or proficiency requirement. Consult the school for placement and continuity in language study.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom texts, communication supports, and their accessibility and attribution need separate consideration.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the locators for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence.

These are the specific Grade 8 standards; the catalog's combined 5-10 download is not a Grade 5-10 band for this record. German is one alternative in [S3], not a requirement to take every listed language. [S2] describes board-selected offerings and the general second-language policy. Read it with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful attention to spoken and visual messages supports language learning. Greater fluency enables learners to sustain exchanges rather than contribute only isolated responses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening and viewing; increased fluency and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Learning German expands how learners can describe themselves and their surroundings. Storytelling connects this expression with experiences and perspectives that others can explore.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expressing ourselves and the world around us; sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Artistic expression brings language and cultural experience together; a work is connected to the people and community whose experience it represents.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as expressions of language and culture, including the creative-works elaboration.

### BI-04

Engaging with another language and culture can reveal new ways of understanding one's own language and cultural assumptions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, acquiring a new language and understanding one's own language and culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Relate German spelling patterns to pronunciation. Interpret central information and relevant supporting details in speech and other forms of text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, German letter patterns and pronunciation; key information and supporting details in speech and other texts.

### CC-02

Use contextual clues, familiar language, and other learning strategies to interpret messages and produce German orally and in writing. Ask for or provide clarification, then check that the intended meaning is understood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to understand and produce oral and written language; seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Make sense of stories and construct spoken and written narratives. Organize their progression with time expressions and transitions, drawing on past, present, and future reference.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehending meaning in stories; narrating stories orally and in writing, with elaboration.

### CC-04

Exchange information and ideas through speaking and writing with others, including the wider community. Select presentation forms that account for one's own abilities and the audience's diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, exchanging ideas and information; presentation formats suited to diverse abilities, with elaborations.

### CC-05

Compare cultural practices and traditions in one's own life with those of German communities, considering their purposes. Participate in experiences with German-speaking people and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural similarities and differences; experiences with German communities and people.

### CC-06

Explore how cultural perspectives affect the interpretation of personal and shared experience. Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, experiences and worldviews through a cultural lens; First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn German spelling patterns that support recognition and pronunciation, including relationships among recurring letter combinations and sounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German letter patterns; Thinking and communicating, letter-pattern pronunciation elaboration.

### C-02

Understand how German word forms and endings relate to grammatical gender, case, and number. Learn the relationship between prepositions and the cases used with them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, German declination, gender/case/number, and prepositions and respective cases, with elaborations.

### C-03

Develop frequently used language for questions, timing and frequency, descriptions, comparisons, interests, feelings, preferences, and opinions. Distinguish formal and informal ways of addressing others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions, time and frequency, descriptions, comparisons, formal/informal language, preferences and emotions, beliefs and opinions.

### C-04

Learn how common verbs and their surrounding wording communicate when an event happens. Use past, present, and future time frames in meaningful contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past, present, and future time frames and common verbs in context.

### C-05

Learn how stories organize characters and events within a place and setting, including the relationship between a problem and its resolution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories and its problem-and-resolution elaboration.

### C-06

Learn about German communities internationally and the cultural life and artistic expression associated with them. Consider the contexts of practices and creative works, including questions of cultural appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of German communities, German communities around the world, and German works of art, with elaborations.

### C-07

Explore First Peoples connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Understand that cultural knowledge relates to particular communities, territories, and practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture through oral histories, identity, and place, with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete Grade 8 web source and its inline elaborations were reviewed. [S1] retains the detailed language examples, cultural contexts, and communication supports. The source explicitly includes declination and preposition/case relationships, but does not supply a compulsory full paradigm inventory. Past/present/future communication is contextual, not a requirement to master every German tense.

Texts may combine oral, written, visual, and digital communication. Storytelling includes imagined as well as real experience. Presentation-format choices must retain the source's attention to diverse abilities; they do not prescribe a particular technology or paid resource.

The source's German communities extend beyond Germany and include communities in Canada and elsewhere. Its place examples are illustrative, not an exhaustive study list or an assertion that a location has one uniform culture.

First Peoples perspectives remain part of this course, not an optional enrichment category. An example involving an Elder does not mandate an interview or grant permission to record or retell community knowledge. Use cultural works and histories only with appropriate context, attribution, and permissions.
