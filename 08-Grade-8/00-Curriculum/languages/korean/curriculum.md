# Korean - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Korean

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Korean compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English, as is the reviewed source. Korean is the language being learned; these metadata do not prescribe the language of classroom instruction. This is not a Korean-language resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Connect Korean speech and writing to meaning, participate in sustained exchanges and storytelling, and interpret language choices in relation to culture, community, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry test, prior-course requirement, or proficiency threshold. The reviewed Grade 8 standards do not state formal prerequisites.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Separately selected learning materials should identify their own accessibility provisions, attribution, and permissions.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for official wording and elaborations and the Grade 8 portion of [S2] for the downloadable presentation. The local IDs support curriculum mapping; they are not official BC codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and course scope:** This is a Grade 8 record, not a summary of the entire Grades 5-10 compilation [S2]. Korean is one alternative in the [Languages 8 catalog][S4], not a requirement for every learner or school. Read the general [Language Education Policy][S3] with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Listening and viewing deliberately helps learners build Korean language that they can use to express themselves and make sense of everyday surroundings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, purposeful listening/viewing and expressing oneself and the surrounding world.

### BI-02

As communication becomes more fluent, learners can contribute to a conversation and respond to others. Stories offer a connected way to share experiences and perspectives within that growing exchange.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fluency in reciprocal interactions and sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Language and creative expression carry cultural experience. Exploring Korean language and cultures also invites learners to reconsider what they know about their own language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as language/culture and learning another language/culture to understand one's own.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Thinking and communicating:** Make connections among Korean pronunciation, sounds, their phonetic representation, written characters, and meaning. Use these connections to understand key information, supporting details, and the meanings of stories in speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, pronunciation/sounds/phonetic representation/characters/meaning; comprehension of key information, supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

**Thinking and communicating:** Use context, familiar language, prior knowledge, and non-verbal information to understand and create spoken and written messages. Exchange information and ideas with others, checking or clarifying meaning when communication is uncertain.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies; exchanging ideas and information; seeking clarification and verifying meaning; associated elaborations.

### CC-03

**Thinking and communicating:** Develop oral and written narratives with a followable progression. Connect events using time expressions and transitions while communicating about past, present, and future situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating stories orally and in writing; Narrate elaboration on time expressions, transitions, and time frames.

### CC-04

**Thinking and communicating:** Choose how to present information with the communicator's and audience's differing abilities in mind. Visual, spoken, and digital supports are possible ways to make meaning accessible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sharing information through a presentation format suited to diverse abilities; presentation-format elaboration.

### CC-05

**Personal and social awareness:** Engage with Korean people and communities, and revisit similarities and differences between their cultural practices and one's own. Consider what practices mean to participants rather than treating a list of cultural features as understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, reviewing similarities/differences in cultural practices and traditions and engaging in experiences with Korean people and communities.

### CC-06

**Personal and social awareness:** Examine personal, shared, and other people's experiences and worldviews in cultural context. Recognize First Peoples knowledge alongside other ways of knowing and knowledge grounded in local communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, examining experiences/perspectives/worldviews through a cultural lens and recognizing First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Korean sentence meaning and social relationships are supported by particles and choices of formality. Learn how these functional words relate parts of a sentence and how polite and casual language choices connect to etiquette.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, particles and language formality/etiquette; elaborations on particles and formal polite, informal polite, and casual endings.

### C-02

An everyday vocabulary and sentence-pattern repertoire supports different questions, references to time and frequency, and communication about preferences, feelings, physical condition, beliefs, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions; time/frequency; preferences/emotions/physical states; beliefs/opinions.

### C-03

Language for events distinguishes past, present, and future contexts. The source illustrates these distinctions through changes in sentence endings rather than an English-language model of verb use.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past/present/future time frames; time-frame elaboration on sentence endings.

### C-04

Knowledge of the people, places, settings, and developing events within a story provides a basis for understanding and composing narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories; elaboration identifying place, characters, setting, and plot.

### C-05

Korean creative works and the practices of Korean communities worldwide connect language learning to cultural experience. Artistic expression and community life are related contexts, not a single fixed account of Korean identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Korean works of art and cultural aspects of Korean communities around the world; art and cultural-aspects elaborations.

### C-06

First Peoples perspectives show relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Traditions, community protocols, and connections to territory offer contexts for understanding these relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture; oral histories, identity, and place elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete web standards and elaborations [S1] and Grade 8 portion of [S2] were reviewed. This summary integrates related expectations and does not reproduce the full set of examples. Texts include oral, written, visual, digital, and combined communication; they are not restricted to print.

Connecting Korean characters, phonetic representation, pronunciation, and meaning remains an explicit competency even though Grade 8 Content does not repeat a separate alphabet list. Do not replace written Korean with a transcription-only curriculum, import another language's writing system, or add an unsupported character quota.

The formality elaboration distinguishes **formal polite, informal polite, and casual** endings. Formality and time reference both involve language choices, but they serve different purposes; one is not a substitute for the other. The example endings do not establish a complete grammar syllabus or a mastery test.

Stories, artistic works, and community experiences require age-appropriate selection and permission where applicable. First Peoples oral histories and cultural knowledge remain within this curriculum and require community context and protocols; listing them is not permission to reproduce a work or knowledge.

No particular vocabulary list, festival, trip, online service, or presentation format is mandated by this reference. Program approval is established separately from the English source language; it does not prescribe the classroom language or make this a French-language learning resource.
