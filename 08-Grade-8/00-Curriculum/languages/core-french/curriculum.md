# Core French - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Core French

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, as an approved second/additional-language choice. Core French develops French as an additional language; it is not French Immersion Language Arts or Francophone first-language curriculum. Its program mapping here is deliberately distinct from those pathways; see the [language-study provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed provincial source is also English. French is the language being learned. Neither metadata field prescribes English classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange information in French, communicate about experiences across time, and connect language learning with diverse Francophone cultural experiences.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry or proficiency requirement. Schools determine placement and continuity within their language offerings.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Learning texts, accessibility supports, and classroom materials must be selected and attributed separately.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the locators for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence.

This record addresses the specific Grade 8 standards, not all grades in the catalog's combined 5-10 download. Under [S2], boards select second-language offerings, with Core French the default where no alternative is offered for the English-language pathway. [S3] lists course alternatives, not a requirement to take them all. Read the general policy with the Required Areas of Learning Order's program-specific requirements and exceptions, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending closely to heard and viewed French supports understanding. As fluency develops, learners can contribute more fully to exchanges in which participants respond to one another.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening and viewing; fluency and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

French enables learners to communicate about themselves and their surroundings. Stories give experiences and viewpoints a connected form that can be shared with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expressing ourselves and the world around us; sharing experiences and perspective through stories.

### BI-03

Creative expression offers an encounter with Francophone cultures through work grounded in those communities' experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, authentic Francophone cultures through creative works, including the creative-works elaboration.

### BI-04

The languages learners use and the communities they engage with shape how they interpret culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, languages, communities, and understanding culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect French spelling patterns with pronunciation, and identify central information and supporting detail across texts. Sound-pattern work includes attention to silent letters and connections or omissions between neighbouring words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, French letter patterns and pronunciation, including silent letters, liaisons, and elisions; key information and supporting details in texts.

### CC-02

Choose comprehension and expression strategies appropriate to the learner and situation. Use context, language clues, prior knowledge, or reference supports, and ask questions or make requests when meaning is unclear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to support communication and seeking clarification, with elaborations.

### CC-03

Participate in spoken and written exchanges of information and ideas using complete sentences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, exchanging ideas and information in complete sentences orally and in writing.

### CC-04

Understand and retell the important information and events in stories. Create simple narratives whose sequence is clear through expressions of time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehending and retelling stories; narrating simple stories, with elaborations.

### CC-05

Investigate Francophone communities internationally and share what is learned. Explore connections between French and Indigenous communities, including the experiences expressed by Indigenous people who use French.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Francophone communities around the world; connections between Indigenous communities and the French language, with elaboration.

### CC-06

Describe cultural life in Francophone communities, explore its expression through creative works, and identify ways to engage with Francophone people and experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, creative works, cultural aspects of Francophone communities, and ways to engage in experiences.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring French spelling patterns that help explain shared or predictable sounds, including patterns encountered in rhyming words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, French letter patterns and its sound-grouping and rhyme elaboration.

### C-02

Build frequently used vocabulary and sentence patterns for questions, timing and frequency, descriptions, and comparisons. Include ways to explain preferences, feelings, and physical states, and to communicate beliefs or opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary and sentence structures: questions, time and frequency, descriptions, comparisons and contrasts, reasons, beliefs and opinions.

### C-03

Learn how wording changes when describing earlier events, current circumstances, or future events. Use common language structures to communicate within past, present, and future time frames.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, communication in past, present, and future time frames and the sentence-change elaboration.

### C-04

Understand how stories bring together a location and setting, characters, and a connected course of events.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about Francophone communities in different parts of the world and the cultural features of communities. Connect practices and celebrations with their historical, geographic, and social contexts rather than reducing culture to isolated symbols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of communities; Francophone communities around the world; cultural aspects of Francophone communities, with elaborations.

### C-06

Understand the ethical differences between engaging with cultural expression and taking it out of context or using it without appropriate permission. Recognize plagiarism as a distinct concern when using another person's work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, ethics of cultural appropriation and plagiarism, including the cultural-appropriation elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete Grade 8 web source and its inline elaborations were reviewed. [S1] retains the detailed language examples, communication strategies, cultural contexts, and examples of texts. The past/present/future expectation is not a requirement to master every French tense or a prescribed conjugation syllabus.

Texts include oral, written, visual, and digital forms, including authentic and adapted material. Stories may draw on real or imagined experience; the source does not prescribe a reading list or require every example of a creative work or community encounter.

The connection between Indigenous communities and French is not simply a list of French-speaking places. The source also discusses how Indigenous writers have used French to address colonization's effects on their families and communities. Preserve community-specific voices and the distinction between using French and maintaining Indigenous languages. Cultural works, oral histories, regalia, and knowledge require appropriate context and permissions; none are reproduced here.

The English-language record provides no French-language teaching materials and makes no claim of immersion proficiency or Francophone first-language coverage.

Core French appears in the approved program-guide lists, including the collective French-language list. That list also contains distinct immersion and first-language guides: it does not assign every guide to every program. The inspected sources do not establish Core French as the normal or required Grade 8 immersion or Francophone pathway, nor do they prohibit enrolment by learners from those programs. Core French cannot replace immersion English Language Arts or French language arts, and cannot satisfy Francophone students' requirement for a language other than French.
