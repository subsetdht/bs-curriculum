# Spanish - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Spanish

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Spanish compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed provincial source is English. Spanish is the language being learned. The source and summary languages do not prescribe classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange information in Spanish, organize narrative communication across time, and explore cultural experiences and perspectives within diverse communities.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry or proficiency requirement. Consult the school for placement and continuity in language study.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom texts, media, and accessibility supports require separate selection and attribution.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the locators for official wording and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence.

This record covers the Grade 8 standards, not all grades in the catalog's combined 5-10 document. Spanish is an alternative in [S3], not compulsory for every student. [S2] describes board-selected offerings and the general second-language policy. Read it with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful listening and viewing help learners understand a new language. As fluency grows, they can contribute more actively to sustained, back-and-forth communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening and viewing; increased fluency and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Spanish broadens the ways learners can express personal ideas and describe their surroundings. Narrative gives experiences and perspectives a connected form to share with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expressing ourselves and the world around us; experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative works connect language with cultural experience and the people whose lives and perspectives inform the work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as expressions of language and culture, with elaboration.

### BI-04

Encountering another language and culture provides a basis for reflecting on one's own language and cultural understandings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, acquiring a new language and understanding one's own language and culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use relationships between Spanish letter patterns and pronunciation to interpret language. Identify central information and meaningful supporting details in speech and other texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, Spanish letter patterns and pronunciation; key information and supporting details in speech and other texts.

### CC-02

Draw on contextual and non-verbal clues, familiar language, and other strategies to understand and produce spoken and written Spanish. Request or provide clarification and check the resulting understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to understand and produce oral and written language; seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-03

Understand stories and compose spoken and written narratives with a clear progression. Connect events using time expressions and transitions, referring to past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehending meaning in stories; narrating stories orally and in writing, with elaboration.

### CC-04

Exchange information and ideas orally and in writing with others, including people beyond the classroom. Choose presentation forms that fit one's own abilities and the audience's diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, exchanging ideas and information; presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-05

Investigate cultural life in Hispanic communities, compare practices and traditions with one's own, and consider their purposes. Engage in experiences with Hispanic people and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, investigating cultural aspects of Hispanic communities, comparing cultural practices, and community experiences.

### CC-06

Examine how culture shapes personal, shared, and others' experiences and worldviews. Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, the cultural lens; First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn Spanish spelling patterns that support predictable pronunciation, including recurring word endings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Spanish letter patterns; Thinking and communicating, pronunciation elaboration.

### C-02

Develop frequently used language for asking questions, describing people and objects, making comparisons, and discussing time and frequency. Include ways to communicate interests, preferences, emotions, physical states, beliefs, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions and its communication topics.

### C-03

Understand that describing events in different time frames requires changes in wording. Use common verbs in context to distinguish past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past, present, and future time frames and the wording-change and common-verb elaborations.

### C-04

Learn how stories connect characters and events to place and setting, including how a problem and its resolution contribute to the narrative.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories, with elaboration.

### C-05

Learn about the cultural life of Hispanic communities internationally and their artistic expression. Consider the context of works and the ethical issues of appropriation and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of Hispanic communities around the world and Hispanic works of art, with elaborations.

### C-06

Explore First Peoples connections among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Relate those understandings to specific communities and territories, not an assumed uniform culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture through oral histories, identity, and place, with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete Grade 8 web page and its inline elaborations were reviewed. [S1] retains the detailed language examples, text definitions, and cultural contexts. Past/present/future communication is contextual: it does not prescribe every Spanish tense, a complete conjugation inventory, or a fixed vocabulary count.

Texts include oral, written, visual, and digital communication and combinations of these forms. Stories can draw on real or imagined experience. Presentation formats should respond to diverse abilities without turning a suggested digital medium or other aid into a compulsory resource.

The source uses the term *Hispanic communities* and includes communities in different parts of the world. Its country examples are not an exhaustive list, nor do they imply that every person in a country speaks Spanish or shares one identity.

First Peoples perspectives are part of the language-culture relationship, not detached enrichment or a claim that Indigenous cultures are all Spanish-speaking. Work involving Elders, oral histories, cultural practices, and creative expression requires appropriate local context, attribution, and permissions. Examples of community encounters do not mandate travel, interviews, or reproduction of cultural works.
