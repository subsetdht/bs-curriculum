# Italian - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Italian

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Italian compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed provincial source is English. Italian is the language being learned, not the language of this summary.

**Learning objective:** Interpret and exchange information in Italian, develop connected narratives and simple opinions, and explore cultural perspectives in Italian communities and one's own experience.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry or proficiency requirement. Schools determine placement and the continuity of their language offerings.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom texts, materials, and accessibility supports require separate selection and attribution.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the locators for the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence.

The target and source scope are Grade 8, even though the catalog also links a combined 5-10 document. Italian is one course alternative in [S3], not a language required of every learner. [S2] describes board-selected offerings and the general second-language policy. Read it with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attentive listening and viewing help learners interpret a new language. Developing fluency supports more active participation in exchanges that depend on responding to one another.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, intentional listening and viewing; increased fluency and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

Italian provides further ways to talk about personal life and the surrounding world. Stories connect events with the experiences and perspectives a speaker or writer wants to share.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expressing ourselves and the world around us; sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative works connect expression with the cultural experiences of their makers and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as expressions of language and culture, with elaboration.

### BI-04

Learning about another language and culture invites learners to reconsider how their own language and culture shape understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, acquiring a new language and understanding one's own language and culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Connect Italian spelling patterns with pronunciation. Use changes in intonation and tone to distinguish kinds of utterance and communicate emotion.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, Italian letter patterns and pronunciation; intonation and tone, including question/statement patterns and emotion.

### CC-02

Identify important information and supporting details in texts. Use context, familiar language, non-verbal clues, and other strategies to improve understanding and produce spoken and written Italian.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, key information and supporting details in texts; strategies to understand and produce oral and written language.

### CC-03

Exchange information and ideas orally and in writing, including simple beliefs and opinions. Seek clarification and check meaning; present information in a form appropriate to one's own and others' diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, exchanging ideas and information, simple beliefs and opinions, clarification/verification, and presentation formats.

### CC-04

Understand the significant information in stories and create spoken and written narratives. Make their progression understandable through time expressions and transitions across past, present, and future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehending stories and narrating stories orally and in writing, with elaborations.

### CC-05

Compare cultural practices in one's own experience with those of Italian communities, attending to their purposes. Engage with Italian people and communities through cultural experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, cultural similarities and differences; experiences with Italian people and communities.

### CC-06

Analyze how cultural viewpoints shape personal, shared, and others' experiences and worldviews. Recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives, other ways of knowing, and knowledge grounded in local cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, analyzing experiences through a cultural lens; First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn recurring Italian letter patterns that support reading and pronunciation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Italian letter patterns; Thinking and communicating, letter-pattern pronunciation relationships.

### C-02

Develop frequently used vocabulary and sentence patterns for asking questions, describing people, objects, and locations, and discussing time and frequency. Include language for interests, preferences, feelings, physical states, beliefs, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary and sentence structures and its communication topics.

### C-03

Understand temporal reference in Italian across past, present, and future. The source elaboration identifies *passato prossimo* and *imperfetto* alongside *presente* and *futuro semplice*.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past, present, and future time frames and the named-tense elaboration.

### C-04

Learn how stories relate characters and a course of events to their place and setting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories.

### C-05

Learn about cultural life in Italian communities internationally and the contributions of Italian Canadians across time. Encounter Italian creative works with attention to cultural context, appropriation, and plagiarism.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural aspects of Italian communities around the world, past and present contributions of Italian Canadians, and Italian creative works, with elaborations.

### C-06

Explore First Peoples relationships among language, oral histories, identity, and place. Connect these understandings with particular community practices and territories rather than treating them as a single generic culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture through oral histories, identity, and place, with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete Grade 8 web page and its inline elaborations were reviewed. [S1] contains the fuller language and cultural examples. The elaborations include questions about quantities, direct-object pronoun examples, and time expressions. These support interpretation of the communication expectations, not a separate exhaustive grammar checklist.

The Italian source names two past tenses as well as present and future forms; a summary must not collapse its scope into present-tense conversation alone. Naming these forms does not add an expectation to master every Italian tense or prescribe conjugation tables and a teaching order.

Texts may be oral, written, visual, digital, or combinations of these. Accessible presentation choices do not require a specific technology. Storytelling and cultural engagement examples do not set a compulsory reading list, event, or travel requirement.

Italian cultural study is not limited to Italy: the source includes other communities and specifically Italian Canadian contributions. First Peoples knowledge remains part of the curriculum. Engagement with Elders, histories, and creative works needs community-specific context, attribution, and permissions; no cultural work is supplied or authorized for reuse here.
