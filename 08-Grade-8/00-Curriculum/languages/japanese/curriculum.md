# Japanese - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Japanese

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Japanese compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English, as is the reviewed source. Japanese is the language being learned; these metadata do not prescribe the language of classroom instruction. This is not a Japanese-language resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Use Japanese to understand and exchange increasingly connected messages, relate experiences through stories, and explore relationships among language, cultural expression, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry test, prior-course requirement, or proficiency threshold. The reviewed Grade 8 standards do not state formal prerequisites.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Separately chosen texts and learning materials need their own accessibility, attribution, and permission checks.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following each locator to the official expectations and elaborations. The Grade 8 portion of [S2] supplies a second official presentation. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and course scope:** These are Grade 8 standards, not a Grades 5-10 band; [S2] compiles several grades. Japanese is one alternative in the [Languages 8 catalog][S4], not a requirement for every learner or school. Read the general [Language Education Policy][S3] with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Attending carefully to what is heard and seen gives learners a growing basis for expressing themselves and describing their surroundings in Japanese.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, purposeful listening/viewing and expressing oneself and the surrounding world.

### BI-02

Greater fluency makes conversation a shared exchange rather than a one-way message. Storytelling extends that exchange by conveying experience and viewpoint.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fluency in reciprocal interactions and sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative expression offers insight into the people who use a language. Encountering Japanese language and cultures can also change how learners understand their own linguistic and cultural lives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as language/culture and learning another language/culture to understand one's own.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Thinking and communicating:** Connect Japanese writing with pronunciation and meaning, and use those relationships when interpreting speech and other texts. Identify central information and supporting details, including the meaning of narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, relationships among characters/sounds/meaning; comprehension of key information, supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

**Thinking and communicating:** Draw on context, familiar language, prior knowledge, and non-verbal cues when understanding and composing messages. Sustain spoken and written exchanges by noticing uncertainty and checking what participants mean.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies for oral/written language; exchanging ideas and information; seeking clarification and verifying meaning; associated elaborations.

### CC-03

**Thinking and communicating:** Organize spoken and written stories so an audience can follow their development. Use time expressions and connections between events to communicate across past, present, and future situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating stories orally and in writing; Narrate elaboration on time expressions, transitions, and time frames.

### CC-04

**Thinking and communicating:** Match the way information is shared to the abilities of both the communicator and the audience. Oral, visual, written, or digital supports can make a message more accessible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sharing information through a presentation format suited to diverse abilities; presentation-format elaboration.

### CC-05

**Personal and social awareness:** Explore Japanese communities through interaction and cultural experiences, describe aspects of their lives, and compare the meanings of practices and traditions with one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, describing Japanese cultural aspects; comparing practices and traditions; engaging with Japanese people and communities.

### CC-06

**Personal and social awareness:** Examine how culture informs personal and shared experiences and interpretations of the world. Recognize First Peoples knowledge, other ways of knowing, and knowledge rooted in local communities rather than treating one perspective as universal.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, examining experiences/perspectives/worldviews through a cultural lens and recognizing First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Hiragana and katakana are distinct components of Japanese writing that represent sounds. Learning their forms and sound relationships supports the connection between written and spoken messages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, hiragana and katakana; writing-system elaboration.

### C-02

An everyday repertoire of words and sentence patterns supports asking different kinds of questions, locating activities in time and describing frequency, and communicating preferences, feelings, bodily states, beliefs, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions; time/frequency; preferences/emotions/physical states; beliefs/opinions.

### C-03

Communication about events includes past, present, and future time references. Narrative knowledge helps arrange people, settings, events, difficulties, and their resolution into a story that others can follow.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past/present/future time frames and common elements of stories; related elaborations.

### C-04

Japanese creative works and the lives of Japanese communities worldwide provide cultural contexts for language learning. These contexts extend beyond Japan and include the experiences of communities elsewhere.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Japanese works of art and cultural aspects of Japanese communities around the world; art and world elaborations.

### C-05

Japan's Indigenous peoples are part of the course's cultural knowledge. The source identifies Ainu and Okinawan peoples; they must not be collapsed into a single, undifferentiated account of Japanese culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, indigenous peoples of Japan; elaboration identifying Ainu and Okinawan peoples.

### C-06

First Peoples perspectives connect language with oral histories, identity, and relationships to place. Cultural practices, community protocols, and connections to territory help illuminate those relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture; oral histories, identity, and place elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Both the full web source [S1] and the Grade 8 standards and elaborations in [S2] were reviewed. This record groups related expectations; the sources retain the precise wording and examples. Texts include spoken, written, visual, digital, and combined forms, not just printed passages.

The writing-system Content names **hiragana and katakana**. Do not replace them with Roman-letter transcription or infer a required kanji inventory from kanji appearing in an example. The source specifies no character quota here. Similarly, past/present/future communication does not imply three separate verb forms: the time-frame elaboration illustrates one form for both present and future contexts.

Japanese Indigenous peoples and First Peoples perspectives in BC are distinct parts of this curriculum, not interchangeable cultural labels. First Peoples knowledge and cultural works require appropriate community context, attribution, and permissions. Mention of an Elder or an art form in an elaboration does not authorize reproducing knowledge or prescribe a particular visit, work, or activity.

The record does not impose a vocabulary list, textbook, online account, trip, or uniform presentation medium. Program approval is established separately from the English source language; it does not prescribe the classroom language or make this a French-language learning resource.
