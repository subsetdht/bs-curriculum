# Punjabi - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Punjabi

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Punjabi compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English, as is the reviewed source. Punjabi is the language being learned; these metadata do not prescribe the language of classroom instruction. This is not a Punjabi-language resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Use Punjabi speech and writing to interpret information, carry out exchanges and tasks, and tell stories, while examining how language relates to cultural practices, perspectives, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry test, prior-course requirement, or proficiency threshold. The reviewed Grade 8 standards do not state formal prerequisites.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Separately selected learning materials should make their script, accessibility, attribution, and permissions clear.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and its elaborations, using [S2]'s Grade 8 section as the downloadable presentation. The local IDs identify repository summaries, not official BC codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and course scope:** These are Grade 8 standards; [S2] compiles several distinct grades from 5 through 10. Punjabi is one alternative in the [Languages 8 catalog][S4], not a requirement for every learner or school. Read the general [Language Education Policy][S3] with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Paying attention to what is heard and seen helps learners acquire Punjabi that they can use to express themselves and talk about their surroundings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, purposeful listening/viewing and expressing oneself and the surrounding world.

### BI-02

Increasing fluency enables active contributions and responses in conversation. Stories connect language into accounts that let others encounter a person's experiences and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fluency in reciprocal interactions and sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative works connect language with cultural experience. Learning through another language and culture can also deepen understanding of one's own ways of communicating and living.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as language/culture and learning another language/culture to understand one's own.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Thinking and communicating:** Interpret key information and supporting details in spoken and other texts. Understand stories and retell their important information in both spoken and written Punjabi.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, comprehension of key information/supporting details and comprehending/retelling stories; Comprehend and retell elaboration.

### CC-02

**Thinking and communicating:** Use context, familiar language, prior knowledge, and non-verbal cues to interpret and create messages. Attend to intonation and tone of voice so questions, statements, and emotions can be understood and expressed.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, language-learning strategies and effective use of intonation/tone of voice; associated elaborations.

### CC-03

**Thinking and communicating:** Use Punjabi to respond to questions, follow instructions through a task, and exchange ideas and information orally and in writing. Repair uncertainty by asking for or providing clarification and checking the intended meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, following instructions and responding to questions; exchanging ideas/information; seeking clarification and verifying meaning.

### CC-04

**Thinking and communicating:** Tell connected stories orally and in writing, using time expressions and transitions to make events understandable. Communicate across past, present, and future situations rather than presenting events without a clear sequence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating stories orally and in writing; Narrate elaboration on time expressions, transitions, and time frames.

### CC-05

**Thinking and communicating:** Choose a presentation form that works for the abilities of the learner and the audience. Spoken, visual, or digital supports can make the same information accessible in different ways.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sharing information through a presentation format suited to diverse abilities; presentation-format elaboration.

### CC-06

**Personal and social awareness:** Learn through experiences with Punjabi people and communities, and compare their practices and traditions with one's own. Consider the purposes and meanings of practices, not only their outward features.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, comparing cultural practices/traditions and engaging in experiences with Punjabi people and communities; similarities/differences elaboration.

### CC-07

**Personal and social awareness:** Examine how culture informs personal, shared, and other people's experiences and views of the world. Recognize First Peoples perspectives and knowledge together with other ways of knowing and local cultural knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, examining experiences/perspectives/worldviews through a cultural lens and recognizing First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Punjabi writing and pronunciation are related through patterns of letters and marks. Explore patterns that share sounds as well as combinations that change how a word is pronounced.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, relationships between Punjabi letter patterns and pronunciation; elaborations on rhyming words and combinations of letters/symbols.

### C-02

An everyday language repertoire supports questions, references to time and frequency, descriptions of people, objects and locations, and directions. It also provides ways to communicate preferences, feelings, bodily states, personal interests, beliefs, and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions; time/frequency; descriptions; preferences/emotions/physical states; interests/beliefs/opinions; directions.

### C-03

Language choices include formal and informal ways of addressing others. Changes in wording also communicate whether situations belong to the past, present, or future; social address and time reference are different uses of those choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, formal/informal modes of address and past/present/future time frames; associated elaborations.

### C-04

Understanding and making stories involves connecting characters, places and settings, and a developing sequence of events, including a problem and its resolution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories; associated elaboration.

### C-05

Punjabi works of art and the cultural practices of Punjabi communities worldwide provide contexts for language learning. Community experience and creative expression should be understood in their settings rather than as one uniform culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Punjabi works of art and cultural aspects of Punjabi communities around the world; related elaborations.

### C-06

First Peoples perspectives connect language with oral histories, identity, and place. Traditions, community protocols, and connections with territory help explain these relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture; oral histories, identity, and place elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full web standards and elaborations [S1] and Grade 8 section of [S2] were reviewed. This is an integrated summary, not an inventory of every example. Texts include oral, written, visual, digital, and combined communication.

The web elaborations show Punjabi examples in **Gurmukhi**. The standard is about relationships between letter patterns and pronunciation; do not infer a separate required Shahmukhi unit, a transcription-only substitute, or an alphabet or word-count target. Plain-text extraction of [S2] does not reliably preserve its Punjabi example lettering; use the readable web elaborations to inspect those examples.

Comprehending and retelling an existing story, narrating a story, following task instructions, and responding to questions are all present. A generic conversation-only summary would omit important Grade 8 skills. Voice and intonation communicate meaning and emotion; they do not replace the separate learning about written patterns.

First Peoples knowledge remains within the subject and requires appropriate community protocols. Punjabi and First Peoples cultural works need attribution and permissions, and comparison does not make distinct communities interchangeable. An elaboration mentioning an Elder, festival, or online exchange does not require that activity or authorize reproducing cultural material.

This reference prescribes no textbook, vocabulary list, trip, online service, or single presentation medium. Program approval is established separately from the English source language; it does not prescribe the classroom language or make this a French-language learning resource.
