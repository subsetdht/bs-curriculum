# Mandarin - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > Mandarin

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make Mandarin compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English, as is the reviewed source. Mandarin is the language being learned; these metadata do not prescribe the language of classroom instruction. This is not a Chinese-language resource or an authorized translation.

**Learning objective:** Connect Mandarin sounds, phonetic notation, and Chinese writing to meaningful communication; exchange and narrate experiences while exploring Chinese communities and relationships among language, culture, and identity.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry test, prior-course requirement, or proficiency threshold. The reviewed Grade 8 standards do not state formal prerequisites.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Learning materials selected separately should state their script, accessibility provisions, attribution, and permissions.

**How to use:** Follow the locators into [S1] for official standards and elaborations. The Grade 8 portion of [S2] presents the course as Mandarin Chinese. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes, a teaching sequence, or assessment levels.

**Grade and course scope:** Only Grade 8 is summarized; the Grades 5-10 download [S2] is a compilation, not a single grade band. Mandarin is one alternative in the [Languages 8 catalog][S4], not a requirement for every learner or school. Read the general [Language Education Policy][S3] with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Purposeful listening and viewing help learners gain the Mandarin they need to communicate about themselves and the world they encounter.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, purposeful listening/viewing and expressing oneself and the surrounding world.

### BI-02

Growing fluency supports exchanges in which participants respond to each other. Narratives provide a connected way to contribute experiences and perspectives, not just isolated information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fluency in reciprocal interactions and sharing experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative works reveal connections between language and cultural experience. Exploring another language and culture also gives learners a new basis for reflecting on their own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as language/culture and learning another language/culture to understand one's own.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

**Thinking and communicating:** Relate Mandarin sounds to phonetic notation, Chinese characters, and meaning. Interpret important information and the details that support it in different texts, including the meanings conveyed through stories.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, relationships among sounds/phonetic representation/characters/meaning; comprehension of key information, supporting details, and stories.

### CC-02

**Thinking and communicating:** Use context, gestures, voice, prior knowledge, and familiar language to understand and produce oral and written messages. Exchange ideas and information with others while resolving uncertainty through clarification and checks on meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies for understanding and producing language; exchanging ideas and information; seeking clarification and verifying meaning; related elaborations.

### CC-03

**Thinking and communicating:** Create spoken and written narratives whose progression is clear to an audience. Connect events through time expressions and transitions; the narration elaboration includes communicating across past, present, and future contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating stories orally and in writing; Narrate elaboration on time expressions, transitions, and past/present/future time frames.

### CC-04

**Thinking and communicating:** Select a way of sharing information that accommodates the abilities of both the presenter and the audience. Communication may be supported by visual, spoken, or digital means rather than a single required format.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, sharing information through a presentation format suited to diverse abilities; presentation-format elaboration.

### CC-05

**Personal and social awareness:** Engage with Mandarin-speaking people and Chinese communities, describe cultural aspects encountered, and compare the purposes of practices and traditions with those in one's own experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, describing Chinese cultural aspects; comparing practices/traditions; engaging with Mandarin-speaking people and Chinese communities.

### CC-06

**Personal and social awareness:** Consider how cultural contexts shape personal, shared, and other people's experiences and worldviews. Recognize First Peoples knowledge, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge as part of this inquiry.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, examining experiences/perspectives/worldviews through a cultural lens and recognizing First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Mandarin sound learning includes phonetic systems and changes in tone. The competency elaborations connect pronunciation with Pinyin letters or Zhuyin symbols and address tone as well as basic intonation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, phonetic systems and tonal variations; Thinking and communicating elaborations for sounds and phonetic representation.

### C-02

Knowledge of Chinese writing includes frequently used characters and their basic parts and radicals. These written forms are related to pronunciation and meaning but are not the same thing as phonetic notation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, commonly used Chinese characters and basic parts/radicals; Thinking and communicating, relationships among phonetic representation, characters, and meaning.

### C-03

Everyday words and sentence patterns support asking different questions, expressing time and frequency, explaining preferences, feelings, and physical states, and communicating beliefs and opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary, sentence structures, and expressions: questions; time/frequency; reasons for preferences/emotions/physical states; beliefs/opinions.

### C-04

The Content time-frame focus is on past and present situations. Its elaborations illustrate ways to indicate that an event is complete or that an activity is in progress.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past and present time frames; elaboration on completion and activity in action.

### C-05

Narrative knowledge brings together people, locations and settings, a sequence of events, and the relationship between a problem and its resolution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories; associated elaboration.

### C-06

Chinese works of art and the cultural lives of Chinese communities worldwide provide contexts for interpreting language and experience. This includes cultural expression across communities, not only within one country.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Chinese works of art and cultural aspects of Chinese communities around the world; works-of-art elaboration.

### C-07

First Peoples perspectives link language with oral histories, identity, and a sense of place. Community practices, protocols, and connections with territory inform these relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture; oral histories, identity, and place elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The full web source [S1] and Grade 8 standards and elaborations in [S2] were reviewed. This condensed record groups expectations without reproducing the complete example inventory. Texts can combine oral, written, visual, and digital communication.

**Keep the time-frame distinction visible:** Content explicitly names **past and present**, while the **Narrate competency elaboration includes future** as well. Neither should be silently rewritten to match the other. The completion and ongoing-action examples do not prescribe English-style tense rules.

The phonetic-representation elaboration describes **Zhuyin symbols or Pinyin letters**, and the character elaboration allows **traditional or simplified** forms. Do not turn these alternatives into a requirement to master both phonetic systems and both character sets. Phonetic notation does not replace learning Chinese characters, and no required character count is supplied here.

The Content phonetic-systems example places the term Hanyu Pinyin beside Zhuyin symbols. For the distinction between the systems, consult the separate, explicit phonetic-representation competency elaboration; this summary does not repeat that example as though the systems were identical.

Cultural examples are not permission to reproduce a work or community knowledge. First Peoples connections need appropriate local context and protocols; Chinese communities should not be represented as culturally uniform. No specific art work, online service, visit, or vocabulary list is prescribed by this reference. Program approval is established separately from the English source language; it does not prescribe the classroom language or make this a French-language learning resource.
