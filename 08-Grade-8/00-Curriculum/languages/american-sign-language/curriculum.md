# American Sign Language (ASL) - Grade 8

[Curriculum index](../../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 8](../../README.md) > [Languages](../README.md) > American Sign Language (ASL)

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, and Francophone, as an approved second/additional-language option when offered. These labels do not make ASL compulsory or replace the distinct French language-arts curricula or immersion English Language Arts. See the [program-specific language provisions](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed provincial source is English. The language being learned is ASL. English source wording does not turn signing into an English-speaking or English-writing requirement.

**Learning objective:** Understand and exchange increasingly connected signed messages, organize stories across time, and explore language, identity, and perspectives in Deaf and other communities.

**Prerequisites:** This reference adds no entry or proficiency requirement. Consult the school for placement and the local language pathway.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Classroom communication formats and accessibility supports require separate planning; this is not a lesson or materials list.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the locators for the official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs identify this repository's summaries, not official BC standard codes, assessment levels, or a teaching sequence.

The target and source standards are Grade 8; the catalog's combined 5-10 download does not make these grade-banded standards. ASL is one of the alternatives in [S3], not one of several languages all students must take. [S2] describes board-selected offerings and the general second-language policy. Read it with the program-specific provisions and exceptions in the Required Areas of Learning Order, including section 6 for qualifying local Indigenous-language programs, as explained in the [grade overview](../../README.md#language-study-and-course-choices).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Careful visual attention helps learners make sense of signing. Growing fluency makes sustained, back-and-forth participation more possible.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, viewing with intent and reciprocal interactions.

### BI-02

ASL gives learners ways to communicate about themselves and their surroundings. Narratives extend this expression by connecting events with personal experience and viewpoint.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, expressing ourselves and the world around us; experiences and perspectives through stories.

### BI-03

Creative expression carries cultural experience as well as language; understanding a work involves attending to the community from which it comes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creative works as expressions of language and culture, including the creative-works elaboration.

### BI-04

Encountering ASL and Deaf culture can change how learners understand their own language and cultural experience, alongside their understanding of Deaf language and culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, acquiring a new language and understanding one's own language and Deaf language and culture.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Interpret how changes in a sign's handshape, movement, or location change meaning. Identify the main information and relevant supporting details in communications.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, relationships among handshapes, movement, and location; key information and supporting details.

### CC-02

Build and interpret messages using context, prior knowledge, and visual language clues, including compound or iconic signs and facial or mouth features. Resolve uncertainty and check understanding through appropriate signed and non-manual responses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, strategies to understand and produce language; seeking clarification and verifying meaning, with elaborations.

### CC-03

Exchange information and ideas in complete ASL sentences. Present information in formats responsive to both the communicator's and the audience's diverse abilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, complete ASL sentences and presentation formats suited to diverse abilities.

### CC-04

Tell and retell stories with an understandable sequence, using temporal connections and reference to past, present, and future. The source allows narrative work in ASL or written language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Thinking and communicating, narrating and retelling stories and its time-frame and language elaborations.

### CC-05

Investigate Deaf communities internationally and share comparisons. Relate one's cultural practices to those of the local Deaf community, and explore opportunities to participate in Deaf cultural experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, Deaf communities around the world, local cultural comparisons, and Deaf cultural experiences.

### CC-06

Consider how culture shapes one's own and others' experiences and worldviews. Recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives, other ways of knowing, and knowledge held locally.

**Source locator:** [S1], Personal and social awareness, the cultural lens; First Peoples perspectives, other ways of knowing, and local cultural knowledge.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how non-manual features contribute meaning and grammar alongside the hands. Facial expression, eye gaze, mouth forms, head movement, and body positioning can distinguish questions, emotional intensity, spatial relationships, or duration. Coordinate eye gaze with pointing that establishes a reference (deixis).

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-manual signals and their elaboration, including WH-face, mouth morphemes, shoulder shift, and eye gaze with deixis.

### C-02

Develop frequently used signs and ASL structures for asking questions, describing people and objects, discussing interests, comparing, and expressing feelings, physical states, and basic beliefs and opinions. Learn organizational features such as topic/time placement, listing, ordering, and indicating choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common high-frequency vocabulary and ASL structures, including the structures elaboration and communication topics.

### C-03

Understand how temporal signs and their placement establish when events occur and how long they last. Distinguish past, present, and future reference within the ASL timeline.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, past, present, and future time frames and the ASL-timeline elaboration.

### C-04

Learn the organizing elements of stories and encounter creative expression from Deaf culture. These provide contexts for understanding how experiences can be represented and shared.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, common elements of stories and creative works from Deaf culture; Big Ideas, stories and creative works.

### C-05

Learn about Deaf communities worldwide, their cultural practices, and D/deaf perspectives. Examine changes in society's descriptions, assumptions about capability, and treatment of Deaf people over time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Deaf communities, cultural aspects, society's perceptions through time, and D/deaf perspectives and points of view.

### C-06

Explore First Peoples understandings of the connections among language, histories, identity, and place. Keep these perspectives grounded in particular communities rather than treating them as interchangeable with Deaf cultural experience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, First Peoples perspectives connecting language and culture through histories, identity, and place, with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The complete Grade 8 web page, including its inline elaborations, was reviewed. [S1] retains the detailed non-manual examples, sign-description guidance, narrative supports, and cultural contexts; this record is not an inventory of every example.

The source explicitly distinguishes complete ASL sentences from written language: it does not posit a written form of an ASL sentence. Its option to narrate in writing must not become a requirement to write ASL. Similarly, ASL viewing and visual grammar must not be replaced by spoken-language pronunciation exercises.

The source's `D/deaf` distinction recognizes that cultural identification is not identical for everyone who is deaf. Its discussion of collective community orientation is not a description of every individual's identity. Person-description examples do not justify inferring someone's identity from appearance.

First Peoples elaborations include community histories and residential-school experience. Work involving Elders, histories, cultural knowledge, or Deaf creative works needs appropriate context, consent, and permissions. No community narrative, cultural work, or required encounter is supplied here; examples of visits and events are not compulsory activities.
