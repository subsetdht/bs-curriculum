# Social Studies - Grade 2

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 2](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations under the Required Areas of Learning Order [S4], sections 1-2, not an inference from source language. [S2] and [S3] supply immersion-policy and CSF implementation context.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), separately from classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Explore regional and global communities, their diversity and relationships with environments, and the responsibilities of people and governments, using evidence and different perspectives.

**Prerequisites:** Experiences of familiar communities can support comparison with other places; no particular travel, family history, or prior course is required by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Future materials need their own attribution, accessibility review, and permissions.

**How to use:** Follow the locators to [S1] for official expectations and elaborations. Local IDs identify grouped summaries rather than ministry standards or a teaching order. The source subtitle names the Grade 2 focus, not an elective course choice.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Decisions made close to home can affect distant places, while events and actions elsewhere can affect local life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, reciprocal local and global consequences.

### BI-02

Understanding Canada requires attention to the variety of its regions and communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, Canada's diverse regions and communities.

### BI-03

Global citizenship involves both rights and responsibilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, individuals as global citizens.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate questions by gathering, interpreting, and analyzing information, then communicate findings or decisions. Examine the content and features of different sources to make inferences and draw conclusions. The inquiry elaboration includes simple mapping and ways to organize information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, inquiry processes and skills, evidence, and the inquiry elaboration.

### CC-02

Explain why people, places, and events matter differently to individuals or groups. Sequence evidence of the past, distinguish change from continuity, and recognize causes and consequences of events, decisions, or developments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance, continuity and change, and cause and consequence.

### CC-03

Explain how beliefs, values, worldviews, experiences, and roles can lead people to see a place, issue, event, or person differently.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-04

Make reasoned value judgments about actions, decisions, or events, and consider what can be learned from them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Compare characteristics of communities and cultures in Canada and elsewhere in the world. This study must include **at least one Canadian First Peoples community and culture**, rather than treating First Peoples perspectives as an optional addition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, diverse communities and cultures, including the stated Canadian First Peoples minimum.

### C-02

Explore how communities meet people's needs and wants, recognizing that available goods, services, and ways of obtaining them can differ.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, meeting needs and wants in communities and its elaboration.

### C-03

Study how people and environments relate in different communities. Compare environmental features in other parts of Canada and the world, rather than limiting the inquiry to the immediate neighbourhood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, people/environment relationships and diverse environmental features.

### C-04

Learn about individual rights and responsibilities at regional and global scales, alongside the roles and responsibilities of regional governments.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, regional/global rights and responsibilities and regional governments.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 2 extends the community focus to regional and global relationships. The source does not require a single prescribed set of countries or a uniform account of community life.

The inquiry elaboration describes cardinal directions, map symbols and legends, simple maps, information organizers, and consideration of different responses to problems. These support the inquiry strand; sample research visits, interviews, timelines, and presentation formats are not compulsory projects.

Use specific, appropriately sourced accounts of First Peoples communities. Their histories and contemporary lives are not interchangeable, and studying a community does not authorize copying its stories or cultural works. Do not require learners to reveal private family or migration experiences to participate.

This is a regular program-area reference, not an individual learner checklist. See the [program-order exceptions](../README.md#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) and [S4], section 3.
