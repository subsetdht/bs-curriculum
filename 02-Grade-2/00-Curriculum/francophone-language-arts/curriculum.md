# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 2

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 2](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the first-language program. The Required Areas of Learning Order [S4], sections 1-2, includes Francophone students and specifies French Language Arts for this pathway. This curriculum is distinct from French Immersion Language Arts, English Language Arts, and Core French.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary source is French. This is an independent summary, not an authorized translation, an English instructional requirement, or a French-language teaching resource. Source locators are labelled English descriptions of French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Develop understanding and expression in French through reading, listening, viewing, speaking, writing, and representing, with attention to meaning, coherent communication, and Francophone and Indigenous cultural connections.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experiences communicating and reading in French can help identify supports. This reference does not set eligibility or language-proficiency conditions for the Francophone program.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Later texts and resources need their own French-language suitability, accessibility, and rights review.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the official expectations and elaborations and [S2] for subject context. Pair this record with the shared subjects in the [Francophone pathway](../README.md#francophone). Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Communication develops in response to an audience's words and non-verbal signals. Clear vocabulary and an organized message help the intended meaning reach that audience.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), recipient language and the clarity/organization of a message.

### BI-02

Reading brings decoding together with understanding a text's meaning and structure. Clues in words, sentences, and their context support inference.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), reading beyond decoding and contextual clues.

### BI-03

Stories draw on imagination as well as an author's experiences, dreams, and view of reality.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), origins of stories.

### BI-04

Indigenous narratives contribute to exploring identity at personal, family, generational, and community levels.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), Indigenous narratives and identity.

### BI-05

Communicating in French can build a sense of belonging within a Francophone community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), French communication and Francophone belonging.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Infer unfamiliar word meanings from their roots, visualize information while reading, and identify important information in texts, including Indigenous narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: word roots, visualization, and important information.

### CC-02

Organize ideas by theme using graphic organizers. Activate prior knowledge and connect texts with personal and cultural experiences, including one's Francophone culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: planning/organization, prior knowledge, and cultural connections.

### CC-03

Use studied strategies to communicate accurately and fluently, ask clarifying questions, and share opinions and emotional responses to texts. Adapt voice and gestures to the audience and situation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: expression, clarification, reactions, and audience adaptation.

### CC-04

Read **previously encountered short texts** fluently with attention to the punctuation being studied. Write in short paragraphs, following the studied text structures and language conventions, and use linking expressions to maintain coherence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: previously seen texts, short paragraphs, and relationship markers.

### CC-05

Work toward a common goal with peers while taking their views and ideas into account.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: collaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Connect complex French sounds with their written forms and learn how the plural marker "s" applies to nouns and adjectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), spelling patterns: complex sound-letter correspondences and noun/adjective plurals.

### C-02

Learn how narratives and information texts are organized, including the roles of characters and settings in a story. Use time/place relationships and punctuation to help readers follow an account.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization and literary tools.

### C-03

Develop knowledge of sentence types and groups, grammatical gender and number, related word families, and verb moods and tenses in the texts being studied.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools.

### C-04

Learn strategies for communication and social interaction, reading, and writing. The elaborations connect attentive and respectful communication with meaning-making in reading and the development of writing from a draft through revision and publication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), studied communication/socialization, reading, and writing strategies, with elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The reading-fluency competency explicitly concerns short texts seen before; do not turn it into an unrestricted unfamiliar-text benchmark. Writing uses **short paragraphs**, not only the short simple texts summarized for Grade 2 immersion.

The language elaboration identifies subject, verb, and complement; affirmative, declarative, and interrogative sentences; all persons of the present indicative; and introductory recent past, compound past, and near future. These belong with the studied texts, not a requirement to master every form in isolation. [S2] explains the importance of teaching grammar in context.

The narrative elaboration follows a story from its initial situation through a triggering event, developments, resolution, and final situation. Punctuation guidance includes question and exclamation marks and the hyphen. These clarify the grouped content above; the record is not a complete elaboration inventory.

The source treats texts as oral, written, visual, print, digital, and combined communications. Indigenous narratives and Francophone cultural connections are part of the curriculum, not detached enrichment. Engagement with them requires suitable sources, attribution, and permissions; no cultural or literary works are reproduced.

The CSF's elementary language guidance [S3] introduces English Language Arts in Grade 4. It supports the separate Grade 2 pathway here, but is identified as provider delivery guidance rather than a new provincial learning standard.

This is a regular program-area reference, not an individual learner checklist. See the [program-order exceptions](../README.md#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) and [S4], section 3.
