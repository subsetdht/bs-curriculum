# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 2

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 2](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. The Required Areas of Learning Order [S3], section 2(a), specifies French Language Arts for immersion students. The provincial policy [S2] distinguishes immersion from the first-language Francophone program and places Late French Immersion entry at Grade 6, not Grade 2.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary source is French. This is an original summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English descriptions of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Develop fluent, purposeful communication in French, using strategies to understand texts, organize ideas, tell stories, and connect language with culture.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience using French in the immersion setting may help identify appropriate supports. This reference sets no program-entry or language-proficiency criteria.

**Materials:** None are needed for reading. Future resources must separately establish their French-language suitability, accessibility, and rights.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for official wording and elaborations, and use the seven shared subjects in the [Early French Immersion pathway](../README.md#early-french-immersion). Local IDs identify repository summaries, not ministry standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Growing fluency makes interaction easier, while logically connected ideas help a listener or reader follow a message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), fluency in interaction and organizing/linking ideas.

### BI-02

Encountering other cultures can help learners understand their own cultural experience and developing identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), cultural awareness and identity.

### BI-03

Useful strategies for understanding and expression depend on what a person is trying to do and the context in which communication happens.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), task/context and strategy choice.

### BI-04

Recognizing shared features of fairy and folk tales helps learners understand the genre.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), defining features of the conte.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Notice cultural elements in Francophone and Indigenous texts that also occur in one's own culture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: cultural connections in texts.

### CC-02

Use meaningful word groups, key words, themes, and visualization to understand a text. Organize information by theme to make its relationships clearer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: sentence grouping, themes/key words, visualization, and thematic organization.

### CC-03

Relate a story's characters, setting, and events to the genres being studied, and organize events chronologically when telling a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: story elements, genres, and chronological retelling.

### CC-04

Express ideas, feelings, and experiences with visual and gestural support. Interact spontaneously with peers in complete sentences and ask questions to clarify or extend understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: personal expression, spontaneous interaction, and questions.

### CC-05

Read simple texts fluently and compose short texts whose sentences are structured appropriately and whose ideas follow logically.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: fluent reading of simple texts and short-text writing.

## Content

### C-01

Learn ways to participate attentively and expressively in communication, including active listening and reading, conversational turn-taking, and non-verbal language. Engage with cultural elements alongside these communication practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies and cultural elements.

### C-02

Explore story characters, places, and events, and the different organization of narratives and information texts. Use words that locate ideas in time and place to help connect a message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), story elements and textual organization.

### C-03

Develop knowledge of French written conventions, including punctuation and spelling patterns. The elaborations include the question mark, plural nouns ending in "s", and complex sound patterns.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), written conventions and their elaborations.

### C-04

Learn how nouns, articles, and adjectives work together, including grammatical gender. Build simple sentences and connect the study of verb forms with the genres being read and created.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: noun gender, articles, adjectives, simple sentence structure, and verb moods/tenses.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source's introductory elaboration places this learning in the context of **picture books and contes**, rather than prescribing particular titles. A conte is described in the official English view as a fairy or folk tale. This context is not a separate course choice.

The sentence elaboration includes affirmative, negative, and interrogative forms with subject, verb, and complement. The verb elaboration identifies singular present-indicative forms of action verbs and auxiliaries in the present indicative. These source-specific limits should not be replaced by a broad requirement to master French conjugation tables.

This is additional-language learning in immersion, not English Language Arts translated into French. The [Francophone Language Arts record](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) is distinct, including its expectations for previously encountered reading texts and paragraph writing. Neither record is Core French.

The official elaborations remain at [S1]. Cultural connections do not authorize copying, retelling, or adapting protected Indigenous or Francophone works. No story, prescribed vocabulary list, activity, or assessment is supplied here.

This is a regular program-area reference, not an individual learner checklist. See the [program-order exceptions](../README.md#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) and [S3], section 3.
