# Science - Grade 2

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 2](../README.md) > Science

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations under the Required Areas of Learning Order [S4], sections 1-2. [S2] and [S3] provide immersion-policy and CSF implementation context, respectively.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), separately from the language used for classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Investigate cycles and changes in living things, materials, motion, and water through questions, safe observations, evidence, and connections to the local environment.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with observing and discussing the surroundings can provide an entry point; no formal prerequisite is asserted.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Any later investigation requires its own materials, accessibility arrangements, supervision, and safety planning.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for the complete expectations and elaborations. The local IDs identify this repository's summaries, not official BC standard codes or a prescribed set of experiments.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

An organism's life cycle is connected to the conditions in which it lives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, life cycles adapted to environments.

### BI-02

Materials undergo different kinds of change, including physical and chemical processes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, physical and chemical changes to materials.

### BI-03

Investigating forces helps explain how an object's motion changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, forces and motion.

### BI-04

Water supports life while moving repeatedly through connected parts of the environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, water's importance and cycling through the environment.

## Content

### C-01

Compare life cycles involving metamorphosis with those without it, and examine how offspring resemble and differ from their parents. Include First Peoples applications of life-cycle knowledge, with stewardship and sustainable harvesting among the source's elaborated contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, metamorphic/non-metamorphic life cycles, offspring and parents, and First Peoples use of life-cycle knowledge.

### C-02

Explore physical and chemical ways of changing materials, distinguishing changes in form from changes that produce different substances.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, physical ways and chemical ways of changing materials, with associated elaborations.

### C-03

Investigate types of forces and their effects on motion. The source's elaboration includes contact and at-a-distance forces, balanced and unbalanced forces, and differences in how objects move through or over materials.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, types of forces and its elaboration.

### C-04

Identify sources of water, including local watersheds, and understand why water conservation matters. Fresh-water availability is a concern distinct from the existence of large amounts of water on Earth.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, water sources including local watersheds, water conservation, and their elaborations.

### C-05

Trace water through evaporation, condensation, precipitation, and runoff in a cycle driven by the sun. Include local First Peoples knowledge of water cycles, conservation, and connections with other systems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, the water cycle and local First Peoples knowledge of water; water-cycle elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The four areas of study remain connected to the inquiry competencies; this is not a list of required experiments. Chemical change in the curriculum does not authorize unsupervised heating, burning, or mixing of substances. No experiment or equipment specification is supplied here.

The source includes both biological cycles and the water cycle, not only a general study of habitats or weather. Its elaborations clarify terms and offer examples; consult [S1] rather than treating a particular organism or demonstration as required everywhere.

First Peoples knowledge belongs within the life-cycle, water, and inquiry expectations. Local sources and relationships matter, and permission may be needed for stories or community-specific knowledge. The curriculum is not permission to reproduce a narrative, song, or artwork.

This is a regular program-area reference, not an individual learner checklist. See the [program-order exceptions](../README.md#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) and [S4], section 3.
