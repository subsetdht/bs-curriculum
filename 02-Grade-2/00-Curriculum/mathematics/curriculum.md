# Mathematics - Grade 2

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 2](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations under the Required Areas of Learning Order [S4], sections 1-2. The immersion policy [S2] and CSF guidance [S3] provide separately identified program context.

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`), as distinct from classroom language. This reference is not a French-language learning resource.

**Learning objective:** Use place value, flexible calculation, patterns, spatial reasoning, and representations of data to investigate quantities and relationships in everyday contexts.

**Prerequisites:** Experience counting and representing smaller quantities can help identify entry points; this reference sets no formal admission or proficiency requirement.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. A future learning resource must identify its own materials and accessible representations.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], following the locators for official expectations and elaborations. Local item IDs support curriculum mapping; they are not ministry codes, lesson numbers, or an assessment sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Understanding tens and ones makes quantities through 100 manageable and supports increasingly flexible addition and subtraction.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, numbers to 100 and place value in computational fluency.

### BI-02

An increasing pattern has a regular change that can be described and used to reason about what follows.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, regular change and generalizations in increasing patterns.

### BI-03

Describing and measuring the features of shapes and objects provides ways to compare them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, attributes of objects and shapes.

### BI-04

A picture graph can preserve information from a collection of objects and make that information easier to compare and interpret.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, pictorial representation of concrete items in graphs.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Reason about relationships, make sensible estimates, and develop mental strategies for quantities. Explore mathematical ideas with appropriate technology and models grounded in meaningful situations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and analyzing: reasoning, estimation, mental mathematics, technology, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Build and apply understanding through play, inquiry, visualization, and problem solving with different strategies. Connect problems to place, stories, cultural practices, and perspectives relevant to local First Peoples, the wider local community, and other cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving.

### CC-03

Explain and justify mathematical choices, contribute to discussions using mathematical language, and show thinking through objects, pictures, and symbols.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing.

### CC-04

Reflect on one's thinking and connect mathematics with other mathematical ideas, learning areas, and interests. Include First Peoples worldviews and perspectives when making these connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting.

## Content

### C-01

Develop number sense through 100, including the relationship between tens and ones. Use 25, 50, and 100, together with familiar personal referents, to judge and compare quantities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 100; benchmarks of 25, 50, and 100 and personal referents.

### C-02

Begin developing computational strategies for addition and subtraction facts through 20, while exploring addition and subtraction of quantities through 100. Show changes in quantity with pictures and symbols rather than relying on answers alone.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, facts to 20 (introduction of computational strategies), operations to 100, and representations of change in quantity.

### C-03

Work with both repeating and increasing patterns. Use symbols to express whether quantities are equal or unequal.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, repeating/increasing patterns and symbolic equality/inequality.

### C-04

Measure length directly and begin using standard metric units. The elaboration identifies centimetres and metres, estimation, and recording length, height, and width.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, direct linear measurement and its standard-unit elaboration.

### C-05

Compare and describe two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects using more than one attribute, including relationships between flat shapes and the objects in which they appear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multiple attributes of 2D shapes and 3D objects; associated sorting and shape-identification elaboration.

### C-06

Turn concrete graphs into picture graphs in which each picture represents one item. Use comparative language to discuss the likelihood of familiar events.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, one-to-one pictorial graphs and likelihood of familiar life events.

### C-07

Explore coin combinations totalling up to 100 cents and the choices involved in spending and saving, including connections to needs and wants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy and its elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Keep the numerical limits distinct: number concepts and operations extend to **100**, the fact-strategy introduction concerns **20**, and the money content concerns **100 cents**. The source does not make timed recall or one written calculation algorithm the definition of fluency.

The number elaboration includes counting forward and backward, skip-counting by 2, 5, and 10, comparing and ordering numbers, and even/odd relationships. Read it with the broader number-sense summary, not as a separate sequence of worksheets. Other elaborations supply examples and representations; they do not mandate particular apps, materials, or projects.

The data representation here is **one-to-one**, not a scaled picture graph. First Peoples mathematical connections need appropriate local context and permission; this record does not reproduce cultural designs or stories.

This is a regular program-area reference, not an individual learner checklist. See the [program-order exceptions](../README.md#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) and [S4], section 3.
