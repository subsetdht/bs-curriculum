## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

The [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order][P4], sections 1-2, includes Francophone authorities, programs, and students and specifies the same seven non-language areas through their applicable provincial program guides. It distinguishes English Language Arts from French Language Arts for immersion and Francophone students. This is the primary basis for shared-subject coverage; [individual exceptions](#provincial-requirements-and-delivery) still apply.

### English-language program

Start with [English Language Arts 2](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects in the [subject table](#subjects-and-learning-standards). This pathway contains **eight records**.

### Early French Immersion

Start with [French Immersion Language Arts 2](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the same seven shared subjects. This pathway contains **eight records**.

The [provincial French Immersion policy][P1] requires the program to parallel the regular curriculum in structure and content and says Kindergarten through Grade 2 should be taught entirely in French. The English-language summary documents do not change that instructional context. English Language Arts is not substituted for immersion language arts in this Grade 2 pathway.

### Francophone

Start with [Francophone Language Arts 2](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects. This pathway contains **eight records**, using a distinct first-language curriculum rather than the immersion curriculum.

The [provincial program comparison][P2] identifies the Conseil scolaire francophone de la Colombie-Britannique (CSF, School District 93) as the Francophone program provider and describes French instruction except for English Language Arts. The [CSF elementary page][P3], under Curriculum, also states that its program follows the BC ministry curriculum. This is supporting implementation context; shared-content applicability comes from the ministerial order [P4], not from language-of-instruction charts or translated documents.

The CSF's Langues section introduces English Language Arts in Grade 4. That is **named provider implementation guidance**, not a new provincial subject standard, and it explains why this Grade 2 pathway does not include the English Language Arts record.

Late French Immersion begins at Grade 6 under [P1], so there is no Grade 2 Late Immersion pathway. Core French and immersion language arts are not interchangeable.

## How to use these references

Choose a program, read a record's Scope and use section, then follow source locators for the official wording and elaborations. Big Ideas describe understandings; Curricular Competencies describe skills and processes; Content identifies knowledge.

Each item has a stable local ID. For example, [Mathematics 2: C-01](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-01) has the canonical repository reference `bc-g02-mathematics#c-01`. A bare item ID is ambiguous outside its record. These are not official BC standard codes, priorities, or a teaching sequence; use the [repository linking convention](../../curriculum-format.md#linking-to-records-and-items).

No special materials or formal prerequisites are needed to read these references. Suggestions about prior experience in the records are planning context, not additional admission or proficiency requirements.

## Grade bands and learning across subjects

ADST and Career Education use **K-3 standards**, with Grade 2 as this directory's target grade. Their records preserve the full source grade band instead of inventing Grade 2-only versions.

ADST combines those competencies with Grade 2 content from another learning area; its Content section explains that integration rule. Career Education instead has its own Personal Development and Connections to Community content. These are different arrangements despite the shared K-3 band.

The [BC curriculum model](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/overview) connects knowing, doing, and understanding. [Core Competencies](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies) develop across subjects and do not replace subject-specific Curricular Competencies.

First Peoples perspectives remain within the subjects in which they occur, including science, mathematics, social studies, arts, career learning, and the language arts. Respect community-specific knowledge and permissions rather than treating a curriculum reference as permission to copy a cultural work.

## Provincial requirements and delivery

The subject expectations come from BC curriculum sources and the applicable guides required by [P4]. The provincial immersion policy and program comparison establish program context; CSF guidance is explicitly labelled as provider implementation evidence. No SD43-specific timetable, delivery model, or resource choice is imposed on provincial records.

These are **regular program-area pathways, not rigid individual checklists**. Section 3 of [P4] provides exceptions for a student who must have an IEP because they cannot demonstrate learning against program-guide outcomes, or who receives English language learning services and cannot demonstrate those outcomes because of English proficiency. An IEP or language-support designation alone should not be treated as a blanket exemption; read the order's conditions and the learner's individual arrangements.

Section 4 addresses additional language provisions in later grades: a language other than the program's English or a Francophone student's French in Grades 5-8, and English Language Arts for immersion students in Grades 4-9. It does not add a second-language subject to this Grade 2 pathway. The CSF's Grade 4 English Language Arts introduction remains separately identified provider guidance.

School schedules, local program availability, supports, and resource choices must be confirmed with the relevant provider. This collection does not establish those arrangements.

## Coverage and limits

All ten Grade 2 entries expose core expectations, not course lists requiring a further course inventory. There are no pending summaries or primary-source access blockers in this pass.

All ten records are in **English (`en-CA`)**. Eight use English primary curriculum sources; the two French-language arts records use French primary sources and were also compared with their official English views. **No French-language editions of the repository summaries have been written.** English documentation does not become a French classroom resource through shared curriculum alignment.

Coverage is `core-summary`: important strands and qualifications are retained, but the records do not reproduce every official statement or elaboration. Source revision dates were not established. **2026-09-14 is the alignment review date, not a curriculum edition.**

The [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) categories remain empty. No activities, assessments, learning resources, or tools are added by these references.

Maintain the existing landing URLs, record IDs, and item anchors when revising, and update local navigation and central written-summary coverage together under the [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page).
