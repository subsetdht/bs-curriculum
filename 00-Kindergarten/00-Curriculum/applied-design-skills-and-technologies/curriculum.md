# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone. These shared provincial expectations use the applicability established in the [Kindergarten program scope and sources](../README.md#program-scope-and-sources).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary official source is in English (`en`). Neither identifies the classroom language. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for the distinct language pathways.

**Learning objective:** Develop early design thinking by noticing possibilities, trying ways to make something useful, using tools safely, and sharing how a design developed.

**Prerequisites:** Curiosity and play provide starting points, not formal entry requirements. No previous coding, device use, construction experience, or particular level of manual dexterity is assumed.

**Materials:** Nothing beyond the reference and linked sources is needed to read it. Any later making activity must identify its own accessible materials, suitable tools, supervision, and safety arrangements.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1] and follow each source locator. This is a Kindergarten application of **K-3 ADST competencies**, combined with **Kindergarten content from other learning areas**. It is not a separate Kindergarten technology syllabus or an additional set of K-only standards. Local IDs support curriculum mapping; they are repository references, not official BC standard codes or a teaching sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Wondering about the world can open up design possibilities, and playful exploration helps learners acquire skills for acting on their ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, natural curiosity in design and developing skills through play.

### BI-02

People use technologies as tools to do things that would otherwise be difficult or beyond their capabilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies extending human capabilities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore situations to find needs or possibilities for a design. Bring ideas from personal interests and experiences, build on other people's suggestions, and choose a possibility to develop.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Ideating.

### CC-02

Choose materials and tools and begin making through familiar methods or by learning from someone else's demonstration. Try out changes, work through problems, and use emerging ideas to improve what is being made.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Making, tools/materials, modelling, and trial and error.

### CC-03

Select a way to share a design and an audience for it. Show how it works, explain the making process and its possible contribution to self, family, community, or the environment, judge its success using personal preferences, and reflect on individual and group work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design: Sharing, audience, product demonstration, contribution, evaluation, and reflection.

### CC-04

Handle materials, tools, and technologies safely in both physical and digital settings. Extend existing skills and develop new ones through play and working with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills, safety and skill development.

### CC-05

Investigate how simple tools and technologies that are available to the learner can expand what they can do.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies, exploration of simple, available tools and technologies.

## Content

### C-01

The source does not prescribe a separate ADST content list for Kindergarten. Another learning area's Kindergarten content supplies the subject knowledge for a cross-curricular design or making context; the K-3 ADST competencies supply the design processes and practical skills. Keep both curriculum relationships explicit rather than replacing the partner subject's content with a generic technology topic.

**Source locator:** [S1], introductory content instruction, combining ADST K-3 Curricular Competencies with grade-level content from other areas of learning.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The K-3 band is stated directly in [S1]. This record selects Kindergarten as its target, not as a new subdivision of the band's standards. Do not import Grade 1 content from a partner subject merely because ADST shares competencies across those grades.

The source's Making elaboration uses a broad meaning of a product: it can be a process, service, system, or environment, not only a physical object. Its technology elaboration likewise includes ordinary tools, not only computers. These explanations do not require particular products, software, screens, coding, or commercial sharing.

Official elaborations remain at [S1], especially Applied Design's Making and Sharing and Applied Technologies. Examples clarify possibilities rather than becoming a materials list or a prescribed project. Developmentally appropriate support, accessible ways to manipulate or communicate, and physical and digital safety must be planned for any activity; this reference is not an activity or assessment.
