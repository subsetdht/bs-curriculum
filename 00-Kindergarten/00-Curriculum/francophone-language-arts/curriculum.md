# Francophone Language Arts - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, using the province's first-language French curriculum. This is distinct from Early French Immersion, Late French Immersion, Core French and English Language Arts.

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed authoritative source is in French (fr). Source locators below are labelled English glosses of the French sections and topics. This is an original English reference summary, not an authorized translation or French-language teaching material.

**Learning objective:** Develop meaning-making and expression in French through oral language, images, stories and emerging writing, while connecting personal and cultural experience with class and Francophone community belonging.

**Prerequisites:** Prior experience with French, conversation, pictures and stories can support learning. This reference does not establish program eligibility, admission rules or a requirement for independent reading and writing on entry.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference beyond access to [S1]. Classroom selections need separate checks for French-language suitability, accessibility, cultural context and rights.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the official Kindergarten wording and elaborations. ## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Pictures and written signs can carry a message. Trying to communicate, even when it involves taking a risk, helps a person's language grow.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), meaning in images and written forms and risk-taking in communication.

### BI-02

Stories can connect readers' lived experiences with questions about who they are. Within this learning, First Peoples narratives have an important role in considering identity through family, generations and community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), texts inspired by real situations and First Peoples narratives in individual, family, generational, and community identity.

### BI-03

Using French connects everyday communication with membership in a Francophone community, from a child's immediate surroundings to wider communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), communicating in French and Francophone belonging; community elaboration.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Identify and work with units of spoken language, alongside growing recognition of alphabet letters and words encountered frequently.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: recognizing and manipulating phonological units and recognizing letters and frequent words.

### CC-02

Interpret stories through images, predictions and links to personal and cultural experience. Attend to a story's parts and organization to grasp its overall meaning, including when exploring First Peoples narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: image-supported meaning and prediction, prior knowledge and experience, and story elements and structure including First Peoples narratives.

### CC-03

Express meaning in complete oral sentences, ask questions to clarify texts, and use communication and social strategies to deepen understanding. Participation in class activities and discussions also develops belonging.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: complete oral sentences, questions, communication/socialization strategies, and class participation.

### CC-04

Convey a written message using alphabet letters or emerging non-conventional forms, which may include symbols and drawings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: developing writing through letters or non-conventional writing; symbols/drawings elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how speech can be heard as words, syllables and individual sounds, and recognize rhyme. Develop knowledge of alphabet letters alongside this awareness of spoken language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), phonological awareness: word, syllable, phoneme, and rhyme; spelling regularities: alphabet letters.

### C-02

Know how to orient to a book through features such as its cover, title and direction of print. Learn how characters, place, time and action contribute to a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: book elements; literary tools: story elements and their elaborations.

### C-03

Develop strategies for shared understanding through attentive listening, courtesy and turn-taking. Approach reading by connecting prior knowledge with mental images, predictions, questions and inferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication/socialization and reading strategies, including their elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Unlike the immersion Kindergarten source, this first-language French page explicitly includes **complete oral sentences**. That is an oral communication expectation, not a requirement for conventionally written sentences. Its writing competency allows letters or non-conventional forms, and the page does not state an independent fluent-reading benchmark.

The source treats text broadly, including oral, visual, printed and digital forms. The examples illustrate that breadth rather than prescribing a reading list or requiring children to independently read every kind of text. Reading strategies include inference, but this summary adds no commercial reading level or uniform schedule.

First Peoples narratives belong within the source's Big Ideas and story-comprehension competency, not in a separate enrichment category. Use appropriate cultural context and respect authorship, community-specific protocols and permissions; the curriculum's inclusion of a narrative does not authorize reproduction, adaptation or unrestricted retelling.

The Francophone-community elaboration extends from family, class and school to wider communities. This program's identity and language-learning purposes must not be collapsed into [French Immersion Language Arts K](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) or treated as English Language Arts translated into French.
