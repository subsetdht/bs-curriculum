# Social Studies - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations, supported by [S2].

**Document language:** English. The reviewed official page is in English; classroom language is a separate program matter.

**Learning objective:** Explore identity and families in relation to community, using questions, evidence, time, perspectives, and ideas about fairness to understand familiar experiences.

**Prerequisites:** Experiences of people, groups, and places provide entry points. No single family structure, cultural background, or ability to share a family history is assumed.

**Materials:** None are needed for this reference. Activities should identify their own sources and materials without requiring family possessions, photographs, or private information.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], whose Kindergarten focus is **Identity and Families**. This is a subject focus, not a separate course choice. Follow the source locators for official expectations; local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for the appropriate language-arts companion.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Learning about people and families makes both diversity and common experiences visible within a community. Stories and traditions help people understand their own identity and origins.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, diverse communities with commonalities and personal/family stories and traditions.

### BI-02

Identity and relationships are influenced by people's rights, the roles they take, and their responsibilities toward others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, rights, roles, responsibilities, identity, and healthy relationships.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Investigate questions by gathering, interpreting, and analyzing information, then communicating findings and decisions. Examine different kinds of sources, ask about their features, and use clues to make inferences and draw conclusions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and evidence.

### CC-02

Explain why familiar people, places, objects, or events are significant. Arrange objects, images, or events in sequence, notice change and continuity, and consider causes and consequences in one's own life.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance, continuity and change, and cause and consequence.

### CC-03

Recognize that people can have different perspectives on familiar situations, places, issues, and events.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective.

### CC-04

Identify fair and unfair aspects of actions, decisions, or events, and consider appropriate ways to respond.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Explore similarities and differences among individuals and families, alongside personal and family histories and traditions. Family experiences are varied; no one family form is the standard against which others are judged.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, similarities/differences among individuals and families and personal/family history and traditions; family-diversity elaborations.

### C-02

Distinguish the needs and wants of individuals and families. The elaborations connect these with the work people do in families and communities and invite discussion of differing views about needs and wants.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, needs and wants of individuals and families and associated work and comparison elaborations.

### C-03

Learn about rights, roles, and responsibilities in the groups to which people belong, including responsibilities to oneself, other people, and the environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, rights, roles, and responsibilities of individuals and groups; associated examples.

### C-04

Learn about people, places, and events in the local community and in local First Peoples communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, local community and local First Peoples people, places, and events.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source uses personal, family, and local contexts to develop inquiry and thinking. It does not prescribe one family history, set of celebrations, or community story for every learner. Its examples of artifacts, photographs, family milestones, and natural or built surroundings are possible contexts, not required possessions or disclosures.

First Peoples communities appear explicitly in the content, and First Peoples oral histories and celebrations appear among the elaborations. Keep their local context and use appropriate relationships and permissions; this is not permission to retell or reproduce a community's cultural works.

The complete elaborations remain at [S1]. Use inclusive alternatives when an example would require a learner to reveal sensitive family circumstances, ancestry, or finances. The reference does not turn a local history project or district teaching plan into a provincial requirement.
