# Arts Education - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone. These shared provincial expectations use the applicability established in the [Kindergarten program scope and sources](../README.md#program-scope-and-sources).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary official source is in English (`en`). This reference does not set the language of instruction. Consult the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for the distinct language pathways.

**Learning objective:** Use imagination, inquiry, and purposeful play to create, experience, discuss, and share meaning through dance, drama, music, and visual arts.

**Prerequisites:** No formal arts training is assumed. Learners can enter through their own ways of moving, sensing, communicating, imagining, or making, with appropriate support and adaptations.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Later activities should identify accessible tools and materials, safe participation options, and any permissions required for artworks or cultural practices.

**How to use:** Read with [S1] and use the locators to check official expectations and elaborations. This record summarizes Kindergarten, not a K-3 arts band or a sequence of craft projects. Local item IDs are repository mapping aids, not ministry standard codes or achievement levels.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Creating and encountering art helps people communicate who they are, share ideas, and connect with other people and their communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, individual/community expression and connecting and sharing through the arts.

### BI-02

Purposeful artistic play makes room for questions, discoveries, and imaginative exploration.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, arts engagement, inquiry, and purposeful play.

### BI-03

Movement, dramatic action, music, and visual art each offer distinctive ways to express meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, unique meaning in dance, drama, music, and visual arts.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore how artistic elements, movement, materials, tools, technologies, and processes can be used. Create both independently and with others, using imagination, questions, experimentation, and play to express aspects of self and community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating, artistic exploration, individual/collaborative creation, and expressions of self and community.

### CC-02

Observe and discuss how artists make their work. Build practical and creative skills across art forms, nurture imagination and motivation, and reflect on connections between a creative process and other experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and reflecting, artists' processes, technical development, and reflection.

### CC-03

Make sense of how symbols are used and communicate feelings, stories, observations, ideas, and experiences through the arts. Respond to artworks and experience, document, and share creative work in varied ways while developing deeper engagement with the content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting, symbols, expression, describing/responding, documenting/sharing, and increasingly sophisticated engagement.

## Content

### C-01

In dance, consider the moving body, space, energy or dynamics, time, relationships, and the shape or organization of a dance. In drama, explore characters and how time, place, and plot give an imagined situation meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of dance and drama, including dance form and character.

### C-02

Explore musical pulse, rhythm, tempo, pitch, and loudness or softness. In visual art, use line, shape, texture, and colour, with attention to pattern and repetition.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements, visual-art elements of design, and principles of pattern and repetition.

### C-03

Learn about processes, movements, materials, techniques, and tools that support making art, including manual and digital technologies. Explore ways to represent sounds, ideas, and movement through notation, and encounter dramatic forms and symbols that communicate meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, arts processes/materials/technologies, notation, dramatic forms, and symbolism.

### C-04

Become familiar with local artworks and artistic traditions, and with both traditional and contemporary First Peoples arts and ways of making.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional and contemporary Aboriginal arts and arts-making processes, and local works and artistic traditions.

### C-05

Understand personal and shared responsibilities for physical and emotional safety while creating, experiencing, or sharing art, including care for people, materials, and spaces.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal and collective responsibility, including the safe-learning-environment elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

All four art forms belong in this curriculum; visual art is not a substitute for dance, drama, or music. The listed elements are not exhaustive. Kindergarten explicitly includes **dance form**, but its music list does not include the separate **music form** entry found in Grade 1 [S2]. Its music-dynamics elaboration describes loud and soft. Do not turn Grade 1 additions into required Kindergarten content.

Kindergarten's local-art content and its exploration of how symbols are used should be read in their own wording at [S1]. Shared themes across grades do not make the Kindergarten record a copy of the Grade 1 summary.

The official elaborations explain creative processes, purposeful play, notation, technologies, and possible ways to document or share. They do not prescribe a public performance, a particular device, conventional musical notation, or a fixed collection of projects. Provide accessible ways to participate without requiring children to share private experiences.

The inclusion of First Peoples arts does not authorize copying cultural designs, songs, dances, stories, or ceremonial practices. Seek appropriate community guidance and permissions, credit artists and communities, and distinguish learning about an artistic tradition from permission to reproduce it.
