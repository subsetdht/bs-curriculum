# Kindergarten curriculum

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > K

**Browse:** [Programs](#choose-a-program) | [Subjects and learning standards](#subjects-and-learning-standards) | [Program sources](#program-scope-and-sources) | [Coverage](#coverage-and-limits)

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia, Canada. **Programs covered:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone. **Document language:** English. **Source review date:** 2026-09-14.

All ten source-linked Kindergarten landings have original, source-checked **core summaries**. Seven subject records are shared across the three programs; each program has its own language-arts record. Each pathway therefore contains eight records, with ten unique records across the whole grade.

These references help families and educators find provincial expectations. They are not official wording, complete transcriptions of the standards, lesson plans, assessments, or a required teaching schedule. Read the linked official sources for precise expectations and elaborations.

## Choose a program

[English-language program](#english-language-program) | [Early French Immersion](#early-french-immersion) | [Francophone](#francophone)

### English-language program

Start with [English Language Arts K](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects in the [subject table](#subjects-and-learning-standards). This eight-record pathway supports the English-language program; it does not substitute either French-language arts curriculum for English Language Arts.

### Early French Immersion

Start with [French Immersion Language Arts K](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects in the [subject table](#subjects-and-learning-standards). This eight-record pathway uses the distinct additional-language curriculum, not English Language Arts translated into French or Francophone first-language arts.

The [provincial French Immersion policy][immersion-policy] identifies Kindergarten as the normal Early Immersion entry point and says Kindergarten through Grade 2 should be taught entirely in French. Shared subject expectations do not imply English classroom instruction. The immersion language-arts source is in French, but this repository's summary is in English.

### Francophone

Start with [Francophone Language Arts K](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the same seven shared subjects in the [subject table](#subjects-and-learning-standards). This eight-record pathway supports the distinct first-language Francophone program and its language and cultural-identity purposes.

The [provincial program comparison][program-comparison] identifies Francophone provision from K through 12 and distinguishes it from immersion. It describes French as the language of instruction in subjects other than English Language Arts. The [required-areas order][required-areas], section 2(a), identifies French Language Arts rather than English Language Arts for Francophone students in this Kindergarten subject set. Consult the responsible school authority for eligibility, registration, and local implementation; this reference does not determine admission.

**Not a Kindergarten pathway:** Late French Immersion begins at grade 6 under provincial policy. It is not added to Kindergarten record metadata. Core French is a different language curriculum, not a substitute for either of the French-language arts pathways above.

## How to use these references

Choose the appropriate program, read a record's Scope and use section, and use its source locators to find official wording. **Big Ideas** describe enduring understandings, **Curricular Competencies** describe learning processes and skills, and **Content** identifies knowledge.

Stable item IDs make later resource alignment traceable. For example, [Mathematics K: C-01](mathematics/curriculum.md#c-01) has the canonical repository reference `bc-gk-mathematics#c-01`. These are not official BC standard codes, teaching priorities, or achievement levels. Follow the [linking convention](../../curriculum-format.md#linking-to-records-and-items) when citing an item outside its record.

No special materials or formal prerequisites are needed to read these references. Learner activities need their own materials, accessibility arrangements, and appropriate entry points; these documents do not require particular products or assume that all children develop at the same pace.

## Learning across subjects

The [BC curriculum model](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum/overview) connects knowing, doing, and understanding. The [Core Competencies](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/competencies) encompass Communication, Thinking, and Personal and Social development across learning areas. They do not replace subject-specific competencies or create an extra Kindergarten subject.

ADST and Career Education use **K-3** standards. Their records target Kindergarten while retaining that band in source metadata. ADST specifically pairs its design, skills, and technology competencies with Kindergarten content from other areas; it does not supply a separate required coding or device syllabus.

First Peoples perspectives are retained within the subjects where they occur, including local knowledge, stories, arts, and mathematical and scientific connections. Work with appropriate community guidance, attribution, and permissions. A curriculum reference does not confer permission to reproduce a cultural work or disclose community knowledge.

## Program scope and sources

The [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order][required-areas], sections 1-2, provides the common subject basis. Its definitions explicitly include Francophone education authorities, programs, and students. Section 2 lists the seven shared subjects and distinguishes French Language Arts for immersion and Francophone students from English Language Arts. This is the basis for shared-program metadata; it is not inferred from the availability of an English or French web page.

The [French Immersion policy][immersion-policy] separately establishes parallel curriculum content, Early and Late Immersion entry points, and the language-of-instruction guidance for early grades. The [provincial French-program comparison][program-comparison] identifies Francophone K-12 provision and distinguishes the programs' purposes.

These sources describe provincial expectations. SD43 timetables, school term plans, resource choices, and registration arrangements are not provincial curriculum requirements and are not added to these records. Families should consult their own school authority about local implementation.

The required-areas order includes qualifications in section 3 for certain individual education-plan and English-language-learning circumstances. This collection is a curriculum reference, not an individualized program decision or a rigid checklist for every child.

## Coverage and limits

**Landing coverage:** Ten of ten captured Kindergarten subject landings now link to records. **Written coverage:** Ten source-checked core summaries, supporting three overlapping eight-record program pathways. There are no remaining source-access blockers for these ten entries.

**Language coverage:** All ten summaries use `document_language: en-CA`. Eight primary curriculum sources are in English; the French Immersion and Francophone language-arts sources are in French. French-language editions of the repository summaries and classroom learning resources are not supplied by this pass. An English summary of French curriculum is neither an authorized translation nor a requirement to teach in English.

Each record was compared with its full authoritative curriculum content, not merely a catalog link, page shell, or successful HTTP response. `source-checked` means an author comparison, not ministry endorsement or independent educator review. The records condense the core expectations and selected boundary-setting elaborations; they are not a complete inventory of elaborations. Source revision dates were not established, and the review date is not a curriculum edition.

No lessons, assessments, tools, or unrelated enrichment have been added. The [resources](../01-Resources), [tools](../02-Tools), and [additional-content](../03-Additional-Content) categories remain available for future work, with independent program and language suitability to be documented.

For maintenance, follow the [format guide](../../curriculum-format.md) and [landing-page workflow](../../curriculum-format.md#filling-a-landing-page). Preserve established paths and IDs, and keep landings, grade/program navigation, and central written-summary coverage synchronized.

## Sources and rights

The individual records identify their primary BC sources, source languages, grade scopes, and item locators. Their alignment review date is **2026-09-14**. The required-areas order (sections 1-4), French Immersion policy, provincial French-program comparison, curriculum model, Core Competencies overview, and [BC copyright policy](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/home/copyright) were also reviewed on **2026-09-14**.

The original landing entries were captured from the [BC curriculum catalog](https://curriculum.gov.bc.ca/curriculum) on **2026-09-14**. That catalog-capture date is distinct from the subsequent source-content review, even though they occurred on the same day.

The curriculum remains subject to provincial Crown copyright. This repository supplies original summaries and links, not mirrored curriculum documents, authorized translations, or reproductions of cultural works.
