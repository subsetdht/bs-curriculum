# French Immersion Language Arts - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. This is additional-language learning, distinct from English Language Arts, Core French and first-language Francophone language arts. This Kindergarten record does not cover Late French Immersion.

**Document language:** English (en-CA); the reviewed authoritative source is in French (fr). The source locators below are labelled English glosses of French sections and topics. This original summary is not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource, and its language does not determine classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Begin communicating in French through listening, interaction, images and emerging print, while building participation and belonging in the class.

**Prerequisites:** Conversation, songs, play and experience with pictures can support learning. This reference does not set prior French-proficiency requirements or program admission criteria.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference beyond access to [S1]. Teaching materials need separate French-language suitability, accessibility and rights checks.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for official French expectations and elaborations, using these grouped items to locate relevant learning. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes or a teaching order. Pair this record with the shared subjects and program information in the [Kindergarten overview](../README.md).

The source standards reviewed are for Kindergarten alone. Its examples of learning contexts include songs and illustrated children's books; these are not evidence that children must already read the printed text independently.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Hearing French used and trying out the language models offered provides a starting point for communicating in a new language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), acquiring a new language through listening and reproducing models.

### BI-02

Communication depends on how people make room for one another: attentive listening, courtesy and sharing speaking time support both understanding and respect.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), politeness, listening, and yielding a turn.

### BI-03

Images can give access to meaning before every word is understood. Connecting distinctive written letter forms with speech sounds provides another route into texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), images and text comprehension and letters' graphic forms and sounds.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Participate in French-language activities, discussions, listening and reading, bringing personal experience into new learning. Using French in these shared experiences helps develop belonging in the class.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: class belonging through French, active listening/reading, and connections with personal knowledge.

### CC-02

Use visual information to anticipate meaning and identify and name characters and objects. Build recognition of alphabet letters and their associated sounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Explore and reflect: predictions from visual supports, naming characters and objects, and letter-sound recognition.

### CC-03

Use learned vocabulary spontaneously to express ideas and emotions. Respond to the teacher's directions through speech or non-verbal responses, and draw on communication strategies when interacting with others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: spontaneous expression, responses to instructions, and interaction strategies.

### CC-04

Make a message visible through drawings and emerging writing, including scribbles or strings of letters, while also learning to form the alphabet's letters.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies (English gloss), Create and communicate: non-conventional writing and drawing, and forming letters; non-conventional-writing elaboration.

## Content

### C-01

Become familiar with cultural elements encountered through French and with habits that make conversation work, including attentive listening, turn-taking, greetings and courtesy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies and cultural elements.

### C-02

Learn how features of an illustrated text, such as its title, pages and pictures, support a reader. Recognize how characters, places and events give a story its meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), story elements and textual organization, including the text-elements elaboration.

### C-03

Explore French sound patterns through letter sounds, syllables and rhyme. Develop alphabet knowledge, including recognition of capital letters, alongside this attention to spoken language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), phonological awareness and spelling regularities: alphabet letters and capitals.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Kindergarten learning includes responding without words, drawing and non-conventional writing as well as spoken French and letter formation. Do not replace these expectations with Grade 1 reading or writing of simple sentences, independent fluent reading, or a fixed grammar sequence.

The source's context elaboration describes songs, including nursery rhymes, and picture books whose accompanying text can range from a word to a simple sentence. That description concerns the material used, not a Kindergarten sentence-writing requirement. Digital, printed and multimedia supports are possible; no named product or prescribed vocabulary list is established here.

Cultural elements are included in the Kindergarten content, but the page does not specify a required cultural canon. Choose and attribute material appropriately, including obtaining any permissions needed for First Peoples stories or community knowledge. A cultural learning connection does not grant reproduction or retelling rights.

The source's Kindergarten emphasis on belonging concerns participation in the class through French. Do not import another grade's language structures or assume equivalence with [Francophone Language Arts K](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md). The English document language does not make this an English-language instructional requirement.
