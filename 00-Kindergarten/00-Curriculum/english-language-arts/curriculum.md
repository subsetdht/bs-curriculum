# English Language Arts - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > English Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language. Kindergarten French Immersion and Francophone language arts have distinct curriculum records; this is not a shared language-arts requirement for those programs.

**Document language:** English (en-CA). The reviewed official source is in English (en).

**Learning objective:** Explore how speech, images and emerging print communicate meaning, and use stories and exchanges with others to develop expression, curiosity and a sense of identity.

**Prerequisites:** Experiences with conversation, play, pictures and stories offer useful starting points. They are not entry requirements, and this reference does not assume that children already read or write independently.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference beyond access to [S1]. Any classroom books, recordings or other resources need their own accessibility, age-suitability and rights review.

**How to use:** Read these grouped summaries alongside the official Kindergarten expectations and elaborations in [S1]. Use the local IDs to map resources to this record; they are repository references, not official BC standard codes or a prescribed teaching sequence. Consult the [grade overview](../README.md) for program navigation.

The target grade and source scope are Kindergarten, not Grade 0 or a combined Kindergarten and Grade 1 progression.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Experimenting with language can be enjoyable and creative. Questions, wonder and playful attention to words open up discoveries about language, oneself and the surrounding world.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, creativity and joy, playing with language, and curiosity and wonder.

### BI-02

Stories make room for different personal experiences while helping children explore who they are and how they relate to their families.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, learning about ourselves and our families and everyone's unique story.

### BI-03

Pictures and words offer ways to share meaning; listening and speaking let people bring their experiences into contact with those of others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, sharing texts through pictures and words and connecting through listening and speaking.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Bring experience and information to reading, listening and viewing, and try developmentally appropriate strategies to construct meaning. Explore how print, speech and images work, including distinctions between drawing and writing and connections between letters and sounds.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, sources and prior knowledge, reading/listening/viewing strategies, and foundational concepts.

### CC-02

Engage with stories and other texts, connecting them with personal experience, family and community life to build understanding of identity. Notice how a story's sequence helps make sense of it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Comprehend and connect, active engagement, personal/family/community identity, personal connections, and story structure.

### CC-03

Join exchanges that help people understand one another, listening as well as contributing ideas, feelings, viewpoints and preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, exchanging ideas and perspectives and using language to share ideas, feelings, opinions, and preferences.

### CC-04

Plan and make stories and other texts for different purposes and audiences, experimenting with print and other ways of representing meaning. Use this creation to explore oneself and relationships with family and community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, creating texts about self/family/community and planning and creating for purposes and audiences; planning elaboration.

### CC-05

Explore preparing and sharing an oral story, including using voice to help listeners understand. An original story can be created; sharing an existing story requires permission.

**Source locator:** [S1], Create and communicate, oral storytelling processes and the elaboration on original or existing stories, permission, and vocal expression.

## Content

### C-01

Recognize an ordered beginning, middle and ending in stories. Explore how rhythm, rhyme, humour and imaginative language contribute to the experience of a story.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Story: structure of story and literary elements and devices.

### C-02

Learn ways to make sense of texts and participate in conversation, drawing on pictures, patterns, familiar print and experience. Develop writing processes and ways of thinking and talking about one's own learning as a reader and writer.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Strategies and processes: reading strategies, oral language strategies, metacognitive strategies, and writing processes.

### C-03

Explore how books and print are organized, including reading direction, spaces and punctuation. Distinguish letters from words, connect uppercase and lowercase forms, and develop recognition of most alphabet letters and letter-sound matches and of some familiar words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: concepts of print and letter knowledge, including their elaborations.

### C-04

Develop awareness of speech as words, syllables and smaller sounds. Hear and create rhymes, and explore separating and combining the individual sounds within spoken words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: phonemic and phonological awareness and their elaboration.

### C-05

Use emerging letter forms, scribbles or letter strings to communicate, while distinguishing writing from drawing. Explore the connections among speaking, reading and writing.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, Language features, structures, and conventions: letter formation and the relationship between reading, writing, and oral language.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Kindergarten source emphasizes exploration and developmentally appropriate strategies. It does not state the Grade 1 competency for fluent grade-level reading or impose conventional sentence writing. Its letter-knowledge elaboration specifies **most** letters and letter-sound matches and **some** familiar words; these qualifications should not become an all-letters mastery test or an admission requirement.

Texts include oral, written, visual and digital communication, not only printed books. Elaborations illustrate approaches such as picture-supported meaning-making, familiar environmental print, reflection on learning and supported storytelling. These examples do not prescribe a commercial reading level, a book list or a uniform timetable.

Community and cultural traditions are part of the source's engagement elaboration. When choosing First Peoples stories or other community works, preserve their cultural context and respect the relevant permissions. The oral-storytelling elaboration's reference to sharing an existing story with permission is not a general licence to reproduce or adapt stories.

This record is distinct from [French Immersion Language Arts K](../french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md) and [Francophone Language Arts K](../francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md). It should not be translated into French and substituted for either curriculum.
