# Mathematics - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. The program basis is the required-areas order [S2], not the language of this document.

**Document language:** English. The reviewed curriculum page is in English; neither fact prescribes classroom language or makes this a French-language learning resource.

**Learning objective:** Develop a sense of small quantities and their relationships, and use mathematical thinking to explore patterns, objects, comparisons, and everyday information.

**Prerequisites:** Everyday experiences with sorting, quantities, and play provide possible entry points. This reference does not assume formal counting, calculation, or reading proficiency on entry to Kindergarten.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read the reference. Any later activity should identify its own accessible objects, representations, or technology.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], following the source locators for the official standards and elaborations. The local IDs support curriculum mapping; they are not ministry standard codes or a teaching sequence. Select the appropriate language-arts record separately through the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program).

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

A quantity can be seen as a whole or as smaller parts. Matching each object to a count, together with a strong sense of 5 and 10, provides a foundation for flexible number thinking.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, decomposing quantities and one-to-one correspondence with benchmarks of 5 and 10.

### BI-02

Finding what repeats allows a learner to describe the structure of a pattern.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, identifying repeating elements.

### BI-03

The features of objects provide ways to describe them and to make meaningful comparisons and measurements.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, attributes of objects.

### BI-04

Everyday experience offers a starting point for comparing events that seem likely or unlikely.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, likelihood of familiar events.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore relationships through reasoning, estimation, and developing mental strategies for quantities. Use models of familiar situations and suitable technology to investigate mathematical ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and analyzing, connections, reasonable estimates, mental mathematics, technology, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Build and apply understanding through play and inquiry, using visualization and different approaches to problems. Work with problems connected to place, stories, cultural practices, and perspectives of local First Peoples, the wider local community, and other cultures.

**Source locator:** [S1], Understanding and solving, play/inquiry, visualization, multiple strategies, and locally and culturally connected problem solving.

### CC-03

Make thinking understandable to others through objects, pictures, symbols, and language. Take part in mathematical discussion, using relevant vocabulary and explaining the reasons for ideas and decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and representing, varied communication, mathematical vocabulary, justification, and concrete/pictorial/symbolic representations.

### CC-04

Revisit one's own and others' mathematical thinking, and connect ideas across mathematics, other learning, and personal interests. Include First Peoples worldviews and perspectives when making mathematical connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and reflecting, reflection, connections among ideas and interests, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Develop number concepts through 10, including ways to make 5 and to separate and recombine quantities through 10. The elaborations connect counting and recognizing small groups with understanding how parts make a whole.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 10, ways to make 5, and decomposition of numbers to 10; associated counting and part-part-whole elaborations.

### C-02

Use objects to show and describe a quantity changing within 10. Explore equal and unequal quantities through the idea of balance and imbalance.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, concrete changes in quantity to 10 and equality/inequality as balance/imbalance.

### C-03

Investigate repeating patterns made from two or three elements, attending to the part that repeats.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, repeating patterns with two or three elements; identifying the core in the elaboration.

### C-04

Compare objects directly by length and related linear dimensions, mass, and capacity. For linear comparisons, the elaboration highlights starting objects at a common baseline.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, direct comparative measurement; linear, mass, capacity, and baseline elaborations.

### C-05

Explore two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects through a single attribute at a time. Describing, sorting, and constructing provide ways to notice their features without requiring formal shape names.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, single attributes of 2D shapes and 3D objects; sorting, building, and terminology elaborations.

### C-06

Use objects or pictures in graphs to make information visible. Describe the likelihood of familiar events in everyday terms, rather than through numerical probabilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, concrete/pictorial graphs as visual tools and likelihood of familiar life events.

### C-07

Notice the distinguishing features of coins and explore the use of money through financial role-play.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: attributes of coins and financial role-play.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Kindergarten content limit for number concepts, decomposition, and concrete changes in quantity is **10**. The source does not replace this with Grade 1's range to 20, a written arithmetic syllabus, or a timed fact-recall requirement. Its fluency Big Idea is grounded in one-to-one correspondence and a sense of 5 and 10.

Direct comparative measurement is not the same as Grade 1's measurement with non-standard units. The shape elaboration explicitly says that specific mathematical terminology for naming and identifying shapes and objects is not expected at this level. Financial literacy focuses on coin attributes and role-play; do not substitute a requirement to calculate with coin values.

The full elaborations at [S1] contain possible counting, graphing, technology, and cultural contexts. They are not a compulsory activity list. First Peoples connections belong within mathematical learning, but the curriculum does not grant permission to reproduce cultural designs, stories, or community knowledge.
