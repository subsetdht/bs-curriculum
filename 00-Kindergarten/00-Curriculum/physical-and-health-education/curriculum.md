# Physical and Health Education - Kindergarten

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Kindergarten](../README.md) > Physical and Health Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone. These shared provincial expectations use the applicability established in the [Kindergarten program scope and sources](../README.md#program-scope-and-sources).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the primary official source is in English (`en`). This does not prescribe English instruction. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for language pathways.

**Learning objective:** Build confidence in movement and healthy everyday choices while developing body awareness, personal safety, caring relationships, and awareness of feelings and mental well-being.

**Prerequisites:** Begin with each learner's existing movement and communication experiences. No fitness threshold, sporting experience, or particular physical or sensory ability is assumed.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Any learner activity must identify its own accessible materials, adaptations, appropriate supervision, and safety arrangements.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], including its health, safety, and consent elaborations. This is a Kindergarten curriculum reference, not a health assessment, medical recommendation, or required exercise routine. Local item IDs support resource mapping; they are not official BC standard codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Being physically active each day contributes to healthy living and helps learners build movement skills and physical literacy.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily activity, movement skills, and physical literacy.

### BI-02

Understanding oneself, other people, and the body supports caring relationships and choices about looking after oneself. Health includes physical, mental, and emotional well-being together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, self/others and caring relationships, body knowledge and healthy choices, and the dimensions of well-being.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop and demonstrate fundamental movements in varied activities and settings, and describe how the body reacts to being active. Participate with attention to safety, fair play, and leadership.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Physical literacy, movement skills, bodily responses, safety, fair play, and leadership.

### CC-02

Take part daily in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity and notice opportunities to be active at school, at home, and in the community. Explore different foods and their contributions to health, identify opportunities for health-supporting choices, and identify sources of health information.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Healthy and active living, daily participation, activity opportunities, food exploration, choices, and health-information sources.

### CC-03

Identify and describe situations that are unsafe or uncomfortable. Show respect when participating with others and recognize caring actions among classmates and in families, including an emerging understanding of permission and personal boundaries.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social and community health, identifying unsafe/uncomfortable situations, respectful behaviour, caring behaviours, and consent/boundary elaborations.

### CC-04

Identify and describe practices that support mental well-being, as well as feelings and worries. Become aware of personal skills, interests, and preferences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Mental well-being, supportive practices, feelings/worries, and personal skills/interests/preferences.

## Content

### C-01

Learn techniques for moving in place, travelling, and controlling objects. Learn how to participate in activities undertaken alone or with a partner, movement with rhythm, and games.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-locomotor, locomotor, and manipulative movement skills, and individual/dual activities, rhythmic activities, and games.

### C-02

Understand connections among food, water, and health, and everyday practices that support well-being. The source's examples include rest, activity, food choices, relaxation, and hygiene; they do not establish a required daily routine.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, relationships between food, hydration, and health, and health-promoting practices and their elaborations.

### C-03

Know accurate names for body parts, including private parts, and distinguish welcome, safe touch from touch that is unwanted, harmful, or uncomfortable. Consent and respect for personal boundaries are part of this body-safety learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, body-part names including male and female private parts, appropriate/inappropriate touch, and the related consent elaborations.

### C-04

Develop awareness of different kinds of substances and of hazards in everyday settings. Learn where reliable health information and support can be found, including trusted adults who can help with uncomfortable experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, types of substances, hazards and unsafe situations, and reliable health-information sources; Healthy and active living, health-information-source elaboration on trusted support.

### C-05

Understand how caring actions support people in groups and families, and how emotions can arise and affect oneself and others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, caring behaviours in groups and families, and emotions and their causes and effects.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The Kindergarten source asks learners to identify health-information sources and recognize and describe unsafe or uncomfortable situations. Grade 1 [S2] expands these statements to recognizing basic information from varied sources and describing prevention and response strategies. Those later competencies are not presented as additional Kindergarten requirements.

Likewise, Grade 1's separate Content statements about the effects of activities and safe use or avoidance of substances are not imported here. Kindergarten does already include describing the body's reactions within Physical literacy; this distinction must not erase that competency.

The daily moderate-to-vigorous participation statement in [S1] is retained without adding minutes, fitness scores, or a medical prescription. Listed activities are examples, not compulsory sports. The games elaboration includes traditional First Peoples games; any use of community-specific games needs appropriate cultural guidance and permissions.

Body safety and mental well-being are central, not optional additions to exercise. The source's elaborations connect respectful behaviour with asking permission, respecting a person's refusal, and recognizing boundaries. They identify trusted adults and school staff as possible sources of support and include recognition of concerning behaviour by others, not only unfamiliar people. Use accurate, inclusive, developmentally appropriate language without requiring children to disclose private health information or unsafe experiences.

The food elaboration connects nourishment with growth and energy, as well as family, history, culture, community, and nature. It is not a prescribed menu or a ranking of families' food practices. Consult [S1] for the full elaborations and plan activities for varied developmental, physical, communication, and sensory needs.
