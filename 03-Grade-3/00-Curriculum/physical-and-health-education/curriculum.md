# Physical and Health Education - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Physical and Health Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English; the official source reviewed is in English.

**Learning objective:** Develop movement skills and active-living habits alongside personal safety, positive relationships, and understanding of physical, emotional, and mental well-being.

**Prerequisites:** Begin with each learner's movement, communication, and self-care experiences. No fitness score, particular body type, or level of physical ability is required by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Activities need appropriate supervision, accessibility, equipment, and safety planning.

**How to use:** Read [S1] for official expectations, elaborations, and supports. Local IDs are repository references, not official standard codes, health advice for an individual, or assessment criteria.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Regular moderate-to-vigorous activity supports well-being, while learning movement skills and strategies expands ways to participate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, daily activity at moderate/vigorous intensity and movement skills/strategies.

### BI-02

Health-supporting routines and safety strategies help protect oneself and other people.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, healthy personal practices and safety.

### BI-03

Communication and managing emotions support healthy relationships. Physical, emotional, and mental health influence one another.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, communication/emotion management in relationships and interconnected dimensions of health.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop and apply fundamental movements, concepts, and strategies in varied activities and environments. Monitor exertion to better understand participation and the body's responses.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Physical literacy: movement skills, movement concepts/strategies, and monitoring exertion.

### CC-02

Participate safely and fairly, show leadership, and explain what makes different physical activities positive experiences.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Physical literacy: safety, fair play, leadership, and positive experiences.

### CC-03

Participate daily in moderate-to-vigorous physical activity and identify both opportunities and barriers at school, home, and in the community. Explore strategies for healthy eating in different settings and for pursuing personal healthy-living goals.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Healthy and active living: daily activity, opportunities/challenges, eating choices, and personal goals.

### CC-04

Find health information and support services, and describe ways to avoid or respond assertively to unsafe or uncomfortable situations. Apply strategies that build positive relationships and explain how outdoor activity can connect people with community and environment.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Healthy and active living: information/support; Social and community health: avoidance/assertiveness, relationships, and outdoor connections.

### CC-05

Apply practices that support mental well-being. Describe physical, emotional, and social changes with growth, and consider influences on mental health and self-identity.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Mental well-being: supportive strategies, changes with age, and influences on well-being/self-identity.

## Content

### C-01

Learn techniques for movement in place, movement through space, and controlling objects. Use movement concepts and strategies across individual, paired, rhythmic, and game activities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, non-locomotor/locomotor/manipulative skills, movement concepts/strategies, and types of physical activity.

### C-02

Learn ways of noticing and monitoring exertion. Connect physical activity, sleep, illness prevention, food, and hydration with participation and overall well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, monitoring exertion, health-promoting practices, and nutrition/hydration choices.

### C-03

Know strategies for obtaining health information and for responding to hazards, unsafe situations, or abuse. Appropriate help-seeking and protecting personal boundaries are important parts of safety.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, accessing health information and strategies/skills for hazardous, unsafe, or abusive situations.

### C-04

Understand bullying and its consequences. Learn about effects of different substances and strategies for preventing personal harm.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, nature/consequences of bullying and effects of substances/prevention of harm.

### C-05

Explore the relationship between worries and fears and the factors that shape self-identity. These topics connect self-understanding with emotional and social well-being.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, worries/fears and factors influencing self-identity.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This subject includes safety, relationships, growth, and mental well-being as well as exercise. The official elaborations address personal boundaries, consent, trusted help, and discrimination within these topics. Use developmentally appropriate, inclusive language without requiring learners to disclose unsafe experiences or personal health information.

Daily moderate-to-vigorous participation is a curricular competency. Numerical activity and sleep examples appear in the elaborations; this summary does not convert them into a classroom timetable, individual medical prescription, or fitness threshold. Activity selection and participation need accessibility and safety planning.

The source includes varied movement settings and examples such as traditional Indigenous games. A sample activity is not a required game, and cultural materials need appropriate guidance and permissions. Full elaborations remain at [S1].
