# Arts Education - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Arts Education

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English; the source reviewed is in English.

**Learning objective:** Create, interpret, and communicate through dance, drama, music, and visual arts, making purposeful artistic choices and connecting experiences with culture, place, and other people.

**Prerequisites:** Previous experiences with movement, sound, dramatic play, or image-making may help. No specialist training or performance threshold is assumed.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Practical work needs separately specified accessible tools, materials, spaces, and cultural or performance permissions.

**How to use:** This is one Grade 3 curriculum including four art forms, not a course-choice list. Read with [S1]; local IDs support mapping to original summaries rather than official ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Making art brings physical action and thought together. Creative work develops through exploration and inquiry as well as deliberate choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, mind/body in art-making and exploration/inquiry/purposeful choice.

### BI-02

Dance, drama, music, and visual arts each offer distinctive means of shaping and communicating meaning.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the four arts as unique languages.

### BI-03

Encountering and making art can connect personal experiences with the experiences of others.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, connections between people's experiences through the arts.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Select artistic elements, processes, materials, movements, tools, technologies, and environments to develop work. Create individually and collaboratively through imagination, inquiry, experimentation, and purposeful play.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating: artistic choices and individual/collaborative creation.

### CC-02

Explore identity, belonging, culture, and place through artistic experiences, considering relationships among communities, cultures, and the arts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Exploring and creating: identity/place/culture/belonging and cultural/community relationships.

### CC-03

Observe and question how artists work, predict possibilities, and refine ideas and technical skills across art forms. Reflect on creative processes and connect other learning and personal experience with planning, making, and interpreting art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and reflecting: artists' processes, refinement, reflection, and connections to other areas of learning.

### CC-04

Use symbolism and creative expression to communicate ideas, feelings, and experiences. Describe and respond to visual and performing arts, including constructive feedback.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting: symbolism, expression, response, and feedback.

### CC-05

Document and share creative work in varied ways, apply learning in new settings, and demonstrate increasingly developed engagement with artistic content.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and documenting: transfer, documentation/sharing, and increasingly sophisticated application.

## Content

### C-01

Explore how body, space, dynamics, time, relationships, and form shape dance. Learn how choreographic choices develop movement ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, elements of dance and choreographic devices.

### C-02

Understand how character, time, place, plot, and tension contribute to drama. Encounter varied dramatic forms rather than assuming drama means only a scripted stage production.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, drama elements and a variety of dramatic forms.

### C-03

Explore musical time through pulse, duration, rhythm, and tempo, and sound through pitch, timbre, dynamics, form, and texture. These elements help describe how musical ideas are organized and heard together.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, music elements.

### C-04

Develop visual images using line, shape, space, texture, colour, and form. Work with relationships among pattern, repetition, rhythm, contrast, and emphasis, and use image-development strategies to transform an idea into an image.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, visual-art elements/principles of design and image development strategies.

### C-05

Learn processes, materials, tools, techniques, and technologies that support art-making. Explore notation for sound, movement, or ideas and symbolism as a way to represent meaning across the arts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, arts processes/materials/technologies/tools/techniques, notation, and symbolism.

### C-06

Encounter traditional and contemporary Indigenous arts and arts-making, alongside local works and artistic traditions from varied cultures, communities, times, and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, traditional/contemporary Aboriginal arts and diverse local works and artistic traditions.

### C-07

Understand personal and shared responsibilities for physical and emotional safety while creating, experiencing, and sharing art.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, personal and collective responsibility in a safe learning environment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The elements named by the source are not an exhaustive vocabulary list or a prescribed sequence of projects. The Grade 3 curriculum includes all four art forms; visual craft alone does not represent its scope. Technologies include manual as well as digital tools.

Examples of notation, dramatic forms, image development, and choreography remain in the official elaborations at [S1]. Do not convert each example into a required performance or assess a learner only through public sharing.

Indigenous arts include contemporary practices as well as traditions. Permission and community protocols govern use of designs, songs, dances, stories, and ceremonial knowledge; their appearance in curriculum does not authorize copying or imitation.
