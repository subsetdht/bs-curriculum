# Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English; the source reviewed is in English.

**Learning objective:** Develop design thinking and making by noticing possibilities, trying ideas, choosing tools safely, and reflecting on products and collaboration.

**Prerequisites:** Curiosity and opportunities to explore are useful entry points. No coding experience, personal device, or specialist technical training is required by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. A future making activity needs its own accessible materials, tools, and safety arrangements.

**How to use:** [S1] explicitly combines **K-3 ADST competencies with grade-level content from other learning areas**. This Grade 3 record applies that band without inventing a separate technology-content syllabus. Use repository IDs alongside the relevant partner subject's IDs; neither is an official BC code.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Questions and interests can become design possibilities, while playful exploration builds the skills needed to pursue them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, natural curiosity in design and skills developed through play.

### BI-02

A technology is useful because it extends what a person can do, not because it necessarily contains electronics.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, technologies as tools extending human capabilities.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Explore to notice needs and opportunities, generate possibilities from experience and interests, and develop ideas with others before choosing one to pursue.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design - Ideating.

### CC-02

Choose materials and tools and make a product using familiar procedures or approaches demonstrated by others. Revise through trial and error, responding to problems and incorporating useful new ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design - Making.

### CC-03

Choose how and with whom to share a product, explain its development and contribution, and judge the design using personal preferences. Reflect on the effectiveness of both independent and group work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Design - Sharing: audience, demonstration, contribution, evaluation, and reflection on collaboration.

### CC-04

Use materials, tools, and technologies safely in physical and digital settings. Extend existing skills and develop new ones through play and cooperative work.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Skills.

### CC-05

Try simple, available technologies and tools to discover how they can extend personal capabilities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applied Technologies.

## Content

### C-01

The source supplies an integration rule rather than a separate Grade 3 content list: use the K-3 design, skill, and technology competencies with Grade 3 content from another area of learning. The partner subject establishes the knowledge being explored; ADST establishes the design-and-making learning.

**Source locator:** [S1], introductory learning-standard instruction specifying ADST K-3 and grade-level content from other areas.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Grade 3 remains within the K-3 model. Do not import later-grade design stages or technology modules, or invent mandatory coding, robotics, software, or devices. Cite the actual Grade 3 partner expectation when mapping an integrated activity.

The source's elaboration of "product" is broader than a physical object: a designed process or service can also fit. Sharing does not necessarily require a public presentation or online publication. These are scope clarifications, not a prescribed activity.

Trial and error takes place within appropriate safety limits, including privacy and care in digital environments. Full elaborations remain at [S1].
