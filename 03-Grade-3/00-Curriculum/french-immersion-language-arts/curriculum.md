# French Immersion Language Arts - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > French Immersion Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Early French Immersion. This second-language curriculum is distinct from English Language Arts, Core French, and first-language Francophone language arts. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the authoritative source is in French. This is an original reference summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English glosses of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Understand and communicate increasingly developed messages in French through cultural comparison, thoughtful reading, spontaneous conversation, and detailed writing.

**Prerequisites:** Prior immersion experiences provide useful starting points. This reference does not determine program admission or a language-proficiency threshold.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Subsequent texts and activities need independent French-language suitability, accessibility, attribution, and permissions.

**How to use:** Consult [S1] for the official French wording and elaborations, alongside the shared Grade 3 subjects. These are Grade 3 standards; K-9 in the compilation title [S2] is not a standards band. Local IDs are repository references, not official BC codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Connections between people's experiences help them interpret messages. Texts also offer encounters with cultural perspectives beyond one's own.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), connections among lived experiences and cultural perspectives in texts.

### BI-02

Tales offer ways to explore shared human experiences, while attention to a text's organization, clues, and words helps a reader understand its message.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), human dimensions of tales and meaning through structure, clues, and vocabulary.

### BI-03

French has its own patterns and conventions; learning to communicate involves understanding that system rather than transferring another language word for word.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), language-specific systems of rules.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Compare aspects of one's own and other cultures, and consider the values expressed through characters and narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): cultural similarities/differences and values in characters/narrative.

### CC-02

Build meaning by separating compound sentences into meaningful word groups, locating a central idea, and predicting from textual clues. Use a text's features to support understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): sentence grouping, main idea, predictions, and textual elements.

### CC-03

Organize information and ideas to make a message easier to communicate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): organization of information and ideas.

### CC-04

Sustain spontaneous conversation by asking and answering questions and expressing ideas, feelings, and opinions in compound sentences. Develop French expression without relying on calques or literal translation from another language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): compound-sentence speech, spontaneous exchange, and avoiding calques/word-for-word translation.

### CC-05

Read fluently with expressive intonation and respond to texts by drawing on existing knowledge.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): fluent reading with intonation and prior-knowledge-based response.

### CC-06

Produce detailed written texts using the structures being studied, rather than treating writing only as isolated sentence practice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): detailed writing using studied structures.

## Content

### C-01

Learn communication strategies that support attentive listening, appropriate turn-taking, and consideration of another person's perspective. Explore cultural elements alongside these interpersonal practices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), communication strategies and cultural elements.

### C-02

Learn how tales and children's novels work as literary forms. Their elaborations explore characters and events, including a tale's conflict and moral dimensions, without prescribing particular books.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), literary tools: characteristics of tales and children's novels, with elaborations.

### C-03

Understand narrative organization and the features that help readers follow a text. The elaborations connect narrative development with its beginning and resolution, identify aids such as headings and visual features, and focus punctuation work on commas and exclamation marks.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: narrative structure, textual elements, and punctuation.

### C-04

Develop spelling-pattern knowledge and use roots and affixes to understand word formation. The spelling elaboration focuses on noun plurals ending in -x and adjective agreement in gender and number; the affix elaboration distinguishes prefixes and suffixes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), spelling regularities and language tools: roots/affixes; corresponding elaborations.

### C-05

Use reflexive personal pronouns and compound-sentence structures in affirmative and negative forms. The elaboration includes coordination, simple prepositions, and negation with ne...pas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: reflexive personal pronouns and affirmative/negative compound sentences.

### C-06

Connect verb forms with the genres being read and written. The elaboration identifies the present indicative, near future, introductory notions of the imperfect and compound past, and subject-verb agreement.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: verb modes/tenses related to the studied genres, with elaboration.

## Elaborations and boundaries

Preserve the Grade 3 distinction between **compound-sentence work** here and the **complex-sentence writing** named in the separate Francophone curriculum. Do not import Grade 4 requirements such as a structured paragraph, question inversion, or that grade's particular poetry and grammar focus.

Tales and children's novels are explicit content categories, not a prescribed reading list. Detailed writing and fluent, expressive reading do not establish a word count, paragraph count, reading-speed target, or standardized reading level.

The past-tense elaboration introduces notions of two forms; it does not demand comprehensive mastery of both. Other examples and explanations remain at [S1] and [S2].

This grade explicitly addresses cultural perspectives, comparison, and narrative values. Keep those strands visible without importing differently worded cultural expectations from neighbouring grades. Any cultural or literary work still requires suitable attribution, access, and permissions.
