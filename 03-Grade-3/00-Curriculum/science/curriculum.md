# Science - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Science

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English, independently of classroom language. The official source reviewed is in English.

**Learning objective:** Investigate relationships in local ecosystems, the nature of matter, thermal energy, and changing landforms through ethical inquiry and evidence-based explanations.

**Prerequisites:** Experience observing, comparing, asking questions, and recording findings is helpful, not a formal prerequisite.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Investigations require separately specified tools, supervision, accessible participation, and safety arrangements.

**How to use:** Follow [S1] for authoritative expectations and elaborations. The IDs below support curriculum mapping; they are repository references, not official BC codes or a required sequence of investigations.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

The variety of organisms can be understood through classification and through the relationships that sustain ecosystems.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, diversity, grouping, and interactions of living things.

### BI-02

Materials that appear continuous to the eye are made from particles.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, the particulate nature of matter.

### BI-03

Thermal energy has sources and can move between places or materials.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, production and transfer of thermal energy.

### BI-04

Landscapes change as moving air, water, and ice act on them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, wind, water, and ice changing land.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use curiosity and close observation to identify questions about familiar phenomena that science can investigate. Make predictions informed by what is already known.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Questioning and predicting: curiosity, observations, investigable questions, and prior-knowledge predictions.

### CC-02

Suggest ways to investigate a question, consider ethical responsibilities, and use suitable tools safely. Observe local living and non-living things and collect simple data, using formal measurement and digital tools where appropriate.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Planning and conducting: inquiry planning, ethics, tools/measurement, local observations, and data collection.

### CC-03

Interpret local experiences and recognize First Peoples knowledge and perspectives as information sources. Classify and display data in drawings, tables, simple bar graphs, or other suitable forms; find patterns and suggest why results did or did not match predictions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Processing and analyzing data and information: local environment, First Peoples sources, classification, representations, and comparison with predictions.

### CC-04

Draw simple inferences from results and prior knowledge, consider whether an investigation was a fair test, and explain the value of evidence. Notice environmental consequences of one's own and other people's actions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Evaluating: inferences, fair testing, evidence, and environmental implications.

### CC-05

Apply learning beyond the original investigation, design projects with others, and propose or refine solutions. Use individual and shared efforts to care for people and places in the school and neighbourhood.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Applying and innovating: care, cooperative design, transfer, and new/refined ideas.

### CC-06

Represent findings and explanations in accessible forms such as diagrams and simple reports, using digital technology when useful. Express and reflect on personal or shared experiences of place.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating: representation of findings and reflection on place.

## Content

### C-01

Explore biodiversity in the local environment, including the characteristics of plants, animals, and fungi. Use similarities and differences to understand the variety of living things nearby.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, biodiversity in the local environment and its elaboration.

### C-02

Learn from local First Peoples knowledge about ecosystems and relationships among living things and their surroundings. The source's elaboration connects this knowledge with interdependence, energy relationships, and responsibility for environmental care.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, knowledge of local First Peoples of ecosystems; elaborations on ecosystems and stewardship.

### C-03

Understand matter as having mass and occupying space, with atoms as its building blocks. This introduces a particle account of materials without requiring advanced atomic structure.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, matter having mass/occupying space and atoms as building blocks.

### C-04

Investigate where thermal energy comes from and how it transfers. The elaborations illustrate sources such as sunlight and friction and distinguish conduction, convection, and radiation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, sources of thermal energy and transfer of thermal energy; corresponding elaborations.

### C-05

Recognize major landforms in the local area, including relevant local First Peoples knowledge. Explain observable landscape changes through erosion and deposition caused by wind, water, and ice.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, local landforms, local First Peoples landform knowledge, and erosion/deposition.

## Elaborations and boundaries

This grade's four main areas are ecosystems, matter, thermal energy, and land change. Do not substitute neighbouring grades' topics or add a required chemical model, equation-based heat calculation, or universal species list. Examples of food relationships and energy transfer in the elaborations support the core topics; they are not an extra prescribed investigation sequence.

Fair testing, evidence, ethics, and environmental responsibility belong within inquiry, not only in a safety note. Suggested practical work involving heat, materials, organisms, or outdoor places requires its own supervision and risk assessment.

First Peoples ecosystem and landform knowledge is part of the subject. Use appropriate local sources and relationships; the curriculum does not authorize reproducing or adapting community narratives or knowledge.
