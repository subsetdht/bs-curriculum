# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the distinct first-language French program. This is not French Immersion Language Arts or Core French; see the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program).

**Document language:** English (`en-CA`); the official source is in French. This is an original reference summary, not an authorized translation or a French instructional resource. Source locators are labelled English glosses of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Develop purposeful, critical reading and communication in French through interpreting and creating texts, cultural belonging, and understanding narratives and their relationships with identity.

**Prerequisites:** Prior first-language French learning provides a starting point, not an admission or proficiency rule established by this reference.

**Materials:** None are needed to read this reference. Later text selections need separate language suitability, accessibility, attribution, and cultural permissions.

**How to use:** Read [S1] as the authoritative Grade 3 source. Its authors, readers, and speakers work with oral, written, visual, digital, and printed supports, particularly informational texts, letters, and tales. The K-9 compilation title does not make the Grade 3 standards a band. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Meaning depends on how a text is organized and on its clues, words, and language conventions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), structure/clues/words in texts and language-specific rules.

### BI-02

Communication connects personal expression with social life. Using French to share preferences, feelings, and opinions can strengthen belonging to a Francophone community.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), communication as a social act and Francophone community belonging.

### BI-03

Tales explore human concerns that can remain meaningful across generations and places.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), enduring and universal themes of tales.

### BI-04

Indigenous narratives can deepen understanding of identity at personal, family, intergenerational, and community levels.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), Indigenous narratives and dimensions of identity.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Use roots, affixes, and textual clues to work out meanings and make inferences. Trace words or phrases that refer back to earlier information so that connections within a text remain clear.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): roots/affixes, inference, and textual referents.

### CC-02

Compare texts for similarities and differences, and distinguish factual statements from opinions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): text comparison and fact/opinion distinction.

### CC-03

Reconstruct a narrative's organization in order to summarize it. Notice how tales give human characteristics or behaviour to non-human characters.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): narrative reconstruction for summary and human traits of non-human characters.

### CC-04

Develop awareness of the role oral traditions play in Indigenous narratives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): oral traditions in Indigenous narratives.

### CC-05

Communicate accurately and fluently using studied strategies. Establish a purpose before planning a text and its presentation, and respond through questions, connections, preferences, or opinions while respecting others' ideas.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): precision/fluency, communicative intention/presentation, and respectful response.

### CC-06

Compose texts using complex sentences, the structures being studied, and relevant language conventions. Revise one's own writing and other people's texts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): complex-sentence composition, conventions, and revision of own/others' texts.

## Content

### C-01

Learn how informational texts introduce, develop, and conclude ideas, and how letters relate a sender, recipient, and message. Consider organization, register, and layout in written correspondence.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), introductory genre focus and textual organization: informational-text structure and written correspondence; corresponding elaborations.

### C-02

Explore tale characteristics, narrative sequence, and circular organization. The source connects circular structure with an Indigenous narrative example involving departure, a journey, and a changed character's return; this is not a universal formula for Indigenous storytelling.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: tale structure, narrative sequence, and circular structure; literary tools: characteristics of tales; elaboration of circular structure.

### C-03

Use linking expressions to communicate cause and purpose, and punctuation to clarify a text. The Grade 3 punctuation elaboration focuses on the comma.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization: cause/purpose linking expressions and punctuation.

### C-04

Develop spelling-pattern knowledge and use roots and affixes to understand words. The live page's spelling elaboration specifies noun plurals in -x and adjective agreement in gender and number; its word-formation elaboration includes prefixes and suffixes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), spelling regularities and language tools: roots/affixes; corresponding elaborations. See the source discrepancy below.

### C-05

Study sentence structures, word groups, pronouns, and word classes as tools for understanding and writing French. Elaborations include complex and negative sentences; noun, verb, and complement groups; personal, possessive, and interrogative pronouns; common and proper nouns; and the source's quantitative-adjective category.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: sentence structures, groups, pronouns, and word classes; corresponding elaborations.

### C-06

Relate verb modes and tenses to the genres being studied. The Grade 3 elaboration identifies the compound past and near future.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), language tools: genre-related verb modes/tenses and its elaboration.

### C-07

Learn communication and socialization strategies, including considering another person's viewpoint, explaining or clarifying meaning, and correcting one's own expression.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), studied strategies: communication and socialization, with elaboration.

### C-08

Develop reading strategies that combine prior knowledge, questions, important information, inference, and tracking references within a text. Use writing processes that move from preparing and drafting ideas through revision and sharing a finished text.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), studied strategies: reading and writing; corresponding elaborations, including the continuation of the Grade 3 content in [S2].

## Elaborations and boundaries

Informational texts, letters, and tales are the named Grade 3 focus, with exposure to other genres encouraged in the elaboration. Do not substitute the following grade's genre selection or grammar inventory. Complex-sentence writing, inference, textual-reference tracking, fact/opinion distinction, and revision of others' texts are meaningful features of this first-language curriculum, not the same expectations as Grade 3 immersion.

Textual "referents" concern cohesion within a text, not bibliographic citations. The writing-process elaboration's publication stage does not require public online posting, and no word count, reading-speed target, or prescribed book list is established here.

**Source discrepancy:** The live page [S1] identifies adjective gender/number agreement in its spelling elaboration. The elaborated DOCX [S2] instead describes agreement in gender and number with the subject. This record follows the live page for [C-04](#c-04), does not combine the alternatives, and does not infer a revision date from the difference. The core category of spelling regularities is unchanged.

Indigenous narratives, oral traditions, and circular organization occur in different curriculum components and remain visible here. The elaboration does not establish one structure or worldview for every Indigenous community. Access to, reproduction of, and retelling of cultural works require appropriate permissions and protocols.
