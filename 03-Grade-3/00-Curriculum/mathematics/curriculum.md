# Mathematics - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English. The official source reviewed is in English; neither fact determines the classroom language.

**Learning objective:** Use number relationships, representations, and reasoning to investigate whole and fractional quantities, patterns, measurement, shape, data, chance, and money.

**Prerequisites:** Experience with counting, grouping, addition, subtraction, and comparing objects provides useful starting points, not formal entry requirements.

**Materials:** None are required to read this reference. A later activity should identify its own accessible representations, tools, and materials.

**How to use:** Read with [S1], following the source locators for exact expectations and elaborations. Local IDs identify repository summaries, not ministry codes, assessment levels, or a sequence of lessons.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Fractions belong to the number system: they express quantities in relation to a whole rather than simply naming pieces of a picture.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, fractions as numbers representing quantities.

### BI-02

Flexible ways of separating and recombining whole numbers help learners understand and calculate with all four operations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, computational fluency through decomposing and composing.

### BI-03

Recognizing a regular increase or decrease makes it possible to describe how a pattern works beyond the examples already shown.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, regular changes in patterns and generalization.

### BI-04

Using agreed units makes descriptions and comparisons of objects' measurable features more useful to other people.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, standard units and attributes of shapes.

### BI-05

Possible results of an event can be considered in advance and compared in terms of how likely they are.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, likelihood and interpretation of possible outcomes.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Reason about quantities and connections, estimate sensibly, and develop mental strategies. Use models, relevant situations, and appropriate technology to investigate mathematical relationships.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Reasoning and analyzing: reasoning, estimation, mental mathematics, technology, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Build and apply understanding through inquiry, play, visualization, and problem solving with more than one strategy. Work with problems connected to place, stories, cultural practices, and local First Peoples and other community perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Understanding and solving: inquiry, visualization, multiple strategies, and situated problem-solving experiences.

### CC-03

Make thinking understandable through objects, drawings, symbols, speech, and writing. Contribute mathematical language to discussion and explain why a strategy, result, or decision makes sense.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Communicating and representing: mathematical discussion, justification, and concrete/pictorial/symbolic representations.

### CC-04

Reconsider one's reasoning, connect mathematical ideas with other learning and interests, and incorporate First Peoples worldviews and perspectives into those connections.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Connecting and reflecting: reflection, cross-curricular/personal connections, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Develop number sense through 1000. Use counting and place-value relationships to represent, compare, order, and estimate quantities, attending to hundreds, tens, ones, and the role of zero.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 1000; elaborations on counting, ordering, estimation, and place value.

### C-02

Understand fractional amounts through equal sharing and equal-sized parts of a whole. Connect concrete and pictorial models with fraction notation; the source includes regions, sets, and linear models.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, fraction concepts and its elaborations.

### C-03

Add and subtract within 1000 using flexible strategies and estimates. Separately, develop emerging fluency with addition and subtraction facts to 20; the small-fact fluency expectation is not a demand to recall every calculation within 1000.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, addition/subtraction to 1000 and addition/subtraction facts to 20.

### C-04

Develop the meanings of multiplication and division through grouping, sharing, and representations such as arrays. Connect these operations with one another, with repeated addition or subtraction, and with skip-counting.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, multiplication and division concepts; elaborations on models and relationships.

### C-05

Represent patterns that grow or shrink and describe their rules in words and numbers, starting from concrete experiences. Work with one-step addition or subtraction equations in which a number is unknown.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, increasing/decreasing patterns, pattern rules, and one-step equations with an unknown number.

### C-06

Use standard units when measuring length, mass, and capacity. Develop time concepts and relationships among time units, with estimation and familiar reference points supporting understanding.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, measurement using standard units and time concepts.

### C-07

Construct and compare three-dimensional objects by examining their faces, edges, vertices, and related two-dimensional shapes. Recognize that turning an object does not change its defining properties.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, construction of 3D objects and its elaborations.

### C-08

Collect and organize information in one-to-one bar graphs, pictographs, charts, and tables. Use comparative language to discuss chance in simulated events and interpret possible outcomes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, one-to-one correspondence in data displays and likelihood of simulated events.

### C-09

Work flexibly with combinations of coins and bills totalling up to 100 dollars. Connect amounts with ways of earning money and making payments, rather than treating money only as a counting exercise.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy: coins/bills to 100 dollars, earning, and payment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The official elaborations explicitly say that multiplication-fact memorization is **not intended at this level**. For addition facts to 20, they describe recall by most learners at the end of Grade 3; this is not a timed-test requirement or a guarantee about every learner's pace.

The time elaboration does **not expect telling time** at this level. Measurement elaborations introduce perimeter, area, and circumference conceptually, without formulas or calculation using pi. Do not turn these examples into an additional formula syllabus, impose an unsupported fraction-denominator limit, or replace one-to-one data representations with a later-grade requirement.

Examples and cultural contexts remain at [S1]. They are not compulsory projects or permission to reproduce First Peoples designs, stories, or practices. Future resources need their own program, language, accessibility, and rights review.
