# Social Studies - Grade 3

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](../README.md) > Social Studies

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations. See the [program overview](../README.md#choose-a-program) for applicability and language-of-instruction boundaries.

**Document language:** English; the source reviewed is in English.

**Learning objective:** Investigate local First Peoples and Indigenous peoples globally, using evidence, historical thinking, and respectful comparison to understand cultures, social organization, knowledge, and relationships with land.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with questions about community and place can help. This reference assumes neither a shared family history nor prior knowledge of a particular Indigenous people.

**Materials:** None are needed to read the reference. Later activities need appropriately selected sources, attribution, cultural permissions, and accessible formats.

**How to use:** The official Grade 3 focus is **Global Indigenous Peoples**, not a repetition of a solely local-community study. Consult [S1] for the complete expectations and elaborations. Local IDs are repository references, not ministry codes or a list of required peoples to study.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Learning about Indigenous peoples can strengthen respect for cultural diversity while revealing experiences that different societies have in common.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, multicultural awareness/respect and shared aspects of life.

### BI-02

Oral histories, traditions, and community memory carry knowledge across generations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, transmission of Indigenous knowledge.

### BI-03

Understanding Indigenous societies involves relationships among personal well-being, land, spiritual life, and ancestors.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, well-being of self, land, spirits, and ancestors in Indigenous societies.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Frame questions, gather and analyze information, and communicate supported findings or decisions. The inquiry elaborations develop source and map skills: using multiple sources, organizing and acknowledging information, and reading simple maps with directions, legends, and grids.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Social Studies inquiry processes and skills; key-skill elaborations.

### CC-02

Explain why a person, event, or place matters to particular people or groups. Question different kinds of sources and use their content and features to make inferences and draw conclusions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, significance and evidence.

### CC-03

Sequence evidence and events to explain what has changed and what has continued. Identify causes and consequences of developments, actions, and decisions.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, continuity and change, and cause and consequence.

### CC-04

Explain how beliefs, values, experiences, social roles, and worldviews shape different interpretations of people, places, and issues.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, perspective: beliefs, values, worldviews, experiences, and roles.

### CC-05

Make reasoned judgments about actions, decisions, and events, and consider what can be learned from them.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, ethical judgment.

## Content

### C-01

Explore the ways of life and cultural characteristics of local First Peoples and Indigenous peoples elsewhere in the world. Consider both distinctive practices and shared aspects of human life, rather than assuming that all communities are alike.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, cultural characteristics/ways of life and aspects of life common to peoples and cultures.

### C-02

Investigate connections among cultural and technological innovations of local and global Indigenous peoples, attending to their purposes and contexts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, interconnections of cultural and technological innovations.

### C-03

Learn about governance and social organization in local and global Indigenous societies. Compare arrangements with attention to the particular people, place, and time being studied.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, governance and social organization.

### C-04

Use oral histories, traditional stories, and material artifacts as evidence for learning about past First Peoples cultures, with attention to source interpretation and appropriate access.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, oral history, traditional stories, and artifacts as evidence.

### C-05

Explore relationships between people and their environments, including how people use, change, and relate to land.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, relationship between humans and their environment.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source provides possible peoples, topics, inquiry questions, and map skills in elaborations. These support planning; they do not prescribe an exhaustive world survey, a single required community, or a fixed project. Keep Grade 3's global and local Indigenous focus visible.

Treat Indigenous peoples as living, diverse communities, not as interchangeable or confined to the past. Historical evidence and present-day knowledge have contexts and custodians. A curriculum reference is not permission to reproduce an oral history, use a sacred object, copy an artifact, or retell a restricted story.

The holistic Big Idea summarizes a provincial framing; it is not a claim that every community expresses the same beliefs. Use community-specific sources and guidance rather than inventing a universal Indigenous worldview.
