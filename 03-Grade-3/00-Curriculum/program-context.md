# Grade 3 program context

[Curriculum index](../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 3](README.md) > Program context

**Jurisdiction:** British Columbia. **Local providers discussed:** SD43 / Coquitlam and the Conseil scolaire francophone (CSF). **Document language:** English. **Sources reviewed:** 2026-09-14.

This page explains curriculum applicability and language scheduling. It is not a curriculum record, a school timetable, or a determination of an individual learner's program. Provincial requirements and provider-specific guidance are identified separately.

## Provincial basis for the shared subjects

The [Required Areas of Learning in an Educational Program Order][P1], sections 1-2, includes Francophone education authorities, programs, and students and prescribes the common non-language areas for K-9. It distinguishes language arts rather than creating a different mathematics, science, or other non-language subject set for Francophone learners.

This supports the seven shared Grade 3 reference records: Mathematics; Science; Social Studies; Physical and Health Education; Arts Education; Career Education; and Applied Design, Skills, and Technologies. Their program metadata includes English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone.

The [Educational Program Guide Order][P2], section 2(b), formally designates French curriculum guides for Francophone and immersion programs in those areas. It separately identifies first-language French and French-as-a-second-language immersion guides. The [provincial French Immersion policy][P3], under program content, also requires immersion to parallel the English-language program in structure and content.

These are policy grounds for shared subject applicability, not an inference from finding a translated webpage. The seven records were checked against the English curriculum sources named in their metadata; this work does not claim a line-by-line comparison of every French guide. Their English authorship does not make them official French guides or establish English classroom instruction.

### Individual program boundaries

Section 3 of [P1] provides specified individual exceptions when its conditions apply. These concern a student who must have an IEP because they cannot demonstrate learning in relation to the expected outcomes, or a student receiving English language learning services who cannot demonstrate that learning because of insufficient English proficiency. They are not blanket exemptions for every learner with an IEP, every recipient of language-learning services, or every multilingual learner.

The records describe general provincial expectations. They do not determine whether an exception applies or replace an individual educational program.

## Grade 3 English Language Arts

### Provincial French Immersion requirement

The provincial immersion policy [P3], under Early French Immersion, requires English Language Arts **beginning in Grade 4**. Section 4(c) of [P1] likewise requires it for immersion students in Grades 4-9, within that order's scope. Neither establishes a Grade 3 requirement.

The policy's **recommended** language allocations allow French-only instruction through Grade 2 or through Grade 3, followed by an 80% French / 20% English allocation starting in Grade 3 or Grade 4. A Grade 3 transition is therefore possible, but neither that recommendation nor the Grade 4 requirement determines a particular district's Grade 3 schedule.

The [English Language Arts 3 record](english-language-arts/curriculum.md) retains `english-language` as its unconditional program tag. It can be consulted where a provider schedules Grade 3 ELA in Early Immersion, but that conditional use is not a province-wide Grade 3 requirement. It never replaces [French Immersion Language Arts 3](french-immersion-language-arts/curriculum.md).

### SD43 / Coquitlam implementation: unresolved for Grade 3

The reviewed [SD43 French Programs page][D1] and [French Immersion FAQ][D2] identify K-2 as fully French and give an 80% French allocation for Grades 4-5. **Both omit Grade 3.**

[Administrative Procedure 210][D3], section 6.2, sets a goal of Early Immersion learners reaching English Language Arts achievement equivalent to the regular program by the end of Grade 4. That achievement goal does not identify the year when English instruction starts.

The reviewed district sources therefore do not establish whether formal ELA is scheduled in Grade 3, when it begins, the French/English percentage in that grade, or whether delivery varies by school. Do not extend the K-2 statement, project the Grades 4-5 allocation backward, or use another district's timetable as SD43 evidence. A current school or district statement is still needed for those local arrangements; this is a source limitation, not a claim that SD43 has no such policy.

### CSF implementation: English from Grade 4

The CSF's [elementary-program page][F1], under its language section, places first-language English courses **from Grade 4**, after establishing French foundations through Grade 3. Its curriculum section confirms use of the provincial ministry's curriculum.

This is **CSF provider guidance**, not a rule borrowed from immersion and not evidence of a Grade 3 English course. The Grade 3 Francophone pathway therefore uses [Francophone Language Arts 3](francophone-language-arts/curriculum.md) and the seven shared subjects, without asserting a Grade 3 ELA course. The statement is not a prohibition on every incidental encounter with English before Grade 4.

## Program and language limits

The provincial policy [P3] describes Early Immersion entry at Kindergarten, with Grade 1 also possible, and Late Immersion beginning in Grade 6. The reviewed SD43 sources likewise place Late Immersion entry at Grade 6. There is no source-backed Grade 3 Late Immersion pathway here.

Section 4 of [P1] also distinguishes other-language requirements in Grades 5-8 for English-language and Francophone students. Those later-grade provisions do not establish a Grade 3 Core French course. Core French, immersion language arts, and Francophone language arts are not interchangeable.

Every current Grade 3 summary is written in English. The two French language-arts sources are in French; the seven shared-subject records and ELA record cite English sources. Document language, official source language, and classroom instruction language remain separate. French-language editions, learning resources, and assessments have not been authored.
