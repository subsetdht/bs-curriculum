# Mathematics - Grade 1

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 1](../README.md) > Mathematics

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** English-language, Early French Immersion, and Francophone; shared provincial subject expectations under [S2], sections 1-2.

**Document language:** English. This reference does not prescribe the language of classroom instruction.

**Learning objective:** Develop number sense and use mathematical reasoning to describe quantities, patterns, space, measurement, and everyday information.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with counting small collections and Kindergarten mathematical play may help; this is a planning suggestion, not a formal entry requirement.

**Materials:** No special materials are required to read this reference. Learning resources should state their own materials and accessibility options.

**How to use:** Read alongside [S1], using the source locators for the official expectations. Local IDs support resource mapping; they are not official BC standard codes or a teaching sequence.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Quantities through 20 can be understood in terms of tens and ones. Modelling addition and subtraction within 10 in several forms helps build fluency and understanding of operations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, numbers to 20 and computational fluency with numbers to 10.

### BI-02

Recognizing the part that repeats makes a pattern understandable and predictable.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, repeating elements in patterns.

### BI-03

Shapes and objects can be investigated by comparing and measuring their features.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, attributes of objects and shapes.

### BI-04

Organizing objects into graphs makes comparisons visible, with one object representing one counted item.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas, concrete graphs and one-to-one correspondence.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Reason about quantities, make sensible estimates, and develop mental strategies. Explore mathematics through real situations, models, and appropriate technology rather than treating it only as written calculation.

**Source locator:** [S1], Reasoning and analyzing, reasoning, estimation, mental mathematics, technology, and contextual modelling.

### CC-02

Investigate problems through play, inquiry, visualization, and more than one possible strategy. Include meaningful connections to place, stories, cultural practices, and local First Peoples and other community perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Understanding and solving, mathematical understanding, visualization, multiple strategies, and contextual problem solving.

### CC-03

Show and explain thinking with materials, pictures, symbols, and mathematical language. Listen and contribute to discussion, and give reasons for mathematical choices.

**Source locator:** [S1], Communicating and representing, discussion, justification, and concrete, pictorial, and symbolic representations.

### CC-04

Revisit one's thinking and connect mathematical ideas with other learning and personal interests. Recognize connections between mathematics and First Peoples worldviews and perspectives.

**Source locator:** [S1], Connecting and reflecting, reflection, connections, and First Peoples perspectives.

## Content

### C-01

Develop a sense of quantity through 20, including how numbers relate to one another and how 10 can be composed from different parts.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, number concepts to 20 and ways to make 10.

### C-02

Understand addition and subtraction processes through 20. Use objects and spoken explanations to show how a quantity changes.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, addition and subtraction to 20 and change in quantity to 20.

### C-03

Work with repeating patterns involving more than one feature or element, and understand when quantities are equal or unequal.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, repeating patterns and equality/inequality.

### C-04

Compare two-dimensional shapes and three-dimensional objects. Measure directly with non-standard units, considering both units that vary in size and units that are consistent.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, direct measurement with non-uniform/uniform units and comparison of 2D shapes and 3D objects.

### C-05

Represent data with objects in one-to-one graphs, and compare how likely familiar events are using appropriate everyday language.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, concrete graphs and likelihood of familiar life events.

### C-06

Learn about coin values and the use of money in exchanges.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content, financial literacy.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The source deliberately distinguishes the Big Idea about modelling operations **to 10** from Content about understanding operations **to 20**. Do not flatten these into a single limit, assume fluent recall of every fact to 20, or add a timed-drill requirement.

The official elaborations provide examples of counting, strategies, representations, measurement, and cultural connections. They remain at [S1]; this summary does not make every example a compulsory activity or resource. First Peoples connections require appropriate local context and permissions.
