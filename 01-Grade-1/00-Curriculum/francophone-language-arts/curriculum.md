# Francophone Language Arts - Grade 1

[Curriculum index](../../../curriculum-index.md) > [Grade 1](../README.md) > Francophone Language Arts

[Big Ideas](#big-ideas) | [Curricular Competencies](#curricular-competencies) | [Content](#content) | [Sources and rights](#sources-and-rights)

## Scope and use

**Programs:** Francophone, the French first-language program. This is distinct from English Language Arts, French Immersion Language Arts, and Core French.

**Document language:** English; the authoritative curriculum is in French. This is an original reference summary, not an authorized translation or a French classroom resource. Source locators use labelled English glosses of the French sections and topics.

**Learning objective:** Develop understanding and expression in French through speaking, listening, reading, writing, and visual communication, connecting language and texts with personal experience, culture, and Francophone belonging.

**Prerequisites:** Familiarity with stories, conversation, sounds, and print offers starting points, not admission or proficiency requirements. Confirm program eligibility and learning supports with the responsible school authority.

**Materials:** No special materials are needed to read this reference. Text selections and activities require their own language-suitability, accessibility, and permissions review.

**How to use:** Read with the official Grade 1 expectations and elaborations in [S1]. The curriculum introduction [S2] explains the first-language program's broader approach, not additional Grade 1 standards. Pair this record with the shared subjects in the [Francophone pathway](../README.md#francophone). Local item IDs are repository references, not official BC standard codes.

This record targets Grade 1 only. The K-12 scope of the introduction does not extend this record's source grade scope. Oral, written, and visual texts, in print and digital forms, support the learner's roles as reader, author, and speaker.

## Big Ideas

### BI-01

Language and texts give learners ways to explore their own experiences and the surrounding world. Using French can also nurture a sense of participation in Francophone communities.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), discovering self and world through texts; French communication and Francophone belonging.

### BI-02

Indigenous narratives offer ways to consider identity in relation to family, generations, and community, as well as the individual.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), Indigenous narratives and individual, family, generational, and community identity.

### BI-03

Understanding a message involves how it is arranged as well as what it says. Reading therefore requires attention to meaning and structure, not only working out individual words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), decoding, meaning, and text structure; content and structure in communication.

### BI-04

Beginning writers can convey ideas before they know how to write every word. Willingness to try communicating supports further growth in French.

**Source locator:** [S1], Big Ideas (English gloss), communicating in writing with developing word knowledge; risk-taking and language development.

## Curricular Competencies

### CC-01

Develop awareness of spoken language by separating and combining sound units. When engaging with a text, use pictures as clues and explain its main message in one's own words.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): phonological segmentation and blending, images as supports, and reformulating the main idea.

### CC-02

Bring personal and cultural knowledge to interpretation, making connections with lived experience. Follow a story's organization and key elements, including in Indigenous narratives, to help understand it.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Explore and reflect (English gloss): prior knowledge and personal/cultural connections; story elements and structure, including Indigenous narratives.

### CC-03

Participate in conversation by asking questions, expressing opinions, and linking texts with experience. Make sentence-level ideas connect logically and events follow a recognizable sequence; use these skills in short, clear oral presentations.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): sentence structure and logical connections, questions and opinions, personal links, event sequence, and short oral presentations.

### CC-04

Read familiar short texts fluently, taking cues from the punctuation at sentence endings.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): fluent reading of previously encountered short texts and sentence-final punctuation.

### CC-05

Use a model to shape a brief story, and bring accurate spelling of frequently used words into written communication.

**Source locator:** [S1], Curricular Competencies, Create and communicate (English gloss): spelling frequently used written words and composing a short story from a model.

## Content

### C-01

Explore the sound patterns within words, including syllables, individual speech sounds, and rhyme. Connect speech with writing through letter-sound relationships and the French sound combinations identified in the source.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), phonological awareness: words, syllables, phonemes, and rhyme; spelling patterns: sound-symbol correspondence and complex sounds.

### C-02

Learn how simple sentences and written conventions make ideas readable, including word order, word spacing, capitals, and full stops. Work with verb forms in the texts studied: the present indicative and awareness of past and future time.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), written conventions and language tools; elaboration on verb moods and tenses associated with studied texts.

### C-03

Understand how a text's visual features and layout help convey meaning. In stories, connect the characters, setting, time, and action with a progression from an opening through events to a resolution.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), textual organization and literary tools: text features, story structure, and story elements, with their elaborations.

### C-04

Build a repertoire for interacting, reading, and writing. This includes listening and taking turns respectfully; using prior knowledge, visualization, predictions, questions, and inferences to understand texts; and drafting and composing written messages.

**Source locator:** [S1], Content (English gloss), strategies under study: communication/socialization, reading, and writing, with their elaborations.

## Elaborations and boundaries

The reading-fluency competency concerns **previously encountered short texts**. Do not replace it with an English Language Arts benchmark or the immersion curriculum's simple-sentence expectation. The writing competency includes a model-supported story and accurate spelling of frequently used words, not independent mastery of every word or an imported spelling list.

The source's verb elaboration names the present indicative and notions of past and future in relation to texts studied. It does not establish a complete conjugation syllabus for Grade 1. Precise French sound examples, terminology, and other elaborations remain at [S1]; they are not reproduced as a separate teaching sequence here.

"Text" includes oral and visual communication, not only printed books. [S2] connects language learning with Francophone identity and intercultural understanding, and treats Indigenous perspectives as part of the curriculum, not a detached enrichment unit. Its examples from later grades are not Grade 1 requirements.

Use appropriate community guidance, attribution, and permissions for Indigenous narratives and other cultural works. Studying their place in identity is not permission to reproduce, adapt, or retell a community's story. This reference does not require public disclosure of a learner's personal or family history.
